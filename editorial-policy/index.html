Editorial Policy

CountryPilot's editorial policy is built around a simple principle: a guide should help a reader make a better-informed decision and should make clear where a changing rule must be checked. Volume, search traffic or publishing speed should not substitute for usefulness, source quality or transparency.

Last updated: October 5, 2026Editorial contact: hello@countrypilot.info

1. Who publishes the content

Articles are published under the organizational byline CountryPilot Editorial Team. The byline identifies the publication responsible for the page. CountryPilot is a small, founder-led desk: the founder writes the flagship guides, and a review desk fact-checks every guide against official sources before publication. It is not intended to imply that an article was written by a licensed lawyer, doctor, financial adviser or government official unless a specific qualified contributor is separately and accurately identified.

2. Why we publish

CountryPilot exists to help people research international mobility decisions: entering a country, studying, finding scholarships, understanding work authorization, relocating, arranging practical finances, comparing travel risks and preparing documents. Pages should serve that reader need even if the reader arrived directly rather than through a search engine.

3. How articles are created

Our workflow can include topic planning, collection of reference material, structured outlines, software-assisted drafting, editing, link review, formatting and later updates. Automation and AI-assisted tools may be used to organize information or support drafting. They are not treated as sources and should not be used as proof of a legal, financial, medical, educational or immigration rule.

Where a page contains changing requirements, the reader should be able to identify the external sources that govern or support the subject. We prefer to state uncertainty rather than invent a precise requirement that is not supported by a reliable source.

4. Original value standard

CountryPilot does not consider the mere existence of a long article to be evidence of quality. A useful guide should add organization, decision logic, checklists, source context, comparisons or practical explanation rather than simply restating another page. Repetitive framework language may be used where the same planning principle genuinely applies, but each article should still make its own topic, decision path and source route clear.

5. Source and evidence standard

For policy-sensitive claims, first-party sources are preferred whenever practical: governments, regulators, embassies and consulates, official institutions, universities, scholarship providers, exam bodies and the official sites of relevant services. International organizations and operational references can be authoritative for their own subject areas. Secondary sources may provide context or discovery value but should be identified as such.

6. Dates and freshness

A page's “updated” date indicates that the page itself was changed. It should not be interpreted as a guarantee that every external rule was re-confirmed on that date. We avoid using a fresh date merely to create the appearance of verification. For changing rules, users should check the live source immediately before acting.

7. High-impact topics

Immigration status, travel safety, health, insurance, banking, loans, employment and legal matters can materially affect a person's rights, finances or well-being. CountryPilot therefore treats these as research-sensitive subjects. We use caution with definitive wording, distinguish general information from individualized advice, and direct readers to the responsible authority or qualified professional where personal assessment is required.

8. Commercial independence

Advertising, affiliate arrangements or commercial relationships must not buy a favorable editorial conclusion. Sponsored material, if introduced, should be distinguishable from ordinary editorial content. CountryPilot does not accept payment to guarantee a visa, scholarship, admission, job, financial product approval or government outcome.

9. Corrections

Material factual errors should be corrected when identified. Readers can report an issue to hello@countrypilot.info. Where an error materially changes the meaning of a page, we may add clarification or correction context rather than silently preserving a misleading statement.

10. What readers should do with our guides

Use CountryPilot as a structured research starting point. Before submitting an application, paying a fee, booking non-refundable travel, entering into a contract or relying on a rule, check the live primary source and make sure it applies to your nationality, residence, route, institution, dates and circumstances.

Transparency: We disclose the possibility of AI/software assistance because Google and readers reasonably care about how content is produced. The important standard is whether the page adds useful value, identifies its sources and avoids presenting automated output as an authority.
