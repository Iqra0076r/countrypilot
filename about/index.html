About CountryPilot

CountryPilot is an independent information publication for people researching international travel, visas, study, work, scholarships, relocation, banking and practical country planning. Our goal is to turn scattered public information into structured guides that help readers identify what to check, which authority matters, and what questions to resolve before they make a decision.

Independent publicationGlobal coverageLast updated: October 5, 2026

What we publish

CountryPilot publishes country guides and topic-specific explainers covering entry rules, travel health and insurance, safety planning, education, scholarships, employment, work authorization, relocation, accommodation, banking, money transfer and related international-mobility topics.

What we do not do

CountryPilot is not a government authority, embassy, law firm, university, insurer, bank, lender, recruitment agency or visa processor. We do not issue visas, approve applications, guarantee admission, arrange employment, sell immigration outcomes or replace advice from a qualified professional.

Our editorial purpose

International rules are often spread across government pages, consular guidance, regulators, universities, official program providers and operational references. CountryPilot organizes that information into a practical research path. A useful page should help a reader understand the issue, know what evidence or eligibility questions matter, and reach the relevant primary source before acting.

We write for readers who may be comparing destinations, preparing an application, planning a trip, evaluating a scholarship, checking a job opportunity or organizing a move. Because many of these decisions affect money, legal status, safety or education, our pages are designed to separate general planning guidance from rules that must be confirmed with the responsible authority.

Who creates CountryPilot content

Articles are published under the CountryPilot Editorial Team byline. This is an organizational byline rather than a claim that every page was written by a named lawyer, immigration adviser, doctor, financial adviser or other regulated professional. We do not create fictional experts or use invented credentials.

How content is produced

CountryPilot uses a structured editorial workflow that can include topic planning, source collection, software-assisted organization, drafting and editing. Automation or AI-assisted tools may be used to help organize material, create outlines, standardize formatting or support drafting. Those tools are not treated as factual authorities. Readers should judge important claims by the cited source and, for changing rules, by the live page of the responsible authority.

We are intentionally transparent about this workflow because the value of a guide should come from its usefulness, source path, clarity and accuracy—not from pretending that software was never involved. Our Editorial Policy explains this in more detail.

How we choose sources

For policy-sensitive subjects we prefer first-party and authoritative material: government departments, immigration authorities, embassies and consulates, regulators, official universities, scholarship providers, exam bodies, international organizations and the official sites of the services being discussed. Operational sources such as IATA may be useful for travel-document checks, while reputable secondary sources can help readers discover options or understand context. Secondary sources should not replace an authority that controls the rule.

See our Sources Policy for the source hierarchy used across the site.

Updates, dates and corrections

CountryPilot distinguishes a page update from a claim that every underlying rule has been freshly verified. A page may be updated for design, structure, links or editorial clarity even when a government rule has not changed. For time-sensitive requirements, readers should use the cited live source immediately before applying, paying, booking or travelling.

If we learn that a material statement is inaccurate, outdated or misleading, our aim is to correct it promptly and, where useful, clarify what changed. See the Corrections Policy.

Independence and advertising

CountryPilot may be supported by advertising. Advertising does not give an advertiser the right to determine our editorial conclusions, eligibility explanations or source selection. When Google AdSense or another advertising service is used, advertising and data practices are governed by the relevant provider terms as well as our Privacy Policy.
