Why the advertised fee is not the whole transfer cost

An international transfer can have several price components. A provider may charge a visible fixed fee, build a margin into the exchange rate, route the payment through intermediary banks or deliver funds to a recipient account that charges an incoming-transfer fee. A “zero fee” transfer can therefore still cost more than another service if the exchange rate is less favourable.

Compare how much the recipient actually gets

The cleanest comparison is to use the same amount, currencies and timing, then compare the final recipient amount. The calculator starts from a reference exchange rate you provide and reduces it by the markup you enter. It then subtracts recipient-side deductions separately.

What is exchange-rate markup?

The markup is the gap between your reference market rate and the rate applied to the transfer. For example, a 2% markup means the conversion rate used by the provider is approximately 2% less favourable than the reference rate. The real pricing method can vary, so use the provider's displayed send and receive figures when available.

Do not forget compliance and transfer limits

Large or unusual transfers can require identity, source-of-funds or purpose-of-payment checks. Delivery time, transfer limits, supported currencies, refund rules and customer support can matter as much as the headline price, especially for tuition, deposits or relocation payments.
