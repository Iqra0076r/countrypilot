How to compare two legal-fee quotes

Do not compare only the headline attorney fee. Put each written quote into the same categories: consultation, professional fee, government filing fees, medical or translation costs, records, courier or travel, and any expected follow-up work. Then note what is included, what is excluded, when each amount becomes payable, and whether the quoted scope covers one filing stage or the whole matter. A lower headline fee can be more expensive if important work is excluded.

Ask for the scope in writing. Useful questions include whether responses to government requests are included, whether dependants are included, whether appeals or motions are separate, who prepares translations, and whether you will be charged again if the filing strategy changes. The planner is designed to make those quote differences visible rather than to tell you which professional to hire.

When to recalculate your budget

Re-run the estimate whenever the written scope changes, another family member is added, a new government filing is required, or a third-party cost becomes known. Keep the date and assumptions with each calculation so you can compare an early planning estimate with the final written engagement terms. Never treat the contingency amount as a prediction of what a lawyer will charge; it is simply a personal budget buffer.

Verify the professional before paying

For representation in a US immigration matter, verify the attorney with the relevant state licensing authority. USCIS also explains how to find authorised legal services and avoid immigration scams.
