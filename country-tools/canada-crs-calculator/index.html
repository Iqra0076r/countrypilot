Canada · Express Entry planning

Canada CRS Calculator 2026

Check your Express Entry Comprehensive Ranking System score with the official IRCC calculator, then use this guide to understand what contributes to the result and what changed in the current scoring system.

What affects your CRS score?

IRCC ranks eligible Express Entry profiles using age, education, official-language proficiency and Canadian work experience. If an accompanying spouse or common-law partner is included, spouse education, language and Canadian work experience can also contribute. Skill-transferability combinations reward certain mixes of education, language and work experience.

Additional points

Current additional-point factors include an eligible provincial or territorial nomination, strong French-language skills, qualifying Canadian post-secondary education, and an eligible brother or sister in Canada. IRCC caps the additional-points section at 600.

Job offers no longer add CRS points

IRCC removed the former 50- and 200-point CRS bonuses for arranged employment on 25 March 2025. A valid job offer can still matter for eligibility in some Express Entry programs or provincial streams, so it should still be reported where required even though it no longer receives those CRS points.

How to prepare before using the calculator

  1. Know whether your spouse or common-law partner will accompany you and whether they are already a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.
  2. Use the education level supported by your Canadian credential or valid Educational Credential Assessment where required.
  3. Have the CLB/NCLC equivalents for each language-test ability.
  4. Separate Canadian and foreign skilled work experience.
  5. Confirm any Canadian education, provincial nomination, sibling or French-language factors before claiming additional points.

How to use a CRS result productively

Treat the score as a snapshot of the information entered, not as a permanent personal rating. Record the date, your age, education level, language results, Canadian and foreign work experience, spouse factors and any additional-point factors used in the official calculator. If one of those inputs changes, calculate again rather than comparing an old score with a new invitation round.

A useful review separates factors you can realistically update from factors that are fixed. Language results, additional education, Canadian experience, provincial nomination and qualifying family or French-language factors can affect the CRS structure in different ways, but the practical value of any change depends on your actual eligibility and circumstances. Verify the current IRCC criteria before making a financial or immigration decision.

CRS ranking and Express Entry eligibility are different questions

The CRS ranks profiles that are in the Express Entry pool; it does not replace the eligibility rules for the underlying federal programs. Before focusing on a score target, confirm that the information in the profile accurately reflects your credentials and that you meet the requirements for the program under which you are entering the pool. If an Educational Credential Assessment, language test or other evidence expires or changes, update the official profile and then recalculate.

A score is not an invitation guarantee

Express Entry invitation cut-offs vary between rounds and can differ by program or category. A calculated score does not establish eligibility for an Express Entry program and does not predict whether or when IRCC will invite you.
