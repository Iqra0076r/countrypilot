Build a realistic first-year study budget

Tuition is only one part of studying abroad. A useful first-year budget should combine tuition, rent, food, local transport, insurance, visa fees, flights, study materials, deposits and initial setup costs. The calculator lets you use your own university and city estimates instead of relying on a generic national average.

Separate monthly costs from one-off costs

Rent, food and local transport repeat every month, while visa fees, flights and setup costs are usually concentrated around departure and arrival. Keeping these categories separate helps you see how much cash is needed before the first term and how much income or savings must support you during the year.

Budget beyond the minimum visa requirement

A government's proof-of-funds threshold is an immigration rule, not necessarily a comfortable living budget. Actual costs can be higher because of housing shortages, deposits, insurance requirements, books, winter clothing, transport and exchange-rate changes. Use an emergency buffer even when your visa route sets a lower minimum.

Check scholarship and work assumptions carefully

Do not count a scholarship until it is formally awarded, and do not rely on part-time work until you have verified the work conditions attached to your immigration status. Students should also check whether health insurance is mandatory and whether the university requires a specific form of cover.
