These living-expense amounts do not include tuition or transportation. IRCC requires applicants to show enough resources for tuition, living expenses and transportation, and for programs longer than one year applicants must also explain how the full duration will be financed.

What can support a proof-of-funds file?

IRCC lists examples such as tuition/housing payment evidence, education loans from a bank, scholarships, recent employment evidence, sponsor/support letters, Canadian or foreign bank accounts, GICs, and bank statements. The exact evidence should fit the source of your funds and your circumstances.

For debt planning, continue to Canada Student Loan Eligibility. For broader costs, use the Canada Tuition Financing Guide and Canada Scholarships Guide.

Source: Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC), proof of financial support guidance. CountryPilot is not affiliated with IRCC.

Build a traceable proof-of-funds file

A calculator total does not by itself prove that money is available. Build an evidence file that explains who owns the funds, where they are held, how long they have been available, and what each source is intended to cover. Keep tuition payments, scholarships, education loans, personal savings and family support clearly separated so an officer can follow the financial story without guessing.

If another person is supporting you, keep the support explanation consistent with the bank or income evidence you provide. If a large deposit appears, keep records showing its source. For a program lasting longer than one year, use this first-year calculation as a planning baseline and separately document how you expect later study and living costs to be funded.

Recalculate when your study plan changes

Run the calculation again if tuition changes, the number of accompanying family members changes, your study location or program changes, or IRCC publishes new living-expense amounts. Save the date of the official rule you used. That makes it easier to distinguish a current application budget from an older estimate prepared during university research.

Methodology & citation

The calculator combines the published IRCC living-expense amount for the selected family size with tuition, transportation and any optional buffer entered by the user. It does not assess source-of-funds credibility or Quebec requirements.
