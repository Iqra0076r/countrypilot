What should a relocation budget include?

The first month of an international move is usually more expensive than an ordinary month of living costs. A practical relocation budget should include rental deposits, advance rent, temporary accommodation, flights, visa or permit fees, insurance, local transport, phone setup, utility deposits, furniture or household basics and an emergency reserve.

Why deposits and setup costs matter

Housing can create the largest upfront cash requirement. Some markets require a deposit plus advance rent, and newcomers may also face temporary accommodation while completing local registration or finding a long-term home. Use the calculator with conservative numbers rather than the lowest advertised rent.

Keep an emergency reserve

A separate reserve can protect you if a job starts later than expected, a rental application falls through, exchange rates move against you or an unexpected medical or travel cost appears. The appropriate reserve depends on your situation, but it should be separate from money already committed to deposits, tuition or immigration fees.

Use one currency consistently

This tool intentionally does not convert currencies. Enter every figure in the same currency so the result remains meaningful, then compare the total with your available funds. Official immigration proof-of-funds requirements are separate and should always be checked with the destination authority.
