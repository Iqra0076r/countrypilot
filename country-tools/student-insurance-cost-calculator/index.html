What this calculator is comparing

The tool combines premium payments with the deductible or excess and any additional out-of-pocket costs you expect to pay. It is useful for comparing the financial structure of two plans, but it cannot measure whether the benefits, exclusions or insurer service are suitable for you.

Why the cheapest premium can be misleading

A low monthly premium may come with a higher deductible, lower medical limits, narrower provider networks or exclusions that matter to an international student. Conversely, a higher premium may include services that reduce other costs. Read the policy wording rather than comparing price alone.

Check visa and university requirements separately

Some destinations, universities or visa routes require specific insurance features or minimum coverage periods. A plan that looks inexpensive may still be unusable if it does not satisfy the relevant rule. Always verify current requirements with the official immigration authority and your institution.

Questions to compare before purchasing

  • What medical and hospital benefits are included?
  • What deductible, co-pay or excess applies?
  • Are pre-existing conditions excluded or limited?
  • Is emergency medical evacuation covered?
  • Are sports, mental health, dental or prescription benefits limited?
  • How are claims submitted and reimbursed?
  • Does the policy satisfy the required visa or university period?

Methodology & citation

The calculator compares user-entered premiums, deductibles and expected out-of-pocket costs. It does not rank insurers or assume that the lowest estimated cost satisfies a university or visa requirement.

Use it alongside the student's university waiver criteria, policy documents and any visa-specific insurance rules. Reviewed October 6, 2026.
