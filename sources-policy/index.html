Sources Policy

CountryPilot uses a source hierarchy so readers can tell the difference between a rule-making authority, an operational reference and a secondary discovery source. A source is selected for what it is qualified to establish—not simply because it appears high in search results.

Last updated: October 5, 2026Source corrections: hello@countrypilot.info

Our source hierarchy

LevelTypical sourcesHow we use them
Primary authorityImmigration departments, ministries, embassies/consulates, regulators, official legislation, official universities and scholarship providersPreferred for eligibility, fees, legal requirements, application steps and binding program rules.
Authoritative operational referenceInternational organizations, IATA/Timatic-derived travel guidance, recognized exam bodies and official service operatorsUsed for the operational question within that body's expertise, such as travel-document checks, health guidance or test rules.
Government advisory/contextForeign travel advisories and government guidance written for their own citizensUseful for safety, entry context and practical warnings, but not a replacement for the destination authority that controls immigration law.
Institutional/industry sourceBanks, insurers, universities, employers, transfer providers and other organizations describing their own products or processesUseful for provider-specific terms, fees and procedures; claims are limited to that provider.
Secondary research/discoveryDirectories, comparison portals and reputable specialist publicationsUsed to discover options or add context. We do not treat a secondary page as the final authority for a government rule where a primary source is available.

How sources appear on article pages

Article source sections may contain multiple source types. CountryPilot now labels the role of each source so a reader can distinguish, for example, a government advisory from a scholarship directory. The presence of a link does not mean every sentence on the page is controlled by that organization.

Destination-government sources

For visas and immigration, the destination's immigration authority, foreign ministry, embassy or consulate is generally the strongest source for application rules. Where an article does not yet list such a source, readers should treat travel-industry or third-country advisories as supporting references and locate the destination authority before making an application decision.

Changing information

Fees, forms, permitted activities, processing arrangements, travel advisories, health requirements, bank products and scholarship deadlines can change without notice. A source link can remain valid while the underlying page changes. For this reason, CountryPilot encourages readers to open the live source rather than relying only on a quoted number or old screenshot.

Source conflicts

If two sources conflict, the source with legal or operational responsibility normally takes priority for that issue. For example, a destination immigration authority generally outranks a travel blog on visa eligibility; an official scholarship provider outranks a scholarship directory on the program deadline; a bank's current tariff outranks a comparison article on that bank's fee.

Broken links and archived material

Government and institutional URLs move. A broken link does not automatically make the underlying topic false, but it is a reason to re-check the claim. We may replace broken links with the authority's current page or, where necessary, explain that a rule should be re-verified.

How to suggest a better source

Email hello@countrypilot.info with the CountryPilot page URL and the better primary source. We especially welcome official links that are more direct, more current or issued by the authority responsible for the rule.

Reader rule of thumb: for a decision that affects legal status, money, health or safety, use CountryPilot to understand the question and use the responsible authority to confirm the final answer.
