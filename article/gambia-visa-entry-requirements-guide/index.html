How to use this Gambia visa and entry guide

The Gambia's system is refreshingly simple on paper: visas from Gambian embassies/missions or from immigration officers at official ports of entry (visa on arrival), with ECOWAS and Commonwealth citizens visa-exempt for up to 90 days. The official fee is D3,000 (dalasi) or foreign-currency equivalent — though the US Embassy in Banjul notes The Gambia charges US citizens $200 on a reciprocity basis.

A practical warning: the Immigration Department's live website could not be verified as current (the official fee page survives via archive), and no official processing times, work-permit fees or student fees are published. For anything beyond a tourist entry, verify directly with the nearest Gambian mission. This guide was verified against the Gambia Immigration Department and the US Embassy in Banjul in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for the duration of your stay with blank pages. Visa-on-arrival issuance at Banjul airport and land posts requires the document to be in good condition — damaged passports are a common refusal reason at African ports of entry.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

ECOWAS membership and Commonwealth status decide most cases here: Nigerians (ECOWAS) and Britons (Commonwealth) enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Others use the embassy or port-of-entry route.

Transit and onward travel

Banjul is not a major transit hub, and The Gambia publishes no formal airside-transit regime — assume you need the same entry permission as a visitor even for a connection that exits the transit area. Confirm with your airline before booking split tickets through Banjul.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever Gambia's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the Gambia Immigration Department approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Yellow-fever vaccination is commonly checked on arrival in West Africa — carry the certificate. No other health document is a standard Gambian entry requirement, but regional health notices can add requirements quickly.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance is not a published Gambian entry condition, but it is strongly recommended — medical facilities are basic and evacuation cover matters. No official student-insurance figure is published.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

At Banjul airport or land posts, immigration officers can issue visas at the port of entry — have the fee ready in an accepted currency and keep the receipt. Officers may ask for the return ticket and accommodation; have them accessible. Keep the arrival stamp/entry document for the whole stay.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work permits and student routes exist but no official fee schedule is published — not for work permits, not for student visas, not for processing times. Do not budget from third-party tables. Contact the nearest Gambian mission or the Immigration Department directly for the current requirements and fees before making any commitment. A tourist entry never authorises work.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own Gambia permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether Gambia restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Tourist route: 1) Check whether your nationality is ECOWAS/Commonwealth-exempt (up to 90 days, no visa). 2) Otherwise apply at the nearest Gambian embassy/mission or plan for port-of-entry issuance with the D3,000 fee ready. 3) US citizens: budget $200 on reciprocity grounds. Work/study route: contact the mission first — no published fees or timelines exist, so get the requirements in writing before paying anyone.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming published fees exist for work/study. None are published — third-party tables are guesses.
  • US citizens budgeting D3,000. Reciprocity means $200 for US passports (US Embassy Banjul).
  • Arriving without cash for the port-of-entry fee. Card acceptance is unreliable.
  • Skipping the yellow-fever certificate. Commonly checked in the region.
  • Trusting the live GID website blindly. Its currency could not be verified — cross-check with the mission.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

The Gambia has no online visa portal — applications go through embassies/missions or are issued at ports of entry. Any website selling a 'Gambia e-Visa' is not official. The Immigration Department's own site could not be verified as current, so treat the mission as the authoritative channel.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in Gambia's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the Gambia Immigration Department for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where Gambia requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Have the visa fee ready in cash (dalasi, USD or other accepted currency) — the port-of-entry desk is not reliably card-friendly. Keep the payment receipt with your passport for the whole stay.

Departure and future travel

Extensions of stay are handled by the Immigration Department in-country — start the process before your permitted stay expires, not after. Overstaying risks fines and complications on departure.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) the Gambian mission nearest you — the only reliable source for work/study fees. 2) The GID fee page (archived) for the D3,000 tourist figure. 3) The US Embassy in Banjul for the $200 reciprocity figure. Distrust any site quoting work-permit fees or processing times — none are officially published.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per Gambia's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

The Gambia is simple for tourists — exempt, embassy, or port of entry, D3,000 — and opaque for everything else. If you're visiting, the system works. If you're moving for work or study, the mission is your only reliable source, because no official fees or timelines are published anywhere.
