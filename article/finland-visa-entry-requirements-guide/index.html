How to use this Finland visa and entry guide

Finland runs two separate tracks. Short visits use the harmonised Schengen C visa (€90, applied for at a Finnish mission or VFS centre). Anything longer — work, study, family — is a residence permit applied for online through the Enter Finland e-service, followed by identity verification and biometrics at a mission or VFS centre. There is no Finnish e-Visa and no visa on arrival.

The defining 2026 event: Migri raised processing fees on 1 January 2026 — a first study permit now costs €600 online (was €450) and a work-based permit €750 online (was €590). A widely reported proposal to raise the student funds threshold to €800/month has not been enacted — the legal figure remains €560/month. This guide was verified against migri.fi and um.fi in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Finland applies the standard Schengen passport rule: the travel document must have been issued within the last 10 years and remain valid for at least 3 months beyond your planned departure from the Schengen area, with at least two blank pages. Passports failing either test are refused at the application stage, so check issuance date — not just expiry — before applying.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Finland does not decide visa requirements itself for short stays — the EU's harmonised Schengen list applies. US and UK nationals enter visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Nationals of India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh need a Schengen C visa (€90) before travel.

Transit and onward travel

Airside transit through Helsinki without entering Schengen needs no visa for most nationalities holding onward tickets, but nationals of listed countries need an airport transit visa even without leaving the transit zone — check the Finnish mission's list for your passport. Any itinerary that exits the international transit area (hotel overnight, terminal change landside) counts as entering Finland and needs a full Schengen C visa or visa-free status.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever Finland's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Finland requires no vaccination certificate for ordinary visitors and operates no border health screening. Health documents are not checked at Finnish border control for short stays.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

For the Schengen C visa itself, travel medical insurance of at least €30,000 covering the whole Schengen stay is a mandatory application document (EU Visa Code). For residence permits, non-EU/EEA students must hold health insurance meeting Migri's minimum coverage amounts for the permit's duration. Migri publishes coverage minimums but no official annual cost figure — budget from private providers and verify the coverage meets Migri's stated minimums.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Schengen visitors are admitted for up to 90 days in any 180-day period — the clock counts all Schengen states together, not Finland alone. Border guards may stamp the passport and ask for the return ticket, accommodation and insurance. Overstaying the 90/180 rule triggers entry bans, so track cumulative days if you visit Europe frequently.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work means a residence permit for an employed person: €750 online (€950 on paper) as of 1 January 2026, with positive decisions averaging 23 days (Migri, May 2026). The permit is job-specific — changing employers generally means a new permit. Study means a first residence permit for studies: €600 online (€750 paper) as of January 2026, with 82% decided within 30 days (Migri 2026 bulletin). Students must show €560/month (€6,720 for a year) as a deposit in their own bank account — shared accounts and private sponsorship letters are not accepted. A visitor visa never authorises work or study.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own Finland permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether Finland restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Schengen C route: 1) Confirm you need a visa on the mission's pages. 2) Book the VFS/mission appointment. 3) Bring the €90 fee, €30,000-minimum insurance, itinerary and finances. Residence-permit route: 1) Start the application in the Enter Finland e-service and pay online (cheaper than paper). 2) Book identity verification and biometrics at a Finnish mission or VFS centre. 3) Track the case on migri.fi — use the official processing-time checker rather than agent estimates. 4) Collect the residence-permit card as instructed; the permit, not the application receipt, is what lets you enter.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Budgeting the old Migri fees. Study €450 and work €590 were the 2025 online fees — since 1 January 2026 they are €600 and €750.
  • Treating the €800 funds proposal as law. It is a government proposal, not enacted — the legal student figure is €560/month.
  • Filing on paper. Paper applications cost €150–€200 more than the Enter Finland online route for the same permit.
  • Using a shared or sponsor account for student funds. Migri requires the €6,720 in the applicant's own account.
  • Counting 90 days per visit. The Schengen 90/180 rule counts all Schengen states together.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Finland's digital front door is the Enter Finland e-service on migri.fi: start residence-permit applications there and pay online for the lower fee. Schengen C short-stay applications are not online — they go through Finnish missions or VFS Global centres with an in-person biometrics appointment. Beware lookalike 'Finland visa' sites: the only official channels are migri.fi and the mission/VFS route.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in Finland's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where Finland requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Cash of €10,000 or more (or equivalent) entering or leaving Finland must be declared to Customs — declaration is free, non-declaration risks seizure. Standard EU duty-free allowances apply. Declare anything uncertain; the penalty for non-declaration exceeds any duty.

Departure and future travel

Schengen visitors cannot extend a C visa inside Finland except in narrow force-majeure cases — plan to leave within the 90/180 allowance. Residence-permit holders apply for extensions through Enter Finland before the current permit expires; late applications can break the continuity of residence that counts toward permanent residence.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) migri.fi — fee pages, the processing-time checker and customer bulletins override everything. 2) um.fi (Ministry for Foreign Affairs) for Schengen C fee and mission information. Treat the €800/month student-funds figure as what it is — an unenacted proposal — and distrust any guide quoting pre-2026 Migri fees.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per Finland's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the student proof-of-funds requirement really €800 per month now?No. €800/month is a Finnish government proposal reported in 2026, not enacted law. The legal requirement remains €560/month (€6,720 per year) held as a deposit in the applicant's own bank account (Migri). Check migri.fi for any change before relying on a higher figure.
How long does a Finnish study permit take in 2026?Migri's 2026 customer bulletin: 82% of first study permits decided within 30 days; cases not decided in 30 days are estimated at 90 days. Use Migri's official processing-time checker for the current queue, not agent estimates.
Can I apply for the Schengen visa to Finland online?No. The Schengen C visa goes through a Finnish mission or VFS centre with in-person biometrics. Only residence permits start online via the Enter Finland e-service.
Why is the online application cheaper than paper?Migri's 2026 fee schedule prices the digital channel lower to steer applicants online — e.g. €600 vs €750 for a first study permit. Same permit, same decision; the discount is for using Enter Finland.
Do I need travel insurance for a Finnish visitor visa?Yes — the Schengen C application requires travel medical insurance of at least €30,000 covering the entire Schengen stay. It is a mandatory application document, not a recommendation.
The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Finland rewards applicants who use the right channel: Enter Finland online for residence permits (cheaper and trackable), mission/VFS for Schengen C visas. Budget the 2026 fees (€600 study / €750 work online), hold the €560/month student funds in your own account, and ignore the €800 proposal until it becomes law. Verify everything on migri.fi — it is the single controlling source.
