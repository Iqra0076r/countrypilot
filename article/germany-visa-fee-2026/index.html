Germany Visa Fee 2026

How to use this Germany visa fee guide

Germany runs two visa systems with separate fee schedules: Schengen Type C visas for short stays of up to 90 days, and national Type D visas for work, study, family reunion and the EU Blue Card. Picking the wrong system is the most expensive mistake — the two do not convert into each other, so a Schengen visa can never be turned into a work permit after arrival.

All fees below are the official 2026 figures. Treat the mission's own fee schedule as the final word before paying: fees are set by regulation and can change, so re-check the live page for your specific German embassy or consulate before any payment.

Schengen Type C fees (short stays)

The Schengen visitor fee for Germany is €90 for adults, €45 for children aged 6–11, and free for under-6s (2026, set at EU level). The reduced per-child fees apply per child — every traveller needs a separate application and appointment, including infants, because there are no family Schengen visas.

National Type D fees (work and study)

The national work visa fee is €75 (2026), paid at the embassy or consulate when you apply for a national Type D visa — the route for employment, including the Germany work permit routes, and for study (see the Germany student visa guide).

The EU Blue Card residence permit itself costs about €100 after arrival, paid in Germany once the national visa has done its job of getting you there. Long stays need the national visa issued by the embassy first — a different form, a different fee and a different timeline from the Schengen route.

Mandatory costs beyond the visa fee

The visa fee is only the entry ticket. For Schengen trips, travel medical insurance is a visa condition: the policy must cover at least €30,000 for emergency medical care, hospitalization and repatriation, and be valid across the entire Schengen area for the whole trip. A policy under €30,000 of cover, or one not valid Schengen-wide, means refusal — see our Germany travel insurance and health guide before buying.

Students face the biggest mandatory extra: the Sperrkonto blocked account of €11,904 per year (€992 per month) as proof of funds — unchanged since January 2025. A blocked account funded below €11,904 is the most common student-refusal reason and the most avoidable. Chancenkarte (Opportunity Card) job-seekers need €13,092 (€1,091 per month). Budget these before the fee, not after.

Processing times and when to apply

Schengen decisions typically come within 15 working days (up to 45 for complex cases). National visas take longer: about 4 weeks for Blue Card and pre-approved employment, and up to 12 weeks for other employment and for student visas, because the local Ausländerbehörde in Germany is usually involved — which drives the timeline.

There is no eVisa for Germany: first-time applicants apply on paper with biometrics in person, and biometric appointments are mandatory for first-time Schengen applicants (VIS, 59-month validity). Book the appointment early; the fee is the cheap part of a late application.

How you pay and what happens at the appointment

National visa fees are paid at the mission — check the specific embassy or consulate's fee schedule for the accepted payment method. Your passport must have been issued within the last 10 years and be valid at least 3 months beyond your departure from the Schengen area.

Scam avoidance is straightforward: any site charging far above the €90/€75 official fees is monetizing your anxiety, not your application. Pay only through the mission's designated channels. After arrival, remember the Anmeldung — register your address within 14 days.

Common mistakes that cost money

  • Paying a copycat site. The official fees are €90 and €75 — anything far above that is a private markup.
  • Underfunding the Sperrkonto. Below €11,904 the student application fails on proof of funds alone.
  • Buying under-covered insurance. Policies under €30,000 cover, or not valid Schengen-wide, mean refusal — not a warning.
  • Applying for the wrong system. A €90 Schengen visa cannot become a work permit — that is a fresh €75 national application.
  • Forgetting the €10,000 cash rule. Travellers carrying €10,000 or more must declare it at the border; failure to declare can lead to seizure.
  • One application for the whole family. Every traveller — infants included — needs a separate application, appointment and fee.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a Germany visa fee in 2026?

A Schengen Type C visa costs €90 for adults, €45 for children aged 6–11, and is free for under-6s (2026, set at EU level). A national Type D visa for work or study costs €75 at the embassy.

How much is the Germany work visa fee?

The national work visa fee is €75 (2026), paid at the embassy or consulate. The EU Blue Card residence permit itself costs about €100 after arrival in Germany.

Are there fee reductions for children?

Yes — on the Schengen route, ages 6–11 pay €45 instead of €90, and under-6s pay nothing. Each child still needs a separate application and appointment; there are no family visas.

How long does a Germany visa take in 2026?

Schengen decisions typically within 15 working days (up to 45 for complex cases). National work visas take about 4 weeks with Federal Employment Agency pre-approval and up to 12 weeks otherwise; student visas take 4–12 weeks depending on the mission.

Is there a Germany eVisa I can pay for online?

No — Germany has no eVisa. First-time applicants apply on paper with biometrics in person, and fees are paid through the mission's designated channels. Any site charging far above the €90/€75 official fees is a private markup, not an official channel.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Germany, pick the right system first: Schengen Type C (€90, 2026) for visits up to 90 days, national Type D (€75, 2026) for work or study — they do not convert into each other. Add the mandatory extras to your budget (€30,000-compliant insurance for Schengen trips, the €11,904 Sperrkonto for study), expect the Blue Card permit to add ~€100 after arrival, and re-check the mission's live fee schedule before paying anything.
