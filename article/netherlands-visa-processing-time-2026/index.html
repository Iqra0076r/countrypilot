Netherlands Visa Processing Time 2026: 15-Day Schengen, 1-Month Knowledge Migrants, 60–90 Day Students

The Netherlands decides Schengen visas in about 15 calendar days in 2026 — but residence permits run through sponsors on separate clocks: about a month for knowledge migrants, 60–90 days for students. Here is each timeline.

The Netherlands splits visa timing between two authorities. Schengen short-stay visas are decided by the mission in about 15 calendar days in 2026 (up to 45 for complex cases). Residence permits — for knowledge migrants, Blue Card holders and students — are decided by the IND (Immigration and Naturalisation Service), and the application is filed by the recognized sponsor (employer or university), not the applicant.

That sponsor-first design shapes every timeline: the applicant's clock starts when the sponsor's file is complete. This guide sets out the 2026 timelines for each track, the IND's monthly figures, and why chasing the sponsor beats chasing the IND.

The Schengen clock: ~15 calendar days

The Schengen Type C visa (adult €90, 2026) for short visits is decided in around 15 calendar days, stretching to 45 for complex cases. First-time applicants attend the mission or VFS in person for biometrics; the Netherlands offers no eVisa.

The Schengen track is the straightforward one: complete file, biometrics, decision. Apply with the same margin as any Schengen state — the 15 days is the decision, not the appointment wait, and peak-season VFS slots add their own lead time.

The IND clocks: sponsors file first

Residence permits are an employer/university-driven process. The recognized sponsor files with the IND through its own portal; the applicant then collects the MVV (provisional residence visa) at the mission if required, and may be interviewed there as part of the process.

Knowledge migrants and EU Blue Card holders: IND decisions are mostly made within one month (IND monthly figures, August 2026) — the fastest residence track in the Dutch system, reflecting the recognized-sponsor fast lane. The HSM/Blue Card residence fee is €423 in 2026 (indexed +4.4% from 1 January 2026).

Students: residence decisions take around 60–90 days, stretching longer in the May–August peak when universities file in volume. The university is the sponsor — its international office controls the filing date, so the applicant's leverage is entirely in getting documents to the university early.

What slows a Netherlands file down

  • Sponsor-side delays. The IND clock starts when the sponsor's file is complete — a slow HR department or university international office delays the start, not the decision. Chase the sponsor.
  • May–August student volume. The 60–90 day student range stretches at the long end during peak filing season.
  • Passport validity gaps. The passport must cover the requested permit period — renew early if yours expires mid-process, or the file stalls.
  • MVV interview scheduling. MVV applicants may be interviewed at the mission — the interview slot is a separate queue from the IND decision.
  • Unofficial 'priority' offers. ind.nl and the mission are the only official channels; no agent can guarantee IND approval or sell priority — the IND decides on the file, not on who submitted it.

When to apply: the planning formula

Schengen: apply 4–6 weeks before travel — the 15-day decision plus VFS slot margin.

Knowledge migrant / Blue Card: the employer should file 8–10 weeks before the start date — the 1-month IND norm plus the MVV/collection stage after.

Student: get every document to the university 4–5 months before the semester — the 60–90 day IND clock starts at the university's filing date, and May–August filings run long.

Tracking and the live picture

Schengen files are tracked via the VFS reference; residence files through the sponsor's IND portal — applicants track via the sponsor, not directly. The IND publishes monthly figures on regular migration: read the latest month for the current reality on knowledge-migrant and student decisions.

Official fees are receipted at the mission, VFS or via the sponsor's IND portal — the IND decides on the file, and no intermediary can change that.

Around 15 calendar days as the standard, up to 45 days for complex cases. First-time applicants attend the mission or VFS in person for biometrics.

How long does the Dutch knowledge-migrant visa take?

How long does a Netherlands student visa take?

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Fifteen calendar days for Schengen, about a month for knowledge-migrant residence, 60–90 days for students — with the sponsor's filing date as the true start of every IND clock. Feed the sponsor early, read the IND's monthly figures, and use only ind.nl and the mission.
