Schengen Visa Appointment Booking 2026: Timing, Rules, and No-Slot Fixes

The appointment is the front door of a Schengen application. Here is when slots open, which consulate actually accepts your file, and what to do when nothing is free.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20267 min read

The Schengen visa appointment is where the whole application physically happens: documents are handed over, biometrics are taken, and the €90 fee is paid. Everything else — the booking windows, the consulate rules, the waiting times — exists to get you into that chair. This guide sets out how appointment booking works in 2026, verified against the European Commission's Schengen guidance.

Quick Answer

You can lodge a Schengen application from 6 months before your planned trip and no later than 15 days before travel. Book at the consulate of your main destination — the country where you spend the most nights — or, if the nights are equal, the first country you enter. The decision normally takes 15 days and can stretch to 45 days in complex cases. Booking at the wrong consulate because its slots were free is itself a ground for refusal.

Fee €90 · Children 6–12 €45 · Decision 15–45 days · Updated 2026-10-14 · Reading time 7 minutes

When appointment booking opens: the 6-month / 15-day window

The Visa Code sets two hard edges around when you may apply. You cannot lodge earlier than 6 months before the intended travel date, and the consulate need not accept applications lodged later than 15 days before travel. Between those edges, consulates and their outsourced visa centres release slots on rolling schedules — often weeks or months ahead in peak season. The practical lesson: the 15-day edge is a deadline, not a plan. Summer and December travel means booking the appointment months earlier.

Which consulate to book: the main-destination rule

This is the rule most applicants get wrong. Your application belongs at the consulate of your main destination — the Schengen state where you will spend the most nights. If two or more countries tie on nights, jurisdiction falls to the first country you enter. A consulate that is not competent will not simply process your file kindly; as our Schengen refusal guide notes, applying at the wrong consulate is an established refusal ground. Before booking, count the nights in your itinerary and let the count choose the consulate — not the availability calendar.

What happens at the appointment

Arrive with the complete file. The Commission's baseline document list is the same everywhere: the application form, a photo, the passport, medical insurance, and documents proving purpose, means, accommodation, and intention to return. Biometrics (fingerprints and a photo) are collected if they are not already on file from the previous five years. The €90 fee is collected at or around the appointment — and it is not refunded if the application is refused. Work through our visa document checklist before the day; a blank field reads as a gap.

The fee you pay at the appointment

The standard Schengen visa fee is €90 for adults. Children aged 6 to 12 pay €45. The fee is set at EU level, so it does not vary between member states, and it is charged per application — every reapplication after a refusal costs another €90. See the full breakdown in our Schengen visa fee 2026 guide.

No slots available: what to do

Slot scarcity is real in high-demand cities, especially in spring and summer. What actually helps: check back frequently, because cancelled slots re-enter the pool; apply as early as the 6-month window allows rather than chasing last-minute availability; and book refundable travel only — non-refundable flights prove nothing at the appointment and a refused application costs you both the €90 and the tickets. Never solve a slot shortage by applying at a different country's consulate where nights were not counted: that trades a scheduling problem for a refusal.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Consulate-shopping. Applying wherever a slot was free, regardless of main destination.
  • Applying inside the 15-day edge. The consulate can refuse to accept a late application outright.
  • Arriving with an incomplete file. Insurance that misses the €30,000 minimum or the full-trip dates, funds without statements, or accommodation for only some nights.
  • Booking non-refundable travel before the decision. Refundable bookings are fine; irreversible ones are a gamble.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance can I book a Schengen appointment?

Applications open 6 months before travel. Booking the appointment as early as that window allows is the single best defence against slot shortages.

Can I apply at any Schengen country's consulate?

No. Jurisdiction follows your main destination (most nights), or the first country entered if nights are equal. The wrong consulate can refuse the file.

How long after the appointment does the decision take?

Normally 15 days from lodging, extendable to 45 days when further scrutiny is needed.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Book early, book at the right consulate, and arrive complete: the 6-month-to-15-day window is generous if you use it, the main-destination rule decides where you file, and a finished file with €30,000 of insurance and consistent documents is what the appointment is really checking.
