How to use this Australia visa and entry guide

Australia offers three doors for short visits, and choosing the wrong one is the most common error. Door 1 — Electronic Travel Authority (subclass 601): for eligible nationalities including the United States and United Kingdom, applied for through the Australian ETA app, AUD $20 service charge, no other fee. Door 2 — eVisitor (subclass 651): free, lodged online via ImmiAccount, for holders of eligible European passports. Door 3 — Visitor visa (subclass 600): for everyone else — including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh — applied for online, from AUD $250 for the Tourist stream since the 1 July 2026 fee increase (up from AUD $200).

The controlling source is the Department of Home Affairs at immi.homeaffairs.gov.au: the visa listing pages, the monthly visa-processing-times publication, and ImmiAccount/VEVO. This guide was verified against Home Affairs in October 2026. The July 2026 fee round raised charges ~25% across nearly all categories — older guides quoting AUD $200 for the 600 or AUD $2,000 for the student visa are out of date.

Passport validity and document condition

Australia does not impose a strict six-month passport-validity rule: your passport must be valid for the duration of your stay. That said, arriving with weeks of validity left is asking for trouble at check-in — renew early, because airlines apply their own caution on top of the legal minimum.

Digital linkage matters more than paper here: the ETA, eVisitor and Visitor 600 are all electronic grants linked to a specific passport number — nothing is stamped or stuck in. Renew the passport and the visa does not follow it; you must apply again and update your details. Verify the grant in VEVO (Visa Entitlement Verification Online) before departure.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Match your passport to the right door. Subclass 601 (ETA): citizens of listed countries (US, UK, Canada, Japan, Singapore, South Korea, Malaysia, Brunei, Taiwan, Hong Kong among them) — apply in the Australian ETA app, AUD $20 service charge, 12-month validity, up to 3 months per visit. Subclass 651 (eVisitor): eligible European passport holders — free via ImmiAccount, 12-month validity, up to 3 months per visit. Subclass 600 (Visitor): everyone else, including all five of our core nationalities — Tourist, Business Visitor, Sponsored Family and ADS streams; Tourist stream from AUD $250 (1 July 2026), usually 3 months per stay and up to 12 months in some cases, multiple entries common.

British-passport holders qualify for the 601 or the free 651 — but holders of other British passport types (BNO, BOTC, BOC) generally need the 600. Check the eligibility lists on Home Affairs rather than assuming.

Transit and onward travel

Australia requires most transit passengers to hold a Transit visa (subclass 771) — free of charge — unless they already hold a valid 601, 651, 600 or another eligible visa. The 771 requires you to remain airside and depart within 72 hours; it is applied for through ImmiAccount like any other visa.

Do not assume a short layover in Sydney or Melbourne is visa-free: Australia has no visa-free airside transit for most nationalities. Confirm your requirement on Home Affairs with the exact itinerary — being refused boarding at origin for lack of a 771 is a routine, avoidable failure.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

The Australian Border Force (ABF) assesses the genuine visitor requirement: is this a temporary stay for the stated purpose? Carry the passport linked to your electronic grant, a return or onward ticket, proof of funds, accommodation details, and evidence of ties to home (employment, enrollment, family).

Officers pay particular attention to the 600's conditions — especially condition 8101 (no work) and, where imposed, condition 8503 (no further stay), which blocks onshore applications for another visa. Know which conditions are on your grant (check VEVO) before you fly, because breaching one can lead to cancellation at the border.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Australia sets no general vaccination requirement for short-term visitors and no health document is checked at the border for an ETA, eVisitor or Visitor 600 trip. Health examinations apply to longer or permanent visas — not to holidays.

The one health-adjacent rule to know: visa holders must be free from tuberculosis as a visa condition on several categories. For a standard short visit this is a background condition, not a document to carry. If Home Affairs ever requests a health examination for your application, it will say so in ImmiAccount — anything else is not a requirement.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is not an entry condition for the 601, 651 or 600 — but Australia's remoteness makes it unusually important: medical evacuation from a regional area can cost tens of thousands of dollars, and visitors pay full price for hospital care. Buy comprehensive cover with high medical limits and evacuation before departure.

For students the position is mandatory: Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) is required for the entire stay on a Student visa (subclass 500), from a government-authorised provider. Single cover runs roughly AUD $630–$800/year (12-month policy, 2026 comparisons: ahm ~$631, nib ~$632, Allianz Care ~$665, Bupa ~$698); couples and families pay several thousand. Some work visas require Overseas Visitor Health Cover (OVHC) — check the conditions on your specific grant.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

On the 601 and 651, each visit is capped at 3 months — the grant itself is typically valid 12 months with multiple entries. On the Visitor 600, the stay is usually 3 months per visit, though longer periods up to 12 months are granted in some circumstances; the grant letter states your limit.

Watch the conditions on the grant: 8101 means no work (unpaid volunteering is allowed only where no Australian resident would otherwise be paid for it), and 8503 'no further stay' — where imposed — prevents applying for most other visas while in Australia. Overstaying any of these can trigger a 3-year exclusion period on future applications. Check and obey the exact conditions in VEVO.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A visitor grant never authorizes work for an Australian employer — and 'working remotely for my foreign employer' while in Australia sits in a grey area best avoided on a 600. Real work needs a work visa: the Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482) replaced the old TSS on 7 December 2024 and costs from AUD $4,015 for the main applicant (1 July 2026, up from AUD $3,210).

Study beyond 3 months needs the Student visa (subclass 500): from AUD $2,500 (1 July 2026 — the fourth increase in three years, from AUD $710), plus mandatory OSHC and proof of funds of AUD $29,710/year for the primary applicant plus tuition and travel (requirement in force since 10 May 2024). The 1 July 2026 fee round lifted charges ~25% across the board with no advance announcement — budget from the current Home Affairs schedule, not from memory.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Australian citizens — including dual nationals — must enter and leave Australia on an Australian passport. A dual Australian/UK citizen cannot use an ETA or eVisitor on the British passport; carriers are required to enforce this, and attempting it causes denied boarding, not a waiver at the airport.

Every traveller needs their own authorization — children cannot be included on a parent's ETA or visa, and each child needs their own passport. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter; while not a statutory entry requirement, ABF officers may question the arrangement.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

601/651 route: 1) Confirm eligibility on Home Affairs. 2) For the 601, apply in the Australian ETA app (AUD $20 service charge); for the 651, lodge free via ImmiAccount. 3) Wait for the grant — often fast — and verify it in VEVO. You must be outside Australia both when you apply and when the decision is made. 600 route: 1) Choose the right stream (Tourist, Business Visitor, Sponsored Family, ADS). 2) Lodge online via ImmiAccount with passport biodata scan, funds evidence, itinerary and ties. 3) Pay from AUD $250 (Tourist stream, 1 July 2026; onshore Tourist-stream applications cost AUD $630). 4) Provide biometrics only if Home Affairs requests them. 5) Check the grant letter's conditions (8101/8503) in VEVO before flying.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Paying a third-party site for an ETA/eVisitor. The 651 is free on ImmiAccount and the 601 costs AUD $20 in the official app — intermediary sites add large service fees for the same lodgement.
  • Applying for the 600 when the free 651 would do (eligible European passports) — or assuming the 651 covers non-European passports.
  • Quoting pre-July-2026 fees. The ~25% fee hike moved the 600 from AUD $200 to $250 and the student visa to AUD $2,500.
  • Ignoring grant conditions. Working on an 8101 condition or overstaying a 3-month stay can mean cancellation and a 3-year exclusion.
  • Renewing the passport after the grant. The electronic visa is linked to the old number — update or re-apply.
  • Assuming transit is visa-free. Most nationalities need the free 771 transit visa.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Home Affairs assesses the genuine temporary entrant requirement: is the visit really temporary, and are the funds real? A strong 600 file shows consistent income or savings over several months (not a sudden lump deposit), a clear purpose tied to dates (event, family occasion, itinerary), and strong incentives to return — ongoing employment, studies, property, family.

Previous travel history to Australia or other developed countries, complied with, is the single best supporting evidence. Address any prior refusal head-on with new evidence rather than re-lodging the same documents; case officers can see the full history in the system.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Australia's system is fully digital: the Australian ETA app (official, for the 601), ImmiAccount (for the 651, 600, 771 and everything else), and VEVO to check any grant's status and conditions. There are no stickers, stamps or labels — the grant lives in the system against your passport number.

Scam avoidance: the 651 is free and the 601 costs AUD $20 — any site charging far more for 'ETA processing' is a markup intermediary, not a government channel. No one can expedite a Home Affairs decision for a fee. Keep your ImmiAccount credentials: you will need them for extensions, condition checks and future applications.

Translations, certifications and document format

Typical 600 documents: passport biodata-page scan, bank statements or payslips, employment letter, detailed itinerary, evidence of ties to home, and invitation details for family visits. The Sponsored Family stream additionally requires the Australian sponsor's undertaking (and possibly a security bond); the Business Visitor stream needs evidence of the business purpose.

Documents not in English need certified English translations (NAATI-accredited translators are the standard in Australia). Upload clear colour scans — poor-quality images are a common cause of processing delays and requests for re-submission.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Australia does not interview visitor-visa applicants as a matter of course, and biometrics are collected only if Home Affairs requests them — many 600 applicants are never asked. If requested, you attend a collection centre for fingerprints and photograph; the request arrives through ImmiAccount with a deadline, so monitor the account, not just email.

At the border, ABF officers can question travellers, examine baggage and cancel visas where conditions are breached or the traveller is not a genuine visitor. SmartGates (automated passport control) are available to eligible ePassport holders and usually mean no officer contact at all.

Families and group travel

Every traveller needs their own ETA, eVisitor or visa — children cannot ride on a parent's grant, and each child needs their own passport. Family members lodge separate applications (a group can be managed through one ImmiAccount), and each is decided individually.

For the Sponsored Family stream, the Australian sponsor's obligations attach per visitor — understand the undertaking (including any security bond) before sponsoring relatives. If one family member is refused, address that refusal's reasons before re-lodging rather than submitting an identical file.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Carrying AUD$10,000 or more (or foreign equivalent) into or out of Australia must be declared — the declaration is free; non-declaration risks seizure. Australia's biosecurity regime is among the world's strictest: declare all food, plant material, animal products, wooden items and hiking gear soiled with earth. On-the-spot fines apply for undeclared risk goods, and detector dogs meet every international flight.

Duty-free allowances apply per adult (alcohol, tobacco); check the ABF site for current quantities. Medications should be in original packaging with a prescription for controlled substances.

Departure and future travel

Check the conditions on your grant before assuming you can extend: where condition 8503 ('no further stay') is imposed on a 600, you generally cannot apply for another visa while in Australia and must depart first. Where it is not imposed, onshore extensions are possible but not guaranteed — apply before the current stay expires.

Overstaying a 600 can trigger a 3-year exclusion period for future visa applications — a severe consequence for what is often a casual overstay. A clean record of entries and timely departures is the foundation of every future Australian application.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) immi.homeaffairs.gov.au visa-listing pages for eligibility, charges and conditions — they override everything. 2) The monthly visa-processing-times publication for timelines. 3) VEVO for your own grant's conditions. Agent blogs and forums are fine for process colour, but the 1 July 2026 fee round (~25% increases, unannounced) invalidated a large share of older guides — any page still quoting AUD $200 for the Visitor 600 or AUD $2,000 for the Student 500 is out of date as of October 2026.

Never rely on a single published processing figure: use Home Affairs' interactive tool for the current distribution on your subclass.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid for the whole stay; electronic grant (601/651/600) verified in VEVO against this passport number
  • Grant conditions checked — especially 8101 (no work) and 8503 (no further stay)
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation details accessible offline
  • Funds evidence and ties-to-home documents packed
  • Travel insurance with Australian medical + evacuation cover
  • AUD$10,000+ declaration prepared if applicable; biosecurity goods declared
  • 771 transit visa secured if transiting without an eligible visa
  • Certified English translations for non-English documents

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for Australia, or will an ETA do?

It depends on your passport. Eligible nationalities (including the US and UK) use the ETA (subclass 601, AUD $20 via the official app) or the free eVisitor (subclass 651, eligible European passports). Nationals of India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines, Bangladesh and other non-eligible countries need a Visitor visa (subclass 600, from AUD $250 since 1 July 2026). Check the eligibility lists on Home Affairs.

How long does an Australian visitor visa take?

Home Affairs' August 2026 monthly publication puts combined Visitor visas (600/601/651) at under 1 day median — but that blends the near-instant ETAs with 600s, which vary by nationality and season. Use the department's live interactive tool for your subclass, and lodge 600 applications 6–8 weeks before travel in busy periods.

How long can I stay in Australia per visit?

Up to 3 months per visit on the ETA (601) and eVisitor (651). The Visitor 600 is usually 3 months per stay, with up to 12 months granted in some circumstances. The grant letter states your limit — and its conditions.

Can I work in Australia on a visitor visa?

No. Visitor grants carry condition 8101 (no work). Real work needs a work visa such as the Skills in Demand (subclass 482, from AUD $4,015 since 1 July 2026). Breaching 8101 can lead to visa cancellation.

What changed for Australian visas in 2026?

On 1 July 2026, visa application charges rose ~25% across nearly all categories with no advance announcement: the Visitor 600 went from AUD $200 to $250, the Student 500 from AUD $2,000 to $2,500, and the Skills in Demand 482 from AUD $3,210 to $4,015. Budget from the current Home Affairs schedule.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Australia, match your passport to the right door: ETA 601 (AUD $20) or free eVisitor 651 for eligible nationalities, Visitor 600 (from AUD $250) for everyone else — including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh. Everything is electronic and linked to your passport number, so verify the grant in VEVO, respect the 3-month stays and the 8101/8503 conditions, and budget from the post–1 July 2026 fee schedule. Confirm charges and processing times on Home Affairs before you lodge.
