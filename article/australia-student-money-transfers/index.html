Decide which direction the money is moving

Funding an Australian student account from overseas and sending Australian dollars home are different payment routes. Check that the provider supports the sending country, destination, currency and payment method you need. Do not assume a provider available to an Australian resident can also onboard a parent abroad.

For tuition, start with the university’s payment instructions in its official student portal. Confirm the required currency, invoice reference and deadline before comparing services. Ask the university whether it requires payment through a designated channel.

Compare the final recipient amount

Moneysmart advises comparing the exchange rate, fees, delivery method and transfer time. Rates and costs vary between providers. The ACCC also recommends comparing supplier quotes and cautions that commercial comparison websites may not include every option or be independent.

Request quotes at approximately the same time. Record the total debited from the sender and the amount expected to reach the recipient after disclosed charges. If the provider cannot state whether intermediary or receiving-bank fees apply, leave that uncertainty visible.

Check the provider and recipient

Moneysmart says Australian money transfer services must be registered with AUSTRAC and links to its registration search. Check the legal business name. Registration is not a promise that a transfer is risk-free or that a particular quote is good value.

Independently confirm changed bank details using the university’s or recipient’s known contact channel. A message claiming an urgent tuition discount is not a substitute for the official invoice. Never send tuition to a personal account simply because an agent supplied the details.

Allow for the university’s reconciliation time

A provider’s delivery estimate and the date a university marks an invoice paid are separate checkpoints. Ask the finance office how long it normally takes to match payments, and include the exact student reference requested. Keep the quote, confirmation, transfer reference and invoice together.

Before authorising, ask whether the rate is fixed, when the quote expires, how cancellation works and what happens if a beneficiary detail is wrong. If the deadline is close, contact the university about an approved payment route rather than choosing solely on the lowest advertised price.

If a transfer is delayed

Contact the provider through its official support channel with the transfer reference. Ask whether it needs information from the sender, whether payment has been released and what trace or complaint process is available. Avoid starting a duplicate payment until the first payment’s status is clear.

Moneysmart directs consumers to the provider first and, where the provider is an AFCA member, to the Australian Financial Complaints Authority if the response does not resolve the issue. For a service contracted overseas, check the complaint route in that service’s own terms.

Calculate the true transfer cost

Compare providers using the same sender amount and the same receiving currency. The most useful figure is normally the amount that reaches the recipient after the quoted exchange rate and disclosed fees, not the size of a discount or the word “free” in an advertisement. Save a screenshot or PDF of the quote so you can compare like with like.

Verify the payment route before sending

For tuition, use the payment instructions published by the university or education provider and confirm the beneficiary name, account details, reference format and accepted currency. A correct transfer with a missing student reference can still take longer to reconcile, so treat the payment reference as part of the payment details rather than an optional note.

For living expenses, verify the recipient independently before sending a large amount. If a message says bank details have changed, confirm the change through a known contact method rather than replying to the same message. Urgency, secrecy and requests to move funds through an unfamiliar account are reasons to stop and verify.

Plan for deadlines, delays and refunds

Do not schedule a tuition transfer at the last possible moment. Build in time for provider processing, banking checks and the university's own reconciliation process. Keep the invoice, quote, transfer confirmation and recipient reference together so a delayed payment can be traced without reconstructing the transaction later.

Before paying, also check the refund route. A refund may follow a different exchange rate or fee structure from the original payment, so ask where a refund would be sent and what documentation would be required. Frequently Asked Questions

Is a zero-fee transfer always cheapest?

No. Compare the exchange rate and the final recipient amount for the same sender budget, including disclosed third-party charges.

Can I use any transfer provider to pay tuition?

Check your university’s instructions first. Confirm the accepted channel, recipient details, reference and currency before sending.
