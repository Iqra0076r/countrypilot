How to use this Cameroon visa and entry guide

Visa rules are not a single yes-or-no fact attached to a destination — they depend on your passport, purpose, length of stay, transit airports and sometimes recent travel history. Start with those facts before reading a visa list. The entry-status table below is the fast answer for the most common passports; the detail follows it. Always confirm the decisive rule on the official channel — Cameroon official eVisa portal — before paying or booking.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for at least six months beyond your intended stay with blank pages.

Transit and onward travel

Douala/Yaoundé airside transit on a through ticket normally needs no visa — but given the mandatory eVisa regime, confirm with your airline before booking split tickets.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Bank statements are reported as required with no fixed amount (2026, secondary). Carry them.

Common documents beyond the visa itself: onward or return ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can support yourself. Print everything — phone batteries die at the worst moments.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

A yellow fever vaccination certificate is mandatory for all travellers (reported, multiple sources, 2026). Get vaccinated at least 10 days before travel — this is enforced.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance requirements are not documented; a yellow fever certificate is mandatory for all travellers (reported, multiple sources, 2026).

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Your permitted stay is set by the visa, stamp or entry record — visa validity and authorised duration are not necessarily the same thing. Read the entry stamp and calculate days using the method specified for your route. Overstays in Cameroon can mean fines, detention or a future entry ban — know your date.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work and study need separate Cameroonian authorizations with local sponsorship — the short-stay eVisa never authorizes employment. Long-stay fees are not verified; confirm officially.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Minors generally need their own passport and sometimes a parental consent letter — check the requirements for your departure country too. Dual nationals should check the rules for the passport they plan to enter on, since visa treatment follows the document presented at the border. Refugees, stateless persons and emergency travel-document holders should confirm with Cameroon’s embassy directly, as standard routes may not apply.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

  1. Apply for the mandatory eVisa at evisacam.cm — no VoA exists.
  2. Confirm the current fee (CFA 100,000/150,000 cited — verify).
  3. Get the yellow fever vaccination and certificate (mandatory, reported).
  4. Upload passport scan, photograph and travel details.
  5. Apply at least two weeks ahead (reported 72h/24h processing).

The eVisa needs a passport scan, photograph, yellow fever certificate and travel details on the official portal. Have bank statements ready for the border.

Only use the official channel — Cameroon official eVisa portal. Copycat sites charge markups or harvest passport data. An eVisa approval is an email or a portal record: carry the confirmation and check it shows your name, passport number and permitted entries.

Translations, certifications and document format

Documents not in the local or accepted language may need certified translations — the official checklist states the rule. Certifications, bank statements and invitation letters should be recent (typically within 3 months). Photograph standards matter more than people expect: follow the portal’s specification exactly.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Biometric capture is not documented for the eVisa. Expect standard checks on arrival.

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs their own application or authorisation — visas are not transferable within a family or group. Apply together where the portal allows, and carry the same document set for each person. With minors, bring birth certificates or consent documentation in addition to passports.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

A visa is not a customs clearance. Declare cash above the local reporting threshold, check restricted items before packing (medications, drones, satellite equipment and certain food products are common flashpoints), and know that border officials can inspect devices. Keep receipts for high-value items you intend to bring back out.

Departure and future travel

Before leaving, confirm your exit is recorded — keep boarding passes and entry/exit stamps. If you overstayed or had any immigration incident, note it: future applications to Cameroon and other countries ask about it. Some nationalities need to deregister or obtain exit clearance after long stays; check before a long-term visit.

How to research a route without getting misled

Rank sources by authority: Cameroon’s immigration authority and foreign ministry first, then Timatic/IATA, then consular posts, then recent reputable reporting. Treat aggregator visa sites as leads, not answers — they go stale. If two credible sources conflict, say so in your notes and confirm with the authority; this guide flags conflicts the same way.

A final document audit before travel

Forty-eight hours before departure, check the passport number on the visa or authorisation, validity dates, name spelling, permitted entries and any conditions. Confirm transit airports and terminals have not changed. Put the approval, insurance and accommodation in an offline folder and print critical documents when the destination or airline may expect paper evidence.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Cameroon, the mandatory eVisa at evisacam.cm is the only route — no visa on arrival since April 2023. Verify the cited CFA 100,000/150,000 fees on the portal, and treat the yellow fever certificate as non-negotiable: it's the most-enforced requirement in the file.
