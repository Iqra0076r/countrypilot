How to use this F-1 fee guide

Studying in the United States as an F-1 student means two mandatory payments to the US government: the $185 MRV visa application fee and the $350 SEVIS I-901 fee — $535 in total as of 2026. Figures below were verified against the State Department's fee schedule in October 2026, but fees change by regulation — confirm the live schedule before paying anything. This guide follows the application sequence from our US student visa guide: admission, Form I-20, SEVIS payment, the DS-160, the interview.

The SEVIS I-901 fee also applies to M-1 vocational students (the official fee table lists it for F and M students); M-1 applicants should check the live fee schedule for their current MRV figure. Pay both fees through official US government channels only — never to the school, an agent, or a third-party website.

The $350 SEVIS I-901 fee, explained

SEVIS — the Student and Exchange Visitor Information System — is the US government's database of international students. The $350 I-901 fee registers you in it, and it is paid after you receive Form I-20 from your SEVP-certified school and before you complete the visa application and attend the consular interview — that is the official sequence. Pay it through the official SEVIS system and keep the receipt: you will need the confirmation at the interview. If your visa is refused and you reapply, check the current SEVIS guidance before paying again — rules on fee reuse can change.

The $185 MRV visa application fee

MRV stands for machine-readable visa. The $185 fee (as of 2026) pays for the visa application itself: the DS-160 form at ceac.state.gov and the in-person interview at a US embassy or consulate. It is paid per applicant, and it covers the application — not the outcome: a refusal is not followed by a refund, and reapplying means paying again. After the interview, check whether your nationality owes a visa issuance (reciprocity) fee before arranging passport return — a separate, nationality-specific charge on top of the two standard fees.

When to pay: the correct order

  1. Get admitted to a SEVP-certified school and receive Form I-20.
  2. Pay the $350 SEVIS I-901 fee and keep the receipt.
  3. Complete the DS-160 at ceac.state.gov.
  4. Pay the $185 MRV fee through the embassy's designated channels.
  5. Schedule the interview — then check the wait times in our US visa & entry requirements guide and plan 6–12 months ahead where waits are long.
  6. Attend the interview — fingerprints and a photograph are taken; see our student visa interview guide for what officers ask.

Other costs to budget beyond the two fees

  • Visa issuance (reciprocity) fee — nationality-specific; check before passport return.
  • Proof of funds — the US sets no federal dollar minimum; the school's published cost of attendance on the I-20 is the figure used. See our international student finance guide.
  • Student health insurance — most schools auto-enroll international students; published 2026–27 figures run roughly $2,400–$3,900 per year. See our student health insurance guide.
  • Administrative processing — if required after the interview, it can add weeks and cannot be expedited.
  • Travel to the interview city — budget the trip to the embassy or consulate, which may not be in your home city. Our student bank account guide covers managing money once you arrive.

Fees, interviews and the real timeline

The fees are the cheap part of the timeline — the interview wait is the expensive one. The State Department's Global Visa Wait Times table (updated 21 July 2026) shows waits from about 2 weeks at low-demand posts to 13+ months at the busiest: roughly 10.5 months at Hyderabad, 8.5 at New Delhi and Mumbai, 8 at Karachi, 7.5 at Islamabad, and about 2 months in London. Apply as early as your course start date allows — ideally 6–12 months ahead for high-demand posts.

What to verify before paying

  • Confirm the live fee schedule on travel.state.gov — the $185 and $350 figures are as of 2026.
  • Confirm your school is SEVP-certified and the I-20 details match your passport exactly.
  • Check the Global Visa Wait Times tool for your post before booking anything non-refundable.
  • Verify the embassy's payment channels on its own website — never pay a "visa fee" to a personal bank account, a crypto wallet, or a third-party site.
  • Check whether your nationality owes a visa issuance (reciprocity) fee.

Common fee mistakes to avoid

  • Paying SEVIS before receiving the I-20. The fee attaches to your SEVIS record — you need the I-20 first.
  • Paying the MRV fee on a third-party site. Use the embassy's designated channels only.
  • Treating the fee as a down payment on approval. It covers the application, not the outcome.
  • Booking non-refundable travel before the interview when your post's wait is 8+ months.
  • Forgetting the health-insurance line item — $2,400–$3,900 a year is a real part of the first-year cost.

Final fee checklist

  • Form I-20 received from a SEVP-certified school, details matching the passport
  • $350 SEVIS I-901 fee paid, receipt saved
  • DS-160 completed at ceac.state.gov, confirmation page saved
  • $185 MRV fee paid through the embassy's designated channels
  • Interview scheduled with the wait time factored in (6–12 months ahead at busy posts)
  • Reciprocity/issuance fee checked for your nationality
  • First-year budget includes health insurance ($2,400–$3,900)

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does the US F-1 student visa cost in 2026?

The two mandatory fees total $535: the $185 MRV visa application fee plus the $350 SEVIS I-901 fee, both as of 2026. Also budget a possible nationality-specific issuance fee and health insurance at roughly $2,400–$3,900 per year.

When do I pay the SEVIS fee?

After you receive Form I-20 from your SEVP-certified school, and before you complete the visa application and attend the interview. Keep the payment receipt — you need it at the interview.

Do M-1 students pay the same fees?

The $350 SEVIS I-901 fee applies to both F and M students. Check the live fee schedule for the current M-1 MRV figure before paying.

Is the visa fee refunded if I am refused?

No. The MRV fee covers the application, not the decision, and reapplying means paying again. Budget it as a sunk cost.

Where do I pay the fees?

The MRV fee goes through the embassy's designated payment channels on its own website; the SEVIS fee through the official SEVIS system. Never pay a "visa fee" to a personal bank account, a crypto wallet, or a third-party site.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Budget $535 for the two mandatory F-1 fees in 2026 — $185 MRV plus $350 SEVIS I-901 — paid in order: I-20 first, then SEVIS, then the DS-160 and MRV. Add a reciprocity fee if your nationality owes one, $2,400–$3,900 a year for health insurance, and your school's cost of attendance for proof of funds. The fees cover the application, not the outcome — and the interview wait, not the payment, sets your timeline. Confirm every figure on travel.state.gov before paying.
