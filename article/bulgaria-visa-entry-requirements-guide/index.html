How to use this Bulgaria visa and entry guide

Visa rules are not a single yes-or-no fact attached to a destination — they depend on your passport, purpose, length of stay, transit airports and sometimes recent travel history. Start with those facts before reading a visa list. The entry-status table below is the fast answer for the most common passports; the detail follows it. Always confirm the decisive rule on the official channel — Bulgarian MFA / VFS Global — before paying or booking.

Passport validity and document condition

Schengen rules: passport issued within the last 10 years and valid for at least three months beyond your planned departure from the Schengen area.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Bulgaria is fully Schengen: visa-free 90 days in 180 for US, UK and other exempt nationalities, and a Schengen Type C visa (€90 adults, 2026) for everyone else — the uniform EU fee, confirmed via the Bulgarian MFA. Applications run through VFS Global (reported), and there is no Bulgarian eVisa or visa on arrival.

The EU's Entry/Exit System (EES) rolled out in October 2025 (reported), and ETIAS is expected for visa-exempt travellers from late 2026 (reported) — check both before booking, as they change the arrival experience.

Bulgaria operates no eVisa. Short stays use the Schengen Type C visa via VFS Global and the consulate; there is no online Bulgarian visa application.

Bulgaria offers no visa on arrival. Schengen entry is decided before travel.

Transit and onward travel

Sofia airside transit on a through ticket normally needs no visa if you stay airside — but some nationalities need an airport transit visa even airside. Check the transit list for your passport.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

No fixed official minimum is published for Bulgaria. Schengen consulates assess means of subsistence — carry bank statements.

Common documents beyond the visa itself: onward or return ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can support yourself. Print everything — phone batteries die at the worst moments.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

No vaccination certificate required for entry. Standard European travel health guidance applies.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Schengen travel medical insurance of €30,000 is mandatory for the visa application (reported, 2026).

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Your permitted stay is set by the visa, stamp or entry record — visa validity and authorised duration are not necessarily the same thing. Read the entry stamp and calculate days using the method specified for your route. Overstays in Bulgaria can mean fines, detention or a future entry ban — know your date.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work and study need Bulgarian long-stay D visas with local sponsorship — the Schengen tourist visa never authorizes employment. D fees are not verified; confirm with the consulate and allow the reported 35 business days.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Minors generally need their own passport and sometimes a parental consent letter — check the requirements for your departure country too. Dual nationals should check the rules for the passport they plan to enter on, since visa treatment follows the document presented at the border. Refugees, stateless persons and emergency travel-document holders should confirm with Bulgaria’s embassy directly, as standard routes may not apply.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

2025–26 policy changes

  • EES rollout (reported, Oct 2025): the EU Entry/Exit System records biometric entries/exits — expect it at Bulgarian borders.
  • ETIAS (reported, expected late 2026, EU-wide): visa-exempt travellers expected to need ETIAS authorization — as reported in 2026; confirm before booking.
  1. Determine your category: visa-free (US/UK), Schengen Type C (visits), or D visa (work/study).
  2. For Type C: apply via VFS Global — €90 adults (2026), 15-day decision rule.
  3. Meet Schengen requirements: €30,000 insurance, funds evidence, qualifying passport.
  4. Expect EES biometric registration at the border (from Oct 2025, reported).
  5. For D visas: allow the reported 35 business days and confirm fees with the consulate.

Schengen applications follow the standard checklist via VFS Global. D visas add the purpose-specific file — the consulate's checklist governs.

Common mistakes to avoid

    Treating Bulgaria as non-Schengen. It is fully Schengen — the €90 Type C applies. Skipping insurance. €30,000 Schengen medical cover is mandatory. Assuming ETIAS is live. Expected late 2026, not operational as of October 2026. Working on a Type C visa. Employment needs the D visa with sponsorship. Forgetting EES. Biometric entry/exit registration now applies at the border.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Bulgaria operates no eVisa. Short stays use the Schengen Type C visa via VFS Global and the consulate; there is no online Bulgarian visa application.

Only use the official channel — Bulgarian MFA / VFS Global. Copycat sites charge markups or harvest passport data. An eVisa approval is an email or a portal record: carry the confirmation and check it shows your name, passport number and permitted entries.

Translations, certifications and document format

Documents not in the local or accepted language may need certified translations — the official checklist states the rule. Certifications, bank statements and invitation letters should be recent (typically within 3 months). Photograph standards matter more than people expect: follow the portal’s specification exactly.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Schengen applicants give fingerprints (VIS) — valid 59 months for repeat applications. The EES now also records entries/exits biometrically at the border (reported, from Oct 2025).

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs their own application or authorisation — visas are not transferable within a family or group. Apply together where the portal allows, and carry the same document set for each person. With minors, bring birth certificates or consent documentation in addition to passports.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

A visa is not a customs clearance. Declare cash above the local reporting threshold, check restricted items before packing (medications, drones, satellite equipment and certain food products are common flashpoints), and know that border officials can inspect devices. Keep receipts for high-value items you intend to bring back out.

Departure and future travel

Before leaving, confirm your exit is recorded — keep boarding passes and entry/exit stamps. If you overstayed or had any immigration incident, note it: future applications to Bulgaria and other countries ask about it. Some nationalities need to deregister or obtain exit clearance after long stays; check before a long-term visit.

How to research a route without getting misled

Rank sources by authority: Bulgaria’s immigration authority and foreign ministry first, then Timatic/IATA, then consular posts, then recent reputable reporting. Treat aggregator visa sites as leads, not answers — they go stale. If two credible sources conflict, say so in your notes and confirm with the authority; this guide flags conflicts the same way.

A final document audit before travel

Forty-eight hours before departure, check the passport number on the visa or authorisation, validity dates, name spelling, permitted entries and any conditions. Confirm transit airports and terminals have not changed. Put the approval, insurance and accommodation in an offline folder and print critical documents when the destination or airline may expect paper evidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for Bulgaria?

US and UK citizens enter visa-free for 90 days in 180. Others need a Schengen Type C visa (€90 adults, 2026). Bulgaria is fully Schengen — no eVisa or VoA exists.

Is Bulgaria in the Schengen area?

Yes — fully, including land borders. The uniform Schengen €90 fee and 90/180 rule apply.

How much is the Bulgaria Schengen visa?

€90 for adults, €45 for children 6–12, free under 6 (2026, Bulgaria MFA).

What is EES?

The EU Entry/Exit System — biometric registration of entries and exits, reported as rolled out from October 2025. Expect it at Bulgarian borders.

Can I work in Bulgaria on a tourist visa?

No — work needs a long-stay D visa with Bulgarian sponsorship.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Bulgaria, it's Schengen all the way: €90 Type C (2026) or visa-free for the US/UK, via VFS Global. Expect EES biometric registration at the border, watch for ETIAS in late 2026, and route work plans through the D visa.
