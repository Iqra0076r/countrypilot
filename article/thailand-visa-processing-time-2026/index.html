Thailand Visa Processing Time 2026: 5–15 Day e-Visas and the Instant Visa on Arrival

Thailand's e-Visa takes 5–15 business days depending on the embassy in 2026 — while the visa on arrival is issued at the checkpoint in minutes. Here is how to choose the right lane and time it.

Thailand offers two speeds. The e-Visa, applied for online before travel, takes 5–15 business days depending on embassy workload in 2026. The visa on arrival (VoA), for eligible nationalities at designated checkpoints, is issued on the spot — the processing time is the immigration queue.

This guide sets out both timelines, the 2026 fee structure (revised 27 April 2026), which lane suits which traveller, and the application hygiene that keeps the e-Visa clock from stretching.

The e-Visa clock: 5–15 business days

The Thai e-Visa is applied for online through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs system before travel. In 2026, decisions take 5–15 business days — the range reflects embassy workload, not application quality: the same file can take a week at a quiet embassy and three at a busy one.

The 2026 fee structure (revised 27 April 2026, embassies charge local-currency equivalents): tourist single-entry (60-day stay) THB 1,000, tourist multiple-entry (6-month validity) THB 5,000, Non-ED/Non-B single-entry THB 2,000 (multiple THB 5,000), and the Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) at a THB 10,000 base government fee with ~THB 500,000 funds proof (embassy-dependent). The approval document travels with you in print and as a phone backup.

The VoA clock: minutes at the counter

The visa on arrival (15 days, THB 2,000, 2026) at designated checkpoints is the instant lane: no advance application, decision at the counter. For eligible nationalities with simple plans, it removes the timeline question entirely.

The trade-off: 15 days, single entry, and checkpoint queues in peak season. Travellers needing 60 days, multiple entries, or certainty before boarding should take the e-Visa lane instead — the VoA's speed is real but its scope is narrow.

What slows a Thailand file down

  • Last-minute applications. The 5–15 day estimates assume a clean file — queries add weeks, and a portal glitch days before travel is not grounds for expedition.
  • Name/date/amount mismatches. The Thailand document rule is simple: if the officer has to guess, you lose. Labels, dates and amounts must match across the file — per child, per applicant.
  • Embassy workload variance. The same application takes different times at different embassies; the 15-day end of the range is the planning number.
  • Portal downtime. Apply with enough lead time that a system outage does not become a missed trip.
  • Unofficial 'fee' demands. Verify the site is the genuine Thai e-Visa system (Ministry of Foreign Affairs) before entering card details — legitimate portals never ask for payment via crypto, gift cards or personal transfers.

When to apply: the planning formula

e-Visa: apply 3–4 weeks before travel — inside the 2–4 week guidance with margin for the 15-day end of the range and one query round.

Non-ED / Non-B: apply 6–8 weeks before the intended start — the visa is only the first step (work permits are issued separately by the Department of Employment after entry on a Non-B).

DTV: allow 6+ weeks — funds-proof verification is embassy-dependent and the least predictable of the lanes.

VoA: no advance timing needed — but confirm your nationality's eligibility and the designated checkpoints before flying.

Tracking and the live picture

e-Visa applications are tracked on the Thai e-Visa portal (thaievisa.go.th) — the Ministry of Foreign Affairs system is the only official channel. Requirements vary by embassy (no fixed nationwide Non-ED proof-of-funds figure is published), so the competent embassy's page is the live source for document specifics.

Keep the approval document in print and on the phone; the e-Visa is checked against the passport at boarding and arrival.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does the Thailand e-Visa take in 2026?

5–15 business days depending on embassy workload. Apply at least 2–4 weeks before travel — the estimates assume a clean file, and queries add weeks.

How long does the Thailand visa on arrival take?

It is issued at the designated checkpoint on arrival (15 days, THB 2,000 in 2026) — the 'processing time' is the immigration queue, not an application wait.

What are the Thailand visa fees in 2026?

Revised schedule from 27 April 2026: tourist single-entry THB 1,000, tourist multiple-entry THB 5,000, Non-ED/Non-B single-entry THB 2,000, DTV base fee THB 10,000, VoA THB 2,000.

Is the Thai e-Visa site legitimate?

Use only the Ministry of Foreign Affairs e-Visa system at thaievisa.go.th. Legitimate portals never ask for payment via crypto, gift cards or personal bank transfers.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Five to fifteen business days for the e-Visa, minutes at the counter for the visa on arrival — Thailand gives you the choice of certainty or speed. Apply 3–4 weeks ahead for the e-Visa lane, keep every document consistent, and use only the official MFA portal.
