South Korea Visa Fee 2026: Full Cost Guide

Every official South Korea visa and K-ETA fee for 2026 in one place: short-stay USD 40, long-stay USD 60, K-ETA KRW 10,000 — plus the nationality variations and the real costs the fees never cover.

Quick Answer

A South Korea short-stay single-entry visa (up to 90 days) costs USD 40 on the 2026 Embassy of Korea fee schedule, while long-stay single-entry visas (91+ days, D-2/D-4 study and E-series work) cost USD 60. The K-ETA is KRW 10,000 and valid 3 years. Fees vary by nationality — Indian nationals pay INR 5,400 for long-stay single entry (effective 1 August 2026), and UK nationals are scheduled at USD 200 for long-stay. Confirm your exact fee on visa.go.kr before paying; copycat sites charge far more for submitting the same application.

Start with the fee table below to find your category, then read the matching section — the fee is only the first line of the budget. Short-stay visitor visa fees

The standard short-term single-entry visa — valid for stays up to 90 days for tourism, family visits or short business trips — costs USD 40 under the 2026 Embassy of Korea fee schedule. This is the route for visa-required nationals from countries such as India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh, who apply at the Korean mission serving their residence.

Budget the fee as a sunk cost: it covers the application, not the approval. A refusal does not return the money, which is one reason to get the category and documents right before paying — the entry guide linked above walks through the evidence officers actually look for.

Long-stay study and work visa fees

D-2 degree students, D-4 language students and E-series work applicants pay the long-stay single-entry rate of USD 60 on the 2026 schedule. A critical sequencing fact: long-stay routes need a Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance secured before the visa application is lodged, so the fee is paid late in a process that starts weeks earlier.

For students, the visa fee is dwarfed by the proof-of-funds requirement — KRW 20,000,000 for D-2 students at Seoul-metropolitan universities and KRW 16,000,000 regionally (D-4: KRW 10,000,000 / KRW 8,000,000), typically required as seasoned balance. The fee is the cheapest line on a student budget by far.

K-ETA fee and validity

The Korea Electronic Travel Authorization costs KRW 10,000 — a one-time payment that buys a 3-year multiple-entry authorization for visa-waiver nationals. Decisions commonly arrive within 24–48 hours, and you must apply at least 72 hours before departure at k-eta.go.kr only.

Before you pay, check one 2026 development: the Ministry of Justice extended the temporary K-ETA exemption through 31 December 2026 for previously exempt nationalities, so some travellers pay nothing at all this year. Our South Korea K-ETA 2026 application guide walks through exactly who needs one and how to apply without hitting the copycat sites.

Fees that vary by nationality

Do not assume the USD 40 / USD 60 headline applies to your passport. Two verified examples of variation: Indian nationals pay INR 5,400 for long-stay single-entry visas under a schedule effective 1 August 2026, and UK nationals are scheduled at USD 200 for long-stay visas — more than triple the base rate. Every nationality has its own line on the Embassy of Korea schedule.

The reliable method: open the fee schedule on visa.go.kr or the embassy page of the mission handling your application, select your nationality, and read your own figure. Third-party blogs routinely quote the USD 40/60 base as if it were universal — it is not.

What the visa fee does not cover

Three costs routinely surprise applicants. First, mandatory student health insurance: D-2/D-4 students must join the National Health Insurance Service at KRW 79,320/month in 2026 (after the 50% student reduction) — see the South Korea travel insurance & health guide for the full picture. Second, the Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance for long-stay routes: it adds lead time (and associated document costs) before the visa application even begins. Third, everything about staying: the visa fee buys entry permission, not housing, deposits, or living expenses.

Add these to the fee to get the real number before you commit: for a D-2 student the honest first-year figure is the visa fee plus 12 months of NHIS plus the funds evidence — the fee itself is a rounding error in that total.

How to pay the official fee safely

Pay only through the channels named on visa.go.kr or by the mission handling your case — typically the Korea Visa Application Center or the embassy's designated bank channel for your jurisdiction. Keep the payment receipt with your application reference; you will need both at boarding and at the border.

The single most expensive mistake is paying a copycat website: unofficial portals mimic the official K-ETA and visa channels closely and charge several times the government fee for submitting the same form. Check the URL character by character, never arrive at the 'official' site via a search ad, and never pay a visa fee to a personal bank account or crypto wallet.

Common fee mistakes to avoid

  • Paying the K-ETA fee when the 2026 exemption covers you — check the exemption status on k-eta.go.kr first; the exemption runs through 31 December 2026.
  • Budgeting USD 40/60 without checking your nationality's schedule — UK long-stay applicants pay USD 200.
  • Forgetting the NHIS insurance cost — KRW 79,320/month for D-2/D-4 students adds up to roughly KRW 950,000 per year.
  • Paying copycat sites — unofficial portals charge several times the government fee for the same submission.
  • Assuming a refusal refunds the fee — fees cover processing, not outcomes; get the category right first.
  • Booking non-refundable travel before approval — never commit money the consulate has not yet earned you the right to spend.

A final cost audit before applying

  • Exact fee confirmed on visa.go.kr for your nationality and category
  • Payment made only through the mission's designated channel — receipt kept with application reference
  • K-ETA exemption status checked on k-eta.go.kr before paying KRW 10,000
  • Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance secured first (long-stay routes)
  • Proof of funds ready: KRW 20M (Seoul) / KRW 16M (regional) for D-2, seasoned balance
  • Mandatory NHIS budgeted: KRW 79,320/month for 2026 student rate
  • Non-refundable travel booked only after approval

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a South Korea visa fee in 2026?

Short-stay single-entry visas (up to 90 days) cost USD 40 and long-stay single-entry visas (91+ days, including D-2/D-4 study and E-series work) cost USD 60 on the 2026 Embassy of Korea fee schedule. Fees vary by nationality — check your own figure on visa.go.kr before paying.

How much is the K-ETA fee for South Korea in 2026?

KRW 10,000, valid for 3 years with multiple entries. The Ministry of Justice extended the temporary K-ETA exemption through 31 December 2026 for previously exempt nationalities, so check your status on k-eta.go.kr — you may not need to pay at all.

Why does my nationality pay a different South Korea visa fee?

Korean consular fees are set per nationality, not at a global flat rate. For example, Indian nationals pay INR 5,400 for long-stay single entry (effective 1 August 2026) and UK nationals are scheduled at USD 200 for long-stay visas. Always read the schedule line for your own passport.

Does the visa fee include health insurance in South Korea?

No. D-2/D-4 students must additionally join the National Health Insurance Service at KRW 79,320/month in 2026 (after the 50% student reduction), and visitors should budget separately for travel medical cover.

Is the South Korea visa fee refunded if my application is refused?

No — the fee covers processing, not the outcome. Double-check the category, documents and funds evidence before paying, and never book non-refundable travel before approval.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

The 2026 fee math is simple — USD 40 short-stay, USD 60 long-stay, KRW 10,000 K-ETA — but your nationality's schedule line is the only figure that counts, so confirm it on visa.go.kr before paying. Check the K-ETA exemption first (extended through 31 December 2026), pay only through official channels, and budget the fee's blind spots: KRW 79,320/month NHIS for students, seasoned KRW 16–20 million in funds evidence, and the issuance certificate's lead time. The fee is processing cost, never a guarantee.
