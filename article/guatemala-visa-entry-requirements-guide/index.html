How to use this Guatemala visa and entry guide

Guatemala has no e-Visa and no visa-on-arrival programme — visas are issued at Guatemalan consulates/embassies or via the IGM in-country, and the CA-4 agreement lets travellers move between Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras and Nicaragua on a shared 90-day allowance. Fees are refreshingly cheap and official: US$25 single-entry / US$100 multiple-entry plus a US$25 submission fee, under the IGM tariff updated January 2025.

Residence is where the money goes: temporary residence for migrant workers runs US$200 (1 year) / US$300 (2 years) / US$500 (3–5 years), students US$100 per authorised year — plus a separate Ministry of Labour work permit. This guide was verified against IGM's official tariff and residence pages in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for at least 6 months beyond arrival with blank pages. The CA-4 shared 90-day clock means your Guatemala stay counts against time in El Salvador, Honduras and Nicaragua too — track it across all four.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Guatemala's consular system applies to visa-required nationalities including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh. US and UK nationals should verify current exemptions on igm.gob.gt — the IGM publishes the exemption lists, and they change.

Transit and onward travel

The CA-4 zone is the transit story: movement between Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras and Nicaragua shares a single 90-day allowance, so a 'transit' through a second CA-4 country still consumes the same clock. Airside connections that never exit the transit area need no additional permission, but any landside step counts as entry.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever Guatemala's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the Instituto Guatemalteco de Migración (IGM) approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Guatemala requires no vaccination certificate for ordinary visitors. No health document is checked at Guatemalan border control for standard arrivals.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance is not a Guatemalan visa condition for visitors. Note the narrow exception: international health insurance is required for migrant workers employed by foreign employers. No official student-insurance cost figure is published.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Admissions run up to 90 days shared across the CA-4 zone — days spent in El Salvador, Honduras or Nicaragua count against the same allowance. Keep the return ticket and accommodation details accessible; officers may ask for proof of funds and onward travel.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Temporary residence for migrant workers: US$200 (1 year) / US$300 (2 years) / US$500 (3–5 years) plus the US$25 submission fee — and a separate work permit from the Ministry of Labour on top. Students: US$100 per authorised year of temporary residence, with economic-solvency proof required (own, family or scholarship — no published minimum amount). A visitor visa never authorises work; the labour permit is a separate process.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own Guatemala permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether Guatemala restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Visitor route: 1) Check the IGM exemption list for your nationality. 2) If a visa is needed, apply at the Guatemalan consulate (US$25 single / US$100 multiple + US$25 submission, per the January 2025 tariff). 3) Track the CA-4 90-day clock across all four countries. Residence route: 1) File through the IGM with the tariff-schedule fees. 2) Workers: obtain the separate Ministry of Labour permit. 3) Students: prepare the solvency evidence (no fixed minimum published).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Ignoring the CA-4 shared clock. 90 days covers four countries together — hopping to Honduras does not reset it.
  • Working on a visitor visa. The Ministry of Labour permit is separate and mandatory.
  • Assuming US/UK exemption without checking. Verify the current IGM exemption list before travel.
  • Forgetting the US$25 submission fee. It is on top of the visa/residence fee.
  • Using 'Guatemala e-Visa' sites. No e-Visa exists — consulates and IGM only.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Guatemala has no e-Visa portal — the IGM website publishes requirements and tariffs, but applications go through consulates/embassies or the IGM in-country. Any 'Guatemala e-Visa' seller is not official.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in Guatemala's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the Instituto Guatemalteco de Migración (IGM) for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where Guatemala requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Declare large cash sums as required. Keep the visa, return ticket and CA-4 entry stamp with your passport — the shared 90-day clock is checked at each CA-4 border.

Departure and future travel

The CA-4 90-day allowance is the hard limit for visitors — leaving Guatemala for Honduras does not buy more time. Residence holders renew through the IGM before expiry; workers keep the labour permit current alongside the residence.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) igm.gob.gt — the tariffario PDF and residence pages are the controlling documents. 2) The IGM exemption lists for US/UK questions. No official processing times exist anywhere — distrust any site quoting them.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per Guatemala's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Guatemala is cheap and consular: US$25–100 visas, no e-Visa, no VOA, and the CA-4 90-day clock governs every itinerary touching four countries. Workers and students budget the residence fees (US$100–500) plus the separate labour permit. The IGM tariffario is the single source of truth — everything else is commentary.
