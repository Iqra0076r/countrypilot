Quick Answer

Japan's requirements hinge on one question: is a Certificate of Eligibility (CoE) involved? Tourists use the visa exemption (US, UK, EU, Singapore), the JAPAN eVISA, or a consular visa (JPY 15,000/30,000 on issuance). Work and study start with a free CoE obtained inside Japan (1–3 months), then a consular visa (~5 working days). Transit visas were abolished on 1 July 2026.

How to use this Japan visa requirements guide

Japan's visa system has a feature most countries don't: the Certificate of Eligibility (CoE). For work and study, the CoE — issued by the Immigration Services Agency inside Japan, usually via your employer or school — comes before the visa. No CoE, no visa; the consulate cannot issue one without it.

Authorities: MOFA (mofa.go.jp/j_info/visit/visa) for visas issued abroad, the Immigration Services Agency (isa.go.jp) for the CoE. Verified October 2026: fees rose fivefold on 1 July 2026, transit visas were abolished the same day, and direct JAPAN eVISA applications opened 15 May 2026.

The three tourist routes

Three routes cover most tourists. First, visa exemption: many nationalities — including the US, UK, EU states and Singapore — enter visa-free for short stays (typically 90 days). Second, the JAPAN eVISA: direct online from 15 May 2026 for Australia, Brazil, Cambodia, Canada, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, Taiwan, the UK and the USA, plus an accredited-agency route for China, the Philippines, Vietnam, Hong Kong, India, Indonesia, Macau, Mongolia, South Korea, Singapore and the UAE (tourism, single-entry, air/sea entry only). Third, the consular visa: JPY 15,000 single / JPY 30,000 multiple from 1 July 2026, charged only on issuance.

Transit: Japan abolished transit visas on 1 July 2026 — transit passengers who need a visa now apply for a short-term stay single-entry visa instead.

Work and study: the Certificate of Eligibility sequence

Work and student visas run on the Certificate of Eligibility: the employer or school obtains the CoE in Japan, and the traveller then applies at the consulate, paying the JPY 15,000 issuance fee. The CoE itself is free; first-time entrants receive the residence card at major airports. Details: our Japan Work Visa Guide.

Documents, biometrics and identity checks

Tourist applications: a passport valid for the entire stay (consulates commonly ask for 6 months' validity at application plus 2 blank pages), a compliant photograph, the application form, an itinerary, and funds evidence. CoE applications are filed in Japan by the sponsor — employment contract or admission letter, company/school documentation, and the applicant's credentials. Documents not in Japanese or English should carry translations. Full list: our Japan document checklist.

No routine interviews — the process is document-driven. A photograph is captured at application; fingerprints and a photo are taken at entry. At the border, officers may ask about purpose, accommodation and funds.

Financial and health requirements

Japan requires no vaccination certificate for ordinary visitors. For finances: no fixed government minimum for student proof of funds, but immigration and schools consistently reference around JPY 2,000,000 per year (about JPY 2.5 million for Tokyo), evidenced with 3–6 months of bank statements. Residents staying 3+ months must enroll in National Health Insurance — about JPY 18,000–30,000 per year (2026), covering 70% of medical costs. Cash over JPY 1,000,000 must be declared. More: our proof-of-funds guide.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Applying for the visa before the CoE exists. The consulate cannot issue without it — the CoE comes first, always.
  • Budgeting the old fees. Fees quintupled on 1 July 2026 — pre-July guides are dangerously stale.
  • Looking for a transit visa. Abolished 1 July 2026 — apply for the short-term single-entry visa instead.
  • Entering as a tourist to job-hunt or convert status. Temporary-visitor status cannot be converted inside Japan.

A final document audit

  • Passport valid for the entire stay (6 months' validity + 2 blank pages for the visa application)
  • CoE in hand before any work/student visa application (1–3 month lead time)
  • Issuance fee budgeted at 2026 rates (JPY 15,000/30,000), paid only on issuance
  • Itinerary, accommodation proof and funds evidence (3–6 months statements for students)
  • NHI enrollment for 3+ month stays; cash >JPY 1,000,000 declaration readiness

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents do I need for a Japan visa?

Tourist: passport valid for the entire stay, a compliant photo, the application form, an itinerary, and funds evidence. Work/student: the Certificate of Eligibility first — filed in Japan by your employer or school — then the consular visa application.

What is the Certificate of Eligibility (CoE)?

The document that unlocks Japanese work and student visas: applied for inside Japan by your employer or school with the Immigration Services Agency, taking 1–3 months. The CoE is free; the consular visa after it costs JPY 15,000 and takes ~5 working days. No CoE, no visa.

How long does Japan visa processing take in 2026?

Tourist visas: about 5 working days of embassy examination (5–10; longer at peak). Work/student: the CoE takes 1–3 months, then about 5 working days (5–7 for students) for the visa.

Do Indians need a visa for Japan?

Yes, unless another exemption applies. Indian nationals can use the JAPAN eVISA via an accredited agency or apply for a consular visa — JPY 15,000 on issuance.

Is there a minimum bank balance for a Japan student visa?

No fixed government minimum exists. Immigration and schools consistently reference around JPY 2,000,000 per year (about JPY 2.5 million for Tokyo), evidenced with 3–6 months of bank statements.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Japan: pick the right route, get the CoE first for work or study (free, 1–3 months), keep the passport valid for the stay, show ~JPY 2,000,000/year in demonstrable funds for study, and budget JPY 15,000 payable only on visa issuance. Verify the live rules on mofa.go.jp before applying.
