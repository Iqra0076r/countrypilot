Ireland Visa Fee 2026

Ireland visa fees 2026: short-stay €60 single / €100 multiple, study visa €60/€100, Critical Skills permit €1,000, residence stamp €300. Verified guide.

Quick Answer

An Irish Short Stay 'C' visa costs €60 single-entry / €100 multiple-entry in 2026, and a Long Stay 'D' study visa costs the same €60/€100. A Critical Skills Employment Permit application costs €1,000 (usually employer-paid, 90% refunded if refused), and the first-issue residence stamp costs €300. All fees are non-refundable.

How to use this Ireland visa fee guide

Irish immigration fees are set by Immigration Service Delivery (ISD) and the Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment, and published on irishimmigration.ie and enterprise.gov.ie.

The fee you pay depends on the visa type and whether you need single or multiple entry — Ireland is not in Schengen, so these are Ireland-only fees under Ireland's own system.

Figures below are the 2026 schedule verified against ISD guidance in October 2026; always re-check the live fee page before paying, because fees can change by regulation.

Short-stay 'C' visa fees

The Short Stay 'C' visa covers tourism, business and family visits.

The fee is €60 for a single-entry visa or €100 for a multiple-entry visa (2026), paid as part of the AVATS online application and non-refundable regardless of outcome.

The fee is per person — every traveller, including infants, needs a separate AVATS application at the same per-person rate.

Study 'D' visa fees

The Long Stay 'D' study visa costs the same as the short-stay band: €60/€100 in 2026.

The visa fee itself is only one part of the student budget — applicants must also pay a minimum €6,000 upfront tuition deposit before applying, hold €10,000 per academic year in living-costs evidence, and carry mandatory private health insurance (about €150–1,200/year depending on tier, 2026).

For the full student framework, see Ireland International Student Checklist.

Work route fees: permits and residence

The work route has two separate fee stages.

First, the Critical Skills Employment Permit application costs €1,000 (2026), decided by the Department of Enterprise — 90% of this fee is refunded if the application is refused.

Second, after arrival, the first-issue residence stamp costs €300 (2026), paid at GNIB/IRP registration.

Entry-visa fees apply on top where the applicant also needs an entry visa after permit approval.

More on the route in our Ireland Work Visa Guide.

Who pays which fee

The €1,000 Critical Skills permit fee is usually paid by the employer, not the applicant — confirm this with the sponsoring employer before submitting.

The €300 first-issue stamp fee is paid by the arriving worker at registration.

Tourist, family-visit and student applicants always pay their own visa fees (€60/€100), plus their own tuition deposit and insurance where applicable.

Build a clean money trail for the remaining funds — our Ireland Proof of Funds Explained guide shows what decision-makers check.

Refunds, refusals and re-application

Visa fees are non-refundable — if a short-stay application is refused, the €60/€100 is gone.

This matters more in 2026 than before: from 1 June 2026, administrative appeals were abolished for most short-stay (Type C) visa refusals, so a refused applicant must file a fresh application and pay the fee again.

The one exception is the permit fee: 90% of the €1,000 Critical Skills fee is refunded on refusal.

Plan the application file carefully the first time — quality is cheaper than re-applying.

Our Ireland Visa Extension Guide covers what happens after approval and renewal.

Verify before paying

Confirm three things on the live source before any payment: 1) the current fee on irishimmigration.ie or enterprise.gov.ie for your exact visa type; 2) the correct payment channel — official fees are receipted through official channels only, never a personal bank account; 3) that you are on the real AVATS/ISD site, not a copycat portal charging large markups for submitting the same form. Fees change by regulation, and outdated third-party guides are the most common source of overpayment.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming the €100 multiple-entry visa is always the better deal — pay only for the entries you need.
  • Forgetting the fee is per person — family applications multiply the cost by headcount.
  • Treating the €60/€100 as the total student cost — the €6,000 tuition deposit and €10,000 funds evidence are the real budget items.
  • Paying an agent a 'guaranteed' fee on top of the official fee — no agent can overturn a refusal or buy a faster decision.
  • Paying fees to a personal account or crypto wallet — official fees go through official channels only.
  • Budgeting the permit fee as the applicant — the €1,000 is usually employer-paid; confirm before committing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is an Ireland tourist visa in 2026?

A Short Stay 'C' visa for tourism costs €60 single-entry or €100 multiple-entry in 2026, applied for via AVATS with the passport submitted in person. The fee is non-refundable and paid per person, including infants.

How much is an Ireland student visa in 2026?

The study visa itself costs €60/€100 in 2026 — but the real student budget is larger: a minimum €6,000 upfront tuition deposit, €10,000 per academic year in living-costs evidence, and mandatory private health insurance (€150–1,200/year).

Is the Ireland visa fee refundable if refused?

No — visa fees are non-refundable. If a short-stay visa is refused (and administrative appeals were abolished for most Type C refusals from 1 June 2026), you must submit a fresh application and pay the fee again. The only partial refund is 90% of the €1,000 Critical Skills permit fee on refusal.

Who pays the €1,000 Ireland work permit fee?

The employer usually pays the Critical Skills Employment Permit fee. Confirm with the sponsoring employer before submitting; 90% is refunded if the permit application is refused.

Does the Ireland visa fee include the residence card?

No. The visa sticker fee (€60/€100) covers entry permission only. After arrival, non-EEA nationals register with immigration and pay the €300 first-issue residence stamp fee for the IRP card.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Budget €60 single or €100 multiple for short-stay and study visas, €1,000 (usually employer-paid) for a Critical Skills permit plus €300 for the first residence stamp — all 2026 figures. Fees are non-refundable, and since June 2026 there is no appeal for most short-stay refusals, so a rejected file means paying again. Confirm every figure on irishimmigration.ie or enterprise.gov.ie before paying.
