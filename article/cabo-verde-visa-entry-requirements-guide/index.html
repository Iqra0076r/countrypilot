How to use this Cabo Verde visa and entry guide

Visa rules are not a single yes-or-no fact attached to a destination — they depend on your passport, purpose, length of stay, transit airports and sometimes recent travel history. Start with those facts before reading a visa list. The entry-status table below is the fast answer for the most common passports; the detail follows it. Always confirm the decisive rule on the official channel — Cabo Verde EASE pre-registration portal — before paying or booking.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for the duration of your stay — six months beyond travel is the safer standard.

Transit and onward travel

Sal/Praia airside transit on a through ticket normally needs no visa. EASE pre-registration still applies to arrivals — complete it.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

No official proof-of-funds figure is published. Carry bank statements as a matter of course.

Common documents beyond the visa itself: onward or return ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can support yourself. Print everything — phone batteries die at the worst moments.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

No vaccination certificate required for entry from most countries. Standard travel immunizations apply.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is not documented as mandatory — but recommended; island medical facilities are limited.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Your permitted stay is set by the visa, stamp or entry record — visa validity and authorised duration are not necessarily the same thing. Read the entry stamp and calculate days using the method specified for your route. Overstays in Cabo Verde can mean fines, detention or a future entry ban — know your date.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work and study need separate Cabo Verdean authorizations with local sponsorship — the visitor entry never authorizes employment. Fees are not published; confirm officially.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Minors generally need their own passport and sometimes a parental consent letter — check the requirements for your departure country too. Dual nationals should check the rules for the passport they plan to enter on, since visa treatment follows the document presented at the border. Refugees, stateless persons and emergency travel-document holders should confirm with Cabo Verde’s embassy directly, as standard routes may not apply.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

2025–26 policy changes

  • VoA abolished for 96 nationalities (reported, 1 Jan 2026): pre-arrival visa required — multiple consistent secondary sources.
  • TSA double charge (reported, from Jul 2026): failing to pre-register on EASE reportedly doubles the TSA — pre-register.
  1. Check whether your nationality is among the 96 needing a pre-arrival visa (reported, from Jan 2026) or is visa-exempt (UK/EU/US/CA/NO/CH/IS reported).
  2. Complete EASE pre-registration and pay the TSA — do not skip it (double charge reported from Jul 2026).
  3. If a pre-arrival visa is needed, apply about three weeks early (reported 2–15 business days).
  4. Confirm the cited €53.50 total on the EASE platform before paying.
  5. Carry the EASE confirmation for boarding and arrival.

Prepare passport, the EASE pre-registration confirmation, TSA payment proof, travel bookings and financial evidence. The EASE platform's checklist governs.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Only use the official channel — Cabo Verde EASE pre-registration portal. Copycat sites charge markups or harvest passport data. An eVisa approval is an email or a portal record: carry the confirmation and check it shows your name, passport number and permitted entries.

Translations, certifications and document format

Documents not in the local or accepted language may need certified translations — the official checklist states the rule. Certifications, bank statements and invitation letters should be recent (typically within 3 months). Photograph standards matter more than people expect: follow the portal’s specification exactly.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Biometric capture is not documented. Expect standard checks on arrival.

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs their own application or authorisation — visas are not transferable within a family or group. Apply together where the portal allows, and carry the same document set for each person. With minors, bring birth certificates or consent documentation in addition to passports.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

A visa is not a customs clearance. Declare cash above the local reporting threshold, check restricted items before packing (medications, drones, satellite equipment and certain food products are common flashpoints), and know that border officials can inspect devices. Keep receipts for high-value items you intend to bring back out.

Departure and future travel

Before leaving, confirm your exit is recorded — keep boarding passes and entry/exit stamps. If you overstayed or had any immigration incident, note it: future applications to Cabo Verde and other countries ask about it. Some nationalities need to deregister or obtain exit clearance after long stays; check before a long-term visit.

How to research a route without getting misled

Rank sources by authority: Cabo Verde’s immigration authority and foreign ministry first, then Timatic/IATA, then consular posts, then recent reputable reporting. Treat aggregator visa sites as leads, not answers — they go stale. If two credible sources conflict, say so in your notes and confirm with the authority; this guide flags conflicts the same way.

A final document audit before travel

Forty-eight hours before departure, check the passport number on the visa or authorisation, validity dates, name spelling, permitted entries and any conditions. Confirm transit airports and terminals have not changed. Put the approval, insurance and accommodation in an offline folder and print critical documents when the destination or airline may expect paper evidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for Cabo Verde?

UK, EU, US, Canadian and several other nationals are reported as visa-exempt for 30 days; 96 nationalities need a pre-arrival visa since 1 January 2026 (reported). Everyone completes EASE pre-registration.

Is there visa on arrival in Cabo Verde?

Abolished for 96 nationalities from 1 January 2026 (reported, multiple consistent sources). Do not rely on it.

What is EASE?

Cabo Verde's official pre-registration portal — required for all arrivals, plus the TSA fee. Skipping it reportedly doubles the TSA charge since July 2026.

How much is the Cabo Verde visa?

Cited at €22.67 visa + €30.83 TSA = €53.50 total (2026, secondary citing EASE). Confirm on the platform before paying.

How long does the Cabo Verde visa take?

Reported at 2–15 business days (2026). Apply about three weeks early.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Cabo Verde in 2026, the old visa-on-arrival playbook is dead for 96 nationalities — pre-arrival visa via EASE, three weeks early, with the cited €53.50 total to verify on the platform. Exempt or not, complete EASE pre-registration or risk the reported double TSA charge.
