How to use this Cabo Verde visa and entry guide

Visa rules are not a single yes-or-no fact attached to a destination. They depend on the passport or travel document, nationality or residence status, the purpose and length of the trip, the place of departure, transit airports and sometimes recent travel history. Start with those facts before reading a visa list. IATA’s Travel Centre is useful for a personalised document check, while destination immigration or consular instructions control the application itself. Within Cabo Verde Visa & Entry Requirements Guide, use this point when working through “How to use this Cabo Verde visa and entry guide”, then compare it with the current IATA Travel Centre guidance linked below.

Entry to Cabo Verde is passport- and purpose-specific. The practical categories to check are visa-exempt entry, electronic authorisation or eVisa where offered, visa on arrival where offered, and a visa obtained before travel. Transit, work, study, family residence and long stays can have different rules from an ordinary visit.

Passport validity and document condition

Check the minimum passport validity that applies on the day of arrival and, where relevant, on the day of departure. Do not use a generic six-month rule as a substitute for the destination rule: some countries require six months, some require a shorter period, and some tie validity to the intended stay. Confirm whether emergency passports, refugee travel documents, alien passports or temporary documents are accepted. For readers using Cabo Verde Visa & Entry Requirements Guide, this is a “Passport validity and document condition” issue rather than a stand-alone rule; verify it against IATA Travel Centre before committing money or documents.

Also check blank-page requirements, damage rules and whether a passport reported lost or stolen can be used after recovery. Airlines may deny boarding when a document does not meet Timatic requirements even if the traveller believes immigration will make an exception. Identify the correct visa or entry route

Work through four possibilities in order: visa-exempt entry; an electronic travel authorisation or eVisa where the destination offers one; visa on arrival where it is available to the passport holder; and a visa obtained before departure. The correct answer can change with nationality, diplomatic or service passport status, residence permits held in another country and the precise purpose of travel. For readers using Cabo Verde Visa & Entry Requirements Guide, this is a “Identify the correct visa or entry route” issue rather than a stand-alone rule; verify it against IATA Travel Centre before committing money or documents.

A visitor permission normally covers tourism, family visits, short business meetings or similar temporary activity only to the extent the local rules allow. Paid employment, long study, family residence, journalism, volunteering, research, religious work and other activities may require a different permission even when the traveller could enter visa-free as a tourist. Transit and onward travel

Transit is its own immigration question. A traveller who does not intend to enter Cabo Verde may still need an airport-transit or landside-transit permission if changing terminals, collecting baggage, staying overnight or passing border control. Conversely, holding a visa for Cabo Verde does not guarantee that a separate transit country will admit the traveller.

Cabo Verde has no land borders listed in the country dataset used for this guide. For international travel, that usually makes airport, seaport or other maritime entry planning more important than overland border planning.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Even with a valid visa or visa-free entitlement, border officers can ask about the purpose and length of stay. Keep evidence that tells one coherent story: accommodation, host details where relevant, return or onward travel, enough funds, insurance where required, contact information and documents linked to the reason for the trip. The amount and form of financial evidence are route-specific. Avoid relying on screenshots that omit the account holder, unexplained last-minute deposits or a sponsor letter that is inconsistent with the itinerary. If a host or sponsor is funding the visit, check whether the destination requires a formal invitation, guarantee or certified document rather than a casual letter. For readers using Cabo Verde Visa & Entry Requirements Guide, this is a “Evidence immigration officers may ask to see” issue rather than a stand-alone rule; verify it against IATA Travel Centre before committing money or documents.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Entry health rules can be triggered by where the traveller has recently been, not only by nationality. Yellow-fever certificates are a classic example. Check the WHO travel-health material and the destination’s current entry instructions if the itinerary includes countries with relevant disease risk or long airport transits. Carry medication in original packaging where practical, check whether controlled medicines need an import permit or doctor’s letter, and keep enough supply for likely delays. Health documentation and customs rules can be separate from the visa decision. Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance rules can depend on the traveller’s nationality, visa category, length of stay and activity. Even when a standard visitor is not required to show a policy at the border, a visa application, tour operator, employer, school or residence process may impose its own insurance condition. If proof of insurance is required, check minimum medical cover, territorial validity, dates, repatriation wording and whether the certificate must be in a particular language. Buying a cheap policy only to satisfy a document checklist can leave major exclusions that become important during the trip. Arrival permission and permitted stay

A visa is often permission to travel to the border rather than an unconditional right of entry. Check the admission stamp, electronic record or entry card after arrival and understand how the authorised stay is calculated. Calendar days, nights, visa validity and permitted duration are not always the same thing. If the plan changes, check extension or status-change rules before the existing permission expires. Overstay can lead to fines, detention, removal, future visa problems or an entry ban. Do not rely on a hotel, employer or agent saying an overstay is harmless. Work, study and residence are separate questions

Do not use a visitor route as a substitute for work or residence permission. A company may be willing to hire a traveller while immigration law still requires a work permit, sponsorship approval or status change before employment starts. The same applies to full-time study, dependants and family residence. If the trip could become a move, research the long-stay route before departure. Some countries allow certain applications from inside the country; others require the applicant to leave and apply from a country of nationality or lawful residence. Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Children travelling with one parent, with relatives or alone may need consent letters, birth certificates or custody documents. Airlines can have documentation rules in addition to border requirements. Dual nationals should check whether Cabo Verde expects its own citizens to enter or leave on a national passport and whether consular assistance can be limited for dual citizens.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

  1. Record passport nationality, document type, expiry date and residence status.
  2. Enter the complete itinerary—including transit points—into a current entry-requirement checker such as IATA Travel Centre.
  3. Open the destination’s official immigration, foreign-ministry or embassy instructions for the identified route.
  4. Check passport validity, visa/eVisa/authorisation, health documents, insurance, funds and onward-ticket rules.
  5. Save the application receipt and final approval as offline files, not only in email.
  6. Re-check requirements shortly before departure because airlines use live document-rule databases.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming a friend with a different passport has the same visa rule.
  • Counting visa validity as the number of days permitted to remain.
  • Ignoring transit-country requirements.
  • Booking non-refundable travel before understanding processing time and approval risk.
  • Using a tourist permission for work or long-term study.
  • Relying on a social-media post instead of a current official source.
  • Failing to verify entry requirements again shortly before departure.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

A strong application is internally consistent. The dates on the application, invitation, accommodation, flight plan, employer or school evidence and bank records should make sense together. If an itinerary changes after documents were prepared, update the evidence rather than leaving contradictory dates for an officer to interpret. For this Cabo Verde Visa & Entry Requirements Guide research path, the practical checkpoint is “Building a stronger visa or entry file”; use IATA Travel Centre to confirm the live requirement where it can change.

Organise the file around the legal questions the authority must decide: identity, lawful purpose, ability to fund the trip, accommodation, intention to comply with the authorised stay, and any route-specific eligibility. Avoid flooding the file with irrelevant material. Clear, current and traceable evidence is more useful than a large bundle with no explanation. For readers using Cabo Verde Visa & Entry Requirements Guide, this is a “Building a stronger visa or entry file” issue rather than a stand-alone rule; verify it against FCDO Cabo Verde travel advice before committing money or documents.

Fees, appointments and processing time

Visa fees and processing estimates can change. Check the amount on the official application channel immediately before payment and keep the receipt. Where a visa application centre collects documents on behalf of a government, distinguish the government fee from optional commercial services such as courier delivery, premium lounge access or form-filling assistance. Processing estimates are not guarantees. Public holidays, security checks, requests for additional documents, seasonal demand and the need to verify an employer or host can extend a case. Build a buffer between the expected decision date and non-refundable travel. If a passport will be retained during processing, consider other travel that would require it. eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Electronic visa systems make applications faster but also create opportunities for look-alike websites. Start from a government or embassy page and follow its official link to the application portal. Check the domain carefully before entering passport data or card details. Search advertisements can appear above the official result and charge a large service fee for submitting the same form. Save the application number, payment receipt, submitted form and approval file as PDFs. If the approval is linked electronically to the passport, confirm that the passport number is correct. A replacement passport can invalidate or disconnect an existing authorisation in some systems. Translations, certifications and document format

Check whether documents must be original, certified, notarised, legalised, apostilled or translated by an approved translator. A document accepted for one purpose in Cabo Verde may not satisfy a visa requirement for another route. Digital uploads can also have file-size, format and scan-quality rules.

Names should be consistent with the passport. If a bank account, birth certificate, marriage certificate or employment record uses a different spelling, prepare the evidence that explains the difference rather than assuming an officer will infer it. Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Some routes require fingerprints, a photograph, an interview or in-person document verification. Appointment availability can be separate from processing time, so a route advertised as fast can still take longer if the next biometric slot is weeks away. Bring originals where requested and arrive with the same passport used in the application. Families and group travel

Each traveller can have an individual immigration decision even when the trip is booked together. Check whether every child needs a separate visa or authorisation, whether biometrics are required by age, and whether family members need separate proof of funds or insurance. One person’s approval does not automatically validate the rest of the group. For group itineraries, keep a simple table of passport number, expiry date, visa status, insurance status and transit requirements. Customs, cash and restricted goods

Immigration permission and customs permission are different. Check limits or declarations for cash, food, plants, animal products, alcohol, tobacco, professional equipment, drones and expensive goods. Travellers carrying commercial samples or equipment may need temporary-import documentation even when their immigration purpose is legitimate. Medication is especially important because a medicine legal at home can be controlled abroad. Verify the active ingredient, quantity limit and documentation requirement before packing it. Departure and future travel

Keep evidence of lawful departure when the system does not reliably record exits, particularly after unusual border crossings. If the passport receives an entry or exit stamp, check it for obvious errors before leaving the counter where practical. Future visa applications may ask for travel history, previous refusals or overstays, so maintain an accurate record. How to research a route without getting misled

Second, the destination’s official immigration or consular pages explain the legal route and application procedure. Third, independent guides can help organise the process but should never override the first two. This hierarchy is especially important when social media posts describe an exception that applied to a different nationality or date. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for Cabo Verde?

It depends on your passport or travel document, purpose, length of stay and sometimes residence status or transit route. Use a personalised IATA Travel Centre check, then confirm the identified route on official destination immigration or consular instructions. How long can I stay in Cabo Verde?

The permitted stay depends on the permission used. Visa validity and authorised duration are not necessarily the same. Check the visa, entry record or official rule and calculate days using the method specified for that route. Can I work while visiting?

Do not assume so. Visitor permission is usually different from work authorisation. Check whether the planned activity requires a work permit, sponsorship, business permission or another status before starting it. Yes. Passport, health, transit and airline-document requirements can change, and a routing change can add a new transit requirement. Re-check close to departure.
