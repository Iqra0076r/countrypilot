Schengen Visa for Indian Citizens 2026: Requirements, Fee, and Timeline

Indian passport holders need a Schengen visa for every short trip to Europe. This is the complete 2026 picture: fee, documents, funds proof, and the timeline to plan around.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20268 min read

India is one of the largest sources of Schengen applications in the world, and Indian citizens need a visa for every short stay in the Schengen area — there is no visa-free or visa-on-arrival route. The rules are the same EU-wide rules that apply to all applicants, but a few parts of the file matter more for Indian applicants: funds evidence, ties to India, and consulate jurisdiction. This guide covers the 2026 requirements end to end.

Quick Answer

Indian citizens need a Schengen short-stay visa for tourism, business, or family visits. The fee is €90, the decision takes 15 days normally and up to 45 days, and you apply at the consulate of your main destination (most nights) in India — or the first country entered if nights tie. You may apply from 6 months before travel, and no later than 15 days before.

Fee €90 · Decision 15–45 days · Stay up to 90 days in 180 · Updated 2026-10-14 · Reading time 8 minutes

Do Indians need a Schengen visa?

Yes — without exception for short stays. India is not on the EU's visa-exempt list, so tourism, business trips, conferences, and family visits all require the short-stay (Type C) visa issued before travel. The visa allows stays of up to 90 days in any 180-day period across the whole Schengen area — the 90/180 ceiling is rolling, so count previous stays day by day if you travel often.

The document list for Indian applicants

The Commission's baseline list applies: application form, photo, passport (issued within the last 10 years, valid at least 3 months after you leave), travel medical insurance of at least €30,000 covering all Schengen states for the entire stay, and documents on purpose, means, accommodation, and intention to return. For Indian applicants the last two carry the most weight — see the sections below. Run the full list through our visa document checklist before your appointment.

Funds: what Indian applicants must show

"Insufficient means" is one of the most common refusal grounds, and it is judged against daily reference amounts set by each member state — France, for example, works on €65 per day where prepaid accommodation is proven. For Indian applicants, two things matter beyond the balance itself: the money must be demonstrably yours and lawfully available (statements showing regular income over months, not one large unexplained deposit), and it must cover the real total — flights, accommodation for every night, and daily spend. If a family member sponsors the trip, declare it openly with their statements and a signed sponsorship letter; declared support beats an unexplained balance. Total the figure first with our Schengen visa cost calculator.

Ties to India: proving you will return

The single most common refusal tick is doubt that the applicant will leave before the visa expires — a judgment about ties at home, not character. For Indian applicants the convincing file shows a life that pulls you back: an employer's leave letter with a return date, property or a lease, family in India, ongoing commitments, and a history of leaving other countries on time. Self-employed applicants should show business registration and tax filings; students should show enrolment and a return tied to the academic calendar.

Which consulate in India to apply at

Jurisdiction follows your main destination — the Schengen country where you spend the most nights — not the city where slots are free. Applying at the wrong consulate is an established refusal ground. Count the nights in your itinerary and let the count choose the consulate; if two countries tie, apply where you first enter the Schengen area.

Timeline and fee

Fee: €90 per adult application, €45 for children 6–12 — non-refundable. Decision: 15 days standard, up to 45 days. Lodging window: 6 months to 15 days before travel. In practice, Indian applicants travelling in summer or December should lodge as early as the window allows, because high-demand posts in India see the heaviest backlogs. Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Indians get a Schengen visa on arrival?

No. Indian citizens must hold the visa before travel; there is no on-arrival or e-visa route for the Schengen area.

How much bank balance do Indians need for a Schengen visa?

There is no fixed figure: each state sets daily reference amounts (France uses €65/day with prepaid accommodation), and the consulate judges the balance against your real trip cost, built up over months of statements.

Does a Schengen refusal ban future applications?

No. A refusal is recorded in the Visa Information System, but you may reapply at any time — with the ticked grounds fixed, and another €90 fee.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Indian applicants the Schengen visa is won on evidence, not luck: €90 paid once per application, a file built around months of genuine statements, ties to India you can document, insurance at the €30,000 minimum, and an application lodged early at the right consulate.
