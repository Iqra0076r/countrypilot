How to use this Togo visa and entry guide

Togo rewrote its entry rules in 2026: since 18 May 2026, nationals of all African countries enter visa-free for 30 days — announced by the Ministry of Security — with mandatory pre-arrival registration on voyage.gouv.tg at least 24 hours before arrival. Non-exempt nationalities use the Togo eVisa; reported visa-on-arrival availability is disputed and unverified, so do not plan around it.

The pre-arrival registration is the non-negotiable step for everyone: it is checked before boarding. This guide was verified against official Togolese sources in October 2026 — and where figures could not be verified on the official portal (notably the eVisa fee schedule), this guide says so instead of repeating third-party numbers.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport should be valid well beyond your stay with blank pages — apply the conservative six-months-beyond-travel standard, since no precise published formula could be verified for Togo.

Complete the voyage.gouv.tg pre-arrival registration against the passport you will actually carry — a renewed passport after registration means redoing it. Registration must be completed at least 24 hours before arrival.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

African nationals: 30-day visa-free entry since 18 May 2026 + mandatory voyage.gouv.tg pre-arrival registration (24h+ ahead). Non-exempt nationalities: Togo eVisa via the official portal — apply well ahead, since processing figures are third-party only. Everyone: the pre-arrival registration, without which boarding is at risk.

Transit and onward travel

Lomé connections follow the same entry logic as everything else in Togo: the pre-arrival registration on voyage.gouv.tg is mandatory regardless, and non-exempt transit passengers who clear immigration need the eVisa. There is no verified visa-on-arrival fallback.

Airside transit on a single through-ticket without clearing immigration is the only plausible exception — confirm with the carrier, because Togo's pre-arrival requirements are enforced at boarding, not negotiated on arrival.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

At the Togo border, expect questions before you get a stamp. The standard set covers purpose of visit, length of stay, accommodation, and means of support — have the onward ticket, hotel booking or host address, and bank evidence ready rather than buried in luggage.

A visa lets you travel to the border; the officer's decision is what lets you in. Short, truthful, consistent answers beat long explanations. If your plans involve anything beyond tourism — work, study, volunteering — say so only if your visa category actually covers it.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Health documents are a separate gate from the visa for Togo. The key question is certificate-based: does Togo require proof of yellow-fever vaccination (common if you arrive from an endemic country), and are there any current outbreak-related declarations?

Carry the WHO yellow card (International Certificate of Vaccination) if there is any chance it applies — digital photos are not accepted at most borders. Also confirm your insurance covers the actual medical environment: in countries with limited hospital capacity, evacuation cover matters more than the policy's headline medical limit.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance sits in a grey zone for Togo: sometimes a formal visa requirement, always a practical one. Where the embassy or portal names a minimum medical coverage, treat that number as the floor and buy a policy that meaningfully exceeds it.

Read the exclusions before you pay: adventure activities, pre-existing conditions and alcohol-related incidents are the three most common claim rejections worldwide. For long stays, check whether the policy covers the full visa duration — a 90-day policy on a 180-day trip leaves a dangerous gap.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

On arrival in Togo, the officer's admission decision is the operative document. Note the exact date you must leave and keep evidence of it (a photo of the stamp or the electronic record) — 'I thought the visa was valid longer' is not a defence against an overstay finding.

Extensions, where they exist, must be sought before the current permission expires and are never guaranteed. Plan the itinerary inside the granted stay rather than assuming an extension will be available.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Keep the three purposes strictly apart for Togo: visiting, working, studying. Each has its own permission, its own evidence, and its own application channel — a tourist visa or visa-free entry covers none of the other two.

The expensive mistake is assuming intent does not matter: taking a job, enrolling, or freelancing while admitted as a visitor can lead to detention, removal, and multi-year bans. If the plan might involve work or study, secure that status before travelling, not after arrival.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

For families heading to Togo: every traveller, including infants, needs individual documentation. Where an authorization or e-visa system applies, each person — children included — needs their own approval; there are no family bundles.

A consent letter from any non-travelling parent is cheap insurance against difficult questions at the border. Dual citizens must verify Togo's rule on which nationality to travel under — entering on a foreign passport while holding Togo citizenship is an offence in some countries.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Work the Togo application backwards from the travel date. First, identify the exact visa category and its document list on Togolese government travel portal (voyage.gouv.tg). Second, gather funds evidence and bookings — most refusals trace to weak or inconsistent documentation, not to eligibility. Third, submit via the official portal or mission and keep the reference number.

Fourth, wait for the decision before committing money to flights and hotels. Published processing times are targets, not promises; consulates do not compensate for missed trips caused by slow decisions.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Trusting forum anecdotes over Togolese government travel portal (voyage.gouv.tg) — procedures change; the official portal is the only source that matters.
  • Paying fees to personal accounts — official fees go through official channels; anything else is a scam.
  • Letting the passport drop below the validity threshold — airlines deny boarding, and there is no appeal at the gate.
  • Working or studying on a visitor entry — the fastest route to removal and a multi-year ban.
  • Overstaying 'just a few days' — it voids multi-entry visas and must be declared on future applications worldwide.
  • Forgetting onward travel proof — one of the most common refusal reasons at the border itself.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Think of the Togo application as an argument with exhibits. The argument: 'I am a genuine visitor who will leave on time and can afford the trip.' Every exhibit should support exactly that — and nothing should contradict it.

Common contradictions that sink files: an itinerary showing a luxury trip against bank statements showing a thin balance; employment dates that do not match the leave letter; a stated purpose ('conference') with no invitation or registration. Re-read the whole file as a skeptic before submitting.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Online applications for Togo live or die on the portal you choose. Togolese government travel portal (voyage.gouv.tg) is the official channel; everything else is a private business. The tell-tale signs of a copycat: fees several times the official schedule, 'guaranteed approval' language, and payment to personal accounts.

Screenshot every confirmation page and save every email receipt. If the portal issues an approval letter or authorization to board, print it — phone screens fail at exactly the wrong moment, and some carriers require paper.

Translations, certifications and document format

For Togo, assemble two piles: what the checklist demands, and what proves your story. The second pile — employment letter with approved leave, property or tenancy documents, enrollment confirmation, prior travel history — is where approvals are won.

Photographs deserve more care than they get: follow the portal's dimensions, background and recency rules exactly, because a rejected photo restarts the clock. Where documents need legalization or apostille, start early — that process has its own timeline independent of the visa.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

No pre-departure biometric enrollment procedure could be verified for Togo's eVisa or registration system. Expect standard identity checks at the Lomé immigration counter.

Keep the pre-arrival registration confirmation and eVisa approval accessible (printed and on your phone): both are verified before boarding.

Families and group travel

Families should budget per person for Togo: per-person fees, per-person forms, per-person approvals. Children's applications need the same care as adults' — birth certificates where surnames differ, and consent letters where only one parent travels.

Group applications rise or fall together in practice: if the group's stated purpose is a wedding, every file should reflect the wedding. One member with a contradictory story invites scrutiny of all.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

After the immigration stamp in Togo comes customs. The two questions that matter: are you carrying more than the declarable cash threshold, and are you carrying anything restricted — food, medicine, electronics, cultural goods?

Cash thresholds are about reporting, not limits: declare honestly and keep walking. Restricted goods are different — undeclared prohibited items can mean fines or confiscation. Read the customs authority's list for Togo before you pack, not after you land.

Departure and future travel

Departure from Togo deserves the same attention as arrival. Confirm the exit requirements — some countries check for departure cards, exit stamps or proof of onward travel even when leaving.

A documented, on-time departure builds the travel history that makes the next visa easier. Conversely, even a short overstay creates a record that follows you across borders and across years. When in doubt, leave early.

How to research a route without getting misled

For Togo, rank your sources: Togolese government travel portal (voyage.gouv.tg) first, the responsible embassy second, official advisories third. Everything else — forums, influencers, visa-agent marketing — is background noise until confirmed against the top two.

Be especially careful with dated content: fee schedules, processing times and even visa categories change year to year. A 2023 guide quoting fees is not a 2026 answer. Check the publication or 'last updated' date on anything you read, then verify against Togolese government travel portal (voyage.gouv.tg) anyway.

A final document audit before travel

  • Correct visa category for the actual purpose — visiting, working and studying are different permissions
  • Passport validity and blank pages verified against Togo's rule, not a generic 'six months' assumption
  • Application reference, payment receipts and approval kept together
  • Itinerary, funds and purpose consistent across every document
  • Biometrics/interview appointment completed where required, with confirmation
  • Insurance policy documents carried, emergency assistance number saved offline
  • Customs-relevant items checked against Togo's prohibited list

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Africans need a visa for Togo?

No — since 18 May 2026, nationals of all African countries enter Togo visa-free for 30 days. The pre-arrival registration on voyage.gouv.tg (at least 24 hours before arrival) remains mandatory — visa-free does not mean registration-free.

How much is the Togo eVisa?

The fee schedule could not be verified on the official portal from a directly fetchable source — third-party tables in XOF circulate but are unverified. Check the live pricing on voyage.gouv.tg when you apply rather than trusting a quoted figure.

What is Togo's pre-arrival registration?

A mandatory online travel declaration on voyage.gouv.tg, completed at least 24 hours before arrival, required of everyone — including visa-free African nationals and eVisa holders. It is checked before boarding.

Is there visa on arrival in Togo?

Reported availability is disputed and unverified — do not plan around it. Use the eVisa (non-exempt) or the African visa-free regime with registration.

How long does the Togo eVisa take?

Third parties report 5–7 business days (the official site reportedly states a 5-working-day maximum) — not verified officially. Apply early and complete the pre-arrival registration regardless.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Togo in 2026, the story is the May reform: all African nationals enter visa-free for 30 days, everyone registers on voyage.gouv.tg 24+ hours ahead, and non-exempt nationalities take the eVisa — with live portal pricing checked at application time, since the fee table couldn't be verified from outside. The registration is the step travellers miss; the eVisa fee is the figure travellers should not trust from third parties.
