How to use this Schengen appointment guide

Every Schengen visa application ends at an in-person appointment — and the appointment is where files are won or lost. In 2026 the booking rule is uniform: appointments are scheduled online through the country’s official channel. For France that is France-Visas, where the Visa Wizard confirms whether you need a visa, which type, the document list, and where to book. This guide covers where and when to book, what to bring, and the errors to avoid.

Where appointments are actually booked

Each Schengen state runs its own booking channel — there is no single EU-wide system. France routes everything through France-Visas: complete the online application, then book the slot in the same portal. Online booking is now mandatory. The official Wizard is free and definitive; agents add cost, not accuracy. Consulates do not sell appointments, so ignore anyone selling “priority slots”.

When to book: timing the appointment

Schengen decisions take around 15 working days, up to 45 for complex cases (2026) — the appointment, not the document file, is usually the bottleneck. Peak-season slots fill early and online booking is now enforced, so book as soon as your travel window opens. Longer stays take more: work visas average around 30 days, student visas 15 days to 3 weeks (longer in the June–August rush).

Book the right consulate, not the free slot

Apply at the consulate of your main destination — the country with the most nights — or, if nights are equal, the first Schengen country you enter. Filing wherever an appointment was free is one of the official refusal grounds: it invites a refusal, and the €90 fee is not refunded. Count the nights and book where they point. Refusals sit in the shared Visa Information System, so a booking shortcut follows you.

The €90 adult fee (2026) is the uniform EU-wide charge, paid at the visa centre with a receipt — never to a personal account. It is not refunded on refusal. Every applicant carries a fee; the appointment adds no separate official charge.

Documents to bring to the appointment

The Commission’s baseline file: form, compliant photograph, passport, travel medical insurance, and documents on purpose, means, accommodation, and intention to return. Bring originals plus one copy: a passport issued within the last 10 years and valid 3+ months beyond departure (check the issue date, not just expiry); insurance of at least €30,000 for emergency care, hospitalisation and repatriation, valid Schengen-wide for the whole stay, stating cover, territory and dates exactly — without compliant proof, applications are refused; a dated itinerary, 3–6 months of bank statements showing genuine income, an employment letter with leave dates, and accommodation for every night. Funds must be built over time — a sudden unexplained deposit reads as borrowed money. Follow the official document checklist literally — the incomplete file is the most common cause of delay.

Biometrics at the appointment

First-time applicants give fingerprints and a photograph at the visa centre; the data sits in the Visa Information System for 59 months. A brief interview on purpose, funding and ties is standard — keep answers consistent with the written file, since contradictions are an official refusal ground. At the border, officers may re-check purpose and means, so keep the same evidence accessible.

Families and groups: one appointment per traveller

Every traveller — including infants — needs a separate application and appointment, with per-child fees (€45 for ages 6–11, free for under-6s, 2026). Files are decided individually; book together where the system allows. If one member is refused, fix its reason before re-applying rather than resubmitting identical facts — the refusal is in the system either way.

Appointment mistakes that lead to refusal

  • Booking late. Peak-season slots go early and online booking is enforced.
  • Consulate-shopping. Applying where slots are free, not where your nights point, is a refusal ground.
  • Non-compliant insurance. Under €30,000 or not Schengen-wide means refusal.
  • Incomplete file. Gaps become refused grounds on funds, purpose or return.
  • Using an agent. The Wizard is free and definitive; agents add cost, not accuracy.
  • Assuming ETIAS replaces the appointment. Not operational as of October 2026.

If the decision is a refusal

A refusal arrives on the standard EU form (Annex VI to the Visa Code), grounds ticked — usually unproven purpose, insufficient funds, unreliable documents, or doubt you will leave in time. The €90 fee is not refunded. Both doors stay open: appeal under the refusing country’s law, or reapply at any time — no ban, no waiting period. But an unchanged reapplication gets the same answer, and the refusal must be declared. Appeal when the evidence was right and the decision misread it; reapply when the evidence was the problem — fix the ticked grounds before spending another €90.

Scam avoidance around appointments

For France, france-visas.gouv.fr is the only official portal — the pattern holds area-wide: the country’s own consulate or visa portal is the only legitimate booking channel. Ignore lookalike domains selling “guaranteed” visas or priority slots; consulates sell neither, and fees are paid at the visa centre with a receipt, never to a personal account. Treat ETIAS sellers the same way: not operational as of October 2026, so no legitimate ETIAS booking exists.

A final pre-appointment audit

  • Appointment on the official portal — confirmation printed
  • Right consulate: most nights, or first entry if equal
  • Passport: issued within 10 years, valid 3+ months beyond departure
  • Insurance: €30,000+, Schengen-wide, dates matching
  • Purpose, funds, accommodation, return — originals plus one copy
  • Fee ready: €90 / €45 ages 6–11 (2026)

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I book a Schengen visa appointment?

Through the official portal of the country you are applying to. For France, complete the online application on France-Visas, then book the slot in the same portal. Online booking is mandatory.

How much does a Schengen visa cost in 2026?

The uniform EU fee is €90 for adults, €45 for children 6–11, free for under-6s — paid at the visa centre. Not refunded on refusal.

Can I apply at a different consulate to get an earlier appointment?

No. Apply at the consulate of your main destination (most nights), or the first country entered if nights are equal. Filing where a slot was free is a refusal ground.

Do children need their own appointment?

Yes — every traveller, including infants, needs a separate application and appointment with per-child fees. Book together where allowed.

How far ahead should I book the appointment?

As soon as your window opens: ~15 working days for decisions, and slots fill in peak season.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

The appointment is the Schengen bottleneck: book it online through the official portal as soon as your window opens, at the consulate your nights point to, with the full file. Budget €90 for adults and €45 for ages 6–11 (2026), expect biometrics on your first visit (stored 59 months), and about 15 working days for the decision. Book late, book wrong, or trust an agent’s promises, and you pay with time or a refusal.
