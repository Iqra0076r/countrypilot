Who needs a visa for India

Most foreign visitors need a visa to enter India in 2026. The default route is the e-Visa, open to eligible nationalities of around 166 countries, applied for online at indianvisaonline.gov.in - the only official online channel. There is no general visa-on-arrival scheme: arriving without a valid visa or e-Visa approval risks refusal at the border. Nationalities outside the e-Visa scheme, and travellers needing long-stay categories, must use regular consular visas through Indian missions and VFS centres. The detailed route-by-route breakdown is in the India visa and entry requirements guide.

eVisa eligibility and categories

The e-Visa covers four main categories: e-Tourist, e-Business, e-Medical and e-Conference. Eligibility is nationality-based, so always use the portal's eligibility tool for your passport rather than assuming - the list of ~166 countries is the scheme's reach, not a guarantee for every passport. Choose the category honestly: a tourist e-Visa never authorises work, and entering on the wrong category can end your trip at the border. e-Tourist fees run US$10-80 by duration and season (plus a 3-4% transaction charge), with nationality exceptions such as the US (US$160) and the UK 5-year (US$484) - see the India eVisa fee breakdown for the full table.

Passport and document requirements

Carry a passport valid for at least 6 months beyond arrival with at least two blank pages - Indian immigration is strict on this. The passport details on the e-Visa application must match the travel document exactly; even a small mismatch between the approval and the passport can cause problems at the desk. Every child needs their own passport and their own India permission - there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

The mandatory E-Arrival Card

Since 1 April 2026, India requires a mandatory digital E-Arrival Card for all arriving passengers, including OCI holders. It is filed via the Bureau of Immigration site or the Su-Swagatam app and generates a QR code for entry processing. This is a registration step separate from the visa itself - having an approved e-Visa does not cover it. Complete it before you fly and keep the QR code accessible offline alongside your e-Visa approval.

What immigration officers may ask for

Whatever the official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip - bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the e-Visa approval or visa printout and the E-Arrival QR accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied - new passport, different flight, longer stay - say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Permitted activities and length of stay

e-Visa stays follow the granted duration - 30 days, 1 year or 5 years, with per-visit stay limits within that validity - and extensions are not routine. Convert to the proper long-stay category before the e-Visa expires if plans change; overstaying draws fines and future-visa consequences. A tourist e-Visa covers tourism, casual visits and short recreational activity - it never authorises employment. And note the e-Visa is valid only at designated airports and seaports: it is not valid at land borders, so overland travellers must use a regular consular visa.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using lookalike e-Visa sites. Only indianvisaonline.gov.in is official - agencies charge multiples of the government fee.
  • Skipping the E-Arrival Card. Mandatory from 1 April 2026 for everyone, including OCI holders - the visa alone is not enough.
  • Assuming the e-Visa works at land borders. Designated airports and seaports only; overland travellers need a regular visa.
  • Applying too late. Apply a minimum of 4 days before arrival - the standard ~72-hour processing is typical, not an official guarantee.
  • Working on a tourist e-Visa. Employment and project work need their own visa categories; misuse can end the trip.
  • Travelling on a near-expiry passport. 6+ months validity and two blank pages is the rule, and officers enforce it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa to visit India in 2026?Most visitors do. Around 166 nationalities can use the online e-Visa at indianvisaonline.gov.in; everyone else needs a regular consular visa. There is no general visa-on-arrival scheme.
What is the E-Arrival Card?A mandatory digital arrival card from 1 April 2026 for all arriving passengers including OCI holders, filed via the Bureau of Immigration site or Su-Swagatam app - it generates a QR code for entry processing.
How early should I apply for an India eVisa?Apply a minimum of 4 days before arrival. Standard processing runs about 72 hours, but that is typical, not an official guarantee - apply early rather than risking a last-minute refusal.
Can I enter India by land with an eVisa?No - the e-Visa is valid only at designated airports and seaports, not at land borders. Overland travellers need a regular consular visa.
Can I work in India on a tourist eVisa?No - a tourist e-Visa never authorises work. Employment (E) and Project (P) visas are separate categories with their own requirements and fees.
The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

India's 2026 entry rules are portal-discipline rules: indianvisaonline.gov.in only, eligibility checked for your passport, a passport valid 6+ months with two blank pages, and the mandatory E-Arrival Card filed before you fly. Enter through designated airports or seaports, stay within the granted duration, and never work on a tourist e-Visa.
