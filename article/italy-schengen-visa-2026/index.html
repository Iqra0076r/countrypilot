Italy Schengen Visa 2026

How to use this Italy Schengen visa guide

The Schengen (Type C) visa is Italy's short-stay visa for tourism, business and courses up to 90 days. Italy's visa system is run by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MAECI) through Il Visto per l'Italia at vistoperitalia.esteri.it — check your nationality's position and the competent consulate there first. This guide was verified against MAECI consular pages in October 2026; for the fee breakdown across all visa types, see our Italy visa fee 2026 guide.

What the Schengen visa allows — and forbids

A Schengen visa lets you stay up to 90 days in any 180-day period, counted area-wide across all Schengen states — not 90 days per country. Italy is a common first Schengen entry point on multi-country itineraries, and passport control happens at the Italian airport even when Italy is not the final destination.

What it forbids: the Schengen visa never authorizes work in Italy — not for an Italian employer, not remote work for a foreign one. Employment needs the national route (the Decreto Flussi quota system or the quota-exempt EU Blue Card). Overstaying risks fines and future refusals recorded area-wide. For stays over 90 days, the national Type D system — and its different fees — is the only route: read the Italy visa & entry requirements guide for that track.

Fee and processing timeline in 2026

The fee is €90 for adults, €45 for children aged 6–12, free for under-6s — in force since 11 June 2024 and unchanged for 2026. Decisions come within 15 calendar days standard, up to 45 days maximum in complex cases.

Applications are made in person at the competent consulate. First-time applicants provide fingerprints and a photograph (stored in the Visa Information System for 59 months), so plan an in-person appointment — there is no fully online route and no eVisa. The visa is a sticker in the passport, not a digital approval.

Documents the consulate checks

  • Passport — issued within the last 10 years, valid at least 3 months beyond your intended departure from the Schengen area, with blank pages for the sticker and stamps.
  • Photograph — Schengen specification, 35×45 mm.
  • Travel medical insurance — minimum €30,000 cover, valid across the whole Schengen area; a visa condition, not a recommendation.
  • Itinerary — accommodation proof, return or onward ticket.
  • Funds and ties — bank statements and employment or enrolment letters showing you can fund the trip and will return.
  • Children — each child needs a separate passport and application, including infants (at €45 for ages 6–12, free under 6).

Documents not in Italian or English should carry certified translations — some consulates prefer Italian. Consulates decide files on purpose, funds and ties, so dated bookings and genuine statements form the core; compare policy options in our Italy travel insurance & health guide.

Applying step by step

1) Identify the competent Italian consulate on vistoperitalia.esteri.it. 2) Complete the application form and assemble the documents above. 3) Attend in person for biometrics if this is your first Schengen application. 4) Pay €90 (2026) at the consulate with an official receipt. 5) Allow 15 calendar days standard, up to 45 maximum.

Book travel after the visa is issued where possible — a refused or delayed file with non-refundable bookings is the costliest common failure.

At the Italian border

The visa lets you present yourself at the border — the Polizia di Frontiera officer decides admission. Carry the passport with the visa sticker, accommodation proof, the return ticket, insurance proof and funds evidence. Purpose and financing are the standard questions; answer consistently with the application file.

Depart within the 90/180-day allowance — overstaying risks fines and future refusals recorded area-wide. Declare cash of €10,000 or more when crossing the EU's external border; honest declaration carries no tax, failure to declare risks seizure.

No eVisa: scam avoidance

Italy offers no eVisa. Anyone selling an 'Italy eVisa' online is running a scam — the only official channels are vistoperitalia.esteri.it and the consulate. Official fees are receipted at the consulate: never pay a 'visa fee' to a personal account. And remember that no agent can guarantee approval or sell a faster decision; anyone promising either is monetizing your anxiety.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Counting 90 days per country. The Schengen clock is area-wide across all member states.
  • Working on the visa. Never authorized — the Flussi quota or Blue Card national route is the only route.
  • Treating insurance as optional. €30,000 Schengen-wide cover is a visa condition; files arrive refused without it.
  • Paying an eVisa site. Italy has no eVisa — in-person application at the consulate with biometrics (first time).
  • Ignoring the €10,000 cash rule. Declare at the EU external border or risk seizure.
  • Applying at the wrong consulate. Competence follows residence and main destination — check vistoperitalia.esteri.it first.

If your trip might extend beyond 90 days, plan the national route from the start — the Italy visa extension guide explains the limits of stretching a short stay.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the Italy Schengen visa in 2026?

€90 for adults, €45 for children aged 6–12, and free for children under 6. This fee has applied since 11 June 2024 and is unchanged for 2026. Each traveller — including infants — pays the applicable fee with a separate application.

How long does the Italy Schengen visa take?

Standard processing is 15 calendar days, with a maximum of 45 days in complex cases. First-time applicants must attend in person for biometrics (fingerprints and photograph), so allow time for the consulate appointment itself.

Do I need travel insurance for the Italy Schengen visa?

Yes — it is a visa condition, not a recommendation. The policy must provide minimum €30,000 medical cover and be valid across the entire Schengen area. Files submitted without compliant insurance are refused.

Can I work in Italy on a Schengen visa?

No. The Schengen visa never authorizes work. Employment requires the national route: the Decreto Flussi quota system (employer files on a click day, then the €116 national work visa) or the quota-exempt EU Blue Card (national visa also €116).

Does the visa guarantee I will be let into Italy?

No. The visa lets you present yourself at the border — the Polizia di Frontiera officer decides admission. Carry your accommodation proof, return ticket, insurance proof and funds evidence; overstaying risks fines and future refusals recorded area-wide.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Italy's Schengen visa in 2026, the recipe is simple: confirm your route on vistoperitalia.esteri.it, apply in person at the competent consulate (€90 adults / €45 children 6–12 / free under 6), bring €30,000 Schengen-wide insurance, valid funds and ties evidence, and biometrics if it's your first time. Allow 15 calendar days (up to 45), never work on the visa, and keep the 90-day clock area-wide — not per country.
