Australia Skilled Occupation List 2026: How to Check Your Occupation

Use the live Home Affairs occupation list to map an occupation to the relevant visa, list and assessing authority—without assuming that appearing on one list makes you eligible for every skilled visa.

By CountryPilot Editorial TeamReviewed 7 October 2026
CountryPilot Verification GuideAustraliaSkilled Occupation List
Do not use a copied static occupation list as your final check. Home Affairs maintains the current searchable skilled occupation list and links the relevant legislative instruments. Occupation eligibility can differ by visa subclass, stream, ANZSCO version and caveat.

What the Australian skilled occupation list tells you

The Home Affairs combined list connects occupations with ANZSCO codes, eligible visa pathways, the applicable occupation list and the relevant skills-assessing authority. Depending on the visa, you may see the Core Skills Occupation List (CSOL), Medium and Long-term Strategic Skills List (MLTSSL), Short-term Skilled Occupation List (STSOL) or Regional Occupation List (ROL).

CSOL vs MLTSSL, STSOL and ROL

ListPlanning meaning
CSOLCore Skills Occupation List used for relevant subclass 482 Skills in Demand and subclass 186 Direct Entry settings. Check the current visa/stream rules and caveats.
MLTSSLMedium and Long-term Strategic Skills List used across relevant points-tested and other skilled settings.
STSOLShort-term Skilled Occupation List used for relevant skilled visa settings shown in the official combined list.
ROLRegional Occupation List used for relevant regional pathways.

ANZSCO version matters

Home Affairs currently explains that subclass 186 and 482 programs use ANZSCO 2022, including CSOL occupations, while other skilled visa subclasses use ANZSCO 2013 for MLTSSL, STSOL and ROL. That means an occupation title alone is not enough: check the code and version attached to your intended visa.

How to verify your occupation

  1. Identify the occupation that best matches your actual duties—not merely your preferred job title.
  2. Search the official Home Affairs occupation list.
  3. Confirm the ANZSCO code/version shown for your intended visa.
  4. Check whether your exact visa subclass and stream appear for that occupation.
  5. Read any caveats.
  6. Open the listed assessing authority and confirm its current assessment requirements.
  7. Then verify the separate visa requirements, including points, nomination, sponsorship, salary, English and experience where applicable.

Occupation-list status does not equal visa eligibility

An occupation appearing in the combined list is only one part of a skilled-migration assessment. Your intended subclass can impose separate requirements. For points-tested 189, 190 and 491 planning, use the Australia PR Points Calculator. For employer-sponsored 482, 186 and 494 routes, use the Australia Sponsored Visa Comparison.

If an occupation is removed

Home Affairs states that a subsequent removal does not adversely affect pending nomination or visa applications simply because the occupation was later removed. Existing skilled visa holders may, however, need to re-check the position if circumstances change—for example, a new employer or occupation requires a new nomination.

Editorial method

This page deliberately does not reproduce hundreds of occupations because a copied list becomes stale. It explains how to use the current government source and how to avoid mixing lists, ANZSCO versions and visa subclasses.
