How to use this Sri Lanka visa and entry guide

Sri Lanka's entry system centers on one mandatory step: the Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA), obtained online before travel at the official portal eta.gov.lk — there is no general visa on arrival, and copycat sites charge fees for ETAs that are free or cheaper on the official site. Apply only at eta.gov.lk.

Two 2026 changes reshape the picture. First, Sri Lanka launched a Digital Nomad Visa effective 20 April 2026: a renewable 365-day stay, USD 500 first-year application fee, and a USD 2,000/month minimum foreign-source income (plus USD 500 per dependent). Second, since 25 May 2026 the 30-day tourist ETA is free for citizens of 40 countries (including India and Australia) — but the approved ETA itself remains mandatory before travel. This guide was verified against official Sri Lankan sources in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport must be valid for at least six months beyond your arrival date in Sri Lanka, with at least one blank page. This is enforced at ETA application and at the border — a passport inside the six-month window means refusal.

Because the ETA is linked to the passport number, renewing the passport after ETA approval voids the authorization. Renew first, then apply for the ETA.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Nearly every visitor needs an ETA approved before departure — standard 30-day double-entry tourist ETA costs USD 50 (SAARC nationals USD 20), except the 40 nationalities whose ETA is now free under the May 2026 scheme. Airlines check ETA approval before boarding.

Transit and onward travel

Sri Lanka offers transit ETA arrangements for short stopovers, but the practical rule is simple: if you will clear immigration in Colombo — even for a few hours — you need the appropriate ETA in advance. Passengers remaining airside on a single through-ticket generally do not.

Do not assume a long layover can be spent landside without paperwork: apply for the transit ETA before travel if there is any chance you will exit the transit area. The 30-day tourist ETA covers transit needs for most travellers and is free for the 40 scheme nationalities.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Admission to Sri Lanka is granted by the officer at the port of entry, whatever your visa says. Practical preparation: passport with the visa, printed onward travel, first accommodation address, and proof you can support yourself for the stated duration.

Officers are screening for intent — do your stated plans, funds and ties to home add up to a genuine short visit? Carry employment letters, enrollment certificates or property documents as appropriate. Inconsistency between your documents and your answers is the fastest route to secondary questioning.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

For Sri Lanka, separate the health paperwork from the visa paperwork and check both. Vaccination certificates — yellow fever above all — are demanded by border health officers in many countries regardless of visa type, and the requirement follows your travel history, not your nationality.

If a certificate is required and you cannot produce it, you may be vaccinated on the spot, quarantined, or refused entry depending on the country. Check the current requirement on ETA portal, Department of Immigration and Emigration or the health ministry's site within two weeks of travel, since outbreak rules change faster than visa rules.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Do not confuse 'insurance not checked at the border' with 'insurance not needed' for Sri Lanka. Border officers rarely ask, hospitals always bill. Buy the policy before departure — you cannot buy meaningful cover after you need it.

The useful test is evacuation: if you needed an air ambulance home, would the policy pay? Confirm medical, evacuation and repatriation limits in writing, keep the emergency assistance number offline on your phone, and carry the policy certificate with your travel documents.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Two dates matter when you enter Sri Lanka: the visa's expiry and the stay granted at the border. The second one governs. Verify it immediately on arrival — errors happen, and correcting an admission stamp is far easier inside the immigration hall than weeks later.

An overstay record follows you: it can void a multi-entry visa, trigger entry bans, and must be declared on future applications to most countries. Treat the admitted-until date as a hard deadline.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Sri Lanka treats work, study and visiting as entirely different legal regimes. The question at the border is always 'does this person's permission match what they intend to do' — and officers are trained to probe exactly that.

Remote work is the modern trap: immigration law cares where your body is, not where your employer sits. If you will work while in Sri Lanka, you need work authorization for Sri Lanka; if you will study, you need the student route with the school's documentation. Get the right status first.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Do not assume children's paperwork is simpler for Sri Lanka — it is the same process, per child. Separate applications, separate fees where they apply, separate approvals.

Carry birth certificates when the family name differs between parent and child, and a notarized consent letter if only one parent travels. For dual nationals, the controlling question is which citizenship Sri Lanka recognizes at its border: check before booking, because the answer determines which passport to present.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

The Sri Lanka workflow that avoids most failures: verify the route, prepare the file, apply officially, wait for the decision. Each step has a classic error — wrong category, thin documentation, copycat websites charging multiples of the official fee, and non-refundable bookings made before approval.

Document discipline matters more than speed: consistent dates, names spelled identically everywhere, bank statements that actually cover the claimed funds, and an itinerary a stranger would find credible. Officers decide on the file in front of them.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Last-minute applications — processing estimates assume a clean file; yours may not be, and queries add weeks.
  • Photographs to the wrong specification — a surprising share of rejections start here; follow the portal's photo guide exactly.
  • Hiding a previous refusal — non-disclosure is misrepresentation, far worse than the original refusal.
  • Booking through an unverified agent — if you must use help, verify the agency against the embassy's listed partners.
  • Carrying no funds evidence — border officers can and do ask; 'it's all on my phone' is not an answer.
  • Skipping travel insurance — not always checked, always needed.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Quality in a Sri Lanka file means completeness plus consistency. Complete: every required document present, photographs to specification, forms fully answered with no blanks officers must guess at. Consistent: identical name spellings, matching dates, funds that plausibly cover the itinerary.

Where the rules allow discretion — and they always do — the officer fills gaps with judgment. A tidy, well-ordered file invites the benefit of the doubt; a chaotic one invites refusal. Number your documents and reference them in the cover letter.

Processing: standard ETA applications are typically approved within 24 hours (official guidance: allow 24–72 hours; apply at least one week before travel). Employment residence visas take longer — line-ministry approval typically 2–4 weeks plus immigration approval 2–3 weeks, so apply at least a month ahead. No official processing-time figure could be verified for student residence visas — apply early and track via the department.

Proof of funds: no official minimum is published by the Department of Immigration and Emigration. Universities require bank letters evidencing sufficient funds; one non-official source cites USD 1,500/year in bank encashment receipts for non-university students, but that figure is not officially verified — confirm the expectation with your institution and the department. No official mandatory student health-insurance cost figure is published either.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Treat the Sri Lanka online application like a financial transaction, because it is one. Verify the site is ETA portal, Department of Immigration and Emigration before entering card details; legitimate portals never ask for payment via crypto, gift cards or personal bank transfers.

Apply with enough lead time that a portal glitch does not become a missed trip — online systems go down, and 'the website was broken' is not grounds for an expedited decision. The approval document, once issued, travels with you in print and as a backup on your phone.

Translations, certifications and document format

The Sri Lanka document rule is simple: if the officer has to guess, you lose. Label everything, date everything, and make sure names, dates and amounts match across the file — a passport that says one name and a bank statement showing another needs an explanation attached, not discovered.

Do not submit originals you cannot afford to lose unless required; certified copies are accepted for most supporting evidence. And keep a complete duplicate of the submitted file — if the mission queries anything, you answer from the same papers they hold.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

No pre-departure biometric enrollment procedure could be verified for Sri Lanka's ETA or residence-visa routes — the ETA is a purely online authorization. Expect standard identity checks (photograph, possibly fingerprints) at the immigration counter on arrival.

Keep the approved ETA accessible (printed and on your phone): carriers verify it before boarding, and immigration may ask to see it alongside the passport it is linked to.

Families and group travel

Do not let a child's application be an afterthought for Sri Lanka. Minors need their own passport, their own form, and — where the system requires it — their own interview or biometrics appointment, with its own lead time.

If the family includes dual nationals, check Sri Lanka's entry rule per person before booking: the passport each traveller presents determines the route, and mixing them up at the border causes delays at best.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Plan for Sri Lanka customs as carefully as the visa. Currency declaration thresholds apply per person or per family depending on the country — know which. Keep high-value electronics' receipts accessible in case of questions about temporary import.

Medications in original containers with prescriptions; no loose pills in unmarked bottles. And verify drone, radio and satellite-device rules specifically — these are controlled in many countries and confiscated at the border without the right permit.

Departure and future travel

The trip is not over until you have exited Sri Lanka cleanly. Keep evidence of timely departure: boarding passes, exit stamps, the electronic record where one exists.

Future applications routinely ask about previous overstays and refusals — a clean record is the simplest possible answer. If you needed an extension and got one, keep that paperwork too; it proves you handled the situation through proper channels.

How to research a route without getting misled

The research method that works for Sri Lanka: start at ETA portal, Department of Immigration and Emigration, confirm with the embassy, sanity-check against a current official advisory. Three agreeing official sources beat thirty forum posts.

Watch for the two classic misinformation patterns: copycat sites presenting themselves as official (always verify the domain), and agents describing their own service bundle as if it were the government fee. The government fee is published on ETA portal, Department of Immigration and Emigration; everything above it is someone's margin.

A final document audit before travel

  • Route confirmed on ETA portal, Department of Immigration and Emigration for your specific nationality — not a friend's, not a forum's
  • All names, dates and amounts identical across the application file
  • Translations completed for any document not in the mission's language
  • Photographs to the portal's exact specification
  • Onward travel provable at the border, not just intended
  • Funds evidence covering the whole trip, seasoned over months not days
  • Health and insurance paperwork separate from — and as complete as — the visa file
  • Departure plan inside the permitted stay, with buffer days

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Sri Lanka ETA free now?

For citizens of 40 countries (including India and Australia), the 30-day tourist ETA has been free since 25 May 2026 — but the ETA itself remains mandatory and must be approved before travel. Everyone else pays the standard USD 50 (SAARC nationals USD 20). Apply only at eta.gov.lk.

What is Sri Lanka's Digital Nomad Visa?

Launched 20 April 2026: a renewable 365-day stay for remote workers, with a USD 500 first-year application fee and a minimum USD 2,000/month in foreign-source income (plus USD 500 per dependent). Applications go through the ETA/immigration portal.

How long does the Sri Lanka ETA take?

Most tourist ETAs are approved within 24 hours; official guidance says allow 24–72 hours and apply at least a week before travel. Do not board without the approval — carriers check.

Can I work in Sri Lanka on a tourist ETA?

No. Employment requires an employment residence visa (LKR 20,000 per 12 months, 2026) with line-ministry approval — a 4–7 week process. Remote workers should look at the Digital Nomad Visa instead.

Do I need travel insurance for Sri Lanka?

It is not a published ETA condition, but comprehensive health insurance is listed among student-visa documents — and is plain common sense for any trip. No official mandatory cost figure is published.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Sri Lanka, everything starts at eta.gov.lk — and only there, since copycat sites monetize the same application. The 30-day tourist ETA is USD 50 (free for 40 nationalities since May 2026, SAARC USD 20), usually approved within a day; residence visas run LKR 20,000 per year with longer lead times. Remote workers have a real new option in the April 2026 Digital Nomad Visa. Verify the current scheme list before paying anything.
