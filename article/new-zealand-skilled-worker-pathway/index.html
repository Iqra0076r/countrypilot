If you are planning to work in New Zealand, the first task is to identify the exact work-authorisation route—not to search for a generic ‘work visa’. Employer sponsorship, open permits, skilled-worker routes and job-search permissions can have completely different rules. This guide explains how to match a job and applicant profile to the legal route, what evidence normally matters, and which official page should control the final decision.

Current official context — checked 5 October 2026

The Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) currently requires an offer of full-time work from an accredited employer and, depending on the job, qualification/experience and English requirements. INZ lists a stay of up to five years based on the job, a cost from NZD 1,540, and 80% of recent AEWV applications completed within about 5.5 weeks. The job must offer at least 30 hours of work a week.

Official page: https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/accredited-employer-work-visa/

Choose the legal work route before the job search

New Zealand does not have one universal 'work visa'. The relevant route may be Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) and other work visas. Start by checking whether the occupation, employer and applicant profile fit the route; then target employers and jobs that can actually use it. For the AEWV, employer accreditation, job-check requirements and the employment agreement are central; workers should verify the employer in INZ systems.

This saves time because many vacancies are perfectly genuine but are not suitable for overseas sponsorship. A candidate should know the occupation classification, qualification requirements, professional registration and any salary or skill threshold before accepting a role.

Employer and job requirements

Employer sponsorship, where required, is a legal role rather than a marketing phrase. Verify the employer in any official sponsor/accreditation register and confirm the job title, duties, hours, location and pay. The written offer should match the facts later entered into the immigration application. use Immigration New Zealand (INZ) to confirm the live requirement where it can change.

If a recruiter asks the worker to reimburse prohibited sponsorship or recruitment costs, buy a fake qualification, or pay for a job offer, stop. Legitimate immigration systems increasingly publish anti-exploitation guidance and sponsor compliance rules.

Qualification, language and registration

A visa may accept a job only if the applicant has the skills the role requires. Regulated occupations—healthcare, teaching, engineering, law and others—can require professional registration in addition to immigration approval. Foreign qualifications may also need formal recognition or an educational assessment. re-check Immigration New Zealand (INZ) before relying on it.

Language evidence can come from an approved test, nationality, degree taught in an accepted language or another route-specific exemption. Use only the proof method the official rules recognise.

Application evidence and timing

The strongest work-visa file links the employment evidence to the legal criteria: employer eligibility, contract or offer, occupation code, salary, qualifications, language, identity and any health/character documents. Keep a one-page checklist showing which document proves each criterion. compare it with the current Immigration New Zealand (INZ) guidance linked below.

Timing matters. Some sponsorship documents expire, some permits must be approved before entry, and some routes allow in-country switching only from specified statuses. Check the rule on the date the application is submitted, not when the job search began.
