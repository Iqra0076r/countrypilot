How to read a scholarship listing

Treat every scholarship as a data record. Record: provider, host country, eligible institutions, study level, subject restrictions, nationality rules, funding type, value, duration, opening date, deadline and official application link. In Switzerland, useful discovery points include Swiss Government Excellence Scholarships, university scholarships. Aggregators are excellent for discovery, but the provider's page controls the final rule.

A listing that says 'fully funded' should be unpacked. Does it cover tuition only, or tuition plus stipend, travel, insurance and visa costs? Does the stipend run for the entire degree? Are dependants covered? These details matter more than the label. compare it with the current Erasmus+ / Erasmus Mundus guidance linked below.

Eligibility: eliminate impossible options first

Do not start by writing essays. First eliminate awards that do not fit nationality, degree level, academic background, work experience, age or subject. Then check whether admission to the university is required before the scholarship application. Some awards use a single combined process; others require two separate applications. re-check State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) and cantonal authorities before relying on it.

Create an eligibility grid and keep screenshots or PDFs of the official criteria. If the wording is ambiguous, contact the provider rather than relying on a forum answer. compare it with the current State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) and cantonal authorities guidance linked below.

Funding and hidden costs

For study in Switzerland, compare the award against the full cost of attendance: tuition, mandatory fees, accommodation, food, health cover, visa costs, travel, books and initial settlement. A partial scholarship can still be excellent, but only if the remaining funding gap is realistic.

When a stipend is quoted monthly, ask whether it is paid for 12 months, academic months only, or a fixed award period. When tuition is waived, check whether laboratory, student-services or insurance fees remain. re-check State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) and cantonal authorities before relying on it.

Documents and selection evidence

Common scholarship evidence includes transcripts, degree certificates, references, CV, personal statement, research proposal, language results and proof of admission. Competitive awards often assess more than grades: leadership, development impact, research potential or a clear professional trajectory can matter. use State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) and cantonal authorities to confirm the live requirement where it can change.

References should be chosen for relevance, not status. Give referees the scholarship criteria and enough time to write specific evidence rather than a generic character letter. compare it with the current State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) and cantonal authorities guidance linked below.

Writing a stronger application

A useful essay answers three questions with evidence: what have you already done, why does this programme solve a real next-step need, and what will you do with the opportunity afterwards? Avoid vague claims about passion or changing the world unless they are supported by concrete work, study or community outcomes. Deadline strategy and fraud checks

Work backwards from the official deadline. Reserve time for transcripts, references, language tests and university admission.
