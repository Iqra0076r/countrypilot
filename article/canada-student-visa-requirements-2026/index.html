Canada Student Visa Requirements 2026

Canada's 2026 student visa rules: a study permit for courses over 6 months, DLI acceptance, CAD $23,448 a year in funds, and full IRCC document, medical and processing guidance.

Canada calls its student visa a study permit. Who needs a study permit

Canada's student visa is the study permit. Study for more than 6 months needs a study permit — short courses of 6 months or less do not. Note that the permit is study authorization, not an entry document on its own: applicants from visa-required countries (including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh) also need a visitor visa/TRV (CAD $100 + CAD $85 biometrics), while visa-exempt nationals flying to Canada need a CAD $7 eTA linked to the passport they travel on.

Your passport must be valid for the duration of your proposed travel — a visa or permit cannot outlast the passport it sits in, so renew early if expiry falls inside your study window.

The four core requirements

IRCC's study-permit eligibility turns on four things. First, acceptance by a designated learning institution (DLI) — only DLIs can host international students. Second, evidence of sufficient funds — the 2026 proof-of-funds figures are covered below. Third, admissibility — IRCC may require a medical exam or police certificate depending on the file. Fourth, proof that the applicant will respect the temporary nature of the permit — the file must show the study plan is genuine and time-limited, on the IRCC study-permit page at canada.ca.

DLI acceptance: the first gate

DLI lists and provincial/university admissions pages are the starting point. Programme accreditation, curriculum, graduate outcomes and location often matter more than rankings.

Language tests sit between the acceptance and the permit: IRCC's study-permit context lists English tests such as IELTS, TOEFL and PTE in the admissions pipeline — compare them in our Canada English test comparison.

Proof of funds: the 2026 figures

For applications on or after 1 September 2026, a single applicant outside Quebec must show CAD $23,448 per year, plus first-year tuition and travel — up from CAD $22,895. Older guides still quoting CAD $22,895 are outdated; ignore them. Quebec sets its own figure: CAD $24,617 per year from 1 January 2026.

This is evidence, not a payment to IRCC — bank statements, education loans, scholarships and sponsor funds all count. For how students structure this documentation, see the Canada visitor visa proof-of-funds guide (the same evidence principles apply) and our tuition financing guide for loan fees, grace periods and prepayment rules.

Document checklist

  • Acceptance letter from a designated learning institution (DLI)
  • Passport valid for the duration of proposed travel
  • Proof of funds: CAD $23,448/year (outside Quebec, from 1 Sept 2026) or CAD $24,617/year (Quebec, from 1 Jan 2026), plus first-year tuition and travel
  • Financial evidence: bank statements, loan approval, scholarship letters, sponsor documents
  • Language test results (IELTS / TOEFL / PTE) where the institution requires them
  • Photographs meeting the IRCC specification
  • Medical exam results and police certificate if IRCC instructs them for your file
  • TRV or eTA for entry, depending on nationality

Medical exams and police certificates

Medical examinations enter the picture only for stays longer than 6 months or for applicants who have lived in designated countries — in which case IRCC instructs you to use a panel physician, and the results go directly to IRCC, not into your luggage. Since a study permit by definition covers study longer than 6 months, most study-permit applicants should expect the medical step.

IRCC may request an interview or additional documents (medical exam, police certificate) depending on the file. Respond promptly — the processing clock does not wait for late documents. For health-cover planning once admitted, see our mandatory health cover guide.

Application workflow

1) Secure DLI acceptance and gather language test results. 2) Assemble proof of funds at the 2026 figures plus first-year tuition and travel. 3) Apply online through an IRCC account, paying the CAD $150 fee plus CAD $85 biometrics (fingerprints and a photo at a Visa Application Centre; CAD $170 family maximum). 4) Give biometrics when instructed — the application is not complete without them. 5) Complete the medical exam with a panel physician if instructed, and supply any police certificate IRCC requests. 6) Track the file and respond to any IRCC request for an interview or further documents.

Pay only through IRCC's official channels on canada.ca — the same copycat-scam pattern that charges $50–80 to 'process' a CAD $7 eTA exists around study permits. Anyone who cannot show you an official IRCC receipt for the CAD $150 is not paying IRCC.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Applying to a non-DLI. Only designated learning institutions can host international students — an acceptance from any other school cannot support a permit.
  • Quoting the old funds figure. CAD $22,895 is gone; applications from 1 September 2026 need CAD $23,448 outside Quebec.
  • Confusing the permit with entry documents. Visa-required nationals also need a TRV (CAD $100 + biometrics); visa-exempt flyers need a CAD $7 eTA.
  • Treating the CAD $150 as the total cost. Add CAD $85 biometrics, plus funds, tuition, travel, medical and police-certificate costs.
  • Altering or 'fixing' documents. Never alter scans; explain inconsistencies instead.
  • Applying late. Actuals at busy posts run 5–10 weeks — start the day the acceptance letter arrives.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the Canada student visa requirements in 2026?

Acceptance by a designated learning institution (DLI) for a course longer than 6 months, proof of funds of CAD $23,448 per year (single applicant outside Quebec, from 1 September 2026) plus first-year tuition and travel, the CAD $150 application fee and CAD $85 biometrics, admissibility, and proof you will respect the temporary nature of the permit.

Do I need a study permit for a 6-month course in Canada?

No — study for more than 6 months needs a study permit. Shorter courses do not. You will still need the right entry document for your nationality (TRV or eTA).

What is a DLI and why does it matter?

A designated learning institution is a school approved by a provincial or territorial government to host international students. Only a DLI acceptance letter can support a study permit application — acceptance from any other school does not qualify.

How much money do I need to show for a Canada student visa?

From 1 September 2026: CAD $23,448 per year for a single applicant outside Quebec, plus first-year tuition and travel. Quebec: CAD $24,617 per year from 1 January 2026. Older guides quoting CAD $22,895 are outdated.

How long does a Canada study permit take to process?

IRCC's 2026 service standard is 60 days for study permits applied for outside Canada (80% of complete applications). Recent actuals (week of 22 September 2026): India ~5 weeks, Pakistan ~7 weeks, Nigeria ~10 weeks. Check IRCC's live processing-times tool for your country.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Canada's student visa is a study permit: DLI acceptance first, then proof of funds (CAD $23,448/year outside Quebec, CAD $24,617 in Quebec — 2026 figures), CAD $150 plus CAD $85 biometrics, and a medical exam for most applicants via a panel physician. Apply online through IRCC as soon as the acceptance letter arrives — busy posts run 5–10 weeks — and verify every requirement on canada.ca, because the rules that matter most (funds, processing times) are the ones that move.
