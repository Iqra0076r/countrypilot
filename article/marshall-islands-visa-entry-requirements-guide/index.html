How to use this Marshall Islands visa and entry guide

The Marshall Islands keeps entry simple: visa on arrival for many nationalities (Timatic lists 80+ eligible countries for a free 90-day VOA), with no eVisa system yet. Non-eligible nationalities apply in advance to the Director of Immigration in Majuro — and the official Division of Immigration site publishes the actual checklists (V1 visitor, B1 business, E1 employment, S1 student, G1 general).

There is no formal standalone student visa program with a published fee — students use the standard permit checklists. No official fee schedule or processing SLA is published, so confirm current fees directly with the Division of Immigration (rmiimmigration.org) before travel.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid well beyond your planned stay — many airlines apply a six-month-validity caution at check-in even where the legal minimum is shorter, and a passport expiring mid-trip is the commonest avoidable denial of boarding.

Keep the biodata page clean and undamaged, and carry a photo/scan separately from the document itself.

Transit and onward travel

The Marshalls are rarely a transit point; any stopover follows the same VOA/advance-permit logic — confirm with the airline.

Where a transit visa or eTA-style authorization exists for Marshall Islands, apply for the transit category rather than a full visitor visa — it is cheaper and decided faster.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Border officers assess the same thing everywhere: is this a genuine short visit with the means to support it and a reason to leave? Carry the passport containing (or linked to) your visa/authorization, a return or onward ticket, proof of funds, accommodation details, and evidence of ties to home — employment, enrollment, family. Check the document checklist on https://rmiimmigration.org/.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application; discrepancies between what you told the consulate and what you tell the officer are the fastest route to secondary inspection.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Health entry rules are driven by where you have recently been, not only by your nationality: yellow-fever certificates are the classic trigger, required when arriving from (or transiting) endemic countries. Check the current health requirements with the airline and the destination's health authority close to departure — they change faster than visa rules.

Carry any required certificates in original form; a photo on your phone is a backup, not a substitute.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is rarely stamped at the border but it is the cheapest risk transfer in this guide: medical care abroad is billed at full price to visitors, and evacuation can cost tens of thousands. Buy comprehensive cover with high medical limits before departure, whatever the entry rules say.

Where a student or work visa makes insurance a condition of the grant, keep the policy certificate with your visa documents — it can be asked for at enrollment or permit issuance.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

A visa is usually permission to travel to the border, not an unconditional right of entry — the final admission decision is made by the border officer, who sets the permitted stay (often as a stamp, sticker or electronic record). Check what was actually granted before leaving the counter: the number of days admitted can be shorter than the visa's validity.

Keep the arrival record (stamp, entry slip or electronic registration) for the whole stay — hotels, banks and exit checks may ask for it.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work: No official fee schedule is published — verify the current fee on the official portal or the employer-sponsored route before paying anything. A visitor entry never authorizes local work.

Study: No official fee schedule is published — verify the current fee on the official portal before paying anything. Universities usually sponsor or guide the application — start with the institution's international office.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every traveler needs their own passport and their own visa or authorization — children are never covered by a parent's grant. Minors traveling with one parent, relatives or alone should carry a consent letter from the absent parent(s) plus birth certificates; officers do ask.

Dual nationals must check which passport to use: some states require their own citizens to enter and leave on the national passport, and using the 'wrong' one can mean denied boarding rather than a waiver at the airport.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

A reliable sequence: 1) Confirm the route for your nationality on https://rmiimmigration.org/. 2) Assemble the file — passport, funds evidence, itinerary, ties to home, invitation or enrollment documents. 3) Apply through the official channel only (portal, mission or authorized VFS/TLS centre — never a lookalike site). 4) Pay the official fee and keep the receipt. 5) Track the application and do not book non-refundable travel until the grant is in hand.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Paying a lookalike website. Unofficial 'e-visa' intermediaries add large service fees for the same lodgement — or are outright scams. Start from the official portal.
  • Quoting stale fees. 2026 repriced many countries' charges; older guides are out of date as of October 2026.
  • Assuming a visa means a fixed stay. The border officer sets the admitted period — check the stamp/record.
  • Booking non-refundable travel before the grant. Apply first, pay for flights after.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Decision-makers look for the same three things: consistency (funds, employment and itinerary tell one coherent story over several months — not a sudden lump deposit), purpose (a clear reason tied to dates), and incentive to return (job, studies, property, family). Previous compliant travel history is the strongest supporting evidence there is.

Address any prior refusal head-on with new evidence rather than re-lodging identical documents — the history is visible to the deciding officer.

Fees, appointments and processing time

No official fee schedule could be verified for Marshall Islands in this research — confirm every figure on Official site of the Republic of the Marshall Islands Division of Immi (https://rmiimmigration.org/) or with the mission before paying. Do not trust fee tables on unofficial sites.

Processing: no official processing-time SLA is published — apply well ahead of travel and track the application through the official channel.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Where Marshall Islands offers online applications, there is exactly one official portal — everything else is an intermediary or a scam. Start only from https://rmiimmigration.org/. No one can expedite a government decision for a fee, and no official charges 'express processing' through a third-party site.

Red flags: prices far above the official fee, pressure to pay by wire or crypto, 'guaranteed approval' language, and application forms that harvest passport scans before showing any fee.

Translations, certifications and document format

Typical file: passport biodata-page scan, bank statements or payslips covering several months, employment or enrollment letter, detailed itinerary, proof of ties to home, and invitation or sponsorship documents where relevant. Upload clear colour scans — poor images are a routine cause of delays and re-submission requests.

Documents not in the destination's official language(s) generally need certified translations; check the mission's document list rather than assuming English is accepted everywhere.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Many countries now collect biometrics (fingerprints and photograph) as part of the application — usually at the mission or an application centre after lodging, with a deadline. Monitor the official account or email for the instruction; missing the biometrics appointment can void the application.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for visitor visas, but officers at the border may question any traveler — the evidence file you carry should match the application.

Families and group travel

Each family member — including infants — needs an individual passport and an individual visa or authorization; group applications are lodged per person even when managed through one account. A refusal for one member does not automatically refuse the rest, but address its reasons before re-lodging.

Where a family or sponsored stream exists, the sponsor's obligations (financial undertaking, sometimes a security bond) attach per visitor — understand them before sponsoring relatives.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Separate from immigration, customs rules bite at every border: declare cash above the local threshold (commonly the equivalent of USD/EUR 10,000 — verify for your route), and check restricted goods before packing. Food, plant and animal products draw the strictest controls in many countries; medications should travel in original packaging with prescriptions for controlled substances.

Duty-free allowances are per adult and modest — the 'a few bottles for friends' plan often exceeds them.

Departure and future travel

Before assuming you can extend a stay, check whether the grant allows it — some permissions explicitly forbid in-country extension or status changes, in which case you must depart and re-apply. Where extension is possible, apply before the current permission expires; overstaying even by days can trigger re-entry bans.

A clean record of entries and timely departures is the foundation of every future application to that country — and it shows up in shared systems.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy for Marshall Islands: 1) https://rmiimmigration.org/ — the immigration authority's own pages override everything. 2) The mission's document checklists for your nationality. 3) Your government's travel advice for the destination. Agent blogs and forums are fine for process colour, but 2026 repriced fees and rewrote several countries' entry rules — any page not updated since 2025 is suspect as of October 2026.

Never rely on a single published processing figure: use the authority's own tool or published service standard for the current picture.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid well beyond the trip; visa/authorization verified against the passport number
  • Grant conditions understood (work prohibitions, stay limits, extension rules)
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation details accessible offline
  • Funds evidence and ties-to-home documents packed
  • Travel insurance with medical + evacuation cover
  • Required declarations prepared (cash, customs, health)
  • Official portal bookmarked; application credentials retained
  • No non-refundable bookings made before the grant
