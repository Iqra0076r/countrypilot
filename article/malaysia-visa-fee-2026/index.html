Malaysia visa fees at a glance 2026

Malaysia publishes its 2026 visitor-visa fees in ringgit, and the headline numbers are small — but the total you pay depends on the route. The official Single Entry Visa (SEV) fee is RM 50, plus about RM 105 in eVisa processing charges when you apply online, for a total of roughly RM 155. A Multiple Entry Visa (MEV) costs RM 100. The limited visa-on-arrival facility is RM 200. The mandatory Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC) is free.

These figures are from the Immigration Department of Malaysia and its official e-Visa portal for 2026. Any site quoting higher Malaysia visa fees is marking up the official RM 50 — verify every charge on imi.gov.my before you pay.

Tourist eVisa fees: Single Entry vs Multiple Entry

The eVisa is the online route for visa-required nationalities — including China, India, Nepal, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Myanmar — applied for at the official portal malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my. Our step-by-step walkthrough is in the Malaysia eVisa 2026 guide.

The Single Entry Visa is RM 50 plus roughly RM 105 for eVisa processing — about RM 155 total; the Multiple Entry Visa is RM 100. Check the fee screen on the portal before paying, and see the full Malaysia visa and entry requirements guide for which nationalities use which door.

Visa on arrival: RM 200, but only in narrow cases

Malaysia's visa on arrival costs RM 200 — but it is not a general facility. It applies only in limited, eligible scenarios, including arrivals from Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia or Brunei. Most visa-required nationals must obtain an eVisa in advance. Do not plan a trip — or a budget — around visa on arrival.

Who pays nothing: visa-free entries

Many nationalities enter Malaysia without any visa fee at all. Chinese and Indian nationals currently enjoy visa-free entry until 31 December 2026 under the government's waiver policy — check whether it has been extended before you travel. Many other nationalities receive visa-free social visits, typically 90 days for the United States and the United Kingdom and typically 30 days for ASEAN nationals such as the Philippines.

One charge-free step applies to everyone: the MDAC is mandatory and free, submitted online within 3 days before travel. Visa-free or not, skipping it means delays at the counter.

Student Pass fees (EMGS)

Studying in Malaysia runs through EMGS (Education Malaysia Global Services), and the fee stack is the biggest of any common route. For a new 6–12 month Student Pass: EMGS processing RM 1,600 plus 8% service tax, an eVAL of RM 150, an i-Kad at RM 50, medical screening of RM 250, and a pass sticker of RM 60 — a first-year total of roughly RM 2,700–3,300 before university charges (EMGS Schedule of Fees v1.6, November 2025, current through 2026).

Add EMGS-mandated medical insurance: minimum 12 months at roughly RM 456–958 per year, collected by the university — see our Malaysia travel insurance and health guide. Malaysia publishes no national proof-of-funds floor for Student Pass applicants; your institution sets its own requirement.

Employment Pass fees (ESD)

Work in Malaysia goes through the Expatriate Services Division (ESD); the employer drives and pays for the application. The ESD charge is RM 2,000 (RM 2,160 including 8% SST, raised from RM 800), plus RM 200 per year of EP duration and RM 125 processing on endorsement.

From 1 June 2026 a revised salary framework applies: Category I minimum RM 20,000/month (up from RM 10,000), Category II RM 10,000–19,999, Category III RM 5,000–9,999 — with maximum cumulative EP durations and succession-plan requirements. Employment Pass holders sponsor dependants through a Dependant Pass at an ESD charge of RM 500.

Paying safely: official fees vs markups

The official Single Entry Visa fee is RM 50. Any site charging several times that is a commercial intermediary, not the government — and some harvest passport data. Apply only at malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my, reached from imi.gov.my, so you never land on a copycat through a search ad.

EMGS fees go through your institution and ESD fees through your employer — never to a personal bank account. The MDAC is always free; anyone charging for it is running a scam. Re-check the 2026 schedule on the official portals before paying.

Red flags: fees quoted in USD/EUR at multiples of RM 50, payment demanded to a personal account or crypto wallet, a domain that is not imi.gov.my or malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my, and anyone promising guaranteed approval or faster processing — no official expedite channel is published.

Processing time: budget for uncertainty, not a guarantee

Malaysia publishes no official processing-time standard for tourist eVisas. Third-party blogs quote 2–7 working days, but that is not an official figure — apply early and track on the official portal rather than planning around an unofficial number.

Costs travellers forget to budget

  • Passport validity: Malaysia generally requires 6+ months' validity — renewing an expiring passport is the "visa cost" many travellers miss.
  • Return or onward ticket: routinely asked for at the counter; officers use it as the core evidence of a temporary stay.
  • Medical screening (RM 250): part of the Student Pass EMGS package.
  • Insurance: mandatory for students (~RM 456–958/year); advisable for tourists — visitors pay full hospital rates.
  • Funds evidence: officers assess whether you can fund the trip — carry bank statements, not just a card.

Final fee checklist before you pay

  • Route confirmed on imi.gov.my (visa-free, eVisa, or limited arrival visa)
  • Official portal only — SEV RM 50 + ~RM 105, or MEV RM 100
  • China/India: waiver status checked (visa-free until 31 December 2026 unless extended)
  • MDAC submitted — free, within 3 days before travel
  • Students: EMGS quote in hand (~RM 2,700–3,300 + ~RM 456–958 insurance); workers: employer's ESD application in progress with the post–1 June 2026 salary category confirmed
  • No payment to any personal account or non-government site

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Malaysian tourist eVisa cost in 2026?

The official Single Entry Visa fee is RM 50, plus about RM 105 in eVisa processing charges — budget roughly RM 155 total. A Multiple Entry Visa is RM 100. Apply only at the official portal malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my; third-party sites charge markups.

What is the Multiple Entry Visa fee for Malaysia?

The official Multiple Entry Visa (MEV) fee is RM 100 (2026), applied for online through the official e-Visa portal at malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my.

How much is visa on arrival in Malaysia?

The limited visa-on-arrival facility costs RM 200, but it applies only in eligible scenarios — such as arrivals from Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia or Brunei. It is not a general facility, so most visa-required nationals should budget for an eVisa in advance instead.

Is the Malaysia Digital Arrival Card really free?

Yes. The MDAC is mandatory and free, submitted online within 3 days before travel. Anyone charging a fee for it is running a scam.

Do students pay a separate visa fee on top of the EMGS package?

The Student Pass cost is bundled through EMGS: roughly RM 2,700–3,300 for the first year (processing RM 1,600 + 8% service tax, eVAL RM 150, i-Kad RM 50, medical screening RM 250, pass sticker RM 60), plus roughly RM 456–958 per year in mandated medical insurance. There is no separate tourist-visa fee on top.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For 2026, budget about RM 155 for a Single Entry eVisa (RM 50 visa plus about RM 105 in processing), RM 100 for Multiple Entry, and RM 200 for the rare arrival visa — the MDAC is free. Students face the biggest bill at roughly RM 2,700–3,300 for the first Student Pass year plus RM 456–958 in mandated insurance, while Employment Pass costs sit with the employer at RM 2,000 plus RM 200 per year of duration. Pay only on official portals, apply early, and re-check imi.gov.my before any payment.
