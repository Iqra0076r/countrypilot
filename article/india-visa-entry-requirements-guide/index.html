How to use this India visa and entry guide

India's entry system centres on the e-Visa at indianvisaonline.gov.in — the only official online channel — covering e-Tourist, e-Business, e-Medical and e-Conference for eligible nationalities of ~166 countries. There is no general visa-on-arrival scheme. Everyone else uses regular/paper visas through Indian missions and VFS centres.

The 2026 headline: a mandatory digital E-Arrival Card from 1 April 2026 for all arriving passengers including OCI holders (via the Bureau of Immigration site or Su-Swagatam app), generating a QR code for entry processing. E-Tourist fees run US$10–80 by duration and season (plus 3–4% transaction charges), with nationality exceptions — and note Iran suspended visa-free entry for Indians on 22 November 2025 (see the Iran guide). This guide was verified in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for at least 6 months beyond arrival with at least two blank pages — Indian immigration is strict on this. The passport details on the e-Visa application must match the travel document exactly.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Eligibility is everything: ~166 nationalities can use the e-Visa; the rest need regular consular visas. US and UK nationals use the e-Visa (with their own fee quirks); always check the portal's eligibility tool for your passport rather than assuming.

Transit and onward travel

India has no visa-free airside transit for visa-required nationalities — a transit visa is needed unless you hold a valid Indian visa or are eligible for the e-Visa/TVOA-style facility for your connection. Confirm the transit requirement for your nationality on indianvisaonline.gov.in before booking split tickets through Indian hubs.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever India's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the Bureau of Immigration e-Visa portal approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

No vaccination certificate is a standard Indian entry requirement for ordinary tourists. Check current advisories — requirements can be added during health events.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance is not an Indian visa condition, but it is strongly recommended. No official student-insurance figure is published.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Complete the mandatory E-Arrival Card (from 1 April 2026, including OCI holders) via the Bureau of Immigration site or Su-Swagatam app and keep the QR code ready. e-Visa holders enter through designated airports/seaports — the e-Visa is not valid at all land borders. Keep the return ticket accessible.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Employment (E) and Project (P) visas run €110 / €183 / €274 (up to 6 months / 1 year / 5 years) on the Brussels mission schedule effective 1 April 2026, plus €3 ICWF — fees vary by mission and nationality. Student (S) visas: €73 (Brussels schedule). Missions require evidence of financial support (e.g. 3 months of bank statements) for students, but no official minimum amount is published. A tourist e-Visa never authorises work.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own India permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether India restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

e-Visa route: 1) Verify eligibility for your nationality on the official indianvisaonline.gov.in (the only legitimate portal — lookalikes charge multiples). 2) Apply minimum 4 days before arrival (standard 72-hour processing; not an official SLA). 3) Pay the duration/season fee (US$10–80 standard) plus 3–4% transaction charge. 4) Complete the E-Arrival Card and keep the QR. 5) Enter via a designated airport/seaport. Regular visa route: mission + VFS with the fee schedule of that post.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using lookalike e-Visa sites. Only indianvisaonline.gov.in is official — agencies charge multiples of the government fee.
  • Skipping the E-Arrival Card. Mandatory from 1 April 2026 for everyone, including OCI holders.
  • Assuming the e-Visa works at land borders. Designated airports/seaports only.
  • Budgeting a single 'India visa fee'. Fees vary by duration, season and nationality (US$10–80 standard; UK 5-year US$484; US US$160).
  • Treating 72 hours as guaranteed. Standard processing, not an official SLA — apply early.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

indianvisaonline.gov.in is the only official online channel — e-Visa applications and the E-Arrival Card both live there (beware the .org/.com lookalikes). Regular visas go through missions + VFS centres. OCI postal applications ended 1 April 2026 — in-person at VFS now.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in India's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the Bureau of Immigration e-Visa portal for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where India requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Declare currency over the prescribed limits and restricted goods. Keep the e-Visa printout, E-Arrival QR and return ticket with your passport — the e-Visa is checked at designated ports only.

Departure and future travel

e-Visa stays follow the granted duration (30 days / 1 year / 5 years, with per-visit stay limits) — extensions are not routine. Convert to the proper long-stay category before the e-Visa expires if plans change; overstaying draws fines and future-visa consequences.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) indianvisaonline.gov.in — eligibility tool, fee tables, E-Arrival Card. 2) The specific mission's fee PDF for regular visas (they differ). Treat 72-hour processing and any single 'India visa cost' as what they are — unofficial.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per India's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

India is a portal-discipline destination: indianvisaonline.gov.in only, eligibility checked first, E-Arrival Card filed (mandatory since April 2026), designated ports only. Budget the duration-based e-Visa fee (US$10–80) plus transaction charges, and never trust a lookalike domain with your passport data.
