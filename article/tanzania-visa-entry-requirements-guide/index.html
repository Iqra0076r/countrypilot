How to use this Tanzania visa and entry guide

Tanzania's entry system runs through the official e-Visa portal at eservices.immigration.go.tz (Immigration Services Department), with visa on arrival still offered at designated entry points for eligible nationalities — but the e-Visa is the recommended route, and copycat sites mimicking the portal are a documented problem. Apply only on the official domain.

The 2026 headline: in September 2026 Nigeria was removed from Tanzania's referral-visa category — Nigerian nationals no longer need special immigration clearance and apply through the standard e-Visa process like everyone else. Fee-wise, the ordinary single-entry visa is USD 50 (US passport holders must take the USD 100 multiple-entry instead). This guide was verified against immigration.go.tz in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport must be valid for at least six months beyond your arrival date in Tanzania, with blank pages for entry endorsements. This is enforced at e-Visa application and at the border.

The e-Visa is linked to the passport number — a renewed passport voids it. If Zanzibar is on the itinerary, note its separate mandatory inbound travel-insurance requirement (terms and pricing to be confirmed on the live source — no current authoritative figure could be verified).

Identify the correct visa or entry route

e-Visa (recommended): ordinary single-entry USD 50, valid up to 90 days — apply online before travel. Visa on arrival: still offered at designated entry points for eligible nationalities, but queues and discretion make the e-Visa the safer choice. US passport holders: the bilateral arrangement means you take the USD 100 multiple-entry visa, not the USD 50 single-entry.

Transit and onward travel

Tanzania offers a transit visa category for short stopovers, but the practical guidance is: if you will clear immigration — even briefly — hold the appropriate visa in advance. Airside transit on a single through-ticket without entering is generally fine.

Dar es Salaam connections on separate tickets are the classic trap: leaving the transit area to recheck baggage means entering Tanzania, which means the visa must already exist. The USD 50 ordinary visa covers most such cases — arrange it before travel.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

At the Tanzania border, expect questions before you get a stamp. The standard set covers purpose of visit, length of stay, accommodation, and means of support — have the onward ticket, hotel booking or host address, and bank evidence ready rather than buried in luggage.

A visa lets you travel to the border; the officer's decision is what lets you in. Short, truthful, consistent answers beat long explanations. If your plans involve anything beyond tourism — work, study, volunteering — say so only if your visa category actually covers it.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Health documents are a separate gate from the visa for Tanzania. The key question is certificate-based: does Tanzania require proof of yellow-fever vaccination (common if you arrive from an endemic country), and are there any current outbreak-related declarations?

Carry the WHO yellow card (International Certificate of Vaccination) if there is any chance it applies — digital photos are not accepted at most borders. Also confirm your insurance covers the actual medical environment: in countries with limited hospital capacity, evacuation cover matters more than the policy's headline medical limit.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance sits in a grey zone for Tanzania: sometimes a formal visa requirement, always a practical one. Where the embassy or portal names a minimum medical coverage, treat that number as the floor and buy a policy that meaningfully exceeds it.

Read the exclusions before you pay: adventure activities, pre-existing conditions and alcohol-related incidents are the three most common claim rejections worldwide. For long stays, check whether the policy covers the full visa duration — a 90-day policy on a 180-day trip leaves a dangerous gap.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

On arrival in Tanzania, the officer's admission decision is the operative document. Note the exact date you must leave and keep evidence of it (a photo of the stamp or the electronic record) — 'I thought the visa was valid longer' is not a defence against an overstay finding.

Extensions, where they exist, must be sought before the current permission expires and are never guaranteed. Plan the itinerary inside the granted stay rather than assuming an extension will be available.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Keep the three purposes strictly apart for Tanzania: visiting, working, studying. Each has its own permission, its own evidence, and its own application channel — a tourist visa or visa-free entry covers none of the other two.

The expensive mistake is assuming intent does not matter: taking a job, enrolling, or freelancing while admitted as a visitor can lead to detention, removal, and multi-year bans. If the plan might involve work or study, secure that status before travelling, not after arrival.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

For families heading to Tanzania: every traveller, including infants, needs individual documentation. Where an authorization or e-visa system applies, each person — children included — needs their own approval; there are no family bundles.

A consent letter from any non-travelling parent is cheap insurance against difficult questions at the border. Dual citizens must verify Tanzania's rule on which nationality to travel under — entering on a foreign passport while holding Tanzania citizenship is an offence in some countries.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Work the Tanzania application backwards from the travel date. First, identify the exact visa category and its document list on Tanzania e-Visa portal (Immigration Services Department). Second, gather funds evidence and bookings — most refusals trace to weak or inconsistent documentation, not to eligibility. Third, submit via the official portal or mission and keep the reference number.

Fourth, wait for the decision before committing money to flights and hotels. Published processing times are targets, not promises; consulates do not compensate for missed trips caused by slow decisions.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Trusting forum anecdotes over Tanzania e-Visa portal (Immigration Services Department) — procedures change; the official portal is the only source that matters.
  • Paying fees to personal accounts — official fees go through official channels; anything else is a scam.
  • Letting the passport drop below the validity threshold — airlines deny boarding, and there is no appeal at the gate.
  • Working or studying on a visitor entry — the fastest route to removal and a multi-year ban.
  • Overstaying 'just a few days' — it voids multi-entry visas and must be declared on future applications worldwide.
  • Forgetting onward travel proof — one of the most common refusal reasons at the border itself.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Think of the Tanzania application as an argument with exhibits. The argument: 'I am a genuine visitor who will leave on time and can afford the trip.' Every exhibit should support exactly that — and nothing should contradict it.

Common contradictions that sink files: an itinerary showing a luxury trip against bank statements showing a thin balance; employment dates that do not match the leave letter; a stated purpose ('conference') with no invitation or registration. Re-read the whole file as a skeptic before submitting.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Online applications for Tanzania live or die on the portal you choose. Tanzania e-Visa portal (Immigration Services Department) is the official channel; everything else is a private business. The tell-tale signs of a copycat: fees several times the official schedule, 'guaranteed approval' language, and payment to personal accounts.

Screenshot every confirmation page and save every email receipt. If the portal issues an approval letter or authorization to board, print it — phone screens fail at exactly the wrong moment, and some carriers require paper.

Translations, certifications and document format

For Tanzania, assemble two piles: what the checklist demands, and what proves your story. The second pile — employment letter with approved leave, property or tenancy documents, enrollment confirmation, prior travel history — is where approvals are won.

Photographs deserve more care than they get: follow the portal's dimensions, background and recency rules exactly, because a rejected photo restarts the clock. Where documents need legalization or apostille, start early — that process has its own timeline independent of the visa.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

No pre-departure biometric enrollment procedure could be verified for Tanzania's e-Visa — it is an online authorization. Expect standard identity checks, and possible biometric capture, at the immigration counter on arrival.

Keep the e-Visa approval accessible (printed and on your phone): it is verified before boarding and at the border, and copycat 'approvals' from unofficial sites will not survive scrutiny.

Families and group travel

Families should budget per person for Tanzania: per-person fees, per-person forms, per-person approvals. Children's applications need the same care as adults' — birth certificates where surnames differ, and consent letters where only one parent travels.

Group applications rise or fall together in practice: if the group's stated purpose is a wedding, every file should reflect the wedding. One member with a contradictory story invites scrutiny of all.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

After the immigration stamp in Tanzania comes customs. The two questions that matter: are you carrying more than the declarable cash threshold, and are you carrying anything restricted — food, medicine, electronics, cultural goods?

Cash thresholds are about reporting, not limits: declare honestly and keep walking. Restricted goods are different — undeclared prohibited items can mean fines or confiscation. Read the customs authority's list for Tanzania before you pack, not after you land.

Departure and future travel

Departure from Tanzania deserves the same attention as arrival. Confirm the exit requirements — some countries check for departure cards, exit stamps or proof of onward travel even when leaving.

A documented, on-time departure builds the travel history that makes the next visa easier. Conversely, even a short overstay creates a record that follows you across borders and across years. When in doubt, leave early.

How to research a route without getting misled

For Tanzania, rank your sources: Tanzania e-Visa portal (Immigration Services Department) first, the responsible embassy second, official advisories third. Everything else — forums, influencers, visa-agent marketing — is background noise until confirmed against the top two.

Be especially careful with dated content: fee schedules, processing times and even visa categories change year to year. A 2023 guide quoting fees is not a 2026 answer. Check the publication or 'last updated' date on anything you read, then verify against Tanzania e-Visa portal (Immigration Services Department) anyway.

A final document audit before travel

  • Correct visa category for the actual purpose — visiting, working and studying are different permissions
  • Passport validity and blank pages verified against Tanzania's rule, not a generic 'six months' assumption
  • Application reference, payment receipts and approval kept together
  • Itinerary, funds and purpose consistent across every document
  • Biometrics/interview appointment completed where required, with confirmation
  • Insurance policy documents carried, emergency assistance number saved offline
  • Customs-relevant items checked against Tanzania's prohibited list
  • Someone at home holds copies of your passport, visa and itinerary

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for Tanzania?

Most visitors need one — the e-Visa (USD 50 ordinary single-entry) via eservices.immigration.go.tz is the recommended route. Visa on arrival is still offered at designated entry points for eligible nationalities, but the e-Visa avoids queues and discretion. US passport holders take the USD 100 multiple-entry instead.

How long does the Tanzania e-Visa take?

Within 10 business days for standard applications per immigration guidelines; referral-category nationalities take longer. Apply at least three weeks before travel — and only on the official domain, since copycat sites are a documented problem.

What changed for Nigerians traveling to Tanzania?

In September 2026, Nigeria was removed from the referral-visa category — Nigerian nationals no longer need special immigration clearance and now apply through the standard e-Visa process like other nationalities.

Do I need insurance for Zanzibar?

Zanzibar has a mandatory inbound travel-insurance requirement, but no current authoritative price or terms could be verified — confirm the live requirement before travel rather than relying on third-party figures.

Can I work in Tanzania on a business visa?

The USD 250 business visa covers short-term professional assignments up to 90 days only. Longer employment requires a Class B work permit from the Ministry of Labour — a separate process with its own (unverified here) fee.
