US B-1/B-2 Visa Documents Checklist 2026: What to Prepare

The B-1/B-2 interview is won or lost on the file you carry in. Here is the 2026 documents checklist — what is required, what strengthens the case, and what sinks it.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20266 min read

The US visitor-visa interview lasts minutes, and the officer decides on the file in front of them plus your answers. Applicants who walk in with a complete, consistent file get a smooth interview; applicants who improvise get a refusal. This is the 2026 documents checklist for the B-1/B-2 — required items, strengthening evidence, and the mistakes to avoid.

Quick Answer

The required core: a valid passport, the DS-160 confirmation page, and the $185 MRV fee receipt (2026). The interview then tests purpose and ties — so carry evidence of both: employment, funds, travel history, and purpose documents. One consistent story must run through every page; contradictions are a standalone reason to refuse.

Fee $185 · Form DS-160 · Interview required · Updated 2026-10-14 · Reading time 6 minutes

Required: the three non-negotiables

1) Passport — valid for the trip, with blank pages for the visa. 2) DS-160 confirmation — the online nonimmigrant visa application, completed and submitted, with the confirmation page printed. Every answer on it must match your documents: the form is the spine of the file, and contradictions between the form and the evidence surface at the interview. 3) The $185 MRV fee receipt — the machine-readable-visa fee (as of 2026), paid before the interview is scheduled. Without the receipt, there is no appointment.

Strengthening evidence: purpose

The officer's first question is why you are going. Carry the documents that answer it: a day-by-day itinerary, hotel bookings, a business invitation or conference registration for B-1 travel, family details and an invitation letter for family visits. Refundable bookings are fine — the point is a genuine, specific, verifiable purpose, not a vague "tourism" with nothing behind it.

Strengthening evidence: ties and funds

The second question is whether you will return. The convincing file documents a life at home: an employer's letter with role, salary, and approved leave dates; recent payslips; bank statements showing regular income over months (not a sudden balance); property or lease documents; family ties; and a travel history of leaving other countries on time. Self-employed applicants should bring business registration and tax records. The funds test is consistency, not wealth — statements must match the employment story.

The mistakes that sink files

Four classic failures. 1) Contradictions — dates differing between the DS-160 and the bookings, an employer the officer cannot square with the statements. 2) A file assembled the week before — sudden deposits, fresh employment letters with no history. 3) Non-refundable travel booked before the interview — it proves nothing and risks everything when the queue is long. 4) Letting an agent prepare documents you have not read — you carry the refusal for anything submitted in your name.

After the interview

If approved, check whether your nationality owes a visa issuance (reciprocity) fee before arranging passport return — it is separate from the $185 and not owed by everyone. The visa is often issued for up to 10 years with multiple entries, but each admission is typically up to 6 months on the I-94, set at the port of entry. Keep the whole file: the next application's DS-160 should tell the same story.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents are required for a US B-1/B-2 visa?

Passport, DS-160 confirmation, and the $185 fee receipt — plus evidence of purpose, funds, and ties for the interview.

How much is the US visa application fee in 2026?

$185 MRV fee (as of 2026), paid before the interview is scheduled; not refunded on refusal.

Do I need to book flights before the interview?

No — and non-refundable bookings are a mistake. Refundable itineraries are fine; irreversible ones are a gamble.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Passport, DS-160, $185 receipt — then a file that proves purpose and ties with one consistent story: the B-1/B-2 checklist is short, and every refusal is a file that skipped a line on it.
