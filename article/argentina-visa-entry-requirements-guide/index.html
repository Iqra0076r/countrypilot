How to use this Argentina visa and entry guide

Visa rules are not a single yes-or-no fact attached to a destination — they depend on your passport, purpose, length of stay, transit airports and sometimes recent travel history. Start with those facts before reading a visa list. The entry-status table below is the fast answer for the most common passports; the detail follows it. Always confirm the decisive rule on the official channel — Argentine consular services / Migraciones — before paying or booking.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for the duration of your stay with blank pages — six months beyond travel is the safer standard for consular applications.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Argentina is straightforward for most travellers: most nationalities — including the US and UK — enter visa-free for 90 days, and the old reciprocity fees for Americans, Canadians and Australians are abolished. Where a visa is required, the consular processing fee is USD 150, non-refundable (2026, Argentine Consulate Singapore).

Select nationalities holding US visas can use the AVE electronic travel authorization via Migraciones — decided within up to 20 business days (2026, Argentine Consulate Toronto). Check cancilleria.gob.ar for whether your passport qualifies for visa-free, AVE, or consular-visa entry.

Argentina has no general eVisa; the electronic route is the AVE (Autorización de Viaje Electrónica) for select nationalities, applied for via Migraciones and decided within up to 20 business days (2026, official consulate guidance).

Argentina offers no visa on arrival. Entry is visa-free, AVE, or consular visa — decided before travel.

Transit and onward travel

Through-ticketed airside transit at Ezeiza/Aeroparque normally needs no visa if you stay airside. Leaving the airport requires the appropriate entry permission — check before booking split tickets.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Proof of sufficient funds is reported as required with no fixed amount (2026). Carry bank statements plus accommodation evidence.

Common documents beyond the visa itself: onward or return ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can support yourself. Print everything — phone batteries die at the worst moments.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

No vaccination certificate required for entry from most countries. Yellow fever vaccination is recommended for the northern border regions — not an entry requirement.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is not documented as an entry condition, but recommended — Argentina's private healthcare costs for visitors are significant.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Your permitted stay is set by the visa, stamp or entry record — visa validity and authorised duration are not necessarily the same thing. Read the entry stamp and calculate days using the method specified for your route. Overstays in Argentina can mean fines, detention or a future entry ban — know your date.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work and study need their own visa categories with Argentine sponsorship — the tourist entry does not authorize employment. Category fees are not published officially; confirm with the consulate before budgeting.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Minors generally need their own passport and sometimes a parental consent letter — check the requirements for your departure country too. Dual nationals should check the rules for the passport they plan to enter on, since visa treatment follows the document presented at the border. Refugees, stateless persons and emergency travel-document holders should confirm with Argentina’s embassy directly, as standard routes may not apply.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

2025–26 policy changes

  • Reciprocity fees abolished: the former charges on US, Canadian and Australian nationals are gone — entry is visa-free (official, via turismo.buenosaires.gob.ar).
  1. Check your entry category on cancilleria.gob.ar: visa-free (US/UK/most), AVE (select nationalities), or consular visa.
  2. If a visa is needed, apply at the Argentine consulate — USD 150 non-refundable processing fee (2026).
  3. AVE applicants: apply via Migraciones and allow up to 20 business days (2026).
  4. Prepare financial evidence and travel bookings for the application or border.
  5. Verify the 90-day stay allowance on arrival — overstays complicate future entries.

Consular applications typically need the form, photographs, passport, financial evidence, travel bookings and the USD 150 fee receipt. AVE needs the passport and supporting documents uploaded via Migraciones — follow the consulate's checklist.

Common mistakes to avoid

    Paying the old reciprocity fee. It is abolished — anyone still charging it is running a scam. Assuming AVE is instant. It can take up to 20 business days — apply early. Overstaying 90 days. Fines and future-entry problems follow; extend properly instead. Working on a tourist entry. Employment needs its own visa category. Skipping the consulate check. AVE eligibility is nationality-specific — verify yours.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Argentina has no general eVisa; the electronic route is the AVE (Autorización de Viaje Electrónica) for select nationalities, applied for via Migraciones and decided within up to 20 business days (2026, official consulate guidance).

Only use the official channel — Argentine consular services / Migraciones. Copycat sites charge markups or harvest passport data. An eVisa approval is an email or a portal record: carry the confirmation and check it shows your name, passport number and permitted entries.

Translations, certifications and document format

Documents not in the local or accepted language may need certified translations — the official checklist states the rule. Certifications, bank statements and invitation letters should be recent (typically within 3 months). Photograph standards matter more than people expect: follow the portal’s specification exactly.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Biometric capture is not documented for the tourist process. Expect standard checks at the consulate and border.

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs their own application or authorisation — visas are not transferable within a family or group. Apply together where the portal allows, and carry the same document set for each person. With minors, bring birth certificates or consent documentation in addition to passports.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

A visa is not a customs clearance. Declare cash above the local reporting threshold, check restricted items before packing (medications, drones, satellite equipment and certain food products are common flashpoints), and know that border officials can inspect devices. Keep receipts for high-value items you intend to bring back out.

Departure and future travel

Before leaving, confirm your exit is recorded — keep boarding passes and entry/exit stamps. If you overstayed or had any immigration incident, note it: future applications to Argentina and other countries ask about it. Some nationalities need to deregister or obtain exit clearance after long stays; check before a long-term visit.

How to research a route without getting misled

Rank sources by authority: Argentina’s immigration authority and foreign ministry first, then Timatic/IATA, then consular posts, then recent reputable reporting. Treat aggregator visa sites as leads, not answers — they go stale. If two credible sources conflict, say so in your notes and confirm with the authority; this guide flags conflicts the same way.

A final document audit before travel

Forty-eight hours before departure, check the passport number on the visa or authorisation, validity dates, name spelling, permitted entries and any conditions. Confirm transit airports and terminals have not changed. Put the approval, insurance and accommodation in an offline folder and print critical documents when the destination or airline may expect paper evidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for Argentina?

Most nationalities — including US and UK citizens — enter visa-free for 90 days. India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh need a consular visa (USD 150 processing, 2026); select nationalities may use the AVE.

What is the AVE for Argentina?

The Autorización de Viaje Electrónica — an electronic authorization for select nationalities (often those holding US visas), decided within up to 20 business days via Migraciones (2026).

Do Americans still pay a reciprocity fee?

No — reciprocity fees for US (and Canadian/Australian) nationals are abolished. Entry is visa-free for 90 days.

How much is the Argentina visa fee?

USD 150, non-refundable, where a visa is required (2026, Argentine Consulate Singapore).

Can I work in Argentina on a tourist entry?

No — work needs its own sponsored visa category. The tourist entry never authorizes employment.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Argentina, most travellers simply arrive: visa-free 90 days for the US, UK and most nationalities, with reciprocity fees abolished. Where a visa is needed, budget USD 150 (2026) and a month's lead time — or check AVE eligibility for the electronic route (up to 20 business days).
