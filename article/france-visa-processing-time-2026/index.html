France Visa Processing Time 2026: Schengen Speed, Long-Stay Realities, and the Booking Window

France decides Schengen visas in about 15 working days in 2026, but long-stay work and student visas run on slower, tighter clocks. Here are the official timelines — and the booking windows you cannot afford to miss.

France runs two visa clocks. Schengen short-stay visas — the tourist and business route most travellers use — are decided in around 15 working days in 2026, with complex cases stretching to 45. Long-stay visas for work and study run slower and inside a much tighter application window: long-stay files cannot be lodged more than 3 months before arrival.

Everything starts at France-Visas (france-visas.gouv.fr), the mandatory online portal, and first-time applicants still attend in person for biometrics — the visa is a sticker, not an eVisa. This guide sets out the 2026 timelines by route, the peak-season stretch, and how to place your application inside the windows.

The Schengen clock: 15 working days

The Schengen short-stay visa (tourism, business, family visits up to 90 days) is France's fastest route: around 15 working days from the biometrics appointment in 2026, extending to 45 days for files needing deeper examination. The EU Visa Code sets the legal frame — apply no later than 15 days before travel, and as early as 6 months ahead.

Appointments are booked online through France-Visas — this is now mandatory — and peak-season slots go early. In practice, the appointment wait in June–August can rival the processing time itself, so the '15 working days' only starts once you are in front of the officer. Book the slot first, then perfect the file.

Long-stay: slower decisions, tighter windows

Long-stay visas (work, study, family) are a different process with a French consulate deciding on a fuller file. In 2026, work visas average ~30 days, and student visas run 15 days to 3 weeks — stretching noticeably in the June–August rush when every September-start student applies at once.

The critical constraint: long-stay applications cannot be filed more than 3 months before arrival. That makes the window narrow — gather the employment contract or admission letter, the funds evidence, the insurance certificate and translations before the window opens, so the file is lodged on day one of eligibility rather than week six.

What slows a France file down

  • Peak-season appointment scarcity. France-Visas slots in June–August are the real bottleneck for both Schengen and student routes.
  • Unclear funds evidence. Officers process files in volume — bank statements and sponsorship evidence presented as a messy bundle invite queries; a clearly labelled file moves faster.
  • Missing certified translations. Documents not in French or English need certified translation; untranslated documents stall the file.
  • Études en France sequencing (students). The academic validation and the visa track run in parallel — delays on the academic side compress the visa window.
  • Copycat portals. france-visas.gouv.fr is the only official portal; lookalike domains selling 'guaranteed' visas or priority slots are scams — the consulate sells neither.

When to apply: the planning formula

Schengen: apply 6–8 weeks before travel in normal periods, 8–12 weeks ahead for June–August trips — the appointment slot, not the 15-day decision, is the constraint. Never inside the 15-day legal minimum unless you enjoy risk.

Long-stay work: have the complete file ready the day the 3-month window opens; with ~30-day average processing, that leaves a thin margin for queries.

Long-stay student: prepare everything by May for September starts — the June–August rush stretches the 15-day–3-week norm, and late files miss enrolment.

Tracking and the live picture

Track the file through your France-Visas account after the biometrics appointment. For the current appointment availability and any seasonal advisories, the France-Visas portal and the competent consulate's page are the live sources — third-party blogs quoting 'current' France timelines are usually quoting last season.

Official fees are paid at the visa centre with a receipt (Schengen adult €90, ages 6–11 €45, long-stay €99 in 2026) — never to a personal account.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a France Schengen visa take in 2026?

Around 15 working days from the biometrics appointment, up to 45 days for complex cases. The appointment slot wait in peak season can add weeks on top — book early.

How long does a French student visa take?

15 days to 3 weeks in normal periods, stretching in the June–August rush. Long-stay applications cannot be filed more than 3 months before arrival, so prepare the file before the window opens.

How long does a French work visa take?

Long-stay work visas average around 30 days in 2026, decided on the full file by the consulate.

Can I apply for a France visa 6 months early?

For Schengen short-stay visas, yes — up to 6 months before travel. For long-stay visas, no — the maximum is 3 months before arrival.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Fifteen working days for Schengen, ~30 days for long-stay work, 15 days to 3 weeks for students — with the appointment slot and the 3-month long-stay window as the real constraints. Book France-Visas slots early, prepare the file before the window opens, and use only the official portal.
