UK Bank Account Guide for Newcomers 2026

Confirm identity and address documents

MoneyHelper explains that banks commonly ask for a passport or driving licence, while online applications may include a photograph or video identity check. Depending on the bank, alternatives can include official correspondence, a tenancy agreement or a letter from an employer or place of study. Acceptance is not universal: obtain the chosen bank’s current list.

A newly arrived student should ask the university whether it can issue a banking letter, then check the bank’s requirements for its wording and address. Do not buy a document from someone offering a shortcut.

If you do not yet have a utility bill

Make a document inventory before starting several applications. Record what you have, whose name appears on it, the address shown and its issue date. Contact the bank with those details. Ask whether an original, download or certified copy is needed and whether branch staff can review a case that the app cannot complete.

Choose an account for the payments you need

Write down your first-month tasks: receiving wages or family support, paying rent, setting up regular bills and withdrawing cash. Check that the account supports each task. A product advertised to students may have eligibility conditions that differ from another current account offered by the same provider.

For money arriving from overseas, ask for the receiving instructions, currency conversion terms and any incoming-transfer charges. Do not assume that a domestic account number alone is sufficient for an international payment.

If considering an app-based product, identify the legal provider and ask what protection applies to customer funds. Read its explanation of the account’s status rather than inferring it from the app’s appearance or brand name.

Use a simple annual-cost comparison

Illustration, not current product pricing: an account costing GBP 4 a month has GBP 48 in annual account charges. A second account with no monthly charge but GBP 6 for each of ten transactions you expect to make would cost GBP 60 for those transactions. Compare the complete expected activity before choosing.

Make separate rows for monthly fees, foreign payments, cash withdrawals and optional extras. Include only services you intend to use. Do not treat an overdraft limit or sign-up reward as income for your rent budget.

Your first week after approval

  1. Check your name and contact details in the account record.
  2. Activate secure access and transaction notifications.
  3. Confirm card delivery and payment limits.
  4. Use the bank’s official instructions for your first incoming payment.
  5. Keep another payment method available until access is working.

If the application is declined or paused, ask whether the issue is documentation or product eligibility. Request the next step in writing. Never share a one-time security code with a caller offering to finish the application for you.

Compare the account after the promotion ends

Newcomer and student offers can be useful, but the lasting value of an account depends on its normal terms. Compare the standard monthly charge, cash-withdrawal costs, international card use, transfer fees, overdraft pricing and any conditions attached to fee-free periods or rewards. Record the date on which an introductory offer ends so it does not become an unnoticed recurring cost.

Prepare an identity and address evidence pack

Do not rely on a single document until the bank confirms it is acceptable for your exact application route. Keep clear copies of your identity document, immigration or status evidence where relevant, current address evidence, university or employer correspondence if applicable, and any additional document the bank specifically requests. Names and addresses should be consistent across the documents wherever possible.

If you have only recently arrived and do not yet hold conventional household bills, ask the bank which alternatives it currently accepts. The correct approach is to use an accepted alternative, not to alter a document or purchase unofficial proof of address.

Secure the account from day one

Install the bank's app from its official store listing, create a unique password, enable the available security controls and turn on transaction notifications. Do not disclose one-time passcodes, card PINs or full security credentials to a caller, even if the caller claims an application is waiting for verification.

Keep the bank's official complaint route and fraud-reporting contact details somewhere separate from the banking app. If you are budgeting for study, compare this account decision with the UK Tuition and Budgeting Guide. If you are relocating for employment, the UK Skilled Worker Visa Guide provides the related immigration-planning context.

Frequently Asked Questions

Must every applicant provide a UK utility bill?

Document lists differ. MoneyHelper describes alternative evidence; ask the chosen bank which alternatives it accepts for your circumstances.

Does being an international student guarantee a student account?

No guarantee is made here. Check the specific product’s eligibility and document requirements before relying on an advertised student offer.
