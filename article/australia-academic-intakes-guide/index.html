Current official context — checked 5 October 2026

Home Affairs currently lists the Student visa (subclass 500) at a cost from AUD 2,500, with stay generally aligned to enrolment for up to six years. Students can generally work up to 48 hours a fortnight while the course is in session, with different treatment for masters-by-research/doctoral students. New Student and Student Guardian application rules took effect on 2 October 2026, including changes affecting who can apply in Australia and family inclusion.

Official page: https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500

Understand the education system before choosing a university

Start with the qualification structure in Australia: degree length, academic calendar, credit system, taught/research distinction and whether the programme leads to professional registration. CRICOS-listed providers and institutional admissions are useful starting points. Rankings can help with broad reputation, but programme accreditation, curriculum, graduate outcomes and location often matter more.

For regulated fields, check the professional body before applying. A university may be recognised while a specific qualification still does not grant the professional outcome the student expects. use ScholarshipPortal to confirm the live requirement where it can change.

Admission requirements and realistic shortlisting

Build a three-tier shortlist: ambitious, realistic and safer options. For each programme, record academic prerequisites, language score, prerequisite courses, portfolio/research requirements, application fee and deadline. Compare the exact programme page, not only the university's general admissions page. compare it with the current ScholarshipPortal guidance linked below.

International applicants should also check whether credential evaluation, certified translations or country-specific documents are required. Give yourself time to obtain these before the deadline. the live Australian Department of Home Affairs reference below should be consulted before action.

Intakes and application timeline

Map the intake backwards. A practical sequence is: 10–14 months out, research programmes and funding; 8–12 months out, tests and documents; 6–10 months out, submit university and scholarship applications; after admission, arrange finances, housing and immigration. The exact dates vary by institution. verify it against Australian Department of Home Affairs before committing money or documents.

Do not assume every programme has both autumn and spring entry. Competitive programmes and scholarships may have one annual intake. Confirm the current position using Australian Department of Home Affairs in the source list below.

Tuition, living costs and funding

For Australia, build a first-year budget before accepting an offer. Include tuition, compulsory fees, visa/residence charges, insurance, housing deposit, furniture or setup costs, transport and emergency funds. Then map confirmed funding sources against that budget.

Scholarships should be verified on the official provider page. If loans are used, model currency movement and repayment rather than looking only at the monthly payment. verify it against ScholarshipPortal before committing money or documents.

Admission is not immigration permission

After receiving an offer, review Student visa (subclass 500) requirements on https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500. Immigration authorities may ask for financial evidence, health insurance, language evidence or proof that the course and institution are eligible.

Keep the university and visa timelines aligned. A late admission decision can compress the visa window; a visa delay can affect enrolment. Know the institution's latest-arrival and deferral policy. use Australian Department of Home Affairs to confirm the live requirement where it can change.

After admission: the practical handover

Confirm acceptance, deposits, accommodation, visa documents, travel dates, orientation and registration. Keep digital and paper copies of key documents in separate places. re-check Australian Department of Home Affairs before relying on it.

Before departure, understand work rights, health-care access and any address-registration requirement. These post-arrival rules can be as important as the visa itself. re-check Australian Department of Home Affairs before relying on it.

Create an intake map, not a single deadline

An academic intake is a chain of dates: application opening, priority scholarship deadline, final course deadline, offer response, document submission, deposit or acceptance deadline, enrolment, orientation, course commencement and any latest-arrival date. Institutions may use semesters, trimesters, terms or course-specific teaching periods, so a label such as “mid-year” is not precise enough for planning. Record the exact dates shown by the course provider for the specific year and campus.

Build one row per university or course and mark which dates are fixed and which are rolling. A rolling admission process can still close early if places fill. A course advertised for more than one intake may not offer every specialisation, placement sequence or research start date in every period.

Work backwards from the course start

After identifying the intended intake, work backwards through admission evidence, English testing, academic results, scholarship decisions, acceptance, financial preparation, visa documentation, accommodation and travel. Add contingency time for documents that depend on third parties, such as transcripts, references, translations, financial evidence or professional checks. The purpose is not to predict processing time; it is to identify which tasks can block the next stage if they are left too late.

Keep the visa timeline separate from the university timeline. An offer or confirmation from an institution does not guarantee that immigration processing will finish before orientation. Use current Home Affairs information and the institution's own deferral or latest-arrival policy when deciding how much timing risk you can accept.

When a later intake may be the better decision

A later intake can be preferable if rushing would force you to submit incomplete evidence, miss scholarship consideration, accept unaffordable housing, rely on uncertain funds or arrive too late for compulsory orientation or placement requirements. Compare the consequences of deferring with the cost of forcing the earlier intake. Ask whether your offer, scholarship, deposit or course structure carries over and get any important answer in writing.

Your final intake worksheet should show the course, campus, application deadline, scholarship deadline, acceptance or deposit date, expected document-completion date, visa-preparation milestone, orientation, commencement and deferral fallback. Re-check the worksheet whenever the provider updates a date.

  • Open the current official Australian Department of Home Affairs page and confirm the rule still applies on 2026-10-05 or later.
  • Verify programme recognition, admission requirements, refund rules and the latest visa timeline before paying a large deposit.
  • Write down any fee, threshold, deadline or processing estimate with the date it was checked.
  • Keep the official application link separate from private explanatory articles.
  • Save copies of forms, evidence and confirmations before submitting anything.
  • Do not pay an unverified recruiter, adviser, landlord, scholarship agent or lender because of urgency.
