How to use this Vanuatu visa and entry guide

Vanuatu's entry system is visa-free-first: citizens of ~130 countries enter visa-free for up to 90 days, including the EU, UK, US, Australia, New Zealand, Canada and Japan. The Department of Immigration and Passport Services runs visas.immigration.gov.vu for the Online Visa Portal and applications for those who need them, alongside published fee schedules for visitor, employment, student, residence, business and investment categories.

The 2026 fee picture is unusually well-documented for a Pacific state: visitor visa VT 8,000 (1 month) / VT 12,000 (2 months) / VT 15,000 (3 months), student visa VT 40,000 (first year), employment visa VT 150,000 (first year), and residence/business/investor categories up to VT 1,000,000. Vanuatu's passport must be valid 6 months beyond travel. This guide was verified against immigration.gov.vu in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months beyond travel to Vanuatu, with blank pages. This is a hard published requirement — Vanuatu is explicit where many states are vague.

For visa-free entries, the passport is the visa: keep it clean, valid and well beyond the 6-month line before the long Pacific routing.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Visa-free (~130 countries): up to 90 days — check the current list on immigration.gov.vu. Visitor visa (non-exempt): VT 8,000/12,000/15,000 for 1/2/3 months. Study: student visa VT 40,000 first year. Work: employment visa VT 150,000 first year. Long-term: residence VT 250,000 (first year), business VT 250,000, investor VT 1,000,000.

Transit and onward travel

Vanuatu's limited connections mean almost nobody transits — but the visa-free regime for ~130 countries covers most stopover scenarios. Non-exempt travellers needing to clear immigration should hold the visitor visa in advance via the online portal.

The practical concern is misconnection on infrequent services: build generous buffers. Immigration is the easy part of a Vanuatu transit; the schedule is the hard part.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Admission to Vanuatu is granted by the officer at the port of entry, whatever your visa says. Practical preparation: passport with the visa, printed onward travel, first accommodation address, and proof you can support yourself for the stated duration.

Officers are screening for intent — do your stated plans, funds and ties to home add up to a genuine short visit? Carry employment letters, enrollment certificates or property documents as appropriate. Inconsistency between your documents and your answers is the fastest route to secondary questioning.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

For Vanuatu, separate the health paperwork from the visa paperwork and check both. Vaccination certificates — yellow fever above all — are demanded by border health officers in many countries regardless of visa type, and the requirement follows your travel history, not your nationality.

If a certificate is required and you cannot produce it, you may be vaccinated on the spot, quarantined, or refused entry depending on the country. Check the current requirement on Department of Immigration and Passport Services — visas.immigration.gov.vu or the health ministry's site within two weeks of travel, since outbreak rules change faster than visa rules.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Do not confuse 'insurance not checked at the border' with 'insurance not needed' for Vanuatu. Border officers rarely ask, hospitals always bill. Buy the policy before departure — you cannot buy meaningful cover after you need it.

The useful test is evacuation: if you needed an air ambulance home, would the policy pay? Confirm medical, evacuation and repatriation limits in writing, keep the emergency assistance number offline on your phone, and carry the policy certificate with your travel documents.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Two dates matter when you enter Vanuatu: the visa's expiry and the stay granted at the border. The second one governs. Verify it immediately on arrival — errors happen, and correcting an admission stamp is far easier inside the immigration hall than weeks later.

An overstay record follows you: it can void a multi-entry visa, trigger entry bans, and must be declared on future applications to most countries. Treat the admitted-until date as a hard deadline.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Vanuatu treats work, study and visiting as entirely different legal regimes. The question at the border is always 'does this person's permission match what they intend to do' — and officers are trained to probe exactly that.

Remote work is the modern trap: immigration law cares where your body is, not where your employer sits. If you will work while in Vanuatu, you need work authorization for Vanuatu; if you will study, you need the student route with the school's documentation. Get the right status first.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Do not assume children's paperwork is simpler for Vanuatu — it is the same process, per child. Separate applications, separate fees where they apply, separate approvals.

Carry birth certificates when the family name differs between parent and child, and a notarized consent letter if only one parent travels. For dual nationals, the controlling question is which citizenship Vanuatu recognizes at its border: check before booking, because the answer determines which passport to present.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

The Vanuatu workflow that avoids most failures: verify the route, prepare the file, apply officially, wait for the decision. Each step has a classic error — wrong category, thin documentation, copycat websites charging multiples of the official fee, and non-refundable bookings made before approval.

Document discipline matters more than speed: consistent dates, names spelled identically everywhere, bank statements that actually cover the claimed funds, and an itinerary a stranger would find credible. Officers decide on the file in front of them.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Last-minute applications — processing estimates assume a clean file; yours may not be, and queries add weeks.
  • Photographs to the wrong specification — a surprising share of rejections start here; follow the portal's photo guide exactly.
  • Hiding a previous refusal — non-disclosure is misrepresentation, far worse than the original refusal.
  • Booking through an unverified agent — if you must use help, verify the agency against the embassy's listed partners.
  • Carrying no funds evidence — border officers can and do ask; 'it's all on my phone' is not an answer.
  • Skipping travel insurance — not always checked, always needed.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Quality in a Vanuatu file means completeness plus consistency. Complete: every required document present, photographs to specification, forms fully answered with no blanks officers must guess at. Consistent: identical name spellings, matching dates, funds that plausibly cover the itinerary.

Where the rules allow discretion — and they always do — the officer fills gaps with judgment. A tidy, well-ordered file invites the benefit of the doubt; a chaotic one invites refusal. Number your documents and reference them in the cover letter.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Treat the Vanuatu online application like a financial transaction, because it is one. Verify the site is Department of Immigration and Passport Services — visas.immigration.gov.vu before entering card details; legitimate portals never ask for payment via crypto, gift cards or personal bank transfers.

Apply with enough lead time that a portal glitch does not become a missed trip — online systems go down, and 'the website was broken' is not grounds for an expedited decision. The approval document, once issued, travels with you in print and as a backup on your phone.

Translations, certifications and document format

The Vanuatu document rule is simple: if the officer has to guess, you lose. Label everything, date everything, and make sure names, dates and amounts match across the file — a passport that says one name and a bank statement showing another needs an explanation attached, not discovered.

Do not submit originals you cannot afford to lose unless required; certified copies are accepted for most supporting evidence. And keep a complete duplicate of the submitted file — if the mission queries anything, you answer from the same papers they hold.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

No biometric enrollment procedure could be verified for Vanuatu's visa routes. Expect standard document and identity checks at the immigration counter on arrival, with the visa (where required) pre-approved via the portal.

Keep the approval accessible: for the non-exempt minority, the pre-travel authorization is the document carriers check.

Families and group travel

Do not let a child's application be an afterthought for Vanuatu. Minors need their own passport, their own form, and — where the system requires it — their own interview or biometrics appointment, with its own lead time.

If the family includes dual nationals, check Vanuatu's entry rule per person before booking: the passport each traveller presents determines the route, and mixing them up at the border causes delays at best.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Plan for Vanuatu customs as carefully as the visa. Currency declaration thresholds apply per person or per family depending on the country — know which. Keep high-value electronics' receipts accessible in case of questions about temporary import.

Medications in original containers with prescriptions; no loose pills in unmarked bottles. And verify drone, radio and satellite-device rules specifically — these are controlled in many countries and confiscated at the border without the right permit.

Departure and future travel

The trip is not over until you have exited Vanuatu cleanly. Keep evidence of timely departure: boarding passes, exit stamps, the electronic record where one exists.

Future applications routinely ask about previous overstays and refusals — a clean record is the simplest possible answer. If you needed an extension and got one, keep that paperwork too; it proves you handled the situation through proper channels.

How to research a route without getting misled

The research method that works for Vanuatu: start at Department of Immigration and Passport Services — visas.immigration.gov.vu, confirm with the embassy, sanity-check against a current official advisory. Three agreeing official sources beat thirty forum posts.

Watch for the two classic misinformation patterns: copycat sites presenting themselves as official (always verify the domain), and agents describing their own service bundle as if it were the government fee. The government fee is published on Department of Immigration and Passport Services — visas.immigration.gov.vu; everything above it is someone's margin.

A final document audit before travel

  • Route confirmed on Department of Immigration and Passport Services — visas.immigration.gov.vu for your specific nationality — not a friend's, not a forum's
  • All names, dates and amounts identical across the application file
  • Translations completed for any document not in the mission's language
  • Photographs to the portal's exact specification
  • Onward travel provable at the border, not just intended
  • Funds evidence covering the whole trip, seasoned over months not days
  • Health and insurance paperwork separate from — and as complete as — the visa file
  • Departure plan inside the permitted stay, with buffer days

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for Vanuatu?

Probably not — citizens of ~130 countries (including the EU, UK, US, Australia, New Zealand, Canada and Japan) enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Non-exempt travellers apply for a visitor visa via the online portal: VT 8,000/12,000/15,000 for 1/2/3 months (2026).

How much is a Vanuatu student visa?

VT 40,000 for the first year (2026, per the Department of Immigration and Passport Services). Applications require an annual bank statement — but no fixed funds minimum is published, so confirm expectations directly.

How much is a Vanuatu work visa?

VT 150,000 for the first year of an employment visa (2026). Residence and business visas run VT 250,000 first year; the investor visa is VT 1,000,000.

Is there visa on arrival in Vanuatu?

No general visa on arrival. Non-exempt nationalities arrange the visitor visa through the online portal (visas.immigration.gov.vu) before travel.

How long does a Vanuatu visa take?

No official processing-time figures are published. Apply via the online portal with generous lead time — and plan around flight schedules, which are the real constraint on a Vanuatu trip.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Vanuatu in 2026, most travellers do nothing: ~130 countries visa-free for 90 days, 6-month passport validity, and the immigration counter. Everyone else uses the online portal with one of the Pacific's clearest fee schedules — VT 8,000–15,000 visitor, VT 40,000 student, VT 150,000 employment, VT 250,000 residence/business, VT 1,000,000 investor. No processing figures are published, so lead time is your only lever. Verify your nationality's position on immigration.gov.vu and go.
