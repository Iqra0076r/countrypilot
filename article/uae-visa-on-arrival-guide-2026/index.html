UAE Visa on Arrival 2026: Who Qualifies, Fees, and How It Works

Some travellers skip the UAE's pre-arranged visa entirely and get stamped in on arrival — for AED 100. Here is who qualifies in 2026 and how the two doors compare.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20266 min read

The UAE runs two doors for short visits: the pre-arranged tourist visa bought before departure, and visa on arrival issued at the airport for qualifying travellers. The on-arrival door is cheaper and simpler — but eligibility is narrow and specific. This guide explains who qualifies in 2026, what it costs, and when to use the other door.

Quick Answer

Travellers holding qualifying residence permits from listed countries may receive a UAE visa on arrival: 14 days for AED 100 or 60 days for AED 250. Eligibility turns on the specific residence permit held — it is not a general nationality rule. Everyone else uses the pre-arranged e-visa: AED 250 (30-day single-entry, +5% VAT), AED 690 (30-day multiple), or AED 600 (90-day single) on the 2026 GDRFA schedule.

Who qualifies for visa on arrival

The on-arrival facility is extended to nationals of certain countries who hold valid residence permits from a listed set of countries — the permit is the qualifying document, not just the passport. Because the listed countries and the permit conditions are set by the UAE authorities and can change, verify your specific permit's position before travelling on the assumption. Arriving without eligibility and without a pre-arranged visa is how travellers get put on the next flight home.

The on-arrival fees

Where it applies, the pricing is simple: 14 days for AED 100, or 60 days for AED 250. Both undercut every pre-arranged option on price — the cheapest e-visa starts at AED 250 for 30 days single-entry. The fee is paid at issuance on arrival. Note the durations are fixed to the two options; there is no 30-day or 90-day on-arrival variant.

The other door: pre-arranged e-visa

Travellers who do not qualify for on-arrival use the pre-arranged e-visa, applied for before travel through official channels or a sponsor (airline, hotel, or travel agency). The 2026 GDRFA schedule: AED 250 for 30-day single-entry (+5% VAT), AED 690 for 30-day multiple-entry, AED 600 for 90-day single-entry. Full detail in our UAE tourist visa fee guide.

Which door should you use?

The decision tree is short. Do you hold a qualifying residence permit? Verify it against the current list — if yes, visa on arrival is cheaper and needs no advance application. If no, the pre-arranged e-visa is the only door: apply with margin before departure, because the visa must be issued before you fly and airlines check it at boarding. When in doubt about eligibility, default to the e-visa: a pre-arranged visa that proves unnecessary costs AED 250, while an assumed on-arrival that does not exist costs the whole trip.

What both doors require

Whichever door you use, health insurance is mandatory for visitors — basic plans run about AED 600–1,200 per year — and your passport must cover the stay. Keep the e-visa approval or the on-arrival receipt accessible for the journey, and the payment receipt with your travel documents.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is UAE visa on arrival in 2026?

AED 100 for 14 days or AED 250 for 60 days, for travellers with qualifying residence permits.

Can Indians get UAE visa on arrival?

Only with a qualifying residence permit from a listed country — eligibility turns on the permit, not the passport alone. Verify before travelling.

Is the e-visa cheaper than visa on arrival?

No — where on-arrival applies, it is cheaper (from AED 100) than the cheapest pre-arranged option (AED 250).

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

AED 100 for 14 days or AED 250 for 60 days — if your residence permit qualifies; otherwise the pre-arranged e-visa from AED 250: verify eligibility first, and when in doubt, arrive with the visa already in hand.
