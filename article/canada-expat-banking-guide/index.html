Start with the account-opening rules

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) explains that you may be able to open an account even if you are not a Canadian citizen or live outside Canada; you may need to visit a branch. Being unemployed, having no initial deposit or having been bankrupt does not by itself remove the right to open an account. Identity verification still applies.

FCAC describes an identification route using two reliable documents: one showing your name and address, and another your name and date of birth. Its list includes foreign passports and specified government, utility and financial statements. It also describes an alternative involving identity confirmation by an eligible person. Read the full FCAC requirements before deciding that a missing Canadian driving licence prevents you from applying.

Prepare for the branch appointment

Ask the bank to confirm which of your actual documents it accepts, whether originals are required, and whether an appointment is necessary. A general online checklist may describe one product rather than every account-opening route. If an app rejects a foreign passport, ask whether branch verification is available.

  • List the identity and address documents you already hold.
  • Ask how your overseas address or temporary accommodation will be handled.
  • Confirm whether a newcomer or student offer has separate status requirements.
  • Ask when the debit card and online banking will become usable.

Keep account opening separate from borrowing. Do not assume approval for a deposit account includes a credit card, overdraft or loan.

Compare the cost after the welcome offer

Use the same expected activity for each account: monthly transactions, cash withdrawals, incoming overseas payments and card spending abroad. Record the normal monthly charge alongside the promotional rate. Ask what happens when student eligibility or the introductory period ends.

A first-year budget example

Illustration only, not a bank quotation: Account A charges CAD 0 for three months and CAD 15 for the next nine months: CAD 135 in first-year monthly fees. Account B charges CAD 5 each month: CAD 60 for the year. If all other costs and features were identical, B would cost CAD 75 less despite A’s free introductory period.

Now add your likely transactions. An account with lower monthly fees may still cost more if you frequently pay for services outside its allowance. Keep the comparison in Canadian dollars and do not count a conditional welcome reward until you know you qualify.

If the application is refused

FCAC says a refusal must be provided in writing with complaint information. Ask for that statement and follow the bank’s complaint procedure if you believe the decision was unfair. A bank can refuse in specified circumstances; the right to an account is not unconditional.

Before leaving an appointment, request a clear next step: a missing document, an alternative verification route, or the complaint contact. Never submit an altered statement to satisfy an address check.

Compare the second year, not just the first month

A newcomer offer can reduce early banking costs, but it should not be the only reason to choose an account. Write down the standard monthly fee after the promotion, minimum-balance conditions if any, included transaction limits, ATM access, transfer charges and the cost of services you expect to use after the introductory period ends.

Separate everyday banking from credit building

A chequing or everyday account solves payment and deposit needs, while credit products involve separate eligibility, pricing and repayment obligations. Do not assume that opening an account automatically creates a strong credit history or that a credit limit should be treated as spendable income.

If you choose a credit product later, compare interest, fees, payment dates and the consequences of carrying a balance. Keep rent, tuition and emergency savings in a budget that does not depend on borrowing.

Use a first-30-days banking checklist

  1. Confirm the account's standard fee and the end date of any promotional period.
  2. Set up secure online access and transaction alerts.
  3. Test a small payment or transfer before relying on the account for a major deadline.
  4. Store the bank's official fraud and complaint contacts.
  5. Review your first statement for unexpected fees.
  6. Update your address and tax-residency information when the bank requires it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I open the account before arriving?

Ask the bank about its non-resident application process. An online application or pre-arrival offer may still require an in-person identity check before the account is fully usable.

Which bank is cheapest?

There is no universal cheapest account. Compare the standard annual fees and your expected transactions, using current terms for accounts you are eligible to open.
