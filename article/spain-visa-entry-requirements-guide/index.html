How to use this Spain visa and entry guide

Spain's immigration authority is split: the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (exteriores.gob.es) runs visas through consulates, while the Ministry of Inclusion, Social Security and Migration (inclusion.gob.es) governs residence and work authorizations decided by the Oficina de Extranjería. For most applicants the consulate is the front door and the extranjería office is the back office — know which one owns your question.

This guide was verified against consular pages and 2026 reporting in October 2026. Spain's defining recent event is the new Reglamento de Extranjería (Royal Decree 1155/2024), in force since 20 May 2025 — the largest immigration reform in years — plus the 2026 minimum wage (SMI) of €1,221/month, which lifts linked thresholds like the digital nomad visa's €2,442/month. One figure this guide deliberately omits: no verified official processing time exists for work authorizations, so none is printed — check the consulate instead of trusting a blog's number.

Passport validity and document condition

As a Schengen state, Spain applies the EU-wide rule: the passport must have been issued within the last 10 years and remain valid at least 3 months beyond your intended departure from the Schengen area. Consulates check this at application, airlines at boarding.

For national long-stay visas, ensure the passport covers the initial visa period. Travellers from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh should keep at least two blank pages for the visa sticker and stamps.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

First, visa-free entry: US and UK citizens may visit for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa. Second, the Schengen uniform visa: for tourism, business or family visits up to 90 days — €90 for adults, €45 for children 6–11, free for under-6s (in force since 11 June 2024, unchanged for 2026), plus the external provider (BLS) service charge. Third, national visas: for work, study or stays over 90 days. Work authorizations carry official extranjería fees — Modelo 790-052 applicant fee €203.84 plus Modelo 790-062 employer fee €203.84 (salaries below 2× SMI) or €407.71 (at or above 2× SMI), plus a consular work visa of around €80 (2026); the highly qualified professional route (UGE, Ley 14/2013) costs €73.26 (Modelo 790-038). The student national visa is €90 (2026; slight consulate variations exist).

Transit and onward travel

Spain applies the EU airport-transit-visa list: some nationalities need a transit visa even for an airside-only connection at Madrid-Barajas or Barcelona-El Prat. Verify your nationality's position on the consular pages before booking — transit through Spain is common on Latin America and Africa routings.

Leaving the airport during a layover requires a full Schengen visa. Spain also operates an airport transit visa category distinct from the Schengen short-stay visa — apply for the correct one, as the wrong application wastes the fee.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

A Schengen visa lets you present yourself at the border — the Policía Nacional officer decides admission. Carry the passport with the visa, accommodation proof, the return ticket, insurance proof and funds evidence. Spain is known for checking proof of accommodation and return tickets with particular attention.

National-visa holders should carry the work authorization or admission letter. After arrival, long-stay holders must apply for the TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero) at the Oficina de Extranjería/Policía Nacional — the visa alone does not complete residence.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Spain requires no vaccination certificate for entry and operates no pandemic-era border health checks. No health document is presented at the Spanish border.

The health requirement that matters is insurance (below) — Spanish consulates are notably strict that the policy must have no copayments, no deductibles and no waiting periods; travel insurance alone is rejected for long-stay applications.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance is a Schengen visa condition: minimum €30,000 cover, valid Schengen-wide. For student and residence visas, consulates require fuller health cover — student-focused plans run about €300–600 per year (€25–50/month), and the policy must offer 100% coverage with no copayments, no deductibles and no waiting periods (2026). Broader residency-compliant policies range €600–4,500/year depending on age.

Submit the actual policy certificate, not a quote or a booking confirmation — consulates verify the coverage terms line by line, and 'travel insurance' worded policies are routinely rejected for long-stay files.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Schengen stays are capped at 90 days in any 180-day period, counted across the whole area. Spain enforces entry/exit stamping for UK and US passports, which is how the count is tracked.

National-visa holders are admitted for the visa's duration and must obtain the TIE after arrival. Under the new Reglamento (in force 20 May 2025), initial residence and work permits run 1 year, renewable in 4-year blocks — a structural change from the old renewal rhythm worth knowing before you plan renewals.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A Schengen visa never authorizes work in Spain. Work routes: the standard cuenta ajena (employed) route with the Modelo 790 fees above (€203.84 + €203.84/€407.71 + ~€80 consular, 2026), decided by the Oficina de Extranjería — no verified official processing-time figure exists, so this guide publishes none; ask the consulate for the current reality rather than trusting a blog's number. The highly qualified professional (UGE) route costs €73.26 (2026). The digital nomad visa requires income of €2,442/month for the main applicant (2026, derived from the €1,221/month SMI set by Real Decreto 126/2026).

Students: the national study visa (€90, 2026) with a legal decision period of 1 month from submission (extendable if interviews or extra documents are requested) — and applications must be filed at least 2 months before the programme starts (2026 consular guidance). Proof of funds is 100% of IPREM per month: €600/month (2026 IPREM, unchanged since 2023). The Reglamento's headline social change: arraigo (social-integration) regularization cut from 3 years to 2, with new socio-labor and 'second chance' arraigo categories and a new 5-year permit for non-EU family members of Spanish nationals.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Each child needs a separate passport and visa application — including infants — at €45 for ages 6–11 and free under 6 (2026). Minors travelling without both parents should carry parental consent documentation.

Dual nationals should enter on the passport matching their visa or visa-free status. Non-EU family members of Spanish nationals benefit from the new 5-year permit under the 2025 Reglamento — check the extranjería pages for the current application route.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Schengen uniform visa: 1) Identify the competent consulate or BLS centre. 2) Complete the form and assemble documents (passport, photos, itinerary, insurance, funds, ties). 3) Attend in person for biometrics (first-time applicants). 4) Pay €90 + BLS service charge (2026). 5) Allow 15 calendar days standard, up to 45 maximum (EU Visa Code, 2026) — apply at least 2–3 weeks before travel.

Work (cuenta ajena): 1) The employer initiates the residence/work authorization with the Oficina de Extranjería (Modelo 790 fees paid). 2) On approval, apply for the national work visa at the consulate (~€80, 2026). 3) Enter Spain and apply for the TIE. Processing times are not officially published — get the consulate's current estimate in writing.

Study: 1) Secure admission. 2) Arrange the €600/month funds evidence and the no-copay insurance (€300–600/yr). 3) File at least 2 months before the programme. 4) Pay €90 (2026). 5) Legal decision period 1 month (2026).

Track consular applications via sutramiteconsular.maec.es.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Trusting a blog's work-visa timeline. No official figure exists — the consulate's current estimate is the only honest number.
  • Submitting 'travel insurance' for a long-stay visa. Consulates reject policies with copays, deductibles or waiting periods — get the €300–600/yr compliant plan.
  • Filing the student visa too late. The 2-months-before rule is enforced; the 1-month decision clock starts at submission.
  • Working on a Schengen visa. Never authorized — and the arraigo reform doesn't retroactively legalize it.
  • Ignoring the SMI link. The €2,442/month digital-nomad threshold moves with the minimum wage — 2025 figures are stale.
  • Counting 90 days per country. The Schengen clock is area-wide.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Spanish consulates check the standard trio — purpose, funds, ties — with particular attention to accommodation proof and return tickets. Present dated bookings, genuine bank statements and an employment letter with approved leave. For students, the file is arithmetic: €600/month funds evidence plus the compliant insurance certificate plus admission — consulates verify the coverage terms, so submit the real policy.

For work routes, quality is the employer's extranjería filing: complete Modelo 790 payments and a contract at or above the applicable threshold. An incomplete employer file is the dominant cause of work-route delays.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Spain offers no eVisa: Schengen and national applications are made in person at the consulate or BLS centre (first-time biometrics required). Consular files can be tracked at sutramiteconsular.maec.es.

Scam avoidance: exteriores.gob.es and the consulate are the only official channels. No agent can produce a work authorization faster than the Oficina de Extranjería, and none can quote an official processing time that doesn't exist. Official fees are receipted Modelo 790 forms — never pay a 'visa fee' to a personal account.

Translations, certifications and document format

Originals plus one copy: bank statements, employment letter, admission letter (students), the insurance policy certificate (not a quote), and the itinerary. Documents not in Spanish or English should carry certified translations — some consulates insist on Spanish.

Photographs must meet Schengen specifications (35×45 mm). For work routes, the employer's documentation (contract, company registration, Modelo 790 receipts) is filed with the extranjería office — keep copies of everything the employer submits.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

First-time applicants provide fingerprints and a photograph (VIS, 59 months). Interviews are not routine for Schengen applications but the consulate may call applicants — particularly for long-stay files — to clarify purpose, funding or ties.

At the border, Policía Nacional may re-verify accommodation, return tickets and funds — Spain checks these more often than many Schengen states, so carry the submitted evidence.

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs a separate application and appointment — including infants — at €45 for ages 6–11 and free under 6 (2026). Family reunification for residents runs through the extranjería system as a separate authorization, not through the consulate alone.

A refusal for one member is recorded in the Visa Information System — address the reason before re-applying rather than resubmitting identical facts.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Declare cash of €10,000 or more when crossing the EU's external border — no tax on honest declaration, seizure possible for failure to declare. Standard EU restrictions apply to animal and plant products; medications in original packaging with prescriptions for controlled substances.

Departure and future travel

Depart within the 90/180-day allowance — overstaying risks fines and future refusals recorded area-wide. Keep the TIE valid once issued; it is the re-entry document for residents, and the 1-year initial / 4-year renewal rhythm under the new Reglamento should be diarized from day one.

How to research a route without getting misled

Use this hierarchy: 1) The competent consulate's pages on exteriores.gob.es for visa procedure and fees. 2) inclusion.gob.es / the Oficina de Extranjería for residence and work authorizations. 3) sutramiteconsular.maec.es for tracking. 4) The BLS centre page for submission logistics.

Ignore any work-visa timeline quoted without an official source — none exists. Ignore pre-May-2025 guides on arraigo and permit durations — the Reglamento rewrote them. Anything charging far above the official Modelo 790 and consular fees is selling anxiety.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport issued within last 10 years, valid 3+ months beyond departure, with blank pages
  • Correct visa type — Schengen for ≤90 days, national for work/study
  • Insurance policy certificate (100% cover, no copay/deductible/waiting) for long stays
  • Student file: €600/month funds + admission + timely filing (≥2 months before)
  • Work file: employer extranjería filing complete with Modelo 790 receipts
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation proof accessible offline (Spain checks these)
  • Bank statements and ties-to-home documents packed
  • Cash ≥€10,000 declaration readiness
  • Consulate contact saved for emergencies

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Spain?

Yes. Indian passport holders need a Schengen visa for visits up to 90 days (€90 fee, 2026, plus BLS service charge) — applied for at a Spanish consulate or BLS centre before travel. There is no visa on arrival and no eVisa. Standard processing is 15 calendar days.

How much money do I need for a Spain student visa?

€600 per month — 100% of the 2026 IPREM, unchanged since 2023 — plus a compliant health insurance policy (€300–600/year with 100% coverage and no copayments, deductibles or waiting periods, 2026). Applications must be filed at least 2 months before the programme starts, with a 1-month legal decision period.

What is Spain's digital nomad visa income requirement in 2026?

€2,442 per month for the main applicant (2026) — derived from the 2026 minimum wage (SMI) of €1,221/month in 14 payments set by Real Decreto 126/2026. The threshold moves with the SMI, so 2025 figures are stale.

How long does a Spain work visa take?

There is no officially published processing time for Spanish work authorizations — the Oficina de Extranjería decides, with timelines varying by province and season. This guide deliberately publishes no number; ask the consulate for the current estimate. Any blog quoting a precise figure is guessing.

What changed in Spain's immigration rules recently?

The new Reglamento de Extranjería (Royal Decree 1155/2024) took effect 20 May 2025: arraigo regularization cut from 3 to 2 years, new socio-labor and 'second chance' arraigo categories, initial residence/work permits of 1 year renewable in 4-year blocks, and a new 5-year permit for non-EU family members of Spanish nationals.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Spain, use the consulate for visas (exteriores.gob.es) and the extranjería system for residence and work — and accept that work-authorization timelines have no official figure, so plan around the consulate's current estimate. Budget the verified 2026 numbers: €90 Schengen, the Modelo 790 work fees (€203.84 + €203.84/€407.71 + ~€80), €90 student visa filed 2+ months early, €600/month IPREM funds, and the €2,442/month digital-nomad threshold. Keep the passport inside the 10-year/3-month Schengen rule, carry genuinely compliant insurance, and remember the 90-day clock runs across the whole Schengen area.
