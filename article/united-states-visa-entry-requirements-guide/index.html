How to use this United States visa and entry guide

United States entry rules turn on two questions: what is the purpose of the trip, and does the traveller's passport qualify for the Visa Waiver Program? Citizens of 42 countries — including the United Kingdom — may travel without a visa for up to 90 days with an approved ESTA, applied for only at esta.cbp.dhs.gov ($40.27 total as of 2026). Travellers from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh need a B-1/B-2 visitor visa: a $185 fee, Form DS-160, and an interview at a US embassy or consulate.

The controlling sources are the State Department's Bureau of Consular Affairs at travel.state.gov (fee schedule and the Global Visa Wait Times tool) and US Customs and Border Protection for admission rules. This guide was verified against those sources in October 2026. Interview wait times move weekly and fees change by regulation — re-check the live pages before paying anything or booking non-refundable travel.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport must be valid for at least six months beyond your period of stay in the United States, unless your country is exempt under a country-specific agreement (travel.state.gov). The United Kingdom is exempt; travellers from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh should assume the full six-month rule applies and renew early if in doubt.

CBP also recommends at least one blank page for the admission stamp, and airlines enforce Timatic at boarding — a passport that fails the validity rule means denied boarding, not a discussion at the gate. Emergency and temporary passports may face extra scrutiny; confirm acceptance with the embassy before relying on one.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Work through the routes in order. First, the Visa Waiver Program (VWP): 42 nationalities, ESTA authorization required before boarding, $40.27 total ($10.27 application charge plus $30 on approval, as of 2026), maximum 90 days per visit, no extension and no change to another immigration status. Apply at least 72 hours before departure, and only at esta.cbp.dhs.gov — copycat sites charge large markups for submitting the same form.

Second, the B-1/B-2 visitor visa: for business (B-1), tourism (B-2), or both. The $185 machine-readable-visa fee (as of 2026) covers the application; the visa is often issued for up to 10 years with multiple entries, but each admission is decided separately by the CBP officer, commonly up to 6 months and recorded on the electronic I-94. Third, everything else — work, study, immigration — needs its own petition-based category and is a different process entirely.

Transit and onward travel

The United States has no sterile international transit: every traveller who changes planes on US soil clears immigration, even without leaving the airport. Treat a US transit as a full entry — same documents, same admission decision, same I-94 record.

Travellers from Visa Waiver Program countries may transit on an approved ESTA. Everyone else needs a C-1 transit visa ($185, as of 2026) even for an airside-only connection. If your itinerary routes through the US to a third country, secure the transit permission before the trip; being denied boarding at origin is the common failure mode, not refusal at the US airport.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Admission is decided by the CBP officer at the port of entry — a visa only gives you the right to present yourself for inspection. Carry the passport containing the visa (or ESTA approval), a return or onward ticket, the address where you will stay, proof of funds for the trip, and evidence of ties to home such as an employment letter, property documents or university enrollment.

Officers routinely ask how the trip is funded and when you intend to leave. Answer directly and consistently with your DS-160. If you cannot fund the trip yourself, carry evidence of who is funding it — but note the State Department's position: applicants qualify on their own ties abroad, not on assurances from US-based family or friends.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

The United States sets no general vaccination requirement for short-term visitors, and no pandemic-era entry rules remain for nonimmigrant travellers. There is no health document to present at the border for an ordinary B-1/B-2 or ESTA trip.

The exception is long-term immigration (medical examinations apply to immigrant-visa applicants, a different process). If you have specific health concerns, check the CDC's destination guidance — but for entry itself, the controlling sources are CBP and travel.state.gov, and neither lists a visitor health document.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel health insurance is not a US entry or visa condition — no officer will ask for a policy. It is nevertheless close to essential: US medical care is among the world's most expensive, a single emergency-room visit can run into thousands of dollars, and visitors have no access to subsidized care.

Buy a policy with high medical limits and emergency evacuation before departure. If your plans later shift to study, budget roughly $2,400–$3,900 per year for a university student health plan (2026–27 published figures from American University, Stony Brook and the University of Oregon) — most schools auto-enroll international students.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

The CBP officer sets your admission period on the electronic I-94 — for B-1/B-2 holders this is commonly up to 6 months but can be shorter, and the visa's validity (often 10 years) is not the same as the permitted stay. Retrieve and check your I-94 at i94.cbp.dhs.gov after every entry; the date there is the legal limit, not the visa foil.

VWP/ESTA admissions are capped at 90 days with no extension and no change of status. Overstaying — even briefly — can void the visa and trigger re-entry bars of 3 or 10 years. If you need longer than admitted, file for an extension with USCIS before the I-94 expires; do not simply remain.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A B-1/B-2 visa never authorizes employment — not remote work for a foreign employer while physically in the US, not unpaid 'helping out' at a family business. Work requires a work-authorized category. Note the September 2025 presidential proclamation imposing a $100,000 payment on most new H-1B petitions filed after 21 September 2025 (covering the 2026 cycle), on top of standard USCIS fees — verify current enforceability at state.gov/h-1b-faq before making plans, as legal challenges were reported.

Study requires F-1 status with SEVIS registration ($350 I-901 fee, as of 2026) and a Form I-20 from the school — the school's published cost of attendance is the figure used for proof of funds, since the US sets no federal dollar minimum. Do not enter as a visitor intending to 'sort out' work or study later; misrepresentation at entry is a separate ground of inadmissibility.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

US citizens — including dual nationals — must enter and leave the United States on a US passport. A dual US/UK citizen cannot travel on ESTA using the British passport; airlines are required to enforce this. Each child needs their own passport and, for a visa application, their own DS-160 form — check the embassy page for current interview requirements for minors, as these change.

Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s) with contact details. It is not a universal legal requirement, but CBP officers may ask, and having it avoids delays. Carry the child's birth certificate as well when surnames differ.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Visa Waiver travelers: 1) Confirm your nationality is among the 42 VWP countries. 2) Apply at esta.cbp.dhs.gov at least 72 hours before departure ($40.27, as of 2026). 3) Do not board without an approved authorization — carriers check it.

B-1/B-2 visa applicants: 1) Complete Form DS-160 online. 2) Pay the $185 MRV fee (as of 2026). 3) Schedule the interview at the embassy or consulate — then check the Global Visa Wait Times tool for your post (July 2026 figures: Hyderabad ~10.5 months, Islamabad ~7.5, London ~2). 4) Gather supporting documents on purpose of trip, ties to home, and funds — only the passport, DS-160 confirmation and photo are strictly required at the interview, but officers may request the rest. 5) Attend the interview (fingerprints and photograph taken). 6) After approval, check whether your nationality owes a visa issuance (reciprocity) fee before arranging passport return.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Paying a copycat site for ESTA. The official application is $40.27 at esta.cbp.dhs.gov; third-party sites charge large markups for the same submission.
  • Reading a 10-year visa as a 10-year stay. Visa validity and admitted stay are different things — the I-94 date governs.
  • Booking non-refundable travel before the interview when your post's wait is 8+ months.
  • Entering on ESTA to job-hunt or to adjust status. VWP entrants cannot extend or change status; the 90 days are absolute.
  • Letting the passport slip under six months' validity (unless your country is exempt) — airlines will deny boarding.
  • Working remotely 'because the employer is abroad.' Physical presence while working is what counts, not where the employer sits.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Most B-1/B-2 refusals cite INA section 214(b): the officer was not convinced the applicant would return home. A strong file directly answers that concern — stable employment with a leave-approval letter, property or lease documents, family ties, prior compliant travel history, and bank statements showing you can fund the trip without working in the US.

Keep the story consistent across the DS-160, the interview answers and the documents. Vague itineraries ('two months, visiting friends, not sure where') read as risk; a dated plan with bookings and a return ticket reads as a genuine visit. Previous refusals must be declared honestly — misrepresentation findings are far harder to overcome than a 214(b) refusal.

Interview scheduling is the real timeline driver: the State Department's Global Visa Wait Times tool (updated 21 July 2026) shows visitor-visa interview waits from about 2 weeks at low-demand posts to 13+ months at the busiest (Hyderabad ~10.5 months, New Delhi and Mumbai ~8.5, Karachi ~8, Islamabad ~7.5, London ~2). Administrative processing after the interview can add further weeks and cannot be expedited. Apply as early as your travel window allows — ideally 6–12 months ahead for high-demand posts.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

The US has no eVisa for visitors; the online steps are the DS-160 (ceac.state.gov), the ESTA application (esta.cbp.dhs.gov), and the embassy's appointment system. After the interview, track the case on the CEAC status check.

Scam avoidance: the only official ESTA site is esta.cbp.dhs.gov — domains with 'esta' plus words like 'apply-online' or 'visa-service' are private businesses marking up a $40.27 application. No one can guarantee visa approval or sell an earlier interview slot; anyone promising either is running a scam. Never pay a 'visa fee' to a personal bank account or crypto wallet — MRV fees are paid through the embassy's designated channels only.

Translations, certifications and document format

At the interview, only the passport, DS-160 confirmation page and compliant photograph are strictly required — everything else is supporting evidence the officer may or may not ask to see. Bring originals plus one copy of: bank statements (3–6 months), employment letter with approved leave dates, property or lease documents, prior visas and entry stamps, and the trip itinerary with bookings.

Documents in another language should be accompanied by an English translation; while the State Department does not prescribe a certification format for visitor applications, a translator's signed statement of accuracy avoids disputes. Photographs must meet the 5×5 cm US visa specification — the DS-160 upload validates this automatically.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Almost all B-1/B-2 applicants aged 14–79 attend an in-person interview. Fingerprints and a digital photograph are captured at the appointment. Expect questions on the purpose of the trip, employment, family, prior travel and funding — answered briefly and truthfully, matching the DS-160 exactly.

At the port of entry, CBP may take fingerprints and a photo again and can inspect phones or laptops under border-search authority — travel with a device you are comfortable having examined. Secondary inspection is not an accusation; remain calm, answer directly, and do not volunteer unrelated information.

Families and group travel

Every traveller needs their own authorization: each family member requires a separate DS-160 and interview appointment (including infants), and each VWP traveller — children included — needs their own ESTA. There is no family ESTA or family visa.

When family members are listed in one passport, each still submits a separate application. If one member is refused, the others' applications are unaffected, but do not attempt to 'cover' a refused traveller by rewording the same weak facts — address the refusal reason first. Groups should book interview appointments as close together as the system allows.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Travellers (individually or as a family travelling together) carrying more than $10,000 in currency or monetary instruments must report it on FinCEN Form 105 — it is not taxed or confiscated, but failure to declare can lead to seizure. There is no limit on how much you may bring, only a reporting duty.

US agricultural rules are strict: most fresh fruit, vegetables, meats and plants are prohibited or restricted — declare all food. Medications should be in original packaging with a prescription or doctor's letter for controlled substances. Alcohol and tobacco allowances apply per adult traveller; check CBP's 'Know Before You Go' for current quantities.

Departure and future travel

Keep your I-94 record after every trip — a clean history of entries and timely departures strengthens future applications, while an overstay can void the visa and trigger 3- or 10-year bars. B-1/B-2 holders who need more time must apply to USCIS for an extension before the I-94 expires; VWP travellers cannot extend at all and must leave within 90 days.

A valid visa in an expired passport remains usable: travel with both the old passport (containing the visa) and the new valid passport, provided personal details are unchanged. Lost or stolen US visas must be reported to the embassy; a replacement requires a new application.

How to research a route without getting misled

Use this hierarchy: 1) travel.state.gov for visa categories, fees and the wait-times tool — it overrides everything else. 2) The specific embassy/consulate website for local procedures and document drop-off rules. 3) CBP (cbp.gov) for admission, ESTA and I-94 questions. 4) USCIS (uscis.gov) only for petition-based routes like H-1B.

Treat forums, visa agents' blogs and social-media anecdotes as leads, not answers — interview experiences vary wildly by post and officer. Any site selling 'guaranteed approval' or charging far above the $185/$40.27 official fees is monetizing your anxiety, not your application.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid 6+ months beyond stay (unless your country is exempt) with a blank page
  • Visa foil valid and ESTA approved if transiting/visiting under VWP — check status 72h before departure
  • I-94 retrieval bookmarked (i94.cbp.dhs.gov) for arrival day
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation address accessible offline
  • Funds evidence and ties-to-home documents packed (not only on a phone)
  • Travel insurance policy with US medical coverage
  • FinCEN 105 ready if carrying over $10,000
  • Medications in original packaging with prescriptions
  • Embassy/consulate contact saved for emergencies

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for the United States, or is ESTA enough?

It depends on your passport. Citizens of the 42 Visa Waiver Program countries (including the UK) need only an approved ESTA ($40.27, as of 2026) for tourism or business visits up to 90 days. Travellers from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines, Bangladesh and other non-VWP countries need a B-1/B-2 visa obtained before travel. Check your nationality on travel.state.gov — never assume.

How long does it take to get a US visitor visa interview?

It varies enormously by post. The State Department's Global Visa Wait Times tool (updated 21 July 2026) shows waits from about 2 weeks to over 13 months — for example, roughly 8.5–10.5 months at Indian posts and 7.5–8 months in Pakistan, versus about 2 months in London. Check the live tool for your consulate and apply 6–12 months ahead where waits are long.

How long can I stay in the US on a visitor visa?

The CBP officer decides at each entry and records it on your electronic I-94 — commonly up to 6 months for B-1/B-2 holders, but it can be shorter. The visa's validity (often 10 years) is not the permitted stay. VWP/ESTA visits are capped at 90 days with no extension. Always check your I-94 after arrival.

Can I work remotely while visiting the US on a tourist visa?

No. A B-1/B-2 visa never authorizes employment, including remote work for a foreign employer while you are physically in the United States. Working — in any form — requires a work-authorized immigration category.

What is the $100,000 H-1B fee I keep reading about?

A September 2025 presidential proclamation imposed a $100,000 payment on most new H-1B petitions for workers outside the US, covering the 2026 cycle — on top of standard USCIS fees. It does not apply to petitions filed before the deadline, renewals, or current holders travelling. Because legal challenges were reported, verify current enforceability at state.gov/h-1b-faq before relying on it.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For the United States, the route check comes first: ESTA ($40.27) at least 72 hours before departure if your passport qualifies for the Visa Waiver Program, or a B-1/B-2 visa ($185) with an interview booked months ahead if it does not. Keep six months' passport validity, expect the CBP officer — not the visa — to set your stay on the I-94, and never work on a visitor entry. Verify fees and wait times on travel.state.gov close to departure; they move.
