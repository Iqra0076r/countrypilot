Schengen Visa Rejection Reasons: 12 Causes and How to Fix Each

A Schengen refusal comes on a standard EU form with the grounds ticked. All 12 official rejection reasons explained — and the fix for each before you appeal or reapply.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 12, 20266 min read

A Schengen refusal is not a verdict on you — it is a form. The consulate ticks one or more grounds from a fixed list in the EU Visa Code, and every tick corresponds to evidence that was missing, inconsistent, or unconvincing. This guide works through the twelve causes that drive real-world refusals — and the fix for each.

Quick Answer

A refused Schengen application comes back with the standard EU refusal form (Annex VI to the Visa Code), grounds ticked — most often unproven purpose, insufficient funds, unreliable documents, or doubt you will leave in time. The €90 fee is not refunded. You may appeal under the refusing country's law or reapply at any time: no ban, no waiting period — but an unchanged reapplication gets the same answer.

Fee €90 · Children 6–12 €45 · Decision 15–45 days · Updated 2026-10-12 · Reading time 8 minutes

How to use this guide

Start with your refusal form: find every ground ticked, then read the matching cause below — each fix names the evidence the consulate needs next time. The 12 rejection causes — and how to fix each

A Schengen refusal uses the EU Visa Code's Annex VI form, with the grounds ticked from a fixed list of 12. The most common are an unproven trip purpose, insufficient or unverifiable funds, unreliable documents, missing €30,000 travel insurance, doubt you will leave before expiry, a used-up 90/180 allowance, an invalid passport, and applying at the wrong consulate.

1. The purpose of your stay was not justified

The form's wording: justification for the purpose and conditions of the intended stay was not provided. No bookings, a vague itinerary, an invitation that does not match the purpose. The fix: a dated, day-by-day plan, accommodation for every night, and the document proving the purpose — conference registration, business invitation, or a host's formal invitation.

2. Insufficient funds for the trip

You did not prove sufficient means of subsistence for the stay, the return, or both. Each member state sets a daily reference amount, judged against accommodation: France works on €65 per day where prepaid accommodation is proven. The fix: total the real cost first (flights, accommodation, daily spending), then show a balance covering it with margin — built over time, not assembled the week you apply.

3. The money could not be shown to be yours, lawfully available

The form also covers being unable to acquire the means lawfully. A large, sudden, unexplained deposit reads as borrowed money parked for the application. The fix: statements showing regular income over several months. If someone else pays, say so openly, with their statements and a signed sponsorship letter — declared support beats an unexplained balance.

4. Doubt that you will leave before the visa expires

The single most common tick: your intention to leave the territory of the Member States before the expiry of the visa could not be ascertained — a judgment about your ties at home, not your character. The fix: evidence of a life that pulls you back — an employer's leave letter with a return date, property or a lease, family at home, commitments, a history of leaving on time.

5. Your information was not reliable

Contradictions do the damage: dates differing between form and bookings, an employer who cannot be verified, reservations cancelled before the decision. The fix: one consistent story across every document, with genuine, verifiable bookings — refundable ones are fine; fake or instantly cancelled ones are not.

6. No adequate travel medical insurance

Insurance is a legal requirement, not an extra. Under Article 15 of the Visa Code it must cover at least €30,000 of emergency care, hospitalisation and repatriation, in all Schengen states, for the entire stay. The fix: a compliant policy whose certificate states cover, territory and trip dates exactly.

7. Your 90/180 allowance was already used up

The form records that you have already stayed for 90 days during the current 180-day period. No consulate can issue a visa breaching the 90-in-180 ceiling. The fix: count previous stays day by day — the window rolls, so waiting until earlier days fall outside the 180 restores your allowance.

8. False, forged or fake documents

A forged statement, altered booking or fabricated employer letter is the most damaging ground — and it follows you: the refusal and its reason sit in the shared Visa Information System all consulates check. The fix: never submit one. If an agent submitted a fake without your knowledge, disclose it next time and rebuild with genuine evidence.

9. A security alert or public-policy flag

Two related grounds: an alert in the Schengen Information System (SIS) for refusal of entry, or a member state treating you as a threat to public policy, security, public health or international relations. The fix: rarely a paperwork problem — the alert can only be challenged in the state that issued it, usually with legal advice there.

10. Your passport failed the validity rules

A Schengen passport must have been issued within the last 10 years and stay valid at least 3 months after you leave. The fix: renew first, apply second — and check the issue date, not just expiry: extended-validity passports can breach the 10-year rule while looking valid.

11. You applied at the wrong consulate

Applications belong at the consulate of your main destination (most nights) or, if nights are equal, the first country you enter. Filing wherever an appointment was free invites refusal. The fix: count the nights and apply where they point.

12. An incomplete file

The Commission's baseline list: form, photo, passport, medical insurance, and documents on purpose, means, accommodation, and intention to return. Gaps surface as grounds 2, 3, or 8. The fix: work through our visa document checklist before the appointment, and answer every field — a blank reads as a gap.

Appeal or reapply?

Appeal a Schengen refusal under the refusing country's national law — the deadline in your refusal letter counts — when the evidence was right and the decision misread it. Otherwise reapply at any time, with no ban or waiting period, but fix every ticked ground first: an unchanged file gets the same answer and the €90 fee is not refunded.

Both doors stay open. The Commission confirms a refused applicant is told why and how to appeal; the appeal runs under the national law of the refusing country, so the deadline in your letter counts. A fresh application can be lodged any time — no cooling-off period — and the refusal must be declared, which costs nothing because consulates see it anyway. Appeal when the evidence was right and the decision misread it; reapply when the evidence was the problem, fixing the ticked grounds before spending another €90.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not reapply with the same file — the same ticks return. Never consulate-shop: the refusal sits in the shared Visa Information System. Non-refundable flights never fix a funds refusal; a credible balance and income pattern do. And never let an agent prepare documents you have not seen — you carry any fraud record for everything filed in your name.

  • Reapplying with the same file. Same evidence, same ticks.
  • Consulate-shopping after a refusal. The record sits in the Visa Information System — and the wrong-consulate rule applies anyway.
  • Booking your way out of a funds refusal. Non-refundable flights prove nothing; a credible balance and income pattern do.
  • Letting an agent prepare documents you have not seen. You carry the refusal — and the fraud record — for anything in your name.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a Schengen decision take?

Normally 15 days, extendable to 45 days. Apply from 6 months before travel, at least 15 days ahead.

Does a refusal ban me from the Schengen area?

A refusal is not an entry ban. It is recorded in the Visa Information System; the next application is judged on its own evidence. (An SIS alert, cause 9, is a separate national decision.)

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

A refusal is a checklist, not a mystery: the form tells you exactly which evidence failed. Match every tick to its fix — justified purpose, sufficient funds demonstrably yours, reliable documents, €30,000 of insurance, a valid passport, the right consulate, a convincing reason you will leave — and the reapplication is a new case, not a repeat.
