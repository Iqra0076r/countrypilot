How to use this Sweden visa and entry guide

Sweden's entry rules are decided in one place: the Swedish Migration Agency (Migrationsverket) at migrationsverket.se — the sole authority for visas, work permits and residence permits. Short visits follow the Schengen system (no Swedish e-visa, no visa on arrival); longer stays go through Migrationsverket's online e-service. The 2026 landscape shifted materially, so older guides are unreliable.

Two June 2026 changes matter most. First, the work-permit salary floor rose to 90% of the median salary — SEK 33,390/month at introduction (adjusted annually), with mandatory comprehensive health insurance for stays up to one year. Second, bachelor's and master's students may now work a maximum of 15 hours per week (exceptions for internships and June–August) — exceeding it can mean permit withdrawal. This guide was verified against Migrationsverket in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

For Schengen entry via Sweden, the passport must have been issued within the last 10 years and remain valid for at least 3 months beyond your planned departure from the Schengen area, with at least two blank pages. These are Schengen Code requirements, applied uniformly.

Residence-permit applicants should ensure the passport covers the full requested permit period — a passport expiring mid-permit complicates renewals. Keep copies of the data page with the application file.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Short stay (up to 90 days): visa-exempt nationalities (US, UK, EU and others) enter freely; everyone else needs a Schengen Type C visa (EUR 90 adults, EUR 45 ages 6–11, free under 6) applied for in person at a Swedish mission. Long stay: residence permits for work (SEK 2,200 general; SEK 2,000 EU Blue Card/ICT/seasonal) and study (SEK 1,500 adults, SEK 750 under 18) via Migrationsverket's e-service.

Transit and onward travel

Airside transit through Swedish airports follows Schengen transit rules: most nationalities transiting without leaving the international zone need no transit visa, but nationals of listed countries require an airport transit visa (Type A) even without clearing immigration — check the current list on migrationsverket.se for your nationality.

Any itinerary that exits the transit area — terminal changes requiring immigration clearance, overnight stays — becomes a normal Schengen entry: visa-free nationals proceed, others need the Type C visa in advance. When in doubt, secure the visa; 'I was only transiting' does not override the requirement.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Border officers in Sweden decide admission on the spot, and a visa in the passport is permission to request entry, not a guarantee of it. Keep the return or onward ticket, the address of your first night's stay, and evidence of funds within reach — officers most often ask how the trip is funded and when you plan to leave.

Answer plainly and keep every answer consistent with what you put on the application form. If someone else is funding the trip, carry their details too, but understand the underlying test: officers are assessing whether you will leave when you say you will.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Check health-entry rules for Sweden before you book anything non-refundable: requirements turn on where you have recently been, not just the passport you hold. Yellow-fever certificates are the most common trigger across Africa and South America, and some countries demand proof at the border even for transit.

Beyond certificates, look at the destination's health reality — malaria prophylaxis, dengue precautions, and whether your travel insurance covers medical evacuation. None of this is visa paperwork in the strict sense, but arriving without a required certificate produces the same outcome as arriving without a visa: refusal.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Whether or not Sweden makes insurance a visa condition, treat it as one for yourself. Medical bills for visitors are charged at full private rates in most countries, and a single hospital admission can exceed the entire trip budget several times over.

Match the policy to the trip: medical expenses with a high limit, emergency evacuation and repatriation, and coverage for every activity you will actually do. If Sweden sets a minimum coverage figure for the visa, buy above it — minimums are entry thresholds, not adequate protection.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

The stamp or record you receive on arrival in Sweden — not the visa in your passport — sets how long you may legally stay. Check the admitted-until date before leaving the immigration hall; the visa's validity period and the permitted stay are different things, and confusing them is one of the most common causes of accidental overstay.

If the permitted stay is shorter than your plans, address it through official channels before the date expires — overstaying, even by days, can void the visa and complicate every future application to Sweden and elsewhere.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A visitor entry to Sweden does not authorize work — not for a local employer, not remotely for a foreign one while you are physically present, not 'unpaid help' at a family business. Work requires a work-authorized category, applied for and approved before you start.

Study is equally separate: enrollment needs a student or residence status with the institution's sponsorship or admission letter in the file. Entering as a tourist intending to convert to work or study later is misrepresentation in most systems and can end with removal rather than a status change.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Children need their own travel documents for Sweden — their own passport, and where visas apply, their own application. A child listed in a parent's passport does not inherit the parent's visa.

Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the absent parent(s) with contact details, plus the birth certificate where surnames differ. Dual nationals should check which passport Sweden expects them to use: some countries require entry on their own passport if you hold their citizenship, and using the 'wrong' one can cause refusal.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

A reliable sequence for Sweden: 1) Confirm the correct route for your nationality on Swedish Migration Agency (Migrationsverket) — visa-free, e-visa, or consular visa. 2) Assemble documents before paying any fee: passport, photographs to specification, itinerary, funds evidence. 3) Apply through the official channel only, never a copycat site. 4) Track the application and do not book non-refundable travel until the visa is in hand.

Build slack into the timeline: add two weeks to whatever processing estimate you find, and keep copies of everything submitted. If an interview or biometrics appointment is required, that appointment — not the published processing time — is usually the critical path.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Applying for the wrong category — the requirements you meet must match the visa you request, not the one with the lower fee.
  • Copycat websites — unofficial portals that charge several times the government fee for submitting the same form. Use Swedish Migration Agency (Migrationsverket) only.
  • Non-refundable bookings before approval — never commit money the consulate has not yet earned you the right to spend.
  • Inconsistent paperwork — dates, names and employment details must match across every document.
  • Assuming visa validity equals permitted stay — the admission stamp governs, not the visa foil.
  • Ignoring the funds test — 'I have money' without statements to prove it fails everywhere.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Strong applications to Sweden share one trait: every claim is evidenced. Employment is shown with a dated letter stating role, salary and approved leave — not just a business card. Funds are shown with 3–6 months of statements in your name — not a single screenshot. Ties to home are shown with leases, enrollment letters or family documents — not asserted in a cover letter alone.

Write the cover letter a stranger could follow: who you are, why you are going, what you will do each week, when you return, and how it is funded — each point pointing at an attached document. Officers process volume; clarity is persuasive.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

For Sweden, the only safe online channel is Swedish Migration Agency (Migrationsverket). Copycat domains mimic official portals closely — check the URL character by character, and never arrive at the 'official' site via a search ad.

After submitting, use the portal's own status tracking rather than emailing the mission for updates; consulates do not expedite because you ask. Keep the application reference, payment receipts and the final approval document together — you will need the approval at boarding and at the border.

Translations, certifications and document format

Beyond the mandatory items, the documents that actually decide Sweden applications are funds evidence and ties to home. Bank statements should run 3–6 months, show the account holder's name, and reflect a balance consistent with the trip's cost — a sudden large deposit just before applying raises more questions than it answers.

Translations: any document not in the mission's working language needs a competent translation; requirements vary, so check Swedish Migration Agency (Migrationsverket) rather than assuming. Keep originals and one full copy of everything submitted.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Schengen visa applicants provide fingerprints and a digital photograph at the application center or mission — biometric data is stored in the Visa Information System (VIS) and reused for subsequent applications within 59 months. Children under 12 are exempt from fingerprinting.

Residence-permit applicants similarly provide biometrics when the application is lodged. Keep the appointment confirmation: the biometrics appointment, not the published processing time, is usually the critical path.

Families and group travel

Each traveller to Sweden needs individual documentation — including children. Where electronic authorizations apply, every person needs their own approval; there are no family bundles, and a child's approval is not implied by the parent's.

When one family member faces a refusal, do not simply reapply with identical papers — address the refusal reason first. For groups, align the applications: same itinerary, consistent purpose statements, and appointments booked as close together as the system allows.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Customs is the second border after immigration in Sweden, and it has its own rules. Declare currency above the reporting threshold — declaration is not taxation, but failure to declare can mean seizure. Food, plants and animal products face the strictest controls in most countries; when in doubt, declare.

Medicines need original packaging and, for controlled substances, a prescription or doctor's letter. Check Sweden's prohibited list before packing: drones, satellite communicators and certain medications surprise travellers regularly.

Departure and future travel

Leave Sweden the way you entered: on time and documented. Keep boarding passes and entry/exit stamps — a clean travel history is an asset on every future visa application you will ever make.

If plans change mid-trip, act before the permitted stay expires: seek an extension through official channels where available, or depart and reapply. An overstay record must be declared on future applications to most countries and can void multi-entry visas.

How to research a route without getting misled

Research Sweden entry rules in a strict hierarchy. First, Swedish Migration Agency (Migrationsverket) — the official portal overrides everything else. Second, the specific embassy or consulate handling your nationality, for local procedures. Third, official advisory pages for the security context.

Treat forums, agent blogs and social media as leads, never answers: one traveller's smooth entry is not a rule, and procedures change without announcement. Anything promising 'guaranteed approval' is selling fiction.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid for the required period beyond your stay, with blank pages
  • Visa or authorization approved before non-refundable bookings — approval document printed and backed up
  • Return/onward ticket and first accommodation address accessible offline
  • Funds evidence and ties-to-home documents packed, not only on a phone
  • Travel insurance covering medical, evacuation and the full trip duration
  • Required health certificates (e.g., yellow fever) in the WHO booklet format
  • Medications in original packaging with prescriptions where needed
  • Cash declaration prepared if carrying above the threshold
  • Embassy/consulate emergency contact saved

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Sweden?

Yes — Indian nationals need a Schengen Type C visa (EUR 90, 2026) applied for in person at a Swedish mission, for stays up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Decisions usually come within two weeks.

What is Sweden's work permit salary requirement in 2026?

Since 1 June 2026, the salary floor is 90% of the Swedish median salary — SEK 33,390/month at introduction (adjusted annually). The job must also include comprehensive health insurance for stays up to one year. The general work-permit fee is SEK 2,200.

How long does a Swedish student residence permit take?

Migrationsverket statistics (viewed October 2026) show 75% of higher-education permits decided within 3 months. Apply as soon as you hold the admission decision — and note the maintenance requirement of SEK 10,656/month for 2026 applications.

Can students work in Sweden in 2026?

Since 11 June 2026, bachelor's and master's students on a residence permit may work a maximum of 15 hours per week (exceptions: internships, student representation, research, and June–August). Exceeding the cap can lead to permit withdrawal or extension refusal.

Does Sweden have visa on arrival?

No. Sweden uses the Schengen system exclusively — no e-visa, no visa on arrival. Non-exempt nationals apply for the Schengen visa at a Swedish mission before travel.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Sweden, migrationsverket.se is the beginning and end of the research — it is the sole authority. Short visits run on Schengen rules (EUR 90 visa, ~2-week decisions); work and study go through the e-service with the 2026 realities priced in: SEK 33,390/month salary floor, SEK 10,656/month student maintenance, and the 15-hour weekly work cap. Verify every figure on the live portal; Sweden adjusts thresholds annually.
