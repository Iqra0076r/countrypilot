How to use this Syria visa and entry guide

Syria's entry system is in transition and must be read with unusual care: the official e-Visa portal (evisa.sy), launched May 2024, was showing 'EVisa Coming Soon' as of 24 September 2026 following the December 2024 government transition — it is not operational. The working routes are visa on arrival at land borders with Jordan and Lebanon for many nationalities (typically issued in minutes), and pre-approved Visa Approval Documents via an authorized tour operator for air arrivals into Damascus or Aleppo.

The fee regime was standardized by Decree No. 148/8 (6 July 2025, in force through 2026): nationality-based fees collected in cash USD at airports and all land borders — USD 75 for EU citizens, USD 150 for US/UK citizens (single-entry tourist, 1 month). This guide states plainly where no verifiable figure exists rather than inventing one, and was verified in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport should be valid well beyond the trip with blank pages — apply the conservative six-months-beyond-travel standard, since no precise published formula could be verified and border practice varies by crossing.

Note the well-known regional friction: evidence of travel to Israel in the passport can cause refusal at Syrian borders. Check the current practice for your specific crossing before travel, as enforcement varies.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Land borders (Jordan/Lebanon): visa on arrival for many nationalities, typically issued in minutes — the most reliable route in 2026. Air arrivals (Damascus/Aleppo): no VoA; secure a pre-approved Visa Approval Document through an authorized tour operator before boarding. e-Visa: not operational (portal offline since the transition). Jordan, Malaysia and Mauritania nationals enter visa-free.

Transit and onward travel

There is no meaningful transit-visa framework to rely on for Syria in 2026 — and with the e-Visa portal offline, any itinerary touching Syria should be treated as a full entry via the land-border VoA or pre-approved air-arrival route, with the Decree 148/8 fee in cash USD.

In practice, few travellers transit Syria; those who cross it do so deliberately overland. Do not book air tickets routing through Damascus expecting to sort out entry on arrival — the pre-approved Visa Approval Document must exist before boarding.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Admission to Syria is granted by the officer at the port of entry, whatever your visa says. Practical preparation: passport with the visa, printed onward travel, first accommodation address, and proof you can support yourself for the stated duration.

Officers are screening for intent — do your stated plans, funds and ties to home add up to a genuine short visit? Carry employment letters, enrollment certificates or property documents as appropriate. Inconsistency between your documents and your answers is the fastest route to secondary questioning.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

For Syria, separate the health paperwork from the visa paperwork and check both. Vaccination certificates — yellow fever above all — are demanded by border health officers in many countries regardless of visa type, and the requirement follows your travel history, not your nationality.

If a certificate is required and you cannot produce it, you may be vaccinated on the spot, quarantined, or refused entry depending on the country. Check the current requirement on Syrian e-Visa portal (evisa.sy) — currently offline or the health ministry's site within two weeks of travel, since outbreak rules change faster than visa rules.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Do not confuse 'insurance not checked at the border' with 'insurance not needed' for Syria. Border officers rarely ask, hospitals always bill. Buy the policy before departure — you cannot buy meaningful cover after you need it.

The useful test is evacuation: if you needed an air ambulance home, would the policy pay? Confirm medical, evacuation and repatriation limits in writing, keep the emergency assistance number offline on your phone, and carry the policy certificate with your travel documents.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

In Syria, the admission decision at the border may involve more layers than usual — immigration, security screening, and document verification can each add hours. The admitted stay is still set by the officer, not the visa.

Keep the entry stamp or record safe: in disrupted environments, proving legal entry later (for extension, exit, or any dispute) depends on documents you kept yourself, because official systems may not have reliable records.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Syria treats work, study and visiting as entirely different legal regimes. The question at the border is always 'does this person's permission match what they intend to do' — and officers are trained to probe exactly that.

Remote work is the modern trap: immigration law cares where your body is, not where your employer sits. If you will work while in Syria, you need work authorization for Syria; if you will study, you need the student route with the school's documentation. Get the right status first.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Do not assume children's paperwork is simpler for Syria — it is the same process, per child. Separate applications, separate fees where they apply, separate approvals.

Carry birth certificates when the family name differs between parent and child, and a notarized consent letter if only one parent travels. For dual nationals, the controlling question is which citizenship Syria recognizes at its border: check before booking, because the answer determines which passport to present.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

For Syria, the normal workflow is disrupted: embassies may be closed, portals offline, and published procedures unreliable. 1) Identify which third-country mission currently handles Syria visas — this changes, so verify it now, not from a 2024 guide. 2) Contact that mission directly before preparing anything; ask what they actually accept today. 3) Build the file around what can be verified: passport, photographs, itinerary, funds evidence.

Do not pay any fee until a functioning official channel confirms the amount and the payment method. In disrupted environments, 'agents' selling guaranteed visas are the most active fraud vector — every fee goes through an official mission or it does not get paid.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming any published procedure still works — in Syria's disrupted environment, verify every step with the currently operating mission.
  • Paying 'fees' to intermediaries — with official channels disrupted, fraud flourishes; no legitimate fee is paid to a personal account.
  • Planning travel before a visa exists — processing is unpredictable; nothing is booked until the visa is in hand.
  • Ignoring the security advisory — check your own government's current advisory for Syria; 'do not travel' designations have insurance and legal consequences.
  • Expecting consular help in-country — many embassies in Syria are closed or operating remotely; your government's ability to assist inside the country may be near zero.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Quality in a Syria file means completeness plus consistency. Complete: every required document present, photographs to specification, forms fully answered with no blanks officers must guess at. Consistent: identical name spellings, matching dates, funds that plausibly cover the itinerary.

Where the rules allow discretion — and they always do — the officer fills gaps with judgment. A tidy, well-ordered file invites the benefit of the doubt; a chaotic one invites refusal. Number your documents and reference them in the cover letter.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Online application for Syria is unreliable by definition right now: portals launch, go offline, or never worked. Before trusting any site claiming to be the Syria e-visa portal, verify it is linked from an official government source — a standalone domain with no government linkage is not trustworthy, however professional it looks.

Where no functioning online channel exists, the route is a third-country embassy in person. Confirm which mission, what it accepts, and its current fee by direct contact — and keep every receipt, because disrupted systems lose records.

Translations, certifications and document format

The Syria document rule is simple: if the officer has to guess, you lose. Label everything, date everything, and make sure names, dates and amounts match across the file — a passport that says one name and a bank statement showing another needs an explanation attached, not discovered.

Do not submit originals you cannot afford to lose unless required; certified copies are accepted for most supporting evidence. And keep a complete duplicate of the submitted file — if the mission queries anything, you answer from the same papers they hold.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

No biometric enrollment procedure could be verified for Syria's current entry routes. Expect standard document checks and possible photographing at the border crossing — procedures vary by post and by the evolving administrative situation.

Keep the Visa Approval Document (for air arrivals) and cash USD fee accessible: the land-border process moves fast when documents are ready, and stalls when they are not.

Families and group travel

Do not let a child's application be an afterthought for Syria. Minors need their own passport, their own form, and — where the system requires it — their own interview or biometrics appointment, with its own lead time.

If the family includes dual nationals, check Syria's entry rule per person before booking: the passport each traveller presents determines the route, and mixing them up at the border causes delays at best.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Plan for Syria customs as carefully as the visa. Currency declaration thresholds apply per person or per family depending on the country — know which. Keep high-value electronics' receipts accessible in case of questions about temporary import.

Medications in original containers with prescriptions; no loose pills in unmarked bottles. And verify drone, radio and satellite-device rules specifically — these are controlled in many countries and confiscated at the border without the right permit.

Departure and future travel

Plan your exit from Syria before you enter: routes, border crossings and flight availability can change with little notice. Keep your passport, visa and travel documents on your person, not in checked luggage, and register with your embassy's traveller program if one exists.

Monitor the security situation throughout the stay and leave early if it deteriorates — commercial options disappear fast in a crisis, and evacuation is never guaranteed. A clean, documented, on-time departure matters even more from a conflict zone.

How to research a route without getting misled

The research method that works for Syria: start at Syrian e-Visa portal (evisa.sy) — currently offline, confirm with the embassy, sanity-check against a current official advisory. Three agreeing official sources beat thirty forum posts.

Watch for the two classic misinformation patterns: copycat sites presenting themselves as official (always verify the domain), and agents describing their own service bundle as if it were the government fee. The government fee is published on Syrian e-Visa portal (evisa.sy) — currently offline; everything above it is someone's margin.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a visa on arrival in Syria?

At land borders with Jordan and Lebanon, yes — for many nationalities, typically issued in minutes. But not for air arrivals into Damascus or Aleppo: those need a pre-approved Visa Approval Document arranged through an authorized tour operator before boarding. The e-Visa portal (evisa.sy) is offline.

How much is a Syria visa in 2026?

Under Decree No. 148/8 (in force 2026): USD 75 for EU citizens, USD 150 for US/UK citizens — single-entry tourist, one month, cash USD only, collected at airports and land borders. Fees are nationality-based.

Is the Syria e-Visa working?

No. The portal launched in May 2024 but has shown 'EVisa Coming Soon' since the December 2024 government transition (confirmed September 2026). Do not plan around it.

Can I fly into Damascus as a tourist?

Only with a pre-approved Visa Approval Document secured via an authorized tour operator before departure — there is no visa on arrival at the airport. Arrange it weeks ahead; some nationalities face longer waits.

Does travel insurance cover Syria?

Standard travel policies do not cover Syria. You need specialist high-risk cover — but no verifiable 2026 annual-cost figure exists, so compare specialist providers directly rather than trusting quoted averages.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Syria in 2026, the system is what the transition left behind: land-border visa on arrival (minutes, cash USD 75–150 by nationality under Decree 148/8), pre-approved tour-operator documents for air arrivals, and an e-Visa portal that is offline. Where verifiable figures don't exist — work/student fees, funds minimums, insurance costs — this guide refuses to invent them. Verify each element close to travel; transitional systems change without notice.
