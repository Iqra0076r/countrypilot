Canada Study Permit Proof of Funds 2026: The $23,448 Requirement

Canada raised its student funds bar on 1 September 2026. Here is the new number, what sits on top of it, and how to evidence every dollar.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20267 min read

Of every number in a Canadian study-permit application, the proof-of-funds threshold is the one most likely to be out of date in the guide you are reading — because it moved on 1 September 2026. This guide states the current requirement, what sits on top of it, and how IRCC expects you to prove it.

Quick Answer

For study-permit applications on or after 1 September 2026, a single applicant must show CAD $23,448 per year for living expenses (up from CAD $22,895) — plus first-year tuition and travel costs. The study permit itself costs CAD $150. Funds must be verifiable and available; recent study-permit actuals ran around 10 weeks against the 60-day service standard.

Living costs CAD $23,448/year · From 1 Sept 2026 · Permit fee CAD $150 · Updated 2026-10-14 · Reading time 7 minutes

The $23,448 figure: what changed

IRCC indexes the student cost-of-living requirement, and the 1 September 2026 update raised it from CAD $22,895 to CAD $23,448 per year for a single applicant. Any guide quoting $22,895 is describing the pre-September world. The figure covers living expenses only — it is the floor beneath which the application fails, not the total you will spend.

What sits on top: tuition + travel

The $23,448 is one of three piles. IRCC expects, in addition: first-year tuition (the actual amount on your letter of acceptance — often larger than the living-costs figure), and travel costs to and from Canada. A complete funds file therefore shows $23,448 plus tuition plus travel, all verifiable. Applicants who prove exactly $23,448 and nothing else have proved one third of the requirement.

How to prove it

IRCC accepts the standard evidence set: bank statements showing the funds available, proof of a student loan, paid tuition receipts, or scholarship/funding letters. Whatever the mix, the test is the same: the money must be yours (or your sponsor's, declared), accessible, and documented — not a screenshot of a balance with no history. Sudden, unexplained deposits invite the same scepticism here as in any visa system. Total your exact requirement with our Canada study-permit proof-of-funds calculator.

Funds inside the whole application

Proof of funds is necessary but not sufficient: the study permit also needs the letter of acceptance, identity documents, and (where required) medical and police clearances — all inside the CAD $150 application submitted through an IRCC account. Funds questions are also the most common source of processing delays, which is one reason recent actuals stretched to ~10 weeks. Submit the money evidence cleanly the first time; it is the cheapest speed strategy available.

The number will move again

IRCC updates this threshold periodically — the September 2026 rise is the mechanism working as designed, not a one-off. Before every application cycle, verify the current figure on canada.ca; the difference between $22,895 and $23,448 is small, but a file built on the old number fails on the new one.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How much proof of funds for a Canada study permit in 2026?

CAD $23,448/year living costs (from 1 Sept 2026) plus first-year tuition plus travel.

How much is the study permit fee?

CAD $150 (2026), paid through an IRCC account.

What if my funds are in a parent's account?

Declared sponsor funds count — document the relationship and the sponsor's statements, and make sure the money is accessible, not locked in illiquid assets.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

$23,448 a year for living, tuition on top, travel on top of that, all of it documented and accessible: the study-permit funds bar is arithmetic — meet it exactly, evidence it cleanly, and verify the number is still current before you file.
