How to use this Malaysia eVisa guide

The Malaysia eVisa is an online visa for visa-required nationalities — including China, India, Nepal, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Myanmar — applied for at the official portal malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my. The official Single Entry Visa fee is RM 50, plus about RM 105 in eVisa processing (2026). One extra step is mandatory for almost everyone: the free Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC), submitted within 3 days before travel.

Chinese and Indian nationals should read the next section first: a visa-free waiver runs until 31 December 2026, which may remove the eVisa step entirely. Fees and eligibility move — re-check imi.gov.my before applying.

Who needs the eVisa — and who does not

eVisa route: visa-required nationalities — including Nepal, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Myanmar — apply online in advance at the official portal. Check the waiver first: China and India are currently visa-free until 31 December 2026; if the waiver is extended, no eVisa is needed — otherwise the eVisa applies.

Visa-free route: many other nationalities enter without a visa for social visits — typically 90 days for the US and UK, typically 30 days for ASEAN nationals such as the Philippines — but confirm your country's position on imi.gov.my. The full route map is in the Malaysia visa and entry requirements guide; the fees are in the Malaysia visa fee 2026 guide.

The only official eVisa portal

The only official eVisa channel is malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my, reached via the Immigration Department at imi.gov.my. Look-alike commercial sites charge markups and add no value — some harvest passport data. Never start from a search ad without verifying the domain: the official Single Entry Visa fee is RM 50, so any site charging several times that is not the government.

Step-by-step: applying for the Malaysia eVisa

  1. Confirm your nationality needs the eVisa on imi.gov.my (China/India: check the waiver first).
  2. Reach the portal from imi.gov.my — type the address yourself, never via a search ad.
  3. Create an account — names must match the passport exactly, including name order.
  4. Upload a sharp colour scan of the passport biodata page (6+ months' validity) and a recent ICAO-style photo.
  5. Enter itinerary details matching your declared purpose.
  6. Pay the official fees — RM 50 visa fee plus about RM 105 in eVisa processing (2026).
  7. Track on the official portal — apply early; no official processing-time standard is published.
  8. Print the eVisa approval and keep a digital copy.
  9. Submit the free MDAC within 3 days before travel.
  10. Carry printed copies of the approval, MDAC confirmation, return ticket and accommodation details.

Documents checklist

For the eVisa: passport biodata scan (6+ months' validity), ICAO-style photo, return/onward flight booking, accommodation details, and funds evidence. Keep digital and paper copies of everything submitted — Malaysian counters occasionally ask to see the document behind the approval.

A return or onward ticket is effectively mandatory: officers routinely ask for it, and a one-way ticket with no onward plan is the most common reason for extended questioning.

The mandatory MDAC after your eVisa is approved

The Malaysia Digital Arrival Card is mandatory for almost all foreign arrivals, free, and submitted online within 3 days before travel — whether you enter on an eVisa or visa-free. It does not replace a visa; it is a separate arrival requirement. Every traveller — including infants — needs their own MDAC. Forgetting it means delays at the counter.

Interviews and biometrics

Malaysia has no general visa-interview system for eVisa applicants — most are decided on documents alone. Biometrics (fingerprints and photo) are captured at the Malaysian entry checkpoint on arrival. If you renewed your passport after obtaining an eVisa, it is linked to the old document — carry both passports or re-apply.

Application mistakes that cause refusals and delays

  • Blurry or low-quality uploads — a smudged passport biodata scan or a photo that does not meet specifications is the most common refusal trigger.
  • Name mismatches — transliteration differences between the application and the passport cause MDAC and eVisa hiccups; copy the name order exactly.
  • Itinerary not matching the declared purpose — vague or inconsistent travel plans read as risk.
  • Applying through a look-alike site — marked-up fees and possible misuse of your passport data, with no benefit.
  • Assuming visa-on-arrival is a backup plan — it is limited to specific arrival corridors (RM 200) and is not a general facility.

Scam avoidance

The official Single Entry Visa fee is RM 50 — any site charging several times that is not the government. Never pay an "eVisa fee" from a search-ad site without verifying the domain, and never pay a personal bank account or crypto wallet. No one can guarantee approval or sell faster processing; anyone promising either is running a scam.

At the border: the eVisa is permission to travel, not a guarantee of entry

A Malaysian eVisa is permission to travel to the border, not an unconditional right of entry — the immigration officer at the checkpoint decides admission and stamps your permitted stay. The MDAC speeds up the counter process but does not replace the officer's decision. Carry your printed eVisa approval, MDAC confirmation, return or onward ticket, proof of funds and accommodation details.

Overstaying can lead to detention, fines and future blacklisting — know your permitted stay from the entry stamp and diarise your exit date. For trip planning, see the Malaysia travel insurance and health guide and the Malaysia travel tools and planning guide.

Families: everyone applies separately

Each family member — including infants — needs their own MDAC and their own visa or visa-free entitlement; there is no family bundling. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter and birth certificate; officers may question the arrangement. Groups should submit MDACs with consistent travel dates and keep a shared folder of bookings.

Final checklist before you fly

  • Route confirmed on imi.gov.my (eVisa, visa-free, or limited arrival visa)
  • eVisa applied for at malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my only — RM 50 + ~RM 105 paid
  • Names on the application match the passport exactly
  • eVisa approval printed, plus digital copies
  • MDAC submitted — free, within 3 days before travel, one per traveller
  • Passport valid 6+ months beyond arrival
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation details accessible offline
  • Funds evidence and travel insurance documents packed
  • Fees re-checked against the official 2026 schedule — do not budget from memory or old guides

Frequently Asked Questions

Who can apply for the Malaysia eVisa?

The eVisa is for visa-required nationalities — including Nepal, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Myanmar. China and India are currently visa-free until 31 December 2026, so they need the eVisa only if the waiver lapses. Many other nationalities enter visa-free for social visits. Confirm your country's current position on imi.gov.my.

How much does the Malaysia eVisa cost in 2026?

The official Single Entry Visa fee is RM 50, plus about RM 105 in eVisa processing charges — roughly RM 155 total. A Multiple Entry Visa is RM 100. Apply only at the official portal malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my.

Do I still need the MDAC if I have an eVisa?

Yes. The Malaysia Digital Arrival Card is mandatory and free for almost all foreign arrivals, submitted online within 3 days before travel. It is separate from the visa — the eVisa does not replace it.

What is the official Malaysia eVisa website?

The only official channel is malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my, reached via the Immigration Department at imi.gov.my. Look-alike commercial sites charge markups and some harvest passport data — always verify the domain before paying.

Can Chinese and Indian citizens apply for the eVisa?

They can, but they may not need to: China and India currently enjoy visa-free entry until 31 December 2026 under the waiver policy. Check whether the waiver has been extended before applying — if it lapses, the eVisa route applies.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

The Malaysia eVisa is a straightforward online application at malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my for visa-required nationalities — RM 50 plus about RM 105 in processing, decided on documents alone, with no interview. The two traps are copycat sites charging multiples of the official fee and forgetting the free MDAC within 3 days of travel. Get those right, carry a printed approval with a return ticket, and the checkpoint is the easy part.
