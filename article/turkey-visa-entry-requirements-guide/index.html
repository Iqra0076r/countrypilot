How to use this Türkiye (Turkey) visa and entry guide

Türkiye's entry system is nationality-driven: the official Electronic Visa Application System at evisa.gov.tr (Ministry of Foreign Affairs) serves eligible nationalities with fees calculated per nationality at checkout, many nationalities are visa-exempt up to 90/180 days, and some need sticker visas at missions. After entry, residence permits are applied for on e-ikamet (eikamet.goc.gov.tr) — the visa gets you in, the residence permit lets you stay.

What to know for 2026: eVisa eligibility and fees are per-nationality with no single published global fee schedule (third parties report ~US$50, but that is unverified as a general figure — your checkout total is the authoritative number). Student visas run approximately US$100–150 by nationality and consulate (90-day single-entry education visa, then residence permit via e-ikamet). This guide was verified against official Turkish sources in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport must be valid for at least 60 days beyond the visa's expiry — Türkiye's distinctive formulation (effectively longer than the stay for most visas), with blank pages. This is stricter than it sounds: check the arithmetic against your specific visa's validity.

The eVisa is linked to the passport number. For residence-permit applications on e-ikamet after entry, the passport must cover the requested residence period.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

First, check exemption: many nationalities enter visa-free up to 90 days in 180 — verify yours on evisa.gov.tr. Second, eVisa-eligible: apply on the official portal; the fee is calculated per nationality at checkout. Third, sticker visa: nationalities outside both categories apply at a Turkish mission. Students: education visa (90-day single-entry), then residence permit on e-ikamet after arrival. Workers: work visa, then work permit under the annual Harçlar tariff.

Transit and onward travel

Istanbul is a major transit hub and airside transit on through-tickets generally needs no visa — but any exit from the transit area requires the appropriate entry permission for your nationality (exemption, eVisa, or sticker visa).

Stopover programs and terminal changes that clear immigration are entries, not transits. Given the per-nationality fee calculation, sort out the eVisa before the first flight if there is any chance of going landside — the eVisa cannot be conjured at the transit desk.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

At the Türkiye (Turkey) border, expect questions before you get a stamp. The standard set covers purpose of visit, length of stay, accommodation, and means of support — have the onward ticket, hotel booking or host address, and bank evidence ready rather than buried in luggage.

A visa lets you travel to the border; the officer's decision is what lets you in. Short, truthful, consistent answers beat long explanations. If your plans involve anything beyond tourism — work, study, volunteering — say so only if your visa category actually covers it.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Health documents are a separate gate from the visa for Türkiye (Turkey). The key question is certificate-based: does Türkiye (Turkey) require proof of yellow-fever vaccination (common if you arrive from an endemic country), and are there any current outbreak-related declarations?

Carry the WHO yellow card (International Certificate of Vaccination) if there is any chance it applies — digital photos are not accepted at most borders. Also confirm your insurance covers the actual medical environment: in countries with limited hospital capacity, evacuation cover matters more than the policy's headline medical limit.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance sits in a grey zone for Türkiye (Turkey): sometimes a formal visa requirement, always a practical one. Where the embassy or portal names a minimum medical coverage, treat that number as the floor and buy a policy that meaningfully exceeds it.

Read the exclusions before you pay: adventure activities, pre-existing conditions and alcohol-related incidents are the three most common claim rejections worldwide. For long stays, check whether the policy covers the full visa duration — a 90-day policy on a 180-day trip leaves a dangerous gap.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

On arrival in Türkiye (Turkey), the officer's admission decision is the operative document. Note the exact date you must leave and keep evidence of it (a photo of the stamp or the electronic record) — 'I thought the visa was valid longer' is not a defence against an overstay finding.

Extensions, where they exist, must be sought before the current permission expires and are never guaranteed. Plan the itinerary inside the granted stay rather than assuming an extension will be available.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Keep the three purposes strictly apart for Türkiye (Turkey): visiting, working, studying. Each has its own permission, its own evidence, and its own application channel — a tourist visa or visa-free entry covers none of the other two.

The expensive mistake is assuming intent does not matter: taking a job, enrolling, or freelancing while admitted as a visitor can lead to detention, removal, and multi-year bans. If the plan might involve work or study, secure that status before travelling, not after arrival.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

For families heading to Türkiye (Turkey): every traveller, including infants, needs individual documentation. Where an authorization or e-visa system applies, each person — children included — needs their own approval; there are no family bundles.

A consent letter from any non-travelling parent is cheap insurance against difficult questions at the border. Dual citizens must verify Türkiye (Turkey)'s rule on which nationality to travel under — entering on a foreign passport while holding Türkiye (Turkey) citizenship is an offence in some countries.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Work the Türkiye (Turkey) application backwards from the travel date. First, identify the exact visa category and its document list on Republic of Türkiye Electronic Visa Application System. Second, gather funds evidence and bookings — most refusals trace to weak or inconsistent documentation, not to eligibility. Third, submit via the official portal or mission and keep the reference number.

Fourth, wait for the decision before committing money to flights and hotels. Published processing times are targets, not promises; consulates do not compensate for missed trips caused by slow decisions.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Trusting forum anecdotes over Republic of Türkiye Electronic Visa Application System — procedures change; the official portal is the only source that matters.
  • Paying fees to personal accounts — official fees go through official channels; anything else is a scam.
  • Letting the passport drop below the validity threshold — airlines deny boarding, and there is no appeal at the gate.
  • Working or studying on a visitor entry — the fastest route to removal and a multi-year ban.
  • Overstaying 'just a few days' — it voids multi-entry visas and must be declared on future applications worldwide.
  • Forgetting onward travel proof — one of the most common refusal reasons at the border itself.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Think of the Türkiye (Turkey) application as an argument with exhibits. The argument: 'I am a genuine visitor who will leave on time and can afford the trip.' Every exhibit should support exactly that — and nothing should contradict it.

Common contradictions that sink files: an itinerary showing a luxury trip against bank statements showing a thin balance; employment dates that do not match the leave letter; a stated purpose ('conference') with no invitation or registration. Re-read the whole file as a skeptic before submitting.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Online applications for Türkiye (Turkey) live or die on the portal you choose. Republic of Türkiye Electronic Visa Application System is the official channel; everything else is a private business. The tell-tale signs of a copycat: fees several times the official schedule, 'guaranteed approval' language, and payment to personal accounts.

Screenshot every confirmation page and save every email receipt. If the portal issues an approval letter or authorization to board, print it — phone screens fail at exactly the wrong moment, and some carriers require paper.

Translations, certifications and document format

For Türkiye (Turkey), assemble two piles: what the checklist demands, and what proves your story. The second pile — employment letter with approved leave, property or tenancy documents, enrollment confirmation, prior travel history — is where approvals are won.

Photographs deserve more care than they get: follow the portal's dimensions, background and recency rules exactly, because a rejected photo restarts the clock. Where documents need legalization or apostille, start early — that process has its own timeline independent of the visa.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Biometric enrollment for Turkish visas follows the mission's procedure — expect fingerprints and a digital photograph at the application center or consulate for sticker-visa routes. The eVisa itself is a purely online authorization with no pre-departure enrollment.

Residence-permit applications on e-ikamet after entry include their own biometric step at the provincial migration office. Keep the eVisa approval and passport consistent throughout.

Families and group travel

Families should budget per person for Türkiye (Turkey): per-person fees, per-person forms, per-person approvals. Children's applications need the same care as adults' — birth certificates where surnames differ, and consent letters where only one parent travels.

Group applications rise or fall together in practice: if the group's stated purpose is a wedding, every file should reflect the wedding. One member with a contradictory story invites scrutiny of all.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

After the immigration stamp in Türkiye (Turkey) comes customs. The two questions that matter: are you carrying more than the declarable cash threshold, and are you carrying anything restricted — food, medicine, electronics, cultural goods?

Cash thresholds are about reporting, not limits: declare honestly and keep walking. Restricted goods are different — undeclared prohibited items can mean fines or confiscation. Read the customs authority's list for Türkiye (Turkey) before you pack, not after you land.

Departure and future travel

Departure from Türkiye (Turkey) deserves the same attention as arrival. Confirm the exit requirements — some countries check for departure cards, exit stamps or proof of onward travel even when leaving.

A documented, on-time departure builds the travel history that makes the next visa easier. Conversely, even a short overstay creates a record that follows you across borders and across years. When in doubt, leave early.

How to research a route without getting misled

For Türkiye (Turkey), rank your sources: Republic of Türkiye Electronic Visa Application System first, the responsible embassy second, official advisories third. Everything else — forums, influencers, visa-agent marketing — is background noise until confirmed against the top two.

Be especially careful with dated content: fee schedules, processing times and even visa categories change year to year. A 2023 guide quoting fees is not a 2026 answer. Check the publication or 'last updated' date on anything you read, then verify against Republic of Türkiye Electronic Visa Application System anyway.

A final document audit before travel

  • Correct visa category for the actual purpose — visiting, working and studying are different permissions
  • Passport validity and blank pages verified against Türkiye (Turkey)'s rule, not a generic 'six months' assumption
  • Application reference, payment receipts and approval kept together
  • Itinerary, funds and purpose consistent across every document
  • Biometrics/interview appointment completed where required, with confirmation
  • Insurance policy documents carried, emergency assistance number saved offline
  • Customs-relevant items checked against Türkiye (Turkey)'s prohibited list
  • Someone at home holds copies of your passport, visa and itinerary

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the Turkey eVisa?

It is calculated per nationality at checkout on the official portal (evisa.gov.tr) — there is no single published global fee. Third parties report around US$50, but that is unverified as a general figure. Your checkout total on the official site is the authoritative number — and beware copycat domains charging multiples.

Do I need a visa for Turkey?

It depends entirely on nationality: many nationalities are visa-exempt up to 90/180 days, others use the eVisa, and some need sticker visas at missions. Check your per-nationality position on evisa.gov.tr — a friend's experience with a different passport tells you nothing.

How do I get a residence permit in Turkey?

After entering on the appropriate visa, apply on e-ikamet (eikamet.goc.gov.tr) — the visa gets you in, the residence permit lets you stay. Students: education visa first (90-day single-entry, ~US$100–150), then the permit application after arrival.

How long does a Turkey visa take?

No official processing-time schedule is published. Third parties report eVisa approval in 24–72 hours and sticker visas in weeks — all unverified. Apply early, especially for student and work routes.

Can I work remotely in Turkey on a tourist entry?

No. Visitor entries do not authorize work in any form. Employment needs the work-visa route with a permit under the annual tariff — arranged before starting work, not after arrival.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Türkiye, the portal is the price list: evisa.gov.tr calculates your fee per nationality at checkout (no global schedule exists), exemptions cover many passports up to 90/180 days, and sticker visas serve the rest. Students budget ~US$100–150 for the education visa plus US$500–600/month in demonstrable funds and mandatory health insurance, then move to e-ikamet after entry. Verify your nationality's exact position on the official portal — in Türkiye, nationality is the whole system.
