How to use this Jordan visa and entry guide

Jordan's headline is generous: a visa on arrival (JOD 40) for 120+ countries, issued at airports and most land borders. But the system has two hard edges. First, 53 restricted nationalities need prior approval from the Ministry of Interior before travel — including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh — and the VOA is not available to them. Second, visitors staying more than 14 days must register with the police — a rule tightened from 1 October 2025 with JOD 200 fine risk.

The e-Visa exists via the MOI e-Services portal, but the VOA dominates in practice. Work permits run JOD 300 (first year) / JOD 180 (renewal) — far cheaper than the region's norms. This guide was verified in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for at least 6 months beyond arrival with blank pages. Restricted nationalities should allow weeks for the MOI prior-approval process — this is the long pole, not the flight booking.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

The restricted list decides everything: 53 nationalities (including all five South Asian/African ones below) need MOI approval in advance; everyone else — US, UK and 120+ more — walks up to the VOA counter.

Transit and onward travel

Jordan's VOA covers transit-style entries at airports and most land borders for eligible nationalities. The King Hussein/Allenby Bridge crossing to/from the West Bank has its own procedures — confirm current arrangements before planning that route. Restricted nationalities need the MOI approval regardless of transit purpose.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever Jordan's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the Ministry of Interior e-Services approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

No vaccination certificate is a standard Jordanian entry requirement for ordinary tourists. Check current advisories — requirements can be added during health events.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance is not a Jordanian VOA condition, but it is strongly recommended. No official student-insurance figure is published.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Pay JOD 40 at the VOA counter (cash or card — have both). Keep the return ticket and accommodation accessible. If staying more than 14 days, register with the police — the rule was tightened from 1 October 2025 and fines run to JOD 200. Don't treat registration as optional.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work permit: JOD 300 first year / JOD 180 renewal — employer-sponsored through the Ministry of Labour. Student visas: no official fee figures were verified for 2026 — verify with the mission or university. A VOA entry never authorises work; the police-registration rule applies to long-stay visitors too.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own Jordan permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether Jordan restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

VOA route (120+ nationalities): 1) Fly/arrive at an airport or land border. 2) Pay JOD 40. 3) If staying >14 days: register with police. Restricted route (53 nationalities): 1) Apply for prior MOI approval weeks ahead (allow generous time). 2) Travel on the approval. 3) Register with police if staying >14 days. Work route: employer-led permit (JOD 300/180).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Restricted nationalities flying in for VOA. 53 nationalities need prior MOI approval — the VOA counter will refuse them.
  • Skipping police registration. Mandatory for stays over 14 days since 1 Oct 2025 — JOD 200 fine risk.
  • Assuming the Allenby crossing is routine. It has its own procedures — verify before planning.
  • Budgeting student fees from blogs. No official 2026 figures verified.
  • Working on a VOA. Employer permit required — JOD 300 first year.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

The MOI e-Services portal (eservices.moi.gov.jo) is the official online channel — e-Visa applications live there. But for 120+ nationalities the VOA counter is the practical route. 'Jordan e-Visa' agencies are not official channels.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in Jordan's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the Ministry of Interior e-Services for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where Jordan requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Declare currency and restricted goods as required. Keep the VOA receipt, return ticket and — for longer stays — the police registration proof with your passport.

Departure and future travel

VOA stays can be extended through the authorities before expiry — but the police registration at 14 days is the more urgent deadline. Overstaying draws fines; the registration system is the one visitors most often miss.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) The MOI for the restricted-nationality list and approval process. 2) eservices.moi.gov.jo for the e-Visa. The two facts that matter most: are you on the 53 list? and will you stay past 14 days?

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per Jordan's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Jordan is generous but gated: JOD 40 VOA for 120+ nationalities, prior MOI approval for 53, and police registration past 14 days (JOD 200 fine risk since October 2025). Check the restricted list first, register if you're staying, and verify student fees with the mission — they're officially unpublished.
