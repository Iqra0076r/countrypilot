How to use this Sudan visa and entry guide

Sudan has no functional central immigration portal and no verified e-visa system in 2026 — visas must be arranged in advance at a Sudanese embassy abroad, and since the April 2023 conflict disrupted Khartoum operations, consular services run through third-country Sudanese missions (the Embassy of Sudan in Ottawa publishes the current entry-visa fee schedule, for example). There is no reliable visa on arrival.

The context is non-negotiable: the US Embassy in Khartoum has been closed since 22 April 2023, the US State Department maintains a Level 4 'Do Not Travel' advisory (reaffirmed September 2026), and US Presidential Proclamation 10998 (effective 1 January 2026) suspended US visa issuance to Sudanese nationals. Processing via third-country missions is unpredictable — allow a wide window and verify everything directly with the mission. Where no verifiable figure exists, this guide says so rather than inventing one.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport should be valid well beyond the trip with blank pages — apply the conservative six-months-beyond-travel standard, since Sudanese missions give no precise published formula and a document problem is exceptionally hard to fix in the current environment.

Carry photocopies and digital backups of the passport and visa stored separately from the originals. Replacement options inside Sudan are effectively non-existent for most nationalities while the conflict continues.

Transit and onward travel

Do not route through Sudan as a transit convenience. With Khartoum airport's status disrupted and no transit-visa framework verifiable, any itinerary touching Sudan should be treated as a full entry requiring a visa arranged in advance — and then weighed against the Level 4 advisory.

In practice, the transit question for Sudan in 2026 is moot for most travellers: the airlines and routes that would make transit relevant are themselves disrupted. Choose routings that avoid Sudan entirely unless you have a compelling, verified reason to go.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

At the Sudan border, expect questions before you get a stamp. The standard set covers purpose of visit, length of stay, accommodation, and means of support — have the onward ticket, hotel booking or host address, and bank evidence ready rather than buried in luggage.

A visa lets you travel to the border; the officer's decision is what lets you in. Short, truthful, consistent answers beat long explanations. If your plans involve anything beyond tourism — work, study, volunteering — say so only if your visa category actually covers it.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Health documents are a separate gate from the visa for Sudan. The key question is certificate-based: does Sudan require proof of yellow-fever vaccination (common if you arrive from an endemic country), and are there any current outbreak-related declarations?

Carry the WHO yellow card (International Certificate of Vaccination) if there is any chance it applies — digital photos are not accepted at most borders. Also confirm your insurance covers the actual medical environment: in countries with limited hospital capacity, evacuation cover matters more than the policy's headline medical limit.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance sits in a grey zone for Sudan: sometimes a formal visa requirement, always a practical one. Where the embassy or portal names a minimum medical coverage, treat that number as the floor and buy a policy that meaningfully exceeds it.

Read the exclusions before you pay: adventure activities, pre-existing conditions and alcohol-related incidents are the three most common claim rejections worldwide. For long stays, check whether the policy covers the full visa duration — a 90-day policy on a 180-day trip leaves a dangerous gap.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

In Sudan, the admission decision at the border may involve more layers than usual — immigration, security screening, and document verification can each add hours. The admitted stay is still set by the officer, not the visa.

Keep the entry stamp or record safe: in disrupted environments, proving legal entry later (for extension, exit, or any dispute) depends on documents you kept yourself, because official systems may not have reliable records.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Keep the three purposes strictly apart for Sudan: visiting, working, studying. Each has its own permission, its own evidence, and its own application channel — a tourist visa or visa-free entry covers none of the other two.

The expensive mistake is assuming intent does not matter: taking a job, enrolling, or freelancing while admitted as a visitor can lead to detention, removal, and multi-year bans. If the plan might involve work or study, secure that status before travelling, not after arrival.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

For families heading to Sudan: every traveller, including infants, needs individual documentation. Where an authorization or e-visa system applies, each person — children included — needs their own approval; there are no family bundles.

A consent letter from any non-travelling parent is cheap insurance against difficult questions at the border. Dual citizens must verify Sudan's rule on which nationality to travel under — entering on a foreign passport while holding Sudan citizenship is an offence in some countries.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

For Sudan, the normal workflow is disrupted: embassies may be closed, portals offline, and published procedures unreliable. 1) Identify which third-country mission currently handles Sudan visas — this changes, so verify it now, not from a 2024 guide. 2) Contact that mission directly before preparing anything; ask what they actually accept today. 3) Build the file around what can be verified: passport, photographs, itinerary, funds evidence.

Do not pay any fee until a functioning official channel confirms the amount and the payment method. In disrupted environments, 'agents' selling guaranteed visas are the most active fraud vector — every fee goes through an official mission or it does not get paid.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming any published procedure still works — in Sudan's disrupted environment, verify every step with the currently operating mission.
  • Paying 'fees' to intermediaries — with official channels disrupted, fraud flourishes; no legitimate fee is paid to a personal account.
  • Planning travel before a visa exists — processing is unpredictable; nothing is booked until the visa is in hand.
  • Ignoring the security advisory — check your own government's current advisory for Sudan; 'do not travel' designations have insurance and legal consequences.
  • Expecting consular help in-country — many embassies in Sudan are closed or operating remotely; your government's ability to assist inside the country may be near zero.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Think of the Sudan application as an argument with exhibits. The argument: 'I am a genuine visitor who will leave on time and can afford the trip.' Every exhibit should support exactly that — and nothing should contradict it.

Common contradictions that sink files: an itinerary showing a luxury trip against bank statements showing a thin balance; employment dates that do not match the leave letter; a stated purpose ('conference') with no invitation or registration. Re-read the whole file as a skeptic before submitting.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Online application for Sudan is unreliable by definition right now: portals launch, go offline, or never worked. Before trusting any site claiming to be the Sudan e-visa portal, verify it is linked from an official government source — a standalone domain with no government linkage is not trustworthy, however professional it looks.

Where no functioning online channel exists, the route is a third-country embassy in person. Confirm which mission, what it accepts, and its current fee by direct contact — and keep every receipt, because disrupted systems lose records.

Translations, certifications and document format

For Sudan, assemble two piles: what the checklist demands, and what proves your story. The second pile — employment letter with approved leave, property or tenancy documents, enrollment confirmation, prior travel history — is where approvals are won.

Photographs deserve more care than they get: follow the portal's dimensions, background and recency rules exactly, because a rejected photo restarts the clock. Where documents need legalization or apostille, start early — that process has its own timeline independent of the visa.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

No biometric enrollment procedure could be verified for Sudan's current disrupted consular network. Ask the mission handling your application directly whether fingerprints or photographs are captured — procedures vary by post and may have changed with the relocation of consular services.

Keep every receipt and document the mission issues: in disrupted systems, your own records are the reliable archive.

Families and group travel

Families should budget per person for Sudan: per-person fees, per-person forms, per-person approvals. Children's applications need the same care as adults' — birth certificates where surnames differ, and consent letters where only one parent travels.

Group applications rise or fall together in practice: if the group's stated purpose is a wedding, every file should reflect the wedding. One member with a contradictory story invites scrutiny of all.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

After the immigration stamp in Sudan comes customs. The two questions that matter: are you carrying more than the declarable cash threshold, and are you carrying anything restricted — food, medicine, electronics, cultural goods?

Cash thresholds are about reporting, not limits: declare honestly and keep walking. Restricted goods are different — undeclared prohibited items can mean fines or confiscation. Read the customs authority's list for Sudan before you pack, not after you land.

Departure and future travel

Plan your exit from Sudan before you enter: routes, border crossings and flight availability can change with little notice. Keep your passport, visa and travel documents on your person, not in checked luggage, and register with your embassy's traveller program if one exists.

Monitor the security situation throughout the stay and leave early if it deteriorates — commercial options disappear fast in a crisis, and evacuation is never guaranteed. A clean, documented, on-time departure matters even more from a conflict zone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a visa on arrival in Sudan?

No reliable visa-on-arrival route could be verified for 2026. Visas must be arranged in advance at a Sudanese embassy abroad — and since Khartoum operations were disrupted, that means a third-country mission. Confirm which mission currently accepts applications before preparing anything.

How much is a Sudan visa in 2026?

The verifiable 2026 figures come from the Embassy of Sudan in Ottawa: CAD 235 for tourism/business entry up to 3 months, CAD 620 for 6 months, CAD 1,125 for one year. No verifiable figures exist for work or student visas — confirm with the mission.

Is it safe to travel to Sudan in 2026?

The US State Department maintains a Level 4 'Do Not Travel' advisory (reaffirmed September 2026), the US Embassy in Khartoum has been closed since April 2023, and consular assistance inside the country is effectively unavailable. Check your own government's current advisory — for most travellers, the answer is not to go.

How long does a Sudan visa take?

Commonly cited as up to 10 working days, but reports via the US channel run 4–6 weeks, and wartime processing is inherently unpredictable. Allow the widest window possible and verify with the mission.

Can Sudanese nationals get US visas in 2026?

US Presidential Proclamation 10998, effective 1 January 2026, fully suspended US visa issuance to Sudanese nationals for all nonimmigrant and immigrant categories, with limited exceptions. Check the current position on travel.state.gov.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Sudan in 2026, the paperwork is the least of it: no functional central portal, no verified e-visa, visas only via third-country missions with unpredictable processing (CAD 235 for up to 3 months at the Ottawa mission, the one verifiable schedule). The binding constraints are the closed US Embassy, the Level 4 advisory, and the suspension of US visas for Sudanese nationals. Verify every step with the operating mission — and for most travellers, the correct decision is to not travel until the situation changes.
