Current official context — checked 5 October 2026

Ireland increased employment-permit remuneration thresholds from 1 March 2026. The published roadmap raised the General Employment Permit threshold to €36,605 and the standard Critical Skills threshold to €40,904, with other occupation-specific thresholds. Critical Skills applications should be submitted at least 12 weeks before the proposed employment start date, and the occupation/remuneration rules must be checked on the live permit page.

Teaching: where sponsorship is most plausible

For teaching roles in Ireland, sponsorship is most plausible where employers have persistent skill needs, the occupation fits an immigration route and the candidate can satisfy professional or technical requirements. Do not infer sponsorship from sector demand alone. Verify the occupation classification, licensing/registration and employer's legal ability to hire from overseas.

Tailor the CV to the country's terminology and make regulated credentials obvious. For healthcare, teaching and some engineering roles, professional registration can be the real bottleneck even when jobs are plentiful. Separate the job search from immigration eligibility

Search for jobs and immigration eligibility in parallel. Occupation eligibility, the Critical Skills list, ineligible occupations and permit salary thresholds need to be checked at application time. Create a one-page route summary before applying: route name, sponsor requirement, occupation rule, salary/skill requirement, language rule, professional registration and whether dependants can accompany.

This prevents a common failure: investing months in a job that cannot support the intended immigration route. How to verify an employer or sponsor

Use official sponsor/accreditation registers where they exist, the company's own careers site, business registry details and a professional email domain. Compare the legal employer name on the offer with the entity in the sponsor register. A recruitment agency may be legitimate, but it should be clear who the actual employer is. For Ireland Teaching Sponsorship Jobs, connect this point to the “How to verify an employer or sponsor” decision and re-check Irish Immigration Service and Department of Enterprise, Tourism and Employment before relying on it.

Red flags include payment for a job offer, requests to reimburse prohibited sponsor costs, interviews conducted only through anonymous messaging apps, contracts with a different company name, and salary promises that are inconsistent with the written offer. Build an overseas-ready application

A useful international CV is evidence-led: role, employer, dates, quantified responsibilities, tools/technologies, regulated credentials and language ability. Offer-stage checks

Before resigning or paying relocation costs, verify salary, hours, probation, location, remote-work expectations, relocation support, visa costs, health insurance and family benefits. Check whether the contract is conditional on immigration approval. A genuine offer can still be unsuitable if the salary fails the immigration threshold, the job title does not match the actual duties, or the employer is not eligible to sponsor. From job offer to lawful work

The employer and applicant may have separate steps. In some systems the employer first obtains accreditation, sponsorship approval, labour-market approval or a job check; only then can the worker apply. In others, the processes are linked. Use https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/ to map the actual sequence.

Do not start work until the status authorises it. Entry as a visitor or student does not automatically permit full-time employment.
