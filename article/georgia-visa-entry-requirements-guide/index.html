How to use this Georgia visa and entry guide

Georgia is one of the world's most open visa regimes wrapped around a simple paid layer: many nationalities enter visa-free for up to a full year, and holders of valid US, UK, Schengen, Canadian, Australian, Japanese, Korean, New Zealand, Israeli, Irish or GCC visas/residence permits get 90 days visa-free in any 180. Everyone else uses the e-Visa at evisa.gov.ge — 30 days per entry, 120-day validity, US$20 plus a 2% service charge. There is no visa on arrival.

Watch the totals: a mandatory third-party DuVerify identity check adds US$15 on top of the consular fee, and D-category (immigration) visas — study, work — also run US$20 through the consular channel. This guide was verified against Georgian MFA documentation and evisa.gov.ge in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport must be valid for the duration of your stay with blank pages. Georgia's famously long visa-free stays (up to a year for exempt nationalities) make this worth checking carefully — a passport expiring mid-stay creates a real problem.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Georgia's openness means most travellers never touch the visa system: check the visa-free list first. The e-Visa exists for the nationalities outside it — including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh.

Transit and onward travel

Georgia's e-Visa covers short stays including transit-style visits, but there is no separate transit-visa category and no visa on arrival — a traveller who needs a visa must hold the e-Visa before boarding. Visa-exempt nationalities transit freely. Do not plan a 'transit without visa' routing through Tbilisi unless your nationality is visa-exempt.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever Georgia's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the Georgian e-Visa portal approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Georgia requires no vaccination certificate for ordinary visitors and operates no border health screening for tourists. No health document is checked at Georgian border control.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is not a Georgian e-Visa condition, but the MFA requires travel insurance covering accidents above 30,000 GEL for D-category (immigration) visas. No official annual student-insurance cost figure is published — budget from private providers against the 30,000 GEL coverage minimum.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Visa-exempt entries can run up to a full year — exceptionally generous, but the permission is for visiting, not working. e-Visa holders get 30 days per entry within the 120-day visa validity. Keep the e-Visa printout and return ticket accessible; border officers may ask for proof of funds and accommodation.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work and study use D-category immigration visas at a US$20 consular fee (Georgian MFA), followed by residence formalities in-country. The MFA requires proof of financial support (letter or 3 months of bank statements) for the D3 study visa but states no minimum amount — do not invent one. A tourist e-Visa or visa-free entry never authorises work.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own Georgia permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether Georgia restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Visa-exempt route: just travel — check the exemption list for your nationality first. e-Visa route: 1) Apply at the official evisa.gov.ge (the only legitimate portal). 2) Pay US$20 + 2% service charge, plus the mandatory US$15 DuVerify identity check. 3) Wait for the decision — reported at 5–7 working days (not an official SLA; allow longer in summer). 4) Travel with the approval. D-category route: apply through the consular channel with the MFA's document list, including the financial-support evidence and 30,000+ GEL insurance.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting the US$15 DuVerify check. It is mandatory and on top of the US$20 consular fee.
  • Assuming visa-free means work-free. The 1-year exemption is for visiting — work needs the D-category route.
  • Trusting e-Visa processing estimates as guarantees. 5–7 working days is reported, not an official SLA.
  • Inventing a proof-of-funds number. The MFA states no minimum for the D3 — provide the statements, not a fabricated threshold.
  • Using unofficial e-Visa sites. Only evisa.gov.ge is official.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

The evisa.gov.ge portal is the only official online channel for short-stay visas. D-category applications run through the consular channel (geoconsul.gov.ge per the MFA). Copycat 'Georgia e-Visa' domains charging multiples of US$20 are not official.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in Georgia's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the Georgian e-Visa portal for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where Georgia requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Georgia's customs allowances follow standard international norms — declare large cash sums and restricted goods. Keep the e-Visa approval and return ticket with your passport, not in checked luggage.

Departure and future travel

e-Visa stays are 30 days per entry within the 120-day validity — the visa is not extendable in the ordinary course, so plan departure accordingly. Visa-free visitors staying toward the 1-year mark should keep evidence of funds and onward plans; long continuous stays can draw questions. D-category holders handle extensions through the in-country residence process.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) evisa.gov.ge and the Georgian MFA's published D-category document lists (mfa.gov.ge) — they override everything. 2) The visa-free country list for exemption questions. Treat e-Visa processing estimates and any 'minimum funds' figure as what they are — unofficial.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per Georgia's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Georgia's system is genuinely traveller-friendly — check the visa-free list first, because you may not need a visa at all. If you do, it's the e-Visa at US$20 plus the US$15 identity check, approved before you fly. For study or work, the D-category route runs on the same US$20 consular fee with real financial evidence and 30,000+ GEL insurance. Verify everything on evisa.gov.ge and mfa.gov.ge.
