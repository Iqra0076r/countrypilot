When professional advice is worth paying for

A straightforward application with clear eligibility may be manageable without a lawyer or adviser. Professional help becomes more valuable when there is a refusal, allegation of misrepresentation, criminal/medical issue, complex family history, status violation, urgent deadline, employer compliance problem or legal interpretation that materially affects eligibility. Refusal, review and appeal are different tools

A refusal does not automatically create a right of appeal. Some systems offer administrative review, reconsideration, tribunal appeal, judicial review or a fresh application; each has different grounds and deadlines. The refusal notice is the starting document. Verify regulation before the consultation, not after paying

The first question is not whether an adviser has a polished website; it is whether the individual or organisation is legally authorised to provide the service being offered. The Immigration Advice Authority publishes information explaining how the public can use its Adviser Finder and Register to check regulated immigration advice providers.

Start with the current IAA information on registered advisers and organisations. Where a solicitor or another regulated legal professional is providing the service, verify them with the relevant professional regulator as well.

Keep your own complete client file

Retain the engagement letter, invoices, advice notes, forms, evidence submitted, application confirmations and official decisions. Do not allow the only copy of your immigration history to sit in somebody else's email account. If a representative changes or a dispute arises, a complete file can save substantial time and cost.

If the issue relates to a Skilled Worker job, verify the employer separately using the UK Sponsor Verification Guide. Regulated advice and employer sponsorship are separate checks.

Use the official adviser finder before paying

The UK government's immigration-adviser service directs users to the Immigration Advice Authority's Adviser Finder and Adviser Register. Check the individual or organisation there before sharing documents or paying a fee, and confirm whether the adviser charges for the service.
