Spain Visa Processing Time 2026: 15-Day Schengen, 1-Month Student Decisions, and the Work-Visa Unknown

Spain decides Schengen visas in 15 calendar days in 2026 and student visas within a legal month — but work authorizations have no officially published timeline at all. Here is what is known, and what is honestly unknowable.

Spain is unusually honest about its timelines: two are published, one is not. Schengen short-stay visas are decided within 15 calendar days (up to 45 maximum under the EU Visa Code) in 2026. Student national visas carry a 1-month legal decision period from submission. Work authorizations have no officially published processing time — the Oficina de Extranjería decides, and timelines vary by province and season.

This guide sets out the two known clocks, how to handle the unknown one, and the consulate-first discipline Spain demands. One figure this guide deliberately omits: no verified official processing time exists for work authorizations, so none is printed — check the consulate instead of trusting a blog's number.

The Schengen clock: 15 calendar days

The Schengen uniform visa (adult €90 since June 2024, unchanged in 2026, plus the BLS service charge; €45 ages 6–11) is decided within 15 calendar days as the standard, with a 45-day maximum under the EU Visa Code for cases needing further examination. Applications are made in person at the consulate or BLS centre — first-time biometrics are required — and Spain offers no eVisa.

Apply at least 2–3 weeks before travel as the working minimum; the BLS appointment slot in peak season can consume much of that margin before the 15-day clock even starts. Consular files can be tracked at sutramiteconsular.maec.es.

The student clock: a legal month

Student national visas (€90, 2026) benefit from unusual clarity: a 1-month legal decision period from the date of submission. That is the administrative deadline the consulate works to — not a median, not a target, but the legal frame.

In practice, straightforward student files often clear faster, but the month is the number to plan from. Submit the complete file — admission letter, funds evidence, insurance, medical certificate where required — because an incomplete submission does not pause the month so much as waste it.

The work clock: honestly unknown

Here is the honest position: no officially published processing time exists for Spanish work authorizations. The employer's authorization is decided by the Oficina de Extranjería in the province of employment, and timelines vary by province, season and workload. The fee structure is published (Modelo 790-052 applicant €203.84; Modelo 790-062 employer €203.84/€407.71 below/at+above 2× SMI; consular visa ~€80; highly qualified UGE 790-038 €73.26 — all 2026), but the timeline is not.

The correct move is procedural, not numerical: get the consulate's current estimate in writing before planning around a start date, and build the plan with wide margin. Any blog quoting a precise 'Spain work visa processing time' is guessing — this guide will not join them.

What slows a Spain file down

  • BLS appointment scarcity. In peak season the appointment wait rivals the 15-day Schengen decision itself.
  • Incomplete student files. The 1-month legal period rewards complete submissions and punishes partial ones.
  • Provincial variance (work). The Oficina de Extranjería's workload differs by province — Madrid and Barcelona in hiring surges are the slow end.
  • Document legalization chains. Criminal-record certificates and qualifications often need apostille and sworn translation — start these before the application window, not during it.
  • Unofficial 'estimates'. exteriores.gob.es and the consulate are the only official channels; no agent can produce a work authorization faster than the Oficina de Extranjería.

When to apply: the planning formula

Schengen: apply 4–6 weeks before travel — the 15-day standard plus BLS slot margin.

Student: submit 8–10 weeks before the semester — inside the 1-month legal period with room for document queries and the consulate's actual pace.

Work: the employer should begin the Oficina de Extranjería authorization 4–6 months before the intended start date, and the consulate's written estimate — not a blog figure — sets the visa-stage planning.

Tracking and the live picture

Consular Schengen and national-visa files are tracked at sutramiteconsular.maec.es. Work-authorization progress sits with the employer and the Oficina de Extranjería — the applicant tracks it through the employer, not a public portal.

Official fees are receipted Modelo 790 forms paid through official channels — never pay a 'visa fee' to a personal account, and never trust a precise work-authorization timeline from an unofficial source.
