How Dubai's visa system works

Dubai's visa system is sponsor-based: almost every visa is issued through a sponsor — an airline, hotel, travel agency, employer, university or family member. For Dubai, GDRFA Dubai (gdrfad.gov.ae) handles visa services and the fee schedule, the federal ICP (icp.gov.ae) covers residence matters, and the official information portal is u.ae. There is no Dubai tourist-visa application at an embassy in the conventional sense — the sponsor applies inside the UAE.

Who needs a pre-arranged visa

Travellers from India, Pakistan, Nigeria and Bangladesh need a pre-arranged visa for Dubai — applied for through an airline, hotel or licensed travel agency, valid for 30 or 90 days, typically issued within ~48 working hours (2026). Around 50+ nationalities — including the US, UK, EU states, Japan and Singapore — receive 30 to 90 days on arrival and need no advance visa.

Tourist visa: the documents you need

Dubai tourist applications need little: a clear passport bio-page copy (valid for at least 6 months from arrival), a compliant photograph, and details that match the passport exactly. Most 'rejections' are data mismatches — re-check the passport number character by character before the sponsor submits.

There are no interviews for Dubai tourist visas. The e-visa arrives as a PDF linked to your passport number, filed through the sponsor. The standard fee is AED 250 for a 30-day single-entry visa, plus 5% VAT (2026 GDRFA schedule), with tiers up to AED 1,740 for a 90-day multiple-entry visa.

Visa-on-arrival rules for Dubai

If your nationality qualifies, visa-on-arrival is the simplest route: entry is granted at the counter, typically 30 days (extendable per current GDRFA rules), and for the ~50+ qualifying nationalities it is free. Under the June 2026 expansion, nationals of Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, the Philippines, Kenya and South Africa who hold valid residence permits of the US, EU, UK, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand or Canada also qualify: 14 days for AED 100 or 60 days for AED 250.

Do not confuse visa-on-arrival eligibility with transit: VoA-eligible nationalities may simply enter; everyone else must arrange the transit or tourist visa in advance — Dubai airport will not issue one at the counter to non-eligible nationalities.

Transit through Dubai

Dubai issues dedicated 48-hour and 96-hour transit visas for stopover passengers — applied for before travel through the airline, single-entry, and not extendable. If your layover is under the transit window and you hold an onward ticket, this is the purpose-built route. Confirm requirements with your airline when booking, since transit arrangements are filed through the carrier.

What officers check at the Dubai border

Dubai immigration may ask for the return or onward ticket, hotel booking or host details, and evidence of funds — particularly for first-time visitors. Keep the e-visa PDF on your phone; it is linked to the passport number, but having the file avoids counter delays. Travellers carrying cash and monetary instruments above AED 100,000 (or equivalent) must declare them.

Note the persistent agency rumour of a $4,000 bank-balance requirement: no UAE government source publishes such a rule — carry normal funds evidence and ignore the claim. Our United Arab Emirates travel safety and security guide covers local laws and customs checks in more depth.

Working, studying or living in Dubai

A tourist visa never authorizes employment — working on a visit visa is an offence for both worker and employer. Employment requires an employer-sponsored work permit and residence visa (5–15 working days processing in 2026). The notable self-sponsored route is the Green Visa (5-year residence): government fee components are AED 200 + AED 10 Knowledge Dirham + AED 10 Innovation Dirham (plus AED 500 if applied for inside the UAE), with total first-year all-in costs typically AED 3,500–6,000 (2026).

For students: the university sponsors the entry permit and residence visa, with all-in costs of roughly AED 3,560–5,560 per year — there is no single officially published 'student visa fee', and health insurance is mandatory (about AED 600–3,000 per year, 2026). Post-arrival, residents complete the medical fitness test and Emirates ID biometrics, usually within a 60-day entry permit.

Passport validity and special documents

Dubai requires the passport to be valid for at least 6 months from arrival, with at least one blank page — airlines enforce this strictly. Each child needs a separate visa linked to their own passport, including infants; minors travelling without both parents should carry parental consent documentation. Dual nationals should enter on the passport carrying the visa or visa-on-arrival eligibility and use it consistently — the e-visa is tied to a specific passport number.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Tourist visa (pre-arranged): 1) Choose a sponsor — airline (Emirates, Etihad, flydubai), hotel, or licensed travel agency. 2) Submit the passport copy, photo and application through the sponsor's portal. 3) Pay the applicable fee (e.g. AED 250 for 30-day single-entry, plus 5% VAT, 2026 GDRFA schedule). 4) Receive the e-visa, typically within ~48 working hours (2026). 5) Verify the details match the passport exactly before flying — a passport-number typo voids the visa at the counter.

Residence (work/student): the employer or university applies for the entry permit, then after arrival you complete the medical fitness test, Emirates ID biometrics and insurance — typically 2–4 weeks for students, 5–15 working days for employment (2026). Common mistakes to avoid

  • Believing the $4,000 balance rumour. No government source publishes it — don't reshape your finances around agency folklore.
  • Working on a tourist visa. An offence for both sides; the work permit route takes only 5–15 working days.
  • Assuming visa-on-arrival eligibility. Check the current list — it expanded in June 2026, but most South Asian and African nationalities still need pre-arranged visas.
  • Letting the entry permit lapse. It is not the residence visa — complete medical + Emirates ID inside its 60 days.
  • Flying before the e-visa details are verified. A passport-number typo voids the visa at the counter.
  • Ignoring the 5% VAT. Quoted GDRFA fees exclude it — budget accordingly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Dubai?

Yes, in most cases. Indian passport holders need a pre-arranged tourist visa (from AED 250 for 30 days single-entry, plus 5% VAT, 2026) applied for through an airline, hotel or agency — typically issued within 48 working hours. There is no visa on arrival for Indian nationals without a qualifying US/EU/UK (etc.) residence permit.

Do Pakistanis need a visa for Dubai?

Yes. Pakistani passport holders need a pre-arranged visa (30 or 90 days per visa type) applied for inside the UAE through an airline, hotel or licensed travel agency. Keep the passport valid for 6+ months from arrival.

Is there visa on arrival in Dubai?

For around 50+ nationalities — including the US, UK, EU states, Japan and Singapore — yes: 30–90 days free on arrival. Since June 2026, six more nationalities (Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, Philippines, Kenya, South Africa) qualify with a qualifying residence permit, paying AED 100 for 14 days or AED 250 for 60 days.

Can I work in Dubai on a tourist visa?

No — working on a tourist visa is an offence for both worker and employer. Employment requires an employer-sponsored work permit and residence visa (5–15 working days, 2026); the self-sponsored Green Visa (5 years) is the main alternative.

What passport validity do I need for Dubai?

Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months from the date of arrival, with at least one blank page. Airlines enforce this strictly on Dubai-bound flights.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Dubai, the route check comes first: confirm visa-on-arrival eligibility on the June-2026-expanded list, or arrange a pre-arranged visa through an airline, hotel or agency (from AED 250 + 5% VAT, ~48-hour issuance, 2026) if your nationality needs one. Keep 6 months' passport validity, carry return-ticket and funds evidence, never work on a tourist visa, and ignore unsourced agency folklore like the $4,000 balance myth. Confirm fees on u.ae or GDRFA — they vary by emirate and channel.
