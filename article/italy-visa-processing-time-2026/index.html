Italy Visa Processing Time 2026: 15-Day Schengen, 90-Day Study Ceilings, and the Flussi Calendar

Italy's Schengen visas clear in 15 calendar days in 2026 — but study visas can legally take up to 90 days, and work visas wait on the Flussi decree's click days. Here is the full Italian timetable.

Italy runs three distinct visa clocks. Schengen short-stay visas are decided within 15 calendar days in 2026 (up to 45 maximum). National Type D study visas carry a legal ceiling of 90 days, though most clear in several weeks. Work visas under the Decreto Flussi wait on a quota calendar: the employer's nulla osta (authorization) takes 60 days for non-seasonal work or 20 days for seasonal, and only then does the consular visa clock start.

Italy offers no eVisa — applications are made in person at the competent consulate with biometrics, and the visa is a sticker. This guide sets out each timeline, the Flussi sequencing that dominates work-visa planning, and the pre-enrolment rule that gates every student file.

The Schengen clock: 15 calendar days

For tourism, business and family visits, the Schengen visa (€90 adult since June 2024, unchanged in 2026; €45 ages 6–12) is decided within 15 calendar days of the application, with a 45-day maximum for cases needing deeper examination. Applications go through the competent consulate in person — first-time biometrics are mandatory — and the visa is a sticker.

Fifteen calendar days is the standard, not the floor: straightforward files at quiet consulates often clear faster. But consulate workload varies enormously across the network, so the 15-day figure is a ceiling to plan from, not a promise of speed.

The Flussi clock: quota calendar first, visa second

Most non-EU work migration to Italy runs through the annual Decreto Flussi quota decree. The sequence is fixed: on the published click days, employers submit for the nulla osta al lavoro (work authorization); the nulla osta takes 60 days for non-seasonal work or 20 days for seasonal work from submission; only then does the worker apply for the consular visa (€116 national work/family/elective/Blue Card fee, 2026), which typically follows in weeks.

The planning implication: the visa timeline is the second timeline. Miss the click-day window and there is no queue to join — no agent can secure a Flussi quota place after the click days close, and none can speed the nulla osta. The employer's click-day readiness is the entire game.

The student clock: 90-day ceiling, pre-enrolment gate

National Type D study visas (€50 consular fee, 2026) carry a 90-day legal decision ceiling, but in practice most clear in several weeks — with wide variation between consulates. Check the specific consulate's stated timeline rather than assuming any national average.

The hard gate: Universitaly pre-enrolment validation is mandatory before the consulate will process the visa. The academic track (pre-enrolment) and the visa track run in parallel, and the visa cannot proceed without the pre-enrolment — files that arrive without it are not processed at all. Start the Universitaly process the moment the admission offer arrives; it is the critical path, not an administrative nicety.

What slows an Italy file down

  • Missing pre-enrolment (students). The single most common student-visa failure — the consulate will not process the file without Universitaly validation.
  • Click-day misses (workers). The Flussi calendar is annual and unforgiving; preparation must be complete before the click days open.
  • Consulate variation. Study-visa timelines differ widely between consulates — planning from the wrong consulate's numbers is planning from fiction.
  • Permesso di soggiorno after arrival. The visa is not the end: the residence permit (€40–100 plus ~€76 extras, 2026) must be applied for within 8 days of arrival — budget the post-arrival step into the timeline.
  • Unofficial channels. vistoperitalia.esteri.it and the consulate are the only official channels; official fees are receipted at the consulate, never paid to a personal account.

When to apply: the planning formula

Schengen: apply 4–6 weeks before travel — the 15-day standard plus margin for consulate workload variance.

Study: complete Universitaly pre-enrolment as early as possible, then apply at the consulate 8–12 weeks before the semester — inside the 90-day ceiling with room for the consulate's actual pace.

Work (Flussi): the employer must be click-day ready with the complete file before the decree's submission window opens; after the nulla osta (60/20 days), allow several more weeks for the consular visa.

Tracking and the live picture

Schengen and national files are tracked through the competent consulate — Italy has no single national visa-tracking portal, so the consulate that took the application is the live source. For Flussi, the employer tracks the nulla osta through the Sportello Unico per l'Immigrazione.

Consulate pages publish their current study-visa timelines: read the specific consulate's page, not a national blog average, before committing to dates.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does an Italy Schengen visa take in 2026?

Within 15 calendar days as the standard, up to 45 days maximum for complex cases. The €90 fee has been unchanged since June 2024.

How long does an Italy student visa take?

The legal ceiling is 90 days, but most take several weeks in practice — with wide variation between consulates. Universitaly pre-enrolment must be validated before the consulate processes the file.

How long does the Flussi work authorization take?

The nulla osta takes 60 days for non-seasonal work or 20 days for seasonal work from the click-day submission; the consular work visa then typically follows in weeks.

Does Italy have an eVisa?

No. Applications are made in person at the competent consulate with biometrics (first time), and the visa is a sticker. Universitaly is the academic portal, not a visa portal.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Fifteen calendar days for Schengen, up to 90 for study (usually weeks), and a 60/20-day nulla osta before the Flussi work visa even starts. Italy rewards calendar discipline: pre-enrol early, hit the click days ready, and read your specific consulate's timelines.
