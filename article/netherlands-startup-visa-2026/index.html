Netherlands Startup Visa 2026: The 1-Year Facilitator Route

By Nadeem Tanwari, Founder of CountryPilotReviewed by CountryPilot editorial deskPublished 15 October 2026 · Updated 15 October 2026

The Dutch startup visa is honest about what it is: twelve months to turn an idea into a company, with a state-recognised mentor watching. No pretence of permanent residence, no capital floor, no renewal of the permit itself. The question it asks you is different from every other founder visa in this series — not "how much do you have?" but "who vouches for you, and what happens on day 366?"

The Dutch residence permit for startups gives non-EU founders a maximum of 1 year to develop an innovative product or service in the Netherlands, working with a facilitator recognised by RVO (the Netherlands Enterprise Agency). RVO assesses the innovation and the facilitator and advises the IND; the IND aims to decide within 90 days. There is no minimum investment — you must show sufficient means to live on — and after the year you transition to a self-employed entrepreneur permit or another route. The essential-personnel scheme for hiring key foreign staff currently runs until 1 June 2028.

One year is the whole product

Every other founder visa in this series sells settlement: Spain gives you three years, Portugal two, Ireland two, the UK's Innovator Founder three. The Netherlands gives you one. The official business.gov.nl guidance states it without apology: the permit is valid for a maximum of 1 year, during which you develop the idea into a viable business — the product, the company, the first steps toward the market.

Understanding this is the difference between using the route correctly and being disappointed by it. The startup year is a development grant made of immigration status, not a residence permit with a startup theme. It cannot be renewed as a startup permit. If after twelve months you have a functioning company, you move to the self-employed entrepreneur permit. If you have a job offer, you move to an employee permit. If you have neither, the route ends. Founders who arrive treating year one as "settled in Amsterdam" and only think about the transition in month eleven are the ones who fail the transition — the government gave you the calendar on day one.

The eligibility gate reflects the same pragmatism. The permit is for people from outside the EU, EEA, or Switzerland who want to start an innovative business — there is no age limit, no language requirement, and no personal net-worth or minimum-business-investment requirement. What the state demands instead is a structure it trusts: an innovative product, a recognised facilitator, and a plan.

The facilitator is a licence condition, not a perk

In Portugal's Startup Visa the incubator judges the project; in the Netherlands the facilitator plays a subtly different role. You must sign a cooperation agreement with a facilitator — an experienced business mentor or organisation — that is recognised by RVO. Without that agreement, the IND form's conditions are simply not met, and the application cannot succeed.

The official conditions go further than "find a mentor." The facilitator must be reliable and expert: expertise is shown by a good track record of service, reliability by financial health. Both you and the facilitator must be registered with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK), with the registration numbers in the application. There must be no family relationship up to the third degree between you and your facilitator — the state wants a professional mentoring relationship, not a cousin's signature.

Choosing the facilitator is therefore the first real decision of the application, and it is worth choosing slowly. Facilitators are private organisations — incubators, accelerators, established companies with startup programmes — and they charge their own fees on their own terms, which vary widely. Interview several. Ask what their RVO track record looks like, what their programme actually provides month by month, and whether founders like you have transitioned successfully to the self-employed permit after the year. A facilitator who cannot answer those questions is selling you a signature, and the IND's form is designed to detect exactly that.

RVO's innovation test: what "new" means to the Dutch state

The IND does not judge your innovation itself. As part of the assessment, it consults RVO, the Netherlands Enterprise Agency, which assesses your step-by-step plan for developing the idea into a company and advises the IND on two questions: is the product or service innovative, and is the facilitator reliable and expert? A positive RVO recommendation is, in practice, the decision.

What counts as innovative is broader than founders expect. The official IND explanation accepts both technological and non-technological innovation: a new production technology qualifies, but so does an unconventional approach to work or organisation, or applying known technology to a market where it has not been used. The test is "new to the Dutch market," not "new to science." A logistics platform proven in Southeast Asia but absent in the Netherlands can pass; a me-too food-delivery app cannot, however slick its pitch deck.

The step-by-step plan is the document RVO actually reads. It should walk from the initial idea to the creation of the company: structure and legal form, employees and roles, the innovative product or service, and the activities for establishing the startup. Write it for a sceptical civil servant, not a pitch audience — RVO's advisers assess viability, not charisma. And you must play a significant role in the company: the IND form is explicit that a passive shareholder or investor does not qualify as a startup entrepreneur.

Sufficient means, without a number

Here the Dutch route is unusual in the opposite direction from Spain. Spain says "no minimum" and means the money is nearly irrelevant; the Netherlands says "sufficient means of support" and means it as a real, examined condition — but publishes no fixed figure for the startup permit. The IND's official guidance says you must have lasting and sufficient means of support at the time of application, and points to the income requirements published on ind.nl rather than naming a number in the startup materials.

Note: do not confuse this with the "sufficient means" figures published for other permits. Because the IND does not publish a startup-specific figure, the safe interpretation is literal: enough to support yourself for the full year without state assistance, plus the startup's runway, in a country with Amsterdam-level living costs. Show bank statements, not projections. And remember the facilitator's fees are private and vary — get the figure in writing from your chosen facilitator before you file, because the IND will expect the money to cover it.

The practical filing path: if you are abroad, you usually apply at the Dutch embassy or consulate in your country of residence. Depending on your nationality you may also need a provisional residence permit (MVV) to enter the Netherlands — the IND's nationality-based information shows whether you do. If no MVV is needed (or you already hold a Dutch permit and are switching), you apply directly with the IND's official startup application form. The IND aims to decide within 90 days.

Day 366: the transition to self-employed — or the exit

The startup permit's own guidance ends with the sentence founders should read first: after the maximum one year, you apply for a residence permit as a self-employed entrepreneur, meeting that permit's requirements. This is the real examination. The self-employed permit has its own points-based assessment (covering personal experience, business plan, and added value for the Dutch economy), and the startup year is meant to give you twelve months to build a file that passes it.

There is a transition scheme that can simplify the move: if your facilitator fills in a declaration, the passage to the self-employed permit can be eased. RVO publishes the scheme and the declaration form — get your facilitator's commitment to this early, ideally before the startup year begins, because a facilitator who disappears in month twelve leaves you facing the full self-employed assessment cold.

If the company has not become viable, the honest alternatives are a different permit type you genuinely qualify for — employee, highly skilled migrant, or another scheme — or departure. What does not exist is a second startup year. Plan the transition from month one: the founders who succeed treat the startup permit as the first chapter of the self-employed application, building evidence (revenue, contracts, KvK history, the facilitator's endorsement) from the first quarter.

Hiring during the year: the essential-personnel scheme

One underused feature of the Dutch system deserves its own section. If your startup needs to hire specific foreign experts essential to its development and growth, there is a dedicated residence scheme for essential personnel of startups. Its headline advantage: your startup does not have to meet the recognised-sponsor requirement that applies to the ordinary highly-skilled-migrant permit — a meaningful simplification for a year-old company that could never qualify as a sponsor.

This scheme is currently stated to run until 1 June 2028, and it is a pilot-style instrument — check its live status on ind.nl before building a hiring plan around it. But its existence tells you something important about Dutch startup policy: the state understands that a one-year permit is useless if you cannot bring the people who make the company work. The facilitator vouches for you; the scheme lets you vouch for the people you need.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is the Netherlands startup visa valid?

A maximum of 1 year — it is deliberately a development year, not a settlement permit. After it, you apply for a residence permit as a self-employed entrepreneur (a transition scheme with your facilitator's declaration can simplify this), or for another permit type if you qualify.

What is a facilitator for the Dutch startup visa?

An experienced business mentor or organisation, recognised by the Netherlands Enterprise Agency (RVO), that supports your startup. The IND requires a signed cooperation agreement with one; the facilitator must be reliable and expert (good track record, financial health), both you and the facilitator must be registered with the Chamber of Commerce (KvK), and there may be no family relationship up to the third degree between you.

Is there a minimum investment for the Netherlands startup visa?

No statutory minimum investment or net worth requirement. You must demonstrate sufficient money to live in the Netherlands and to develop the startup — the IND checks lasting, sufficient means of support but publishes no single figure for this permit; use realistic Amsterdam-level living costs plus the startup's runway.

How does the IND decide if my product is innovative?

The IND consults RVO (the Netherlands Enterprise Agency), which assesses your step-by-step business plan and advises the IND on two things: whether your product or service is innovative (technological or non-technological aspects count), and whether your facilitator is reliable and expert. A positive RVO recommendation is effectively required.

How long does the IND take to decide?

The IND aims to decide within 90 days of a complete application. If you live outside the Netherlands you usually apply at the Dutch embassy or consulate, and depending on your nationality you may also need a provisional residence permit (MVV) to enter.

Can I bring family on the Dutch startup visa?

Yes. Your spouse or (registered) partner and children under 18 can be included as family members of the residence permit holder, with their own right to live in the Netherlands.

Can I hire staff during the startup year?

Yes — startups can use the residence scheme for essential personnel, which lets you hire foreign experts essential to the startup's growth without meeting the recognised-sponsor requirement of the highly-skilled-migrant permit. That scheme currently runs until 1 June 2028.

Bottom Line

The Dutch startup visa is a one-year development grant in immigration form: no capital floor, no age limit, no language test — but a hard one-year clock and a facilitator who must be RVO-recognised, KvK-registered, and genuinely involved. Choose the facilitator like a co-founder, write the RVO plan for a sceptic, budget a full Amsterdam year of living costs, and start building your self-employed-permit file on day one. The permit does not renew; the company you build in twelve months is the renewal.
