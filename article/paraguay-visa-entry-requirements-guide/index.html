How to use this Paraguay visa and entry guide

Paraguay entry rules turn on whether your passport is among the 60+ nationalities that enter visa-free for 90 days, or whether you need a consular visa first.

Start at the official portal — Dirección Nacional de Migraciones (https://www.migraciones.gov.py/) — and treat it as the controlling source over any third-party summary. Visitor entry costs — no fee: visa-free 90 days for 60+ nationalities (2026). This guide was verified against the sources listed below in October 2026. Fees, forms and processing times change — re-check the live official pages before paying anything or booking non-refundable travel.

Passport validity and document condition

Most destinations require a passport valid for at least 6 months beyond your stay, with at least one blank page — confirm the exact rule for Paraguay on the official portal or via Timatic before booking, because airlines enforce it at boarding and a short-validity passport means denied boarding, not a discussion at the gate.

Emergency and temporary passports may face extra scrutiny or be refused outright; confirm acceptance with the embassy before relying on one.

Transit and onward travel

Transit is its own immigration question, separate from visiting Paraguay. Whether you need a transit visa depends on your nationality, the airport, and whether you leave the international transit area — some travellers need a full entry permission even for an airside-only connection.

Check your exact itinerary on the official portal or via Timatic before booking: airlines deny boarding when the transit permission is missing, and 'I wasn't entering the country' is not a defence.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

A visa or visa-free entitlement is permission to travel to the border, not a guarantee of entry — the immigration officer decides admission. Carry the passport containing the visa or approval, a return or onward ticket, the address where you will stay, proof of funds for the trip, and evidence of ties to home such as an employment letter, property documents or university enrollment.

Officers routinely ask how the trip is funded and when you intend to leave. Answer directly and consistently with your application. If someone else funds the trip, carry evidence of that too — but know that most systems assess your ties and means, not a sponsor's assurances.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Entry health rules are usually triggered by where you have recently been, not only by nationality: yellow-fever certificates are the classic example, required when arriving from an endemic country. Check the current health-entry requirements for Paraguay on the official portal close to departure, since these change with outbreaks.

No separate pandemic-era entry regime is assumed here — verify the live rules rather than relying on pre-2024 guides, which are now widely stale.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

No verified annual student-insurance figure could be confirmed for Paraguay for 2026 — costs vary widely by provider and coverage, so collect quotes from two or three insurers rather than trusting a single blog figure.

Even where insurance is not an entry condition, travelling without medical cover is a financial risk: a single hospital admission abroad can cost more than years of premiums. Buy a policy with high medical limits and emergency evacuation before departure, and check whether your destination's visa or residence-permit process mandates a specific minimum coverage.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

The immigration officer sets your permitted stay at entry — the visa's validity and the admitted stay are different things. A multiple-entry visa valid for a year does not mean a year-long stay; each admission is decided separately and often stamped or recorded electronically.

Check the admission stamp or electronic record immediately and respect the date on it. Overstaying — even briefly — can void the visa, trigger fines, and complicate every future application to Paraguay and beyond.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work routes run through employer-sponsored permits, not visitor entry — the work-permit/visa fee is PYG Gs. 2,577,275 (2023). Never work on a visitor entry.

Study requires a student visa (PYG Gs. 2,577,275, 2023) and enrollment at a recognized institution.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Each child needs their own passport and, where visas apply, their own application — there is no family visa or family e-Visa. Check the embassy page for current interview or appearance requirements for minors, as these change.

Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s) with contact details plus the child's birth certificate (especially when surnames differ). Dual nationals should check which passport Paraguay expects them to use — some systems require entry on a specific nationality's document.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

1) Confirm your nationality's route in the table above — eVisa/eTA, visa on arrival, visa-free, or consular visa.

2) Start at the official portal (Dirección Nacional de Migraciones, https://www.migraciones.gov.py/) — never a third-party 'visa service' site.

3) Budget the official fee: visitor entry — no fee: visa-free 90 days for 60+ nationalities (2026) — pay only through official channels.

4) Allow the full processing window: on arrival (visa-free) (2026) — apply well before any non-refundable bookings.

5) Gather supporting documents (passport, photos, funds evidence, itinerary, ties to home) and keep originals plus one copy.

6) After approval, verify the visa details (dates, entries, name spelling) immediately — errors are far easier to fix before travel.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Trusting a third-party 'official' visa site. Start at the government portal or embassy — copycat sites charge markups or harvest data.
  • Reading visa validity as permitted stay. A multi-year multiple-entry visa does not mean a multi-year stay; each admission is decided separately.
  • Booking non-refundable travel before the visa is issued — especially where no official processing time is published.
  • Assuming a friend's passport means the same rules for you. Entry regimes differ sharply by nationality; use the table above.
  • Letting the passport slip under the validity threshold — airlines deny boarding, and there is no appeal at the gate.
  • Working on a visitor entry 'because the employer is abroad.' Physical presence while working is what counts.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Most visitor-visa refusals come down to one concern: the officer was not convinced the applicant would return home and could fund the trip. A strong file answers both directly — stable employment with a leave-approval letter, property or lease documents, family ties, prior compliant travel history, and bank statements showing you can fund the trip without working in Paraguay.

Keep the story consistent across the form, the interview answers and the documents. Vague itineraries read as risk; a dated plan with bookings and a return ticket reads as a genuine visit. Previous refusals must be declared honestly — misrepresentation findings are far harder to overcome than a straightforward refusal.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Start every online step at the official portal (https://www.migraciones.gov.py/). Bookmark it; phishing sites mimic government portals closely, down to copied logos and layout.

Scam avoidance: no one can guarantee visa approval or sell an earlier appointment slot — anyone promising either is running a scam. Never pay a 'visa fee' to a personal bank account, mobile-money number or crypto wallet; official fees go through the portal's or embassy's designated channels only. Track the case on the official system, not through an agent's screenshots.

Translations, certifications and document format

Bring originals plus one copy of everything: passport, photographs meeting the specification, bank statements (3–6 months), employment letter with approved leave dates, property or lease documents, prior visas and entry stamps, and the trip itinerary with bookings.

Documents in another language should be accompanied by a translation with a translator's signed statement of accuracy. Photograph specifications vary by mission — follow the exact size and background stated on the official portal or embassy page, since automated systems reject non-compliant photos.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Many missions interview applicants in person and capture fingerprints and a digital photograph (biometrics) at the appointment. Expect questions on the purpose of the trip, employment, family, prior travel and funding — answered briefly and truthfully, matching the application form exactly.

At the port of entry, officers may take biometrics again and can inspect devices under border-search authority — travel with a device you are comfortable having examined. Secondary inspection is not an accusation; remain calm and answer directly. Confirm the current interview and biometric requirements for Paraguay on the official portal, as these change.

Families and group travel

Every traveller needs their own authorization: each family member requires a separate application and, where interviews apply, their own appointment — including infants. There is no family visa or family e-Visa.

If one member is refused, the others' applications are unaffected — but do not attempt to 'cover' a refused traveller by rewording the same weak facts; address the refusal reason first. Groups should book appointments as close together as the system allows.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Most countries require cash declarations above a threshold (commonly the equivalent of USD 10,000) — the cash is not taxed or confiscated, but failure to declare can lead to seizure. There is usually no limit on how much you may bring, only a reporting duty.

Agricultural and food-import rules are often strict: declare all food, plants and animal products. Medications should be in original packaging with a prescription or doctor's letter for controlled substances. Check the current allowances on the official portal before packing.

Departure and future travel

Keep the entry record from every trip — a clean history of entries and timely departures strengthens future applications, while an overstay can void the visa and trigger re-entry bars. If you need more time than admitted, seek an extension before the permitted stay expires; do not simply remain.

A valid visa in an expired passport often remains usable: travel with both the old passport (containing the visa) and the new valid passport, provided personal details are unchanged — confirm this rule for Paraguay before relying on it. Report lost or stolen visas to the issuing mission promptly.

How to research a route without getting misled

Use this hierarchy: 1) the official portal (Dirección Nacional de Migraciones, https://www.migraciones.gov.py/) — it overrides everything else. 2) The specific embassy or consulate website for local procedures. 3) Timatic (via airline sites) for boarding requirements.

Treat forums, visa agents' blogs and social-media anecdotes as leads, not answers — experiences vary wildly by post and officer. Any site selling 'guaranteed approval' or charging far above official fees is monetizing your anxiety, not your application.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid well beyond the stay, with a blank page
  • Visa/e-Visa/Travel Authorization approved and verified — check status before departure
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation address accessible offline
  • Funds evidence and ties-to-home documents packed (not only on a phone)
  • Travel insurance policy with adequate medical coverage
  • Medications in original packaging with prescriptions
  • Embassy/consulate contact saved for emergencies
  • Visa details checked (dates, entries, name spelling) immediately after issuance
