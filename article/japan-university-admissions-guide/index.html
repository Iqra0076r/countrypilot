Current official context — checked 5 October 2026

'Student' is a specific Japanese status of residence. Paid activity outside that status generally requires permission for activities outside the permitted status and is subject to limits/conditions; students should verify the current Immigration Services Agency rule before working.

Official page: https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/16-1

Understand the education system before choosing a university

Start with the qualification structure in Japan: degree length, academic calendar, credit system, taught/research distinction and whether the programme leads to professional registration. Study in Japan/JASSO and institutions are useful starting points. Rankings can help with broad reputation, but programme accreditation, curriculum, graduate outcomes and location often matter more.

For regulated fields, check the professional body before applying. A university may be recognised while a specific qualification still does not grant the professional outcome the student expects. Admission requirements and realistic shortlisting

Build a three-tier shortlist: ambitious, realistic and safer options. For each programme, record academic prerequisites, language score, prerequisite courses, portfolio/research requirements, application fee and deadline. Compare the exact programme page, not only the university's general admissions page. Intakes and application timeline

Map the intake backwards. A practical sequence is: 10–14 months out, research programmes and funding; 8–12 months out, tests and documents; 6–10 months out, submit university and scholarship applications; after admission, arrange finances, housing and immigration. The exact dates vary by institution. For Japan University Admissions Guide, connect this point to the “Intakes and application timeline” decision and re-check Immigration Services Agency of Japan / Ministry of Foreign Affairs before relying on it.

Do not assume every programme has both autumn and spring entry. Competitive programmes and scholarships may have one annual intake. Tuition, living costs and funding

For Japan, build a first-year budget before accepting an offer. Include tuition, compulsory fees, visa/residence charges, insurance, housing deposit, furniture or setup costs, transport and emergency funds. Then map confirmed funding sources against that budget.

Scholarships should be verified on the official provider page. If loans are used, model currency movement and repayment rather than looking only at the monthly payment. Admission is not immigration permission

After receiving an offer, review Student status of residence and visa requirements on https://www.mofa.go.jp/j_info/visit/visa/long/visa6.html. Immigration authorities may ask for financial evidence, health insurance, language evidence or proof that the course and institution are eligible.

Keep the university and visa timelines aligned. A late admission decision can compress the visa window; a visa delay can affect enrolment. Know the institution's latest-arrival and deferral policy. For readers using Japan University Admissions Guide, this is a “Admission is not immigration permission” issue rather than a stand-alone rule; verify it against Immigration Services Agency of Japan / Ministry of Foreign Affairs before committing money or documents.

After admission: the practical handover

Confirm acceptance, deposits, accommodation, visa documents, travel dates, orientation and registration. Keep digital and paper copies of key documents in separate places.
