How to use this Japan visa and entry guide

For work or study, the Certificate of Eligibility (CoE) comes first -- issued by the Immigration Services Agency via your employer or school; no CoE, no visa. Visas are issued abroad by MOFA. Since 1 July 2026, issuance fees rose fivefold (first change since 1978) and transit visas were abolished.

Japan's visa system has a feature most countries don't: the Certificate of Eligibility (CoE).

For work and study, the CoE — issued by the Immigration Services Agency inside Japan, usually via your employer or school — comes before the visa.

No CoE, no visa; the consulate cannot issue one without it.

Understanding this sequence (CoE first, visa second) prevents the most common Japan planning failure.

The authorities are MOFA (Ministry of Foreign Affairs, mofa.go.jp/j_info/visit/visa — visas issued abroad) and the Immigration Services Agency (isa.go.jp, under the Ministry of Justice — CoE and residence matters).

This guide was verified against MOFA consulate notices in October 2026.

The defining 2026 event: visa issuance fees rose fivefold on 1 July 2026 — the first revision since 1978 — and transit visas were abolished the same day.

Passport validity and document condition

Japanese immigration requires your passport to be valid for your entire stay -- there is no six-month immigration rule. But consulates commonly ask for six months' validity plus two blank pages when you apply for the visa itself, a stricter application expectation than the border rule. Check with your airline too if expiry is near.

Japanese immigration requires the passport to be valid for the entire duration of your stay — there is no 6-month-validity immigration rule, though some airlines impose their own boarding requirements, so check with your carrier if expiry is near.

For visa applications, consulates commonly ask for 6 months' validity at the time of application plus 2 blank pages — a practical application rule, not the immigration entry rule. Keep the distinction clear: the CoE and visa process has stricter document expectations than the border itself.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Work the routes in order: visa-exempt nationalities (US, UK, EU states, Singapore) enter visa-free for 90 days; then the JAPAN eVISA (since 15 May 2026) for nationals of Australia, Canada, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, Taiwan, the UK and the USA; then the consular visa -- single-entry JPY 15,000 or multiple-entry JPY 30,000 since 1 July 2026, charged only on issuance.

First, visa exemption: many nationalities — including the US, UK, EU states and Singapore — enter visa-free for short stays (typically 90 days).

Second, the JAPAN eVISA: since 15 May 2026, MOFA offers direct online application for nationals/residents of Australia, Brazil, Cambodia, Canada, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, Taiwan, the UK and the USA, plus an accredited-agency route for China, the Philippines (packaged tours), Vietnam (packaged tours), Hong Kong, India, Indonesia, Macau, Mongolia, South Korea, Singapore and the UAE — tourism, single-entry, air/sea entry only.

Third, the consular visa: single-entry JPY 15,000 / multiple-entry JPY 30,000 from 1 July 2026 (the San Francisco consulate officially states USD 101 / USD 201), charged only when the visa is issued.

Fourth, work/student: CoE first (free), then the visa at the single-entry issuance fee.

Transit and onward travel

Japan abolished transit visas on 1 July 2026 -- the category no longer exists, so transit passengers who need a visa must apply for a short-term stay single-entry visa instead, available via the eVISA route where eligible. Visa-exempt nationalities transiting airside generally need nothing beyond their onward ticket, but confirm with the airline.

Japan abolished transit visas on 1 July 2026. Transit passengers who need a visa must now apply for a short-term stay single-entry visa instead — available via the eVISA route where eligible. Do not search for a 'Japan transit visa' in 2026 guides; the category no longer exists.

Visa-exempt nationalities transiting airside without entering Japan generally need nothing beyond their onward ticket, but confirm with the airline — carrier document checks, not immigration, are the usual friction point at Tokyo's airports.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Japanese immigration officers may ask for the return or onward ticket, accommodation details and evidence of funds. For visa holders, the visa itself plus the CoE reference where applicable should be available.

First-time work and student entrants receive the residence card (zairyū card) at major airports — keep the CoE copy accessible until the card is issued, as the process keys off it.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Japan requires no vaccination certificate for ordinary visitors and operates no pandemic-era border health checks. No health document is presented at the Japanese border.

Long-term residents enter the health system after arrival through mandatory National Health Insurance enrollment (below) — a residence step, not a border document.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is not a Japanese visa or entry condition. For residents staying 3+ months, however, National Health Insurance enrollment is mandatory: students typically pay about JPY 1,500–2,500 per month (roughly JPY 18,000–30,000 per year, 2026), which covers 70% of medical costs.

Short-term visitors should still carry travel cover — Japan's medical costs for the uninsured are high, and the 70%-coverage NHI safety net only applies once you're a registered resident.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Visa-exempt entries are typically granted 90 days ('temporary visitor' status), set by the immigration officer at entry. Visa holders are admitted for the period stated on the visa. Temporary-visitor status cannot be converted to work or student status inside Japan — the CoE route starts from outside.

Overstaying is treated seriously and destroys future eligibility. If plans change, the correct move is to leave and re-enter on the proper status, not to remain.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A tourist entry — visa-free, eVISA or temporary-visitor visa — never authorizes work.

Japan's work system runs on the Certificate of Eligibility: the employer (or its immigration lawyer) obtains the CoE from the Immigration Services Agency in Japan — typically 1–3 months — and the worker then applies for the visa at the consulate, paying the JPY 15,000 single-entry issuance fee (from 1 July 2026).

The CoE itself is free.

Students follow the same sequence: the school obtains the CoE (1–3 months), the student applies at the embassy (visa ~5–7 working days after CoE receipt, 2026), and the issuance fee is JPY 15,000.

There is no fixed government minimum for student proof of funds, but immigration and schools consistently reference around JPY 2,000,000 per year (about JPY 2.5 million for Tokyo and major cities) covering tuition plus living expenses, evidenced with 3–6 months of bank statements.

Entering as a tourist to 'look for work' or to wait out a CoE is not a recognized route.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Each child needs a separate passport and, where applicable, a separate visa or eVISA application — including infants. Minors travelling without both parents should carry parental consent documentation.

Japan does not recognize dual nationality for adults in principle — long-term planners should understand this nationality-law position separately from the visa process. Dual-national minors should enter on the passport matching their visa or exemption status.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Tourist (visa-required): 1) Check whether your nationality qualifies for the JAPAN eVISA (direct or agency route, from 15 May 2026). 2) Apply online or at the consulate with the standard documents. 3) Standard embassy examination is about 5 working days (5–10; longer at peak, 2026). 4) Pay JPY 15,000 single-entry / JPY 30,000 multiple-entry (from 1 July 2026) — only on issuance.

Work/student (CoE route): 1) The employer or school applies for the CoE with the Immigration Services Agency in Japan — allow 1–3 months. 2) Receive the CoE (electronic or original as instructed). 3) Apply for the visa at the consulate with the CoE. 4) Visa issuance takes about 5 working days after submission (5–7 for students, 2026). 5) Pay the JPY 15,000 issuance fee. 6) On arrival at a major airport, receive the residence card.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Applying for the visa before the CoE exists. The consulate cannot issue a work/student visa without it — the CoE comes first, always.
  • Budgeting the old fees. Fees quintupled on 1 July 2026 (¥3,000→¥15,000 single-entry) — pre-July guides are dangerously stale.
  • Looking for a transit visa. Abolished 1 July 2026 — apply for the short-term single-entry visa instead.
  • Entering as a tourist to job-hunt or convert status. Temporary-visitor status cannot convert inside Japan.
  • Assuming USD equivalents. Only the consulate-stated USD 101/201 figures are official — ignore media conversions.
  • Forgetting NHI enrollment. Mandatory for 3+ month residents; the 70% coverage only starts once enrolled.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

For tourist visas, Japanese consulates look for a credible itinerary, stable finances and clear ties home — the standard trio, evidenced with bookings and genuine bank statements.

For CoE applications, quality is the sponsor's filing in Japan: complete employment or enrollment documentation submitted to the Immigration Services Agency without gaps, since a returned CoE application restarts the 1–3-month clock.

Students: the ~JPY 2,000,000/year funds figure is a practical benchmark, not a published threshold — present coherent finances (tuition + living costs covered) rather than a last-minute lump deposit.

Standard embassy examination for tourist visas is about 5 working days (5–10; longer at peak, 2026).

The CoE — the real timeline driver for work and study — takes 1–3 months at the Immigration Services Agency; the consular visa after CoE receipt takes about 5 working days (5–7 for students, 2026).

Start the CoE process the moment the job offer or admission is confirmed; everything else waits on it.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

The JAPAN eVISA (mofa.go.jp/j_info/visit/visa/visaonline.html) is the genuine online route — direct application for the listed nationalities since 15 May 2026, accredited-agency route for the others. It covers tourism, single-entry, air/sea entry only.

Scam avoidance: use only the MOFA portal or accredited agencies — 'Japan eVisa' sites charging large service fees for a free or cheap application are markups. No one can expedite a CoE; the Immigration Services Agency works to its own 1–3-month timeline regardless of who asks.

Translations, certifications and document format

Tourist applications: passport, compliant photograph, application form, itinerary, and funds evidence. CoE applications (filed in Japan by the sponsor): employment contract or admission letter, company/school documentation, and the applicant's credentials — requirements vary by status of residence, so follow the Immigration Services Agency checklist for the specific category.

Documents not in Japanese or English should carry translations. Photographs must meet the consulate's specifications; the CoE process is document-heavy, so completeness at first submission saves months.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Japanese visa applications do not routinely involve interviews — the process is document-driven, at the consulate for tourists and at the Immigration Services Agency for CoE cases. Biometrics (photograph, fingerprints) are captured at entry.

At the border, officers may ask about purpose, accommodation and funds. First-time work/student entrants should have the CoE copy available until the residence card is issued at the airport.

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs a separate visa or eVISA application — including infants — at the per-person issuance fee (JPY 15,000 single-entry from 1 July 2026). Family members of work/student residents apply for 'dependent' status with their own CoE sequence.

Group tourist applications are routine; a refusal for one member doesn't affect the others, but address the reason before re-applying.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Travellers must declare cash above JPY 1,000,000 (or equivalent) on entry. Japan is strict on medications: many common prescription drugs (including some ADHD medications and anything containing stimulants) are prohibited or require prior permission — check the Ministry of Health guidance before packing.

Standard restrictions apply to animal and plant products. Duty-free allowances apply per adult traveller.

Departure and future travel

Temporary-visitor status cannot be extended casually or converted — depart on time, as overstaying destroys future eligibility across all categories. Keep the residence card valid once issued; it doubles as the re-entry permit for mid-term travel.

A clean record of entries and timely departures is the strongest asset for future applications, including any future CoE.

How to research a route without getting misled

Use this hierarchy: 1) mofa.go.jp/j_info/visit/visa for visas issued abroad, including the eVISA country lists. 2) The consulate serving your jurisdiction for local fees (note the officially stated USD 101/201) and submission rules. 3) isa.go.jp for CoE and residence matters. 4) Your employer or school — the CoE sponsor — for the practical sequence.

Ignore any guide quoting the old ¥3,000/¥6,000 fees or describing a transit visa — both died on 1 July 2026. Ignore USD conversions beyond the consulate's official figures.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid for the entire stay (check airline requirements if near expiry)
  • Visa, eVISA approval or exemption eligibility confirmed
  • CoE in hand before any work/student visa application (1–3 month lead time)
  • Issuance fee budgeted at 2026 rates (JPY 15,000/30,000)
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation proof accessible offline
  • Funds evidence (~JPY 2,000,000/yr benchmark for students)
  • NHI enrollment planned for 3+ month stays
  • Medications checked against Japan's prohibited list
  • Cash >JPY 1,000,000 declaration readiness
  • Consulate/embassy contact saved for emergencies

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Japan?

Yes, unless another exemption applies. Indian nationals can use the JAPAN eVISA via an accredited agency (tourism, single-entry, air/sea entry) or apply for a consular visa — issuance fee JPY 15,000 single-entry from 1 July 2026, charged only when the visa is issued. Standard embassy examination takes about 5 working days.

What is the Certificate of Eligibility (CoE)?

The document that unlocks Japanese work and student visas: applied for inside Japan by your employer or school with the Immigration Services Agency, taking 1–3 months. The CoE itself is free. Only after receiving it can you apply for the visa at the consulate (JPY 15,000 issuance fee, ~5 working days). No CoE, no visa.

Did Japan really abolish transit visas?

Yes — from 1 July 2026, per the official consulate fee notice. Transit passengers who need a visa now apply for a short-term stay single-entry visa instead (available via eVISA where eligible). Pre-July 2026 guides describing a transit visa are obsolete.

How much are Japan visa fees in 2026?

JPY 15,000 single-entry / JPY 30,000 multiple-entry from 1 July 2026 — a fivefold increase and the first revision since 1978. The San Francisco consulate officially states USD 101 / USD 201. Fees are charged only when the visa is issued. Ignore other USD conversions; they are not official.

Can I convert a Japan tourist visa into a work visa?

No. Temporary-visitor status cannot be converted to work or student status inside Japan. The work/student route requires a Certificate of Eligibility obtained in Japan first, then a visa application at a consulate — the sequence starts outside the tourist entry.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Japan, the sequence is everything: CoE first (free, 1–3 months via the Immigration Services Agency), visa second (JPY 15,000 on issuance from 1 July 2026).

Tourists use the visa exemption, the JAPAN eVISA (direct since 15 May 2026 for listed nationalities), or the consular visa — and transit visas no longer exist.

Keep the passport valid for the stay, budget ~JPY 2,000,000/year in demonstrable funds for study, enroll in National Health Insurance for 3+ month stays, and verify everything on mofa.go.jp — 2026 rewrote the fee and transit rules.
