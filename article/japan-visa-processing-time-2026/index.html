Japan Visa Processing Time 2026: 5-Day Tourist Visas and the 1–3 Month CoE Clock

Japan's tourist visa takes about 5 working days in 2026 — but work and study visas wait on the Certificate of Eligibility, a 1–3 month process in Japan. Here is how both clocks work, plus the JAPAN eVISA route.

Japan's visa timeline has two layers. The consular visa itself — the sticker or eVISA issued by the embassy — is fast: about 5 working days for tourist visas in 2026. But work and study applicants first need a Certificate of Eligibility (CoE) issued in Japan by the Immigration Services Agency, and that takes 1–3 months — it is the real timeline driver, and no one can expedite it.

This guide sets out the 2026 timelines for tourist, eVISA, CoE and post-CoE consular stages, the July 2026 fee changes, and why the CoE process must start the moment a job offer or admission is confirmed.

The tourist clock: ~5 working days

For tourism, the Japanese embassy or consulate examines the application in about 5 working days in 2026 — the 5–10 day range covers peak periods when consular sections process in volume. Applications need the passport, a compliant photograph, the application form, the itinerary and funds evidence.

From 1 July 2026, issuance fees changed: single-entry JPY 15,000 (consulates state USD 101), multiple-entry JPY 30,000 (USD 201) — charged only on issuance, so a refused application does not cost the fee. The JAPAN eVISA (mofa.go.jp/j_info/visit/visa/visaonline.html) covers tourism, single-entry, air/sea entry only — with direct applications for listed nationalities since 15 May 2026 and an accredited-agency route for the others.

The CoE clock: 1–3 months and non-negotiable

The Certificate of Eligibility is the document that makes work and study visas possible — and it is issued in Japan, not at the embassy. The sponsor (employer or school) files with the Immigration Services Agency, which takes 1–3 months to examine the file. The process is document-heavy: employment contract or admission letter, company/school documentation, and the applicant's credentials, with requirements varying by status of residence.

After the CoE is issued, the consular visa takes about 5 working days (5–7 for students). The arithmetic that matters: CoE (1–3 months) + consular visa (~1 week). Start the CoE process the moment the job offer or admission is confirmed — everything else waits on it, and completeness at first submission saves months because re-examination restarts the queue.

What slows a Japan file down

  • Late CoE starts. Every week of delay before the sponsor files is a week added to arrival — the 1–3 month CoE clock is the dominant variable for work and study.
  • Incomplete CoE documents. The CoE process is document-heavy; an incomplete first submission does not get a quick fix, it gets re-queued.
  • Peak-season consular volume. Cherry-blossom and autumn-foliage seasons stretch the 5-day tourist norm toward 10.
  • Translation gaps. Documents not in Japanese or English should carry translations — the CoE examiner will not guess.
  • eVISA copycats. Use only the MOFA portal or accredited agencies — 'Japan eVisa' sites charging large service fees for a cheap application are markups, and no one can expedite a CoE regardless of who asks.

When to apply: the planning formula

Tourist / eVISA: apply 3–4 weeks before travel — the 5-day examination leaves margin for peak-season stretch and one document query.

Work / study: the sponsor should file the CoE 4–6 months before the intended start date. With a 1–3 month CoE range plus the consular week, planning from the 3-month end is the only safe arithmetic — then add the consulate's 5–7 days after the CoE arrives.

Tracking and the live picture

CoE progress is tracked through the sponsor in Japan — the applicant cannot query the Immigration Services Agency directly. Consular applications are tracked through the embassy or consulate that took the file; eVISA applications through the MOFA portal account.

Keep the distinction clear throughout: the CoE and the visa are separate processes with separate clocks, and the border examination is a third, separate step. The MOFA visa pages are the live source for current requirements.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a Japan tourist visa take in 2026?

About 5 working days at the embassy (5–10 in peak periods). The JAPAN eVISA runs on a similar timeline for eligible nationalities and entry modes.

How long does the Certificate of Eligibility take?

1–3 months at the Immigration Services Agency in Japan. It is the timeline driver for all work and study visas — the consular visa afterwards takes only about 5 working days.

What are the Japan visa fees in 2026?

From 1 July 2026: single-entry JPY 15,000 (consulates state USD 101), multiple-entry JPY 30,000 (USD 201) — charged only when the visa is issued. The CoE itself is free.

Can anyone expedite my CoE?

No. The Immigration Services Agency works to its own 1–3-month timeline regardless of who asks — any service promising faster CoE issuance is misrepresenting the process.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Five working days for the tourist visa, 1–3 months for the Certificate of Eligibility that unlocks work and study — Japan's timeline is really the CoE's timeline. Start the CoE the day the offer or admission lands, file it complete the first time, and use only the MOFA portal or accredited agencies.
