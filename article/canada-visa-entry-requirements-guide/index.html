How to use this Canada visa and entry guide

Canada runs two parallel doors. Visa-exempt travellers flying to Canada — including British citizens — need an eTA (Electronic Travel Authorization): CAD $7, applied for on canada.ca, usually approved within minutes. Visa-required travellers — including nationals of India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh — need a visitor visa (Temporary Resident Visa, TRV): CAD $100 per person, applied for online through an IRCC account, plus biometrics (CAD $85) at a Visa Application Centre. US citizens are visa-exempt and do not need an eTA at all.

The controlling source is Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) on canada.ca: its fee list, the 'check processing times' tool, and the eTA pages. This guide was verified against IRCC publications in October 2026. Processing times are published as service standards but actuals move weekly — always check the live tool before planning around a date.

Passport validity and document condition

For Canada, your passport must be valid for the duration of your proposed travel — IRCC's application kit states that a visa's validity cannot exceed the passport's validity, and UK government travel advice confirms the length-of-stay rule. Canada does not impose a six-month buffer in its official guidance, but leaving no margin is unwise: renew well before expiry so the full multi-year validity of a multiple-entry TRV is usable.

Also note the eTA is electronically linked to a specific passport — renew the passport and you must apply for a new eTA (CAD $7). Airlines verify eTA/TRV status before boarding; a traveller whose authorization is tied to an old passport number will be denied boarding.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Determine your lane first, because the two are not interchangeable: an eTA cannot substitute for a visitor visa, and a visitor visa holder does not need an eTA. Lane 1 — eTA (CAD $7, 2026): visa-exempt foreign nationals flying to Canada (including the UK). Apply on canada.ca; most approvals arrive within minutes, though some take days. Lane 2 — visitor visa/TRV (CAD $100, 2026; family cap CAD $500 for 5+ applying together): nationals of visa-required countries, regardless of whether they arrive by air, land or sea. Lane 3 — neither: US citizens (valid US passport suffices) and Canadian citizens/PRs.

Travellers from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh fall in Lane 2 in essentially all cases. A valid US nonimmigrant visa or recent compliant travel to Canada can simplify the TRV file under IRCC's CAN+ facilitation (reduced financial proof) — declare it in the application.

Transit and onward travel

Canada's transit rules follow the same two lanes. Visa-required nationals who transit through Canada — even without leaving the airport — need a transit visa (free of charge, applied for like a TRV) unless they qualify for the China/Mexico transit programs on specific routings. Visa-exempt nationals flying through Canada need an eTA.

The common mistake is assuming an airside connection is not 'entering' Canada: for visa-required passports it is. If your itinerary connects through Toronto or Vancouver, secure the transit permission with the same lead time as a visitor visa — it is processed through the same system.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) officer decides each admission. Carry: the passport containing the TRV counterfoil (or the passport linked to your eTA), a return or onward ticket, proof of funds for the stay, and evidence of ties to home. Business visitors should add a letter of support from the parent company, a letter of invitation from the Canadian host, and 24-hour contact details for the host (IRCC's business-visitor checklist).

IRCC's TRV kit expects three months of bank statements (waived under CAN+ for travellers with recent Canada travel or a valid US visa). Officers can ask for anything in the file to be produced at the port of entry — keep the application documents accessible, not just the approval.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Canada sets no general vaccination requirement for short-term visitors and no health document is examined at the border for an ordinary TRV/eTA trip. Medical examinations enter the picture only for stays longer than 6 months or for applicants who have lived in designated countries — in which case IRCC instructs you to use a panel physician, and the results go directly to IRCC, not into your luggage.

Do not pay a clinic for a 'Canada entry medical' for a two-week holiday — it is not a requirement. If IRCC requests an exam for your application, the request letter names the process; anything else is a misunderstanding or a scam.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel health insurance is not a Canadian entry or visitor-visa condition. It is strongly advisable anyway: visitors are billed for hospital care, and Canada's costs are high. There is no mandatory federal visitor plan.

The insurance picture changes with longer stays. International students typically buy a provincial or university plan — for example, Ontario's mandatory UHIP at 22 universities costs CAD $948/year for the September 2026–August 2027 plan year (about a 20% rise), while private options run roughly CAD $700–$1,320/year depending on province and plan (2026 figures). New permanent residents face provincial waiting periods (up to 3 months in some provinces) and need interim cover.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Most visitors are admitted for up to 6 months. The CBSA officer may stamp a date in the passport or issue a visitor record specifying a shorter period — that date, not the TRV's multi-year validity, is the legal limit. The TRV counterfoil (often valid up to 10 years, multiple entry) is permission to travel to Canada; the officer's decision is permission to stay.

To stay longer, apply for an extension (visitor record) before your status expires — while the application is pending you hold maintained status and may remain. Leaving and immediately re-entering to 'reset the clock' is a known pattern officers watch for; it can end in a short admission or refusal.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A visitor visa or eTA never authorizes work in Canada — not remote work for a foreign employer while physically in Canada, not 'just helping' at a relative's business. Working needs a work permit: employer-specific (usually requiring an LMIA) or open (e.g., spousal, post-graduation). The work permit fee is CAD $155 per person (2026), plus a CAD $100 open-work-permit holder fee where applicable.

Study for more than 6 months needs a study permit (CAD $150) with proof of funds of CAD $23,448 per year for a single applicant outside Quebec (effective for applications on or after 1 September 2026 — up from CAD $22,895), plus first-year tuition and travel. Quebec sets its own figure: CAD $24,617 (from 1 January 2026). Canada's 2026–2028 Immigration Levels Plan holds permanent resident admissions at 380,000/year while cutting temporary-resident targets — competition for study and work routes is tightening, so file complete applications.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Dual Canadian citizens must fly to Canada on a valid Canadian passport — they are not eligible for an eTA, and airlines will deny boarding to a dual citizen presenting only a foreign passport. This is the single most common dual-nationality failure at check-in.

Children need their own passport and their own eTA or TRV. A minor travelling alone, or with only one parent or a non-parent adult, should carry a letter of authorization signed by both parents/legal guardians, with the supervising adult's details and contact information (IRCC's recommended practice). CBSA officers routinely question the arrangement — the letter prevents a safeguarding delay from becoming a refusal.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

eTA route: 1) Apply on canada.ca (CAD $7, 2026) with the passport you will travel on. 2) Most approvals arrive within minutes; do not fly until approved — carriers verify it. Visitor visa route: 1) Create an IRCC secure account (GCKey or sign-in partner). 2) Complete the forms (IMM 5257 and family information). 3) Pay CAD $100 (2026; family cap CAD $500 for 5+). 4) Book biometrics at a Visa Application Centre — CAD $85 individual / CAD $170 family maximum. 5) Track the application and check IRCC's processing-times tool: service standards are 14 days (visitor, outside Canada), 60 days (study/work, outside Canada), but recent actuals (week of 22 Sept 2026) ran longer at busy posts — e.g., India visitor visas around 27 days. 6) Submit the passport only when IRCC requests it; the counterfoil is placed inside.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Applying for an eTA when you need a TRV (or vice versa). The eTA is only for visa-exempt nationalities — a refused eTA application by a visa-required traveller wastes weeks.
  • US citizens applying for eTAs. They are exempt from both the visa and the eTA.
  • Assuming the 6-month stay is automatic. The officer sets the period; a stamp or visitor record can shorten it.
  • Thin proof of funds. Three months of statements showing stable means beats a last-minute deposit.
  • Forgetting the passport–eTA link. A renewed passport needs a new eTA application.
  • Working on a visitor status — including remote work. It is a status violation with future-application consequences.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

IRCC officers assess three things: purpose, funds and ties. A strong TRV file shows a credible reason for the specific trip (not a generic 'tourism'), three months of bank statements demonstrating stable means for the visit, and concrete ties — employment with approved leave, property, family, enrollment. Prior compliant travel (to Canada or holding a valid US visa) helps under CAN+ facilitation and should be declared with evidence.

Refusals most often cite insufficient ties or funds, or an unclear purpose. Re-applying with the same file plus a cover letter that directly answers the refusal grounds — with new evidence, not new adjectives — is the correct response. Requesting GCMS notes reveals the officer's actual reasoning.

Biometrics appointments at VACs add their own lead time in peak season. Apply 3–4 months before travel for TRVs from high-demand countries.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Canada has no eVisa — the online steps are the eTA application on canada.ca (CAD $7) and the IRCC secure account (GCKey or banking sign-in partner) for TRV, study and work-permit applications, followed by biometrics at a Visa Application Centre. Track everything in the same IRCC account; keep the login credentials — you will need them for extensions and future applications.

Scam avoidance: third-party sites charge CAD $50–80 to 'process' a CAD $7 eTA — the official application takes minutes on canada.ca. No agent can guarantee a TRV approval or buy a faster IRCC decision. Fees are paid only inside the IRCC account or at the VAC; demands for wire transfers or crypto are fraud.

Translations, certifications and document format

Core TRV documents: the IMM 5257 application form, family information form (IMM 5707), passport biodata page, three months of bank statements, employment letter, itinerary, and purpose-specific evidence (invitation letter for family visits; host-company letter for business). Include expired passports showing prior travel — they strengthen the file.

Documents not in English or French need a certified translation plus a copy of the original. IRCC is strict about completeness: an incomplete application can be returned without processing, which costs more time than gathering one more statement upfront.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Canada rarely interviews visitor-visa applicants — identity verification happens through biometrics: fingerprints and a photograph at a Visa Application Centre, required for most applicants aged 14–79 (CAD $85 individual, CAD $170 family maximum, 2026). Biometrics remain valid for 10 years for future applications.

IRCC may still request an interview or additional documents (medical exam, police certificate) depending on the file. At the port of entry, CBSA officers can question, search devices and examine goods under border authority — answer directly and carry the documents behind your application, not just the approval letter.

Families and group travel

Each family member needs their own eTA or TRV — including infants — though one IRCC account can submit the group's applications together. Biometrics fees are capped at CAD $170 for a family applying together, and TRV fees at CAD $500 for families of five or more.

Children travelling with one parent or a non-parent adult need the authorization letter described above. If one family member is refused, the others' applications stand on their own — but do not re-apply for the refused person with an unchanged file; address the refusal grounds first.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Carrying CAN$10,000 or more in currency or monetary instruments into or out of Canada must be declared to CBSA — declaration is free and the money is not taxed; failure to declare risks seizure. Complete the Advance CBSA Declaration (ArriveCAN) before arrival to speed up processing.

Canada restricts firearms (declare and check the rules long before travel — most visitors cannot simply bring one), and controls meat, dairy, plants and seeds. Prescription medications should be in original packaging with the prescription. Cannabis, though legal in Canada, cannot be taken across the border in either direction.

Departure and future travel

A TRV is typically issued as a multiple-entry visa valid up to 10 years (or to passport expiry) — keep it with the passport and protect both. To extend a stay, apply for a visitor record before your status expires; while IRCC decides, you hold maintained status and may remain legally.

Compliant travel history is an asset: it supports CAN+ facilitation on the next application and is viewed positively by other countries' visa officers. An overstay does the opposite — it can lead to a removal order and a one-year exclusion, and it must be declared on future applications everywhere.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) canada.ca's IRCC pages and the 'check processing times' tool — they override everything. 2) The IRCC help centre's 'find out if you need an eTA or a visa' wizard. 3) Your country's Canadian visa office page for local VAC procedures. Consultant and forum content is useful for process colour, but fees, funds thresholds and processing realities in older posts are routinely out of date — the September 2026 proof-of-funds increase alone invalidated a wave of study-permit guides quoting CAD $22,895.

Verify any claim against the IRCC fee list and the weekly processing update before acting on it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa or an eTA for Canada?

It depends on your nationality and how you arrive. Visa-exempt travellers flying to Canada (including UK citizens) need an eTA (CAD $7, 2026). Nationals of India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines, Bangladesh and other visa-required countries need a visitor visa (TRV, CAD $100) whether arriving by air, land or sea. US citizens need neither. Use IRCC's wizard to confirm your lane — the two are not interchangeable.

How long does a Canadian visitor visa take?

IRCC's 2026 service standard is 14 days for visitor visas applied for outside Canada, but actuals vary by post and week — for the week of 22 September 2026, India averaged around 27 days. Check IRCC's live processing-times tool and apply 3–4 months before travel from high-demand countries.

How long can I stay in Canada as a visitor?

Up to 6 months per entry, unless the CBSA officer stamps a shorter date or issues a visitor record. The TRV's multi-year validity is travel permission, not stay permission. Apply for an extension before your status expires if you need longer.

Can I work in Canada on a visitor visa?

No — not for a Canadian employer, not remotely for a foreign employer while physically in Canada. Work requires a work permit (CAD $155, 2026), usually tied to an employer and, in many cases, a Labour Market Impact Assessment.

What changed for Canada immigration in 2026?

Study-permit proof of funds rose to CAD $23,448/year for single applicants outside Quebec (applications from 1 September 2026). The 2026–2028 Immigration Levels Plan holds permanent resident admissions at 380,000/year while reducing temporary-resident targets — competition for study and work routes is increasing.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Canada, the first decision is the lane: eTA (CAD $7) for visa-exempt nationalities flying in, or a visitor visa/TRV (CAD $100 + CAD $85 biometrics) for everyone else — including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh. Keep the passport valid for the trip, expect up to 6 months per admission at the officer's discretion, and never work on visitor status. Check IRCC's fee list and the live processing-times tool before you apply; both move.
