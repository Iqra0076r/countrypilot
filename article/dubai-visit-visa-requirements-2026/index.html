Dubai Visit Visa Requirements 2026: Documents, Fees, and Process

Dubai's visit visa is a pre-arranged e-visa for most travellers — with a priced menu of durations and mandatory insurance. Here are the 2026 requirements end to end.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20267 min read

Most visitors to Dubai need their visa arranged before departure — the pre-arranged e-visa, priced by duration on the 2026 GDRFA schedule. A smaller group with qualifying residence permits can instead be stamped in on arrival. This guide covers both doors: who uses which, what each requires, and what it costs in 2026.

Quick Answer

Most nationalities need a pre-arranged e-visa before flying to Dubai: AED 250 for 30-day single-entry (+5% VAT), AED 690 for 30-day multiple-entry, or AED 600 for 90-day single-entry (2026 GDRFA schedule). Health insurance is mandatory for visitors. Travellers with qualifying residence permits may get visa on arrival: 14 days for AED 100 or 60 days for AED 250.

E-visa from AED 250 · VoA AED 100 / 14 days · Insurance mandatory · Updated 2026-10-14 · Reading time 7 minutes

Who needs the pre-arranged e-visa

If you do not qualify for visa on arrival, the e-visa is the only door: applied for before travel through the official GDRFA/ICP channels or a sponsor — an airline, hotel, or travel agency. The visa must be issued before departure; airlines check it at boarding. Apply with margin: a last-minute application compresses every downstream plan, and there is no on-arrival fallback for the non-eligible.

The documents

The standard e-visa file: a passport valid for the stay, a passport-style photo, proof of travel (return or onward ticket), accommodation details, and health insurance — mandatory for visitors, with basic plans around AED 600–1,200 per year. Requirements vary slightly by sponsor channel, so confirm the exact list where you apply; the constants are the passport, the photo, the ticket, and the insurance.

The 2026 fee menu

Choose by itinerary, not by price alone:

  • 30-day single-entry: AED 250 (+ 5% VAT) — the standard tourist choice
  • 30-day multiple-entry: AED 690 — for hopping in and out within the month
  • 90-day single-entry: AED 600 — long family visits and extended stays

The realistic budget is the fee plus VAT plus mandatory insurance — the sticker price is only the first line. Full fee detail in our UAE tourist visa fee guide.

The other door: visa on arrival

Travellers holding qualifying residence permits from listed countries may be stamped in on arrival: 14 days for AED 100 or 60 days for AED 250 — cheaper than every pre-arranged option. Eligibility turns on the specific permit, not the passport alone: verify your permit's position before travelling on the assumption. Full detail in our UAE visa-on-arrival guide.

Which door, and the mistakes to avoid

Verify on-arrival eligibility first; if it applies, it is the cheapest and simplest route. If not, arrange the e-visa early through official channels. The mistakes: travelling without either (no on-arrival fallback for the non-eligible), choosing the wrong duration (the 90-day single entry exists for a reason), and forgetting the insurance — mandatory means mandatory. Keep the e-visa approval or on-arrival receipt and the payment receipt accessible for the journey.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the Dubai visit visa requirements in 2026?

Passport, photo, return ticket, accommodation details, and mandatory health insurance — with the pre-arranged e-visa issued before departure (AED 250/690/600 by duration).

How much is a 30-day Dubai visa?

AED 250 single-entry (+ 5% VAT) or AED 690 multiple-entry, on the 2026 GDRFA schedule.

Can I get a Dubai visa on arrival?

Only with a qualifying residence permit — 14 days for AED 100 or 60 days for AED 250. Otherwise, the e-visa before travel.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Pre-arranged e-visa before departure for most travellers — AED 250, 690, or 600 by duration, plus VAT and mandatory insurance — or AED 100 on arrival for the permit-holders who qualify: verify which door is yours, then walk through it properly.
