How to use this Lebanon visa and entry guide

Lebanon's headline is generous: a free one-month visa on arrival for eligible nationalities at Beirut airport and land borders — but eligibility is where the complexity lives. The system runs on General Security (Sûreté Générale) discretion, with nationality-based tiers that have tightened for Syrian nationals since December 2024 and specific Lebanon VOA rule changes from 4 May 2026 affecting Iranian travellers.

Embassy visas where needed run about EUR 77 (Poland mission, June 2026); work permits cost LL 6,900,000. Check your government's travel advisory — the security picture matters more than paperwork in Lebanon. This guide was verified in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for at least 6 months beyond arrival with blank pages. Note: Israeli stamps or visas in the passport cause refusal — this is strictly enforced. Check your travel history before booking.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Eligibility is everything: the free one-month VOA covers many Western and Asian passports, but the tiers shift — Syrians tightened since December 2024, Iranians affected by the May 2026 changes. Verify your nationality's current tier before flying.

Transit and onward travel

Lebanon's VOA covers transit-style entries at the airport and land borders for eligible nationalities. Note the Israeli-stamp refusal rule applies to transit too — and the Syria/Lebanon land border has its own dynamics. Verify the current tier for your nationality before booking connections through Beirut.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever Lebanon's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the General Directorate of General Security approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

No vaccination certificate is a standard Lebanese entry requirement for ordinary tourists. Check your government's travel advisory — the security picture matters more than paperwork in Lebanon.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance is not a Lebanese VOA condition, but comprehensive cover is strongly recommended. No official student-insurance figure is published.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Eligible nationalities: the free one-month VOA is issued at Beirut airport and land borders — have the return ticket, accommodation and proof of funds accessible, as General Security exercises discretion. Keep documents ready; questioning can be thorough.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work permit: LL 6,900,000 — employer-sponsored through the Ministry of Labour with General Security involvement. Student visas: no official fee figures were verified for 2026. A VOA entry never authorises work. Verify every figure with the mission before budgeting.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own Lebanon permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether Lebanon restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

VOA route (eligible): 1) Verify your nationality's current tier (note the Syrian tightening since Dec 2024 and Iran VOA changes of 4 May 2026). 2) Check for Israeli stamps — refusal is strict. 3) Fly to Beirut; receive the free one-month VOA. Embassy route: apply at the Lebanese mission (~EUR 77, Poland June 2026 schedule). Work route: employer-led (LL 6,900,000).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Travelling with Israeli stamps. Strict refusal — check before booking.
  • Assuming the old Syrian rules. Tightened since December 2024.
  • Missing the May 2026 Iran VOA changes. Rules shifted 4 May 2026.
  • Treating the VOA as guaranteed. General Security exercises discretion at the border.
  • Ignoring the security advisory. Entry rules are secondary to the security reality.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Lebanon has no e-Visa portal — the General Security website publishes institutional information, and visas go through Lebanese missions. 'Lebanon e-Visa' sellers are not official.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in Lebanon's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the General Directorate of General Security for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where Lebanon requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Declare currency and restricted goods as required. Keep the return ticket, accommodation details and proof of funds with your passport — General Security may ask.

Departure and future travel

The one-month VOA can be extended through General Security before expiry — start early. Overstaying draws fines; the extension system works if used in time.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) general-security.gov.lb — the authority, though tiers shift. 2) Your government's travel advisory for the security picture. 3) The specific mission for embassy fees. Verify your nationality's current tier — the Syrian and Iranian rules moved in 2024–26.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per Lebanon's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked
