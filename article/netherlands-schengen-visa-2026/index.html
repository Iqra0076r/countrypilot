Who needs a Schengen visa for the Netherlands

The Netherlands is a Schengen state, so visits of up to 90 days in any 180-day period fall under the Schengen Type C visa regime. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs handles these applications through missions and VFS centres — not the IND, which decides only stays over 90 days. Passport holders from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh all need a Schengen visa before travelling: there is no visa on arrival and no Dutch eVisa. First-time applicants give fingerprints and a photograph in person (VIS, 59 months), and decisions take around 15 calendar days (up to 45 for complex cases) in 2026. Our full route breakdown is in the Netherlands visa and entry requirements guide.

Who is visa-free

US and UK citizens may visit the Netherlands for up to 90 days in any 180-day period with no visa and no application. The 90-day clock is area-wide across Schengen — it is not reset by hopping between member states. Never count 90 days per country.

The €90 fee and core documents

The 2026 Schengen fee is €90 for adults, €45 for children aged 6–11 and free for children under 6. It is paid at the mission or VFS centre with the application — every traveller, including infants, needs a separate application. The standard file missions look for is: passport, photos, itinerary, travel medical insurance, funds evidence and proof of ties home. Our Netherlands visa document checklist walks through the file item by item, and the current cost side is covered in our Netherlands visa fee 2026 guide.

Passport validity and insurance rules

The EU-wide passport rule applies: the passport must have been issued within the last 10 years and remain valid at least 3 months beyond your intended departure from the Schengen area. Keep at least two blank pages, and remember airlines enforce validity at boarding — a short-validity passport means denied boarding.

Travel medical insurance is a visa condition: minimum €30,000 cover for emergency care, hospitalization and repatriation, valid Schengen-wide. No compliant proof, no visa. For full insurance planning see the Netherlands travel insurance and health guide.

The application workflow

1) Identify the mission or VFS centre for your jurisdiction. 2) Complete the form and assemble the file: passport, 35×45 mm photos, itinerary, insurance, funds, ties. 3) Attend the biometric appointment (first-time applicants). 4) Pay €90 (2026). 5) Allow ~15 calendar days, up to 45 for complex cases. A refusal is recorded in the Visa Information System — fix the reason before re-applying rather than resubmitting the same file.

Transit and the border decision

Schiphol applies the EU airport-transit-visa list: some nationalities need a transit visa even for an airside-only connection, and leaving the airport during a layover requires a full Schengen visa — verify before booking, since this is the most-skipped check at Europe's biggest transfer hubs. A Schengen visa only lets you present yourself at the border; the Royal Marechaussee officer decides admission, so carry the passport with the visa, accommodation proof, return ticket, insurance proof and funds evidence.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Counting 90 days per country. The Schengen clock is area-wide — 90 days in any 180 across all member states.
  • Skipping the transit check. Some nationalities need an airport transit visa even without leaving Schiphol.
  • Paying above the official fee. Anyone charging far beyond €90 for the application is selling anxiety — ind.nl and the mission are the only official channels.
  • Resubmitting a refused file unchanged. Refusals are recorded in the VIS; address the reason first.
  • Entering on a Schengen visa to 'arrange' residence. The MVV residence route starts from outside the Netherlands.
  • Vague purpose and weak ties. Missions look for credible purpose, sufficient funds and strong ties home — evidenced with dated bookings, genuine bank statements and employment letters.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a Schengen visa for the Netherlands?

Yes. Indian passport holders need a Schengen visa for visits up to 90 days — €90 fee (2026), applied for at a Dutch mission or VFS centre, typically around 15 calendar days processing. No visa on arrival, no eVisa.

How long does the Netherlands Schengen visa take?

Around 15 calendar days in 2026, up to 45 for complex cases. Apply well before any non-refundable bookings.

Is travel insurance required for the Netherlands Schengen visa?

Yes — it is a visa condition, not optional. The policy must provide minimum €30,000 cover for emergency care, hospitalization and repatriation, valid across the Schengen area.

Can I work on a Netherlands Schengen visa?

No. A Schengen visa never authorizes work. Dutch work routes are employer-driven through IND-recognized sponsors — see the Netherlands work permit guide on CountryPilot.

Do I need a transit visa at Schiphol?

Possibly: the Netherlands applies the EU airport-transit-visa list at Schiphol, so some nationalities need a transit visa even for an airside-only connection. Leaving the airport during a layover requires a full Schengen visa. Verify your nationality's position before booking.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For a Netherlands short stay, the Schengen visa is a €90, ~15-day, mission/VFS process with €30,000-compliant insurance as a hard condition — needed by Indian, Pakistani, Nigerian, Filipino and Bangladeshi passports, skipped entirely by US and UK passports. Keep the passport inside the 10-year/3-month rule, verify the Schiphol transit position before booking connections, and never treat the visa as permission to work.
