How to use this Tuvalu visa and entry guide

Tuvalu has no official online immigration or e-visa portal — entry permits are issued on arrival by the Immigration Division (Office of the Prime Minister) at Funafuti airport. Most nationalities receive a visa on arrival (up to 1 month); EU/EEA member states (except Ireland), Switzerland and Taiwan enjoy 90-day visa-free entry. There is no e-visa system at all.

The honest framing for Tuvalu: no official fee schedule, no published processing figures, and no verifiable government immigration website could be located in 2026. What follows is the verifiable core — VoA mechanics, the exemption list, and the genuinely notable 2026 development: the Australia–Tuvalu Falepili Mobility Pathway ballot, offering up to 280 Treaty-stream visas per year for Tuvaluans to live, work and study in Australia (inaugural ballot opened June 2025 for program year 2025–26). This guide was verified in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport should be valid well beyond your stay with blank pages — apply the conservative six-months-beyond-travel standard, since no precise published formula could be verified for Tuvalu.

Funafuti is served by limited flights; a passport problem on arrival strands you until the next one. Verify the document twice before the long journey in.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Visa on arrival (most nationalities): up to 1 month, issued at the Funafuti immigration counter — no pre-application. Visa-free (90 days): EU/EEA states except Ireland, Switzerland, Taiwan. No e-visa exists. One non-official guide claims a 100 AUD entry-permit fee at the counter, but no official amount is published — carry cash in AUD and confirm on arrival.

Transit and onward travel

Tuvalu is a destination, not a transit point — Funafuti's handful of weekly connections means almost nobody transits. If your itinerary does route through, the visa-on-arrival framework covers the stopover provided you meet the entry requirements.

The practical transit concern is misconnection, not immigration: with infrequent onward flights, build generous buffers rather than tight connections.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Border officers in Tuvalu decide admission on the spot, and a visa in the passport is permission to request entry, not a guarantee of it. Keep the return or onward ticket, the address of your first night's stay, and evidence of funds within reach — officers most often ask how the trip is funded and when you plan to leave.

Answer plainly and keep every answer consistent with what you put on the application form. If someone else is funding the trip, carry their details too, but understand the underlying test: officers are assessing whether you will leave when you say you will.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Check health-entry rules for Tuvalu before you book anything non-refundable: requirements turn on where you have recently been, not just the passport you hold. Yellow-fever certificates are the most common trigger across Africa and South America, and some countries demand proof at the border even for transit.

Beyond certificates, look at the destination's health reality — malaria prophylaxis, dengue precautions, and whether your travel insurance covers medical evacuation. None of this is visa paperwork in the strict sense, but arriving without a required certificate produces the same outcome as arriving without a visa: refusal.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Whether or not Tuvalu makes insurance a visa condition, treat it as one for yourself. Medical bills for visitors are charged at full private rates in most countries, and a single hospital admission can exceed the entire trip budget several times over.

Match the policy to the trip: medical expenses with a high limit, emergency evacuation and repatriation, and coverage for every activity you will actually do. If Tuvalu sets a minimum coverage figure for the visa, buy above it — minimums are entry thresholds, not adequate protection.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

The stamp or record you receive on arrival in Tuvalu — not the visa in your passport — sets how long you may legally stay. Check the admitted-until date before leaving the immigration hall; the visa's validity period and the permitted stay are different things, and confusing them is one of the most common causes of accidental overstay.

If the permitted stay is shorter than your plans, address it through official channels before the date expires — overstaying, even by days, can void the visa and complicate every future application to Tuvalu and elsewhere.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A visitor entry to Tuvalu does not authorize work — not for a local employer, not remotely for a foreign one while you are physically present, not 'unpaid help' at a family business. Work requires a work-authorized category, applied for and approved before you start.

Study is equally separate: enrollment needs a student or residence status with the institution's sponsorship or admission letter in the file. Entering as a tourist intending to convert to work or study later is misrepresentation in most systems and can end with removal rather than a status change.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Children need their own travel documents for Tuvalu — their own passport, and where visas apply, their own application. A child listed in a parent's passport does not inherit the parent's visa.

Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the absent parent(s) with contact details, plus the birth certificate where surnames differ. Dual nationals should check which passport Tuvalu expects them to use: some countries require entry on their own passport if you hold their citizenship, and using the 'wrong' one can cause refusal.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

A reliable sequence for Tuvalu: 1) Confirm the correct route for your nationality on Immigration Division, Office of the Prime Minister — permits on arrival (no online portal) — visa-free, e-visa, or consular visa. 2) Assemble documents before paying any fee: passport, photographs to specification, itinerary, funds evidence. 3) Apply through the official channel only, never a copycat site. 4) Track the application and do not book non-refundable travel until the visa is in hand.

Build slack into the timeline: add two weeks to whatever processing estimate you find, and keep copies of everything submitted. If an interview or biometrics appointment is required, that appointment — not the published processing time — is usually the critical path.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Applying for the wrong category — the requirements you meet must match the visa you request, not the one with the lower fee.
  • Copycat websites — unofficial portals that charge several times the government fee for submitting the same form. Use Immigration Division, Office of the Prime Minister — permits on arrival (no online portal) only.
  • Non-refundable bookings before approval — never commit money the consulate has not yet earned you the right to spend.
  • Inconsistent paperwork — dates, names and employment details must match across every document.
  • Assuming visa validity equals permitted stay — the admission stamp governs, not the visa foil.
  • Ignoring the funds test — 'I have money' without statements to prove it fails everywhere.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Strong applications to Tuvalu share one trait: every claim is evidenced. Employment is shown with a dated letter stating role, salary and approved leave — not just a business card. Funds are shown with 3–6 months of statements in your name — not a single screenshot. Ties to home are shown with leases, enrollment letters or family documents — not asserted in a cover letter alone.

Write the cover letter a stranger could follow: who you are, why you are going, what you will do each week, when you return, and how it is funded — each point pointing at an attached document. Officers process volume; clarity is persuasive.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

For Tuvalu, the only safe online channel is Immigration Division, Office of the Prime Minister — permits on arrival (no online portal). Copycat domains mimic official portals closely — check the URL character by character, and never arrive at the 'official' site via a search ad.

After submitting, use the portal's own status tracking rather than emailing the mission for updates; consulates do not expedite because you ask. Keep the application reference, payment receipts and the final approval document together — you will need the approval at boarding and at the border.

Translations, certifications and document format

Beyond the mandatory items, the documents that actually decide Tuvalu applications are funds evidence and ties to home. Bank statements should run 3–6 months, show the account holder's name, and reflect a balance consistent with the trip's cost — a sudden large deposit just before applying raises more questions than it answers.

Translations: any document not in the mission's working language needs a competent translation; requirements vary, so check Immigration Division, Office of the Prime Minister — permits on arrival (no online portal) rather than assuming. Keep originals and one full copy of everything submitted.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

No biometric enrollment procedure could be verified for Tuvalu's on-arrival permits. Expect a straightforward document check at the Funafuti immigration counter.

Have the passport and any supporting documents (onward ticket, accommodation) ready at the counter — the process is quick when the file is complete.

Families and group travel

Each traveller to Tuvalu needs individual documentation — including children. Where electronic authorizations apply, every person needs their own approval; there are no family bundles, and a child's approval is not implied by the parent's.

When one family member faces a refusal, do not simply reapply with identical papers — address the refusal reason first. For groups, align the applications: same itinerary, consistent purpose statements, and appointments booked as close together as the system allows.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Customs is the second border after immigration in Tuvalu, and it has its own rules. Declare currency above the reporting threshold — declaration is not taxation, but failure to declare can mean seizure. Food, plants and animal products face the strictest controls in most countries; when in doubt, declare.

Medicines need original packaging and, for controlled substances, a prescription or doctor's letter. Check Tuvalu's prohibited list before packing: drones, satellite communicators and certain medications surprise travellers regularly.

Departure and future travel

Leave Tuvalu the way you entered: on time and documented. Keep boarding passes and entry/exit stamps — a clean travel history is an asset on every future visa application you will ever make.

If plans change mid-trip, act before the permitted stay expires: seek an extension through official channels where available, or depart and reapply. An overstay record must be declared on future applications to most countries and can void multi-entry visas.

How to research a route without getting misled

Research Tuvalu entry rules in a strict hierarchy. First, Immigration Division, Office of the Prime Minister — permits on arrival (no online portal) — the official portal overrides everything else. Second, the specific embassy or consulate handling your nationality, for local procedures. Third, official advisory pages for the security context.

Treat forums, agent blogs and social media as leads, never answers: one traveller's smooth entry is not a rule, and procedures change without announcement. Anything promising 'guaranteed approval' is selling fiction.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid for the required period beyond your stay, with blank pages
  • Visa or authorization approved before non-refundable bookings — approval document printed and backed up
  • Return/onward ticket and first accommodation address accessible offline
  • Funds evidence and ties-to-home documents packed, not only on a phone
  • Travel insurance covering medical, evacuation and the full trip duration
  • Required health certificates (e.g., yellow fever) in the WHO booklet format
  • Medications in original packaging with prescriptions where needed
  • Cash declaration prepared if carrying above the threshold

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for Tuvalu?

For most nationalities: no advance visa — the entry permit is issued on arrival at Funafuti (up to 1 month). EU/EEA nationals (except Ireland), Switzerland and Taiwan get 90 days visa-free. There is no e-visa system.

How much does Tuvalu entry cost?

No official fee is published. One non-official guide claims 100 AUD at the counter — treat that as indicative only and carry cash in AUD. Do not trust precise quoted figures; the government publishes no schedule.

Is there an online visa application for Tuvalu?

No. Tuvalu has no official online immigration or e-visa portal. Entry permits are issued on arrival by the Immigration Division. Any website selling a 'Tuvalu e-Visa' is not official.

What is the Falepili Mobility Pathway?

The Australia–Tuvalu Falepili Mobility Pathway: a ballot offering up to 280 Treaty-stream visas per year for Tuvaluan citizens to live, work and study in Australia (inaugural ballot opened June 2025). It is outbound mobility for Tuvaluans — the most significant visa development touching Tuvalu in 2026.

Can I work in Tuvalu on the entry permit?

No. The on-arrival permit is for short visits. Work arrangements have no verifiable published figures — arrange directly with the Immigration Division well in advance of travel.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Tuvalu, the entry story fits in a sentence: visa on arrival at Funafuti for most (up to a month), 90 days visa-free for the listed Europeans, no online system, no published fee schedule. The honest gaps — work/student figures, funds minimums — are stated, not filled with guesses. And the 2026 headline isn't inbound at all: it's the Falepili Pathway ballot giving Tuvaluans a structured route to Australia.
