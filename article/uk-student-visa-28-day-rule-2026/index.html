UK Student Visa 28-Day Rule 2026

The 28-day rule is the heart of the UK Student visa financial requirement: your maintenance money must have been held continuously for at least 28 days. This guide explains the 2026 figures, which accounts count, and the timing mistakes that fail more applications than any total.

Quick Answer

Under the UK Student visa financial requirement, the maintenance funds you rely on must have been held continuously for at least 28 days. In 2026 that means £1,529 per month for study in London or £1,171 per month elsewhere, for up to 9 months of the course — £13,761 or £10,539 for a full 9-month academic year. If the balance drops below the required amount on even a single day of the 28-day window, the clock restarts. More Student applications fail on this timing than on the total, so plan the 28-day window before you plan the payment.

What the 28-day rule actually requires

The UK Student route does not ask whether you are rich — it asks whether the required money has been continuously available to you. The 28-day rule is how the Home Office tests that: the maintenance funds you rely on must have been held for at least 28 days, with the balance never dropping below the required amount at any point in that window. A balance above the requirement for 27 days that dips below it on day 28 fails just as completely as a balance that was never there — it is a timing test, not a totals test.

The requirement applies for up to 9 months of the course (see the 2026 UK visa fee schedule): multiply the monthly figure by the number of months shown in your education provider's sponsorship documents, capped at nine. Tuition fees you have already paid to the provider may reduce the amount you need to show as maintenance — check the exact reduction on the live GOV.UK Student visa money page before relying on it, since it depends on what the sponsorship documents confirm.

Timing the 28 days against your application

Plan the 28-day window before you plan the application payment. The funds must already have been sitting for the full 28 days when the evidence is assessed — and because the £558 application fee and the £776-per-year Immigration Health Surcharge are non-refundable once processed, applying before the holding period is complete can mean paying them again.

The practical approach: pick your intended application date, count 28 days back, and make sure the balance has been above the required amount for that entire stretch. Confirm the figures are current on the gov.uk money page on the day you submit — financial figures change by regulation too.

Whose account counts and what counts as funds

The Home Office cares about three things in the funds evidence: the amount, the holding period, and whether the money is genuinely available to you. Use the account type the official guidance allows, keep statements covering the required period, and do not shuffle the same money between several accounts to create a larger-looking balance.

GOV.UK's supporting-documents guidance asks for financial documents showing access to sufficient funds — "bank statements which detail the origin of the funds held". That origin-of-funds reading applies here too: a modest balance with a clean history beats a large balance that appeared from nowhere the week before the application. If you apply in a non-sterling currency, leave a buffer — exchange rates move, and a balance that only just meets the threshold can fall below it before assessment.

Money held by a parent

Funds do not have to sit in your own account: the Student route allows money held by a parent, but the evidence changes. Explain the relationship and document that the money is genuinely available to you — the decision-maker will look for proof of the relationship and of the parent's consent. The 28-day clock applies to the parent's account in exactly the same way.

What breaks the 28-day chain

  • A single-day dip. Any day below the required amount restarts the 28-day clock — the balance must stay above the line for the entire window.
  • Borrowed money and temporary transfers. Money parked in the account just for the application, and cash deposits that cannot be verified, read as exactly that.
  • Shuffling between accounts. Moving the same money through several accounts to create a larger-looking balance damages the file rather than helping it.
  • False or inconsistent documents. False bank statements and inconsistent account-holder names are high-risk — false financial evidence can lead to refusal and, in serious cases, misrepresentation findings.

A 28-day checklist that passes

  • Required amount calculated: monthly figure × course months (max 9), reduced only for tuition already paid and confirmed in the sponsorship documents
  • Balance above the required amount for a full, unbroken 28 days — no dips, no shuffling between accounts
  • Currency buffer added if applying in a non-sterling currency
  • Statements or official evidence covering the whole required period, showing the origin of the funds
  • If funds are in a parent's account: relationship evidence and consent documented, 28-day rule satisfied in that account
  • Figures re-confirmed on the gov.uk Student visa money page on submission day

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the UK Student visa 28-day rule?

The maintenance funds for a UK Student visa must have been held continuously for at least 28 days, never dropping below the required amount during that window. A single day below the line restarts the clock.

How much money do I need to hold for 28 days?

In 2026: £1,529 per month for study in London, £1,171 per month elsewhere, for up to 9 months — £13,761 or £10,539 for a full academic year. Tuition already paid may reduce the amount; verify the reduction on the live GOV.UK money page.

Can the money be in my parents' account?

Yes, but the evidence changes: you must document the parent-child relationship and the parent's consent for the funds to be used, and the 28-day clock applies to the parent's account in exactly the same way — the full amount held continuously for 28 days.

Does the 28-day rule apply to the visa fee and health surcharge too?

No. The £558 application fee and the £776-per-year health surcharge are payments made during the application — the 28-day holding rule applies only to the maintenance funds, the money you show as available to support yourself.

What if my balance dips below the required amount for one day?

The 28-day clock restarts from when the balance is back above the required amount. Do not apply until a full, unbroken 28-day window of sufficient funds is behind you — the fee and surcharge are non-refundable once processed.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

The 28-day rule is the timing test behind the UK Student visa financial requirement: £1,529 per month in London or £1,171 per month elsewhere, for up to 9 months, held continuously for at least 28 days with no dips below the line. Plan the window before you plan the payment, keep the statements clean and traceable, and re-confirm the figures on the live GOV.UK money page — most failures are timing, not totals.
