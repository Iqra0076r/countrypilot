How to use this Denmark visa and entry guide

This guide covers how visitors enter Denmark — which authority decides, what it costs, and what changed recently. The deciding source throughout is SIRI’s ‘New to Denmark’ portal (nyidanmark.dk): Denmark applies the Schengen regime for short stays (€90 / DKK 670 in 2026) and processes all residence permits — work, study, family — through SIRI, the Danish Agency for International Recruitment and Integration.

What changed most recently: Denmark raised SIRI residence-permit fees for 2026 (effective 1 Jan 2026): work permits DKK 6,055→DKK 6,810; study permits DKK 2,255→DKK 3,060; au pair/intern DKK 4,210→DKK 4,305; accompanying family DKK 2,380→DKK 3,080.…

The headline numbers: visitor €90 (DKK 670) Schengen visa; €45 children 6–11; student Study residence-permit fee 2026: DKK 3,060 (up from DKK 2,255). Fees and processing times move — re-check SIRI’s ‘New to Denmark’ portal (nyidanmark.dk) before paying anything or booking non-refundable travel.

Passport validity and document condition

Schengen rules set the bar here, not national discretion: your passport must have been issued within the last 10 years and remain valid at least 3 months beyond your planned departure from the Schengen area (Schengen Borders Code). A passport that fails either test means denied boarding — airlines check Timatic before you fly.

Carry at least two blank pages for stamps. Emergency or temporary passports are accepted only in limited circumstances; confirm with the consulate before relying on one.

Transit and onward travel

When your itinerary routes through Denmark to a third country, the transit permission — if one is required for your passport — must be secured before the trip. Airlines enforce it at departure; Denmark airports will not improvise a solution at the counter.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Admission to Denmark is decided at the port of entry, not when the visa is issued. Pack an entry folder: passport with visa, e-Visa or approval printout, return ticket, hotel booking or host address, and bank statements or equivalent funds proof.

Officers may ask how the trip is funded and when you leave. Short, truthful, consistent answers beat long explanations every time.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

For Denmark, verify any health-entry requirements on SIRI’s ‘New to Denmark’ portal (nyidanmark.dk) shortly before departure. As a rule of thumb, yellow-fever rules depend on where you have recently been, not just your nationality — carry the certificate if you have transited an endemic country.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Student health insurance: No separate mandatory insurance premium: non-EU students with a residence permit register for a CPR number and are covered by the Danish public health insurance (as of 2025-2026)

If your trip later extends to study, confirm whether the student route has its own insurance requirement — several countries in this group do, and the minimum cover is specified by the authorities, not by you.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

On arrival in Denmark, your permitted stay is the period stated on your visa or entry stamp. Verify the date on the stamp or system record the same day — errors are far easier to fix at the counter than after you have left the airport.

Do not work on a visitor permission, and do not assume a long-validity visa equals a long stay. Each entry is a fresh admission decision.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Plan the Denmark trip around the correct category from the start with work route around SIRI residence-permit fee 2026: DKK 6,810 (up from DKK 6,055); consular application via diplomatic mission €252 / DKK 1,875 (as of 2026) and study route around Study residence-permit fee 2026: DKK 3,060 (up from DKK 2,255) (as of 2026). A visitor permission covers tourism, family visits and (where the rules allow) short business meetings — it does not cover taking a job, enrolling for a degree, or freelancing for local clients.

If the purpose changes, change the permission first. Entering on the wrong status and hoping to fix it later is how people end up refused, removed, or barred.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Children need individual passports and, where applicable, individual visas or e-Visas for Denmark — there is no family visa. If a child holds two nationalities, use one passport consistently for the whole journey and make sure any required authorisation matches that passport.

Consent letters for minors travelling without both parents: not universally mandatory, frequently asked for. Bring one.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Do it in this order and Denmark entry admin stays boring (which is the goal): 1) Confirm your nationality's route in the table above. 2) Book the consulate appointment early — slots are the usual bottleneck. 3) Assemble the file: passport, photos to specification, funds evidence, itinerary, and purpose documents. 4) Attend, then track the case; do not book non-refundable travel until the visa is in hand.

Build in buffer: consular queues and e-Visa approvals both slip, especially in peak season.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Budgeting from a stale fee guide. The Denmark visitor figure is €90 (DKK 670) Schengen visa; €45 children 6–11 (as of 2026) — older pages quote superseded amounts.
  • Travelling on last year's rules. Denmark changed entry rules in 2025–2026 — check the policy notes in this guide before assuming the old process still works.
  • Booking non-refundable travel before the visa is decided. Processing slips everywhere; the approval comes first, the flights second.
  • Applying at the wrong Schengen consulate. File with the country of your main destination — consulates bounce misdirected applications.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

The Denmark application is won on specifics: named employer, dated itinerary, booked (refundable) accommodation, and funds that plausibly cover the trip. Generic statements — ‘tourism’, ‘visiting friends’ — unsupported by documents are the classic weak file.

Answer only what is asked, truthfully and briefly. Volunteering complications you were not asked about rarely helps.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Bookmark the official SIRI’s ‘New to Denmark’ portal (nyidanmark.dk) (https://www.nyidanmark.dk) before you need it. For Denmark, third-party ‘visa services’ add no authority to your application — at best they retype your answers into the same official form for a fee, at worst they steal your documents.

Pay fees only through the portal's own payment flow, keep every receipt, and never send passport scans over unsecured chat apps to strangers.

Translations, certifications and document format

The Denmark supporting file has four piles: who you are, why you are going, how you pay for it, and why you will come back. Cover all four with originals plus copies, translated where needed with a statement of accuracy.

Match every name and date across documents exactly — inconsistencies, even innocent ones, invite the scrutiny you are trying to avoid.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

If your Denmark route includes an interview, treat it as a verification conversation: the officer is checking that the person matches the paperwork. Bring the file, answer directly, and do not volunteer unrelated complications.

Fingerprint and photo capture is normal. At ports of entry the same applies, plus possible device inspection — keep the trip's documents accessible, not buried in luggage.

Families and group travel

For Denmark, plan one application per person and budget the fees and appointment slots accordingly. Groups should align their interview or submission dates, and everyone should carry their own approval — shared printouts cause confusion at counters.

Where the system allows linked or group submissions, use it; where it does not, do not improvise shortcuts.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Do a customs pre-check for Denmark before packing: cash-reporting threshold, food/agriculture restrictions, medication rules, and duty-free allowances. These differ by country and change — the ten minutes of checking beats the argument at the red channel.

Original packaging and prescriptions for all medication; nothing in unlabelled containers.

Departure and future travel

Think of each Denmark trip as building a record: compliant entries and exits strengthen every future application to Denmark and elsewhere. One overstay can undo years of clean history.

Need more time? File for it properly and early. Letting permission lapse and hoping no one notices is the most expensive mistake on this list.
