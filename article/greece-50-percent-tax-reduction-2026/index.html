Greece 50% Tax Reduction 2026: 7-Year Guide for Workers

Of Europe's three big inbound-worker tax regimes, Greece offers the longest runway after Portugal: seven years with half your Greek work income exempt from tax. But Article 5C rewards Greek paychecks, not Greek postcards — and a single application deadline can cost you a full year. Here's the 2026 reality.

Article 5C of the Greek Income Tax Code (Law 4172/2013) lets qualifying individuals who transfer their tax residence to Greece exempt 50% of their Greek-source employment or business income from income tax for seven tax years. You must not have been a Greek tax resident for five of the previous six years, come from an EU/EEA country or a tax-cooperation jurisdiction, work in Greece through a Greek entity or permanent establishment (or a Greek business activity), and declare you will stay at least two years. Applications go through the myAADE platform, and the timing of your application determines which year the benefit starts.

What Article 5C Is Really Buying: Greek Paychecks, Not Greek Postcards

Greece runs several incentives for newcomers — a flat-tax regime for foreign investors, a 7% flat rate for foreign pensioners — and Article 5C is the one aimed squarely at workers. Introduced for tax years from 2021, refined by ministerial guidance, and updated again by 2025 legislation, it is Greece's "brain gain" instrument: the state's bid to pull skilled professionals — returning Greeks and foreign talent alike — into the domestic labour market.

The design philosophy is narrower than the headlines suggest. Article 5C doesn't reward you for being in Greece; it rewards you for working in Greece, in the specific sense of earning income from a Greek source — employment with a Greek legal entity or a permanent establishment of a foreign company in Greece, or individual business activity established in Greece. A developer living in Athens but billing clients in Berlin through a foreign contract is living the Greek dream, but not the Article 5C dream. The regime is, at its core, a subsidy for economic activity that lands inside the Greek tax base.

The 50% Exemption, Decoded

Here's the distinction that most coverage gets wrong: Article 5C does not cut your tax bill in half. It exempts 50% of your qualifying income from income tax. Take a professional earning €100,000 of eligible Greek employment income. Subject to meeting the conditions, €50,000 is exempt from income tax entirely, and the remaining €50,000 goes through Greece's ordinary income-tax calculation.

That is fundamentally different from computing a normal tax bill and halving it — and the difference grows at higher incomes, where the exempt half would otherwise have faced the top marginal rates. Conversely, the exemption does nothing for income outside its scope: foreign-source income, Greek rental income, dividends, and capital gains all follow their normal rules. Before you model the benefit, separate your income into "Greek work income" and "everything else" — only the first column gets the treatment.

The Five Conditions That Make or Break Your Application

The tax office for foreign tax residents (the competent AADE office) checks these cumulatively. Fail one and the regime doesn't apply:

  1. Five of the last six years abroad. You must not have been a Greek tax resident for five out of the six years immediately before the transfer. This is stricter than it looks for returning Greeks: a single recent year of Greek tax residence inside that six-year window can be fatal.
  2. A qualifying origin country. You must transfer your tax residence from an EU or EEA country, or from a country with which Greece has an administrative cooperation agreement on tax matters. The cooperation requirement is the anti-abuse filter — it keeps the regime's door closed to opaque jurisdictions.
  3. Greek-source work. You must provide employment services in Greece through an employment relationship with a domestic legal entity or a permanent establishment of a foreign company in Greece — or perform individual business/freelance activity established in Greece.
  4. A genuine two-year intention. You declare that you will remain in Greece for at least two years. Unlike Italy's four-year commitment, this is framed as a declaration of intent rather than a clawback trigger — but the tax office can revisit the benefit if the facts contradict it.
  5. Timely application. The application must be filed within the statutory deadlines (see below) with supporting documentation of your prior tax residence and employment.

Note what isn't on the list: there is no minimum salary, no degree requirement, and no restricted profession list of the Portuguese kind. Greece's filter is about where your work happens and where you came from, not what your job title is. For mid-career professionals without elite credentials, that's a meaningfully lower bar than IFICI across the Ionian Sea.

The July 2 Deadline Trap

This is the section that saves people a year. The AADE's official application service sets a split deadline based on when your Greek employment or business activity starts:

  • Start on or before 2 July of a given year → apply by the end of that same year, and the regime covers that year.
  • Start after 2 July → apply by the end of the following year, and the regime covers the following year — not the year you started.

The trap is obvious once you see it: start work on 5 July 2026, and your seven-year clock starts in 2027, not 2026. You've donated five and a half months of Greek-source income to the full tax rate for no reason. If your start date is negotiable — and for freelancers registering a Greek business activity, it usually is — aim for June. Applications are filed digitally through the myAADE platform with TAXISnet credentials to the Tax Office for Foreign Tax Residents & Alternative Tax Regimes, which may request additional documents within 60 days.

Note: This guide is information, not tax advice. Article 5C applications live or die on documentation of your prior tax residence and the Greek source of your income — get a Greek tax adviser to review your file before you file, especially around the application timing.

The Quiet Perks: Tekmiria Exemption and the State-Sector Extension

Two lesser-known features make Article 5C more valuable than the income exemption alone suggests.

First, the tekmiria exemption. Greece's tax system includes "objective expenses" — deemed minimum income calculated from your lifestyle: your main and secondary residences, your cars. Normally these can inflate your taxable base above your actual income. Article 5C beneficiaries are exempt from the deemed-income calculation on their residences and private passenger vehicles for the full seven years, regardless of number. For a professional renting a good Athens apartment and running a car, this is real money that never shows up in the headline "50%" figure.

Second, the regime keeps widening at the edges. By Decision A.1096/2026, the Tax Authority extended the option to people transferring their tax residence to Greece to provide services to the Greek State under public-sector payroll legislation — a niche addition, but a signal that the regime is being maintained and developed rather than left to wither.

Why Seven Years Changes the Relocation Math

Duration is Article 5C's superpower. Five years (Italy) is a chapter; ten years (Portugal) is a decade but gated behind elite professions; seven years is long enough to buy property, enrol children in school, and build a business — while remaining short enough that the state can afford the generosity. For a 35-year-old professional, seven years of half-exempt income covers the most expensive phase of family formation: the years when the mortgage is largest and the salaries are still climbing.

The honest comparison: Portugal's IFICI gives a lower rate (20% flat) for longer (10 years) to fewer people. Italy gives a similar halving for five years with a clawback. Greece gives seven years with the lightest qualification bar of the three — no degree requirement, no profession whitelist — but demands that your economic activity genuinely sit in Greece. If your work can be Greek-sourced, the Greek regime is the easiest of the three to enter and the second-longest to enjoy. If your work can't be Greek-sourced, none of its advantages matter.

Digital Nomads Beware: The Visa Doesn't Carry the Tax Break

Greece's Digital Nomad Visa is a solid immigration product, and it's natural to assume the tax incentive comes stapled to it. It doesn't. Immigration status and tax residence are separate questions — the AADE says so explicitly — and Article 5C is separate from both. The nomad visa is built around remote work for employers or clients outside Greece; Article 5C is built around income from work performed for a Greek entity or Greek business activity. The two products point in opposite directions.

A nomad-visa holder who becomes Greek tax resident (183+ days of presence in any 12-month period can trigger it from day one) without Article 5C simply pays ordinary Greek tax on worldwide income, with treaty relief where available. The planning question isn't "which visa?" but "where does my income come from, and who employs me?" — answer that first, and the choice between the nomad visa, Article 5C, or both becomes clear. Get it backwards and you can hold the right visa while paying the wrong tax.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Greece's Article 5C tax regime?

Article 5C of the Greek Income Tax Code (Law 4172/2013) lets qualifying individuals who transfer their tax residence to Greece exempt 50% of their Greek-source employment or individual business income from income tax for seven tax years. Applications are handled by the AADE's Tax Office for Foreign Tax Residents & Alternative Tax Regimes.

Who qualifies for the Greek 50% tax reduction?

You must not have been a Greek tax resident for five of the six years before the transfer, you must transfer your residence from an EU/EEA country or a country with an administrative tax-cooperation agreement with Greece, you must work in Greece through a Greek entity or permanent establishment (or a business activity established in Greece), and you must declare you will remain in Greece for at least two years.

Does the Greek digital nomad visa automatically give me the 50% tax break?

No. The visa is immigration status; Article 5C is a separate tax regime. The nomad visa is designed for remote work for foreign employers or clients, while Article 5C requires Greek-source work income — employment with a Greek entity or a Greek business activity. A nomad-visa holder who becomes Greek tax resident without qualifying for Article 5C pays ordinary Greek tax.

What is the application deadline for Article 5C?

Per the AADE: if your employment or business activity starts on or before 2 July of a year, apply by the end of that year for same-year coverage. If it starts after 2 July, apply by the end of the following year — and the regime then starts from that following year. File through the myAADE platform with TAXISnet credentials.

Does the 50% exemption cut my whole tax bill in half?

No — it exempts 50% of the qualifying income from income tax; the other 50% is taxed under normal Greek rules. Only Greek-source employment and business income qualifies. Foreign income, rental income, dividends, and capital gains follow their ordinary treatment.

How long does the Greek 50% reduction last?

Seven tax years — the longest of Europe's three main inbound-worker regimes after Portugal's ten (Italy offers five). The duration is one of the regime's biggest advantages for anyone planning a genuine multi-year relocation.

Are there any other benefits besides the income exemption?

Yes. Beneficiaries are exempt for seven years from Greece's deemed-income (tekmiria) calculation on their main and secondary residences and private vehicles. And Decision A.1096/2026 extended the regime to people transferring tax residence to provide services to the Greek State.

Bottom Line

Greece's Article 5C is the most accessible of Southern Europe's three big inbound tax regimes — seven years, half your Greek work income exempt, no profession whitelist — but it pays for Greek economic activity, not Greek sunsets. If your work can genuinely be sourced through a Greek entity or business, time your start for the first half of the year and file on time. If it can't, no deadline trick will make the regime fit.
