How to use this Guinea visa and entry guide

Guinea made the e-Visa mandatory for all visa-required nationalities (launched April 2023) — up to 90-day stays, with US and Canadian e-Visa holders eligible for up to 5 years. The official line from the DCPAF (Border Police): no general visa on arrival — even UAE nationals are directed to the e-Visa.

The fee is US$100 for the e-Visa (rise reported August 2026). Two honest caveats: no official processing times are published by the DCPAF, and an August 2026 news report of extended 45-day processing for EU nationals is unconfirmed — do not rely on it. Work and student fees have no official published figures. This guide was verified against DCPAF guidance in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for at least 6 months beyond arrival with blank pages. Print the e-Visa approval — airlines check it before boarding, and Guinean border posts work from the approval reference.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Every visa-required nationality — including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh — uses the same mandatory e-Visa. There is no consular bypass and no walk-up visa on arrival.

Transit and onward travel

Guinea publishes no transit-visa regime — the e-Visa is the instrument for any entry, including itineraries that exit the transit area. Do not plan a landside connection through Conakry without the e-Visa approved; the DCPAF's no-VOA position is explicit.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever Guinea's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the DCPAF eVisa portal (Border Police) approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Yellow-fever vaccination is commonly checked on arrival in West Africa — carry the certificate. No other health document is a standard Guinean entry requirement.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance is not a published Guinean e-Visa condition, but it is strongly recommended. No official student-insurance figure is published.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Present the e-Visa approval at Conakry — the 90-day stay runs from entry. Keep the return ticket and accommodation details accessible. The reported August 2026 processing changes for EU nationals are unconfirmed; do not plan around them.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Long-stay, work and student routes exist but no official fee figures are published — third-party numbers (e.g. a US$500 long-stay visa or US$1,000 work authorization from 2012-era rules) are not official and must not be relied on. Verify current requirements and fees with the nearest Guinean mission before any commitment. A visitor e-Visa never authorises work.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own Guinea permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether Guinea restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

e-Visa route: 1) Apply on the official DCPAF portal (paf.gov.gn) — the only legitimate channel. 2) Pay the US$100 fee. 3) Travel with the printed approval — no official processing SLA is published, so apply well ahead. 4) US/Canadian holders: the e-Visa can carry up to 5 years' validity. Work/study route: verify fees and requirements with the mission first — nothing is officially published online.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Expecting visa on arrival. The DCPAF position is explicit: e-Visa only, even for UAE nationals.
  • Planning around the '45-day EU processing' report. Unconfirmed news — not an official DCPAF announcement.
  • Budgeting third-party work-visa fees. The US$500/US$1,000 figures are 2012-era and unofficial.
  • Assuming published processing times exist. None are officially published — apply early.
  • Using unofficial e-Visa sites. Only paf.gov.gn is official.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

The paf.gov.gn portal is the only official online channel. Copycat 'Guinea e-Visa' domains are not official — the DCPAF runs the single legitimate system.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in Guinea's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the DCPAF eVisa portal (Border Police) for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where Guinea requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Declare large cash sums as required. Keep the e-Visa approval printout, return ticket and yellow-fever certificate with your passport.

Departure and future travel

The e-Visa stay runs up to 90 days — extensions go through the Guinean authorities before expiry. Overstaying risks fines and future refusals.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) paf.gov.gn — the DCPAF portal. Treat the August 2026 fee/processing news report as what it is — unconfirmed until the DCPAF publishes it. No official processing times or work/student fees exist anywhere; distrust any site quoting them.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per Guinea's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Guinea is a one-portal system: paf.gov.gn → US$100 e-Visa → 90 days, no VOA. The honest gaps — no published processing times, no work/student fee schedule — are features of the system, not oversights in this guide. Verify the gaps with the mission, and don't plan around unconfirmed news reports.
