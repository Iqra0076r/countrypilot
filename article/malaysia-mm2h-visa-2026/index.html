Malaysia MM2H 2026: Silver, Gold & Platinum Tiers

Malaysia My Second Home stopped being a retirement visa years ago — it's now a tiered capital-commitment programme, and the brochure price isn't the real price. The fixed deposit gets the headlines, but the house you're obliged to buy (and can't sell for a decade) is what actually decides whether MM2H is for you.

Malaysia's MM2H programme, run by the Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture, offers four categories in 2026: Silver (USD 150,000 fixed deposit, minimum RM 600,000 property, 5-year renewable pass), Gold (USD 500,000 deposit, RM 1,000,000 property, 15 years), Platinum (USD 1,000,000 deposit, RM 2,000,000 property, 20 years, work and business permitted), and SEZ/SFZ for special economic zones (USD 65,000 or USD 32,000 deposit by age band, 10 years, property in the designated zone). Every tier requires a property purchase after approval, and up to 50% of the fixed deposit may be withdrawn only for approved purposes.

MM2H stopped being a retirement visa — and that's the point

The first thing to unlearn about Malaysia My Second Home is the name. MM2H is not a retirement visa, and there is no retiree category, no pension test, and no age-based pricing. The Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture's own portal describes four categories — Platinum, Gold, Silver, and SEZ/SFZ — each a bundle of financial conditions attached to a renewable long-term social visit pass. Applicants must be at least 25 for the first three tiers and 21 for the zone tier. A 62-year-old retiree applying for Silver faces exactly the same money test as a 26-year-old remote worker. After the minimum age, the only age-linked term 2026 reviews report is the stay obligation: applicants aged 25–49 are expected to spend 90 days a year in Malaysia, while those 50 and over are not.

This matters because it reframes the comparison. Panama and Costa Rica ask "can you support yourself?" and price the answer in monthly income. Malaysia asks "how much capital will you commit to Malaysia?" and prices the answer in deposits and property. If your wealth is a pension, the Southeast Asian income-test countries are your lane. If your wealth is savings you're willing to park, MM2H starts making sense — but only once you price the whole commitment, which is what this guide does.

The four tiers, priced like financial products

Think of the tiers the way the ministry priced them: as escalating commitments that buy escalating certainty. Silver asks for a USD 150,000 fixed deposit in a Malaysian bank, a one-off participation fee of RM 1,000, and a home worth at least RM 600,000 — and returns a five-year renewable pass. Gold asks for USD 500,000, RM 3,000 participation, a RM 1,000,000 home, and returns fifteen years. Platinum asks for USD 1,000,000, RM 200,000 participation, a RM 2,000,000 home — and returns twenty years plus the right to work and run a business, the only tier that does. The fourth category, SEZ/SFZ, covers the special economic and financial zones: USD 65,000 deposit for ages 21–49, USD 32,000 for 50 and over, a ten-year pass, and property inside the designated zone (in practice, Forest City, Johor).

A few line items sit outside the headline table. Processing runs RM 5,000 for the principal applicant plus RM 2,500 per dependent; renewal fees after the full pass term run RM 1,500 to RM 5,000 by tier. The deposit must sit in a Malaysian financial institution licensed under the Financial Services Act 2013 (or its Islamic equivalent), and after approval you may withdraw up to half of it — but only for the residence purchase, education, medical treatment, or tourism in Malaysia. Applications go through MOTAC-approved agents only; self-submission isn't permitted, and every application passes Royal Malaysia Police security screening.

The house you can't sell for ten years is the real price tag

Here's the term that re-prices every tier: the property purchase is compulsory, and multiple 2026 reviews of MOTAC's published terms report that the home cannot be sold for ten years — on pain of losing the pass — with the clock starting at your first visa endorsement, not at the purchase date. The only stated exception is upgrading to a more expensive property that still meets your tier's minimum, with immigration approval. This is the single most consequential line in the programme, and it's the one most agent brochures bury.

Run the real math. Silver's "USD 150,000" headline becomes, in practice: USD 150,000 locked in a Malaysian bank plus a RM 600,000 home (around USD 145,000–150,000 at 2026 rates) that you can't exit for a decade — plus stamp duty, which since January 1, 2026 is a flat 8% for non-citizens on residential transfers, with no MM2H exemption reported. That puts roughly RM 48,000 of duty on a Silver-minimum home alone. Gold and Platinum scale from there: RM 80,000 of duty on a RM 1,000,000 home, RM 160,000 on a RM 2,000,000 one. These are 2026-reported figures; confirm the current duty schedule before you budget, but the structural point stands regardless of the exact rate: the deposit is the part you eventually keep; the property is the part you're married to.

Platinum's quiet superpower: the only tier that lets you work

Silver, Gold, and SEZ/SFZ are residence-only products: no employment, no business. Platinum is the exception — participants may work and conduct business in Malaysia. For the semi-retired founder, the consultant who isn't done, or the investor who wants optionality, this is the tier's real product, and the twenty-year pass is the packaging.

But notice the economics. Platinum's premium over Gold is USD 500,000 of extra deposit, a RM 197,000 jump in participation fee, and a doubled property minimum — all to buy work rights and five extra years. That's a rational trade for someone whose Malaysian business will generate returns, and a terrible trade for someone who just wants a long, quiet retirement (Silver does that for a fraction of the commitment). Price the work right against your actual plans, not against the prestige of the top tier. And if employment is your primary goal rather than a side option, compare Platinum honestly against Malaysia's Employment Pass, which is the purpose-built work route with employer sponsorship and salary bands — MM2H was never designed to be a work visa, and Platinum is the expensive workaround, not the product.

Note: Tax treatment is part of Platinum's appeal: 2026 reviews report no tax on foreign funds brought in and no tax on fixed-deposit profit. Confirm the current position with a Malaysian tax adviser — programme tax notes are summaries, not rulings.

The SEZ/Forest City corner: cheap entry, narrow exit

The SEZ/SFZ category looks like a bargain next to the national tiers — USD 32,000 to USD 65,000 of deposit and a ten-year pass — but the bargain has a shape. The property must be inside the designated zone, which in practice means Forest City in Johor: a single development, with the developer's own price guidance (reported around RM 500,000 minimum) sitting alongside state foreign-buyer policies. You're buying into one micro-market, not into Malaysia.

Whether that's a problem depends on your intentions. If you genuinely want to live in the Johor–Singapore corridor and the numbers work for a decade of residence, the zone tier is the cheapest long-stay ticket Malaysia sells. If you're buying the ticket and hoping the market will be kind, remember the lesson of the national tiers: exit is the hard part. A discounted entry fee doesn't discount the commitment — it concentrates it.

What 2026 reviews agree on — and what you must check on the portal

By late 2026, independent reviews of the programme converge on a stable picture: the 2022–2024 tier reforms remain fully in force, no structural changes to thresholds, deposits, or the property mandate were announced in 2026, and Silver continues to dominate approvals (roughly four in five, per 2025 data). The programme is also visibly tightening enforcement — 2026 saw fake-pass syndicates prosecuted and MOTAC officials publicly pressing participants to honor tier conditions.

What they don't converge on — and what you must verify yourself — are the operational details: the exact stay-obligation wording by age band, the current agent fee schedule, and the precise withdrawal-approval process for the fixed deposit. The single authoritative source is the ministry's own portal, mm2h.gov.my, and the category pages MOTAC publishes there. When a blog and the portal disagree, the portal wins. Treat every figure in this guide as the 2026-published position to be re-confirmed on the portal before you sign anything — that's not hedging, it's how Malaysia's most rule-dense residence programme is meant to be used.

The agent question: why you can't file alone

Unlike most visa programmes, MM2H doesn't let you apply directly — your file goes through a MOTAC-approved agent. This is a structural feature, not a suggestion, and it changes how you should shop. The agent prepares the file, liaises with the one-stop centre, and shepherds the police-screening and medical steps. Agent fees reported in 2026 run roughly RM 40,000 to RM 70,000 by tier, on top of the government processing and participation fees — which means the agent is a five-figure line item in every tier's real budget.

Because the agent is mandatory, agent selection is the highest-leverage decision in the whole process, and it's where applicants get hurt. Work only with agents licensed through official MOTAC channels, verify the license independently rather than taking a website badge at face value, and be wary of anyone who promises to shortcut the financial tests — 2026's fake-pass prosecutions are a reminder that the programme's enforcement has teeth, and a fraudulent file can poison not just your application but any future Malaysian immigration matter. Get the fee schedule in writing before the agent touches your documents, confirm exactly which government fees are included versus passed through, and ask how many files at your tier the agent placed in the last twelve months. An agent who can't answer that question clearly is telling you something.

The deeper point: the agent requirement means MM2H's true entry price always has two authors — the ministry's published terms and the market's service fees. Price both before you commit to either.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the MM2H tiers in 2026?

The Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture runs four categories: Silver (USD 150,000 fixed deposit, RM 600,000 minimum property, 5-year pass), Gold (USD 500,000 fixed deposit, RM 1,000,000 property, 15-year pass), Platinum (USD 1,000,000 fixed deposit, RM 2,000,000 property, 20-year pass, work and business allowed), and SEZ/SFZ for the special economic zones (USD 65,000 or USD 32,000 fixed deposit depending on age, 10-year pass, property in the designated zone).

Is property purchase really mandatory under MM2H?

Yes. Every tier requires buying a residential property at or above the tier minimum after approval — RM 600,000 for Silver, RM 1,000,000 for Gold, RM 2,000,000 for Platinum. Multiple 2026 reviews of MOTAC's published terms report that the property cannot be sold for ten years without risking the pass.

Can I withdraw my MM2H fixed deposit?

After approval you may withdraw up to 50% of the deposit, but only for approved purposes: buying your residence, education, medical treatment, or tourism in Malaysia. The remainder must stay in the Malaysian account.

Can I work in Malaysia on MM2H?

Only on the Platinum tier, which permits work and business. Silver, Gold, and SEZ/SFZ participants may not work or run a business under the programme. If employment is your goal, Malaysia's Employment Pass is the separate route.

Is there a minimum age for MM2H?

Principal applicants must be at least 25 for Silver, Gold, and Platinum, and at least 21 for SEZ/SFZ. There is no retiree discount — a 62-year-old applying for Silver faces the same financial test as a 26-year-old. Applicants aged 25–49 report a 90-day-per-year minimum stay obligation; those 50 and over report none.

How do I apply for MM2H — can I apply myself?

Applications must be submitted through a MOTAC-approved MM2H agent; self-submission is not permitted under the current structure. Use only licensed agents listed through official channels, and expect Royal Malaysia Police security screening as part of the process.

Is MM2H a retirement visa?

Not officially. MM2H is a renewable long-term social visit pass open to applicants 25 and up, with no retirement-specific category or age-based pricing. It works well for retirees, but it is a capital-commitment programme, not a pension-based retirement visa like Panama's or Costa Rica's.

Bottom Line

MM2H in 2026 is a capital-commitment product wearing a retirement programme's name: Silver, Gold, and Platinum price your commitment from USD 150,000 to USD 1,000,000, and every tier obliges you to buy a home you can't sell for ten years. Pick the tier by the capital you can genuinely lock up and the years you actually want — and re-verify every figure on mm2h.gov.my before you commit, because the portal, not any guide, is the programme's source of truth.
