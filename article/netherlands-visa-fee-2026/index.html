The €90 Schengen visa fee

The short-stay Schengen (Type C) visa for the Netherlands costs €90 for adults in 2026, set by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. This is the visa used for tourism, business trips and family visits of up to 90 days in any 180-day period. It is paid at the mission or VFS centre when the application is submitted — applications from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh all go through this route, since none of those nationalities is visa-free for Schengen. For the documents the fee accompanies, see our Netherlands visa document checklist.

US and UK citizens do not need this visa at all: they may visit the Netherlands visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Our full route breakdown is in the Netherlands visa and entry requirements guide.

Fees for children

Children aged 6–11 pay a reduced Schengen fee of €45 (2026), and children under 6 are free. Every traveller — including infants — needs a separate application and appointment; there is no family-rate single application.

Residence permit fees: €423 and the 2026 indexation

Stays over 90 days are decided by the IND (Immigratie- en Naturalisatiedienst) at ind.nl, not the mission — and the fee schedule is different. The 2026 application fee for Highly Skilled Migrant (HSM) and EU Blue Card residence permits is €423, indexed +4.4% from 1 January 2026. These routes are employer-driven: the employer must be an IND-recognized sponsor. The recognized-sponsor requirement matters for the fee too — an HSM application filed through a non-recognized sponsor fails before any fee discussion matters.

For salary context: the 2026 HSM thresholds are €5,942 per month (age 30+), €4,357 (under 30), with a reduced €3,122 rate and a reduced Blue Card threshold of €4,754. Full work-route detail is in our Netherlands work permit guide.

Fees this guide deliberately does not print

We do not publish a figure for the 2026 student residence-permit fee or the monthly study funds norm (normbedrag), because published sources conflict and neither could be verified against ind.nl. The study norm is revised every 1 January. For students, the university acts as sponsor and files the IND application — confirm the current fee and norm in writing from the university's immigration desk or ind.nl before budgeting. Our Netherlands student visa guide covers the student route.

How the fee is paid

Official fees are receipted at the mission, at VFS, or through the sponsor's IND portal — never to a personal bank account, never in crypto. Schengen biometrics (fingerprints and a photograph, VIS, 59 months) are captured at the first in-person appointment, and the €90 is paid alongside it. For residence routes, the recognized sponsor typically files and pays through the IND's employer or university portals.

Processing time matters more than the fee

The €90 Schengen decision takes around 15 calendar days in 2026 (up to 45 for complex cases). IND knowledge-and-talent residence decisions are mostly decided within one month (IND monthly figures, August 2026); student residence applications take around 60–90 days, longer in the May–August peak. The real cost of a Netherlands visa is rarely the fee — it is a booked flight against a missed timeline.

Fee scams to avoid

  • Anyone charging far above the €90/€423 official figures for the application itself is selling anxiety, not a visa. ind.nl and the mission are the only official channels.
  • No agent can guarantee IND approval or sell priority processing — the IND decides on the file, not on who submitted it.
  • Never pay a visa fee to a personal account — official fees are receipted at the mission, VFS or via the sponsor's IND portal.
  • Stale student figures. Blogs still circulate old study-fee and funds-norm numbers; verify on ind.nl because the norm resets every 1 January.

Before you pay

  • Confirm your route first — Schengen for ≤90 days; MVV + IND residence for longer (entry requirements guide)
  • Budget exactly €90 (adult) / €45 (6–11) / free (under 6) for Schengen
  • Confirm employer is an IND-recognized sponsor before any HSM fee discussion
  • Verify the student fee and funds norm on ind.nl — do not trust a blog figure
  • Pay only through official channels: mission, VFS, or the sponsor's IND portal
  • Allow 15 days (Schengen), ~1 month (work residence), 60–90 days (student)

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the Netherlands visa fee in 2026?

The Schengen Type C visa costs €90 for adults, €45 for children aged 6–11 and is free for children under 6 (2026, Ministry of Foreign Affairs). The Highly Skilled Migrant / EU Blue Card residence permit costs €423, indexed +4.4% from 1 January 2026.

How much is the Netherlands student visa fee in 2026?

This guide deliberately does not print a figure: published sources conflict and neither could be verified against ind.nl. The IND study fee and monthly funds norm are revised regularly — confirm the current figures on ind.nl or with your sponsoring university before budgeting.

Where do I pay the Netherlands visa fee?

Official fees are receipted at the mission, at VFS, or through the sponsor's IND portal. Never pay a visa fee to a personal bank account or crypto wallet — anyone asking you to is running a scam.

Can anyone guarantee my visa will be approved?

No. No agent can guarantee IND or mission approval or sell priority processing — the IND decides on the file, not on who submitted it. Anyone charging far above the €90/€423 official fees is selling anxiety, not a visa.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Budget €90 for an adult Schengen application (€45 for ages 6–11, free under 6) and €423 for an HSM or Blue Card residence permit in 2026 — and pay only through official channels. Do not budget a student residence fee from a blog figure; verify it on ind.nl. Fees are the cheap part of a Netherlands application: the expensive mistake is missing the 15-day (Schengen), one-month (work) or 60–90-day (student) processing window.
