This guide sets out the sequence and the current official checks that should be completed before paying large, non-refundable costs.

Current official context — checked 5 October 2026

Study immigration normally combines admission at a recognised institution, proof of financing and health-insurance/residence requirements. DAAD, Hochschulkompass, uni-assist and the university itself answer different questions; the immigration authority controls residence permission, while the institution controls admission.

Official page: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/types/studying

What the student route actually authorises

In Germany, the study route is student visa/residence permit for study. The immigration permission is tied to genuine study at an eligible institution and normally carries conditions about enrolment, academic progress and work. Admission and immigration are separate decisions: an offer from a university does not guarantee a visa, and a visa does not replace the institution's enrolment rules.

Start with the institution's recognition and the official immigration page. Confirm whether the applicant needs a visa before travel, whether a residence permit is issued after arrival, and whether the school must be on a government-approved list. Where family members are relevant, check the current dependant rules rather than assuming a spouse or child can automatically accompany the student.

Eligibility and financial evidence

A credible student application normally connects four things: a real course, academic preparation, financial capacity and a plausible study plan. German federal immigration authorities / Make it in Germany may require proof of tuition, living expenses, return travel, insurance or other maintenance. The exact figure can change, so record the amount and the date it was checked on the official site.

Financial evidence should be traceable. Large last-minute deposits, unexplained transfers or borrowed funds presented as savings can create credibility questions. Prepare a simple funds schedule showing the source, account holder, currency and what each amount will pay for.

Documents and application sequence

Build the file in the same order the decision-maker will assess it: identity; admission/enrolment; finances; language evidence if required; immigration history; health/character evidence where required; and family documents if relevant. Keep names, dates and addresses consistent across passports, forms, school records and bank evidence.

Do not upload a document simply because another country asks for it. Use the current checklist generated by the relevant official application system. If a document is not in an accepted language, follow the authority's translation rules.

Work while studying and after graduation

Work rights are not a universal student benefit. Germany may cap hours, distinguish term time from holidays, or restrict the types of work allowed. Some countries changed student work rules in 2026, so an old university blog can be materially wrong. Check the conditions printed on the permit and the current government page.

Post-study work is also a separate immigration decision. If Germany offers a graduate or job-search route, map its application deadline and eligibility before the student finishes the course. Do not assume that completing a degree automatically creates permanent residence.

What to verify before paying tuition

Before making a non-refundable payment, confirm the institution, course start date, immigration eligibility, refund policy if the visa is refused, accommodation deadlines and the latest visa processing guidance. Save a PDF or screenshot of the fee/refund terms that applied when you paid.

A sensible rule is to separate admission readiness from visa readiness. Both need to be strong before large irreversible costs are incurred.
