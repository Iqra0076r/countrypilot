Schengen Visa Cost 2026

The Schengen visa costs €90 in 2026 for adults and €45 for children aged 6–12. The full application cost — fees, mandatory insurance and what a refusal costs — explained.

Quick Answer

A Schengen short-stay visa costs €90 for adults and €45 for children aged 6 to 12 in 2026. The fee is collected when you apply and is not refunded on refusal — a fresh application means paying again. Children under 6 are exempt, and France’s long-stay visas carry separate fees. Mandatory travel insurance and any residence taxes sit on top of the fee.

The 2026 Schengen visa fee, in full

The standard 2026 Schengen short-stay fee is €90 for adults, set under the EU Visa Code fee schedule and charged once per application. Applicants aged 6 to 12 pay €45.

The fee is paid at the application stage and, crucially, it is non-refundable: a refusal means the money is gone, and a reapplication means paying again. Before spending a second fee, read the Schengen visa rejection reasons guide and match every ticked ground to its fix.

Who pays less — or nothing

Children aged 6 to 12 pay €45; children under 6 are exempt under the fee schedule France publishes. Each child still needs a separate passport and a separate visa application.

France’s long-stay visas (work, study, stays over 90 days) sit outside the short-stay fee: €99 in 2026, or €50 in Études en France countries — plus about €200 + €25 in residence taxes after arrival (see the France visa and entry requirements guide).

A refused application costs the same

If your Schengen application is refused, the €90 is not refunded. You may appeal under the national law of the refusing country, or reapply at any time — no ban, no waiting period — but an unchanged reapplication gets the same answer and costs another €90.

A refusal comes back on the standard EU refusal form (Annex VI to the Visa Code) with the grounds ticked — most often unproven purpose, insufficient funds, unreliable documents, or doubt you will leave in time. Match every tick to its fix before paying again; the Schengen visa rejection reasons guide walks through all twelve official grounds.

What the €90 does not cover

The application fee pays for the examination of your file — nothing else. Three costs every budget must add on top.

Travel medical insurance is a legal requirement, not an extra. Under Article 15 of the Visa Code it must cover at least €30,000 of emergency care, hospitalisation and repatriation across all Schengen states — a separate purchase, and non-compliant insurance means refusal.

Proof of means is separate from the fee. France works on a daily reference amount of €65 per day where prepaid accommodation is proven — money you must be able to show, not money you hand over.

Long-stay France applicants should add the post-arrival residence-permit tax and stamp duty (about €200 + €25) that the €99 visa fee does not include.

The decision timeline affects your total cost

Schengen decisions are normally made within 15 days, extendable to 45 days for complex cases. You can apply from 6 months before travel but no later than 15 days before — cut it close and a 45-day extension can force last-minute rebookings.

Apply early and the cost stays €90; apply late and the real cost is the rebooked itinerary — see France visa processing time 2026.

How and when you pay

The fee is paid at the application stage. France routes applications through France-Visas: complete the online form, book the appointment, then attend in person with biometrics (first-time applicants) and pay €90 (2026) — fingerprints and a photograph, stored in the Visa Information System for 59 months.

Prepare the file before you pay, not after: the France application document checklist covers the documents consulates expect — a clear, complete bundle avoids the refusal that would cost you a second fee.

Cost mistakes to avoid

  • Reapplying unchanged after a refusal. No cooling-off period exists, but an unchanged file gets the same answer — fix the ticked grounds before spending another €90.
  • Buying non-compliant insurance. Under €30,000 of cover, or cover not valid Schengen-wide, means refusal — and the fee is gone either way.
  • Forgetting France’s long-stay follow-on costs. The €99 visa fee is not the total: about €200 in residence-permit tax plus €25 stamp duty follow after arrival.
  • Applying at the 15-day minimum. A 45-day extension can strand a trip — rebooked travel costs far more than an early application.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Schengen visa cost in 2026?

The standard short-stay fee is €90 for adults and €45 for children aged 6 to 12; children under 6 are exempt. The fee is charged per application and is not refunded on refusal.

Is the Schengen visa fee refunded if my application is refused?

No. The €90 is not refunded. You may appeal under the refusing country's national law or reapply at any time, but a fresh application means paying again — so fix the refusal grounds first.

Does the €90 include travel insurance?

No. Insurance is a separate mandatory purchase: at least €30,000 of emergency care, hospitalisation and repatriation cover across all Schengen states under Article 15 of the Visa Code.

How much is France’s long-stay visa fee in 2026?

€99 — or €50 in countries covered by the Études en France procedure — plus about €200 in residence-permit tax and €25 stamp duty payable after arrival.

When should I apply for a Schengen visa?

From 6 months before travel, no later than 15 days before. Decisions normally take 15 days but can stretch to 45 for complex cases.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

The 2026 Schengen visa fee is €90 for adults and €45 for children aged 6 to 12 — paid per application and never refunded on refusal. Budget the mandatory €30,000 insurance separately, remember France’s long-stay follow-on taxes if that is your route, and apply early: a rushed reapplication is where the real extra cost appears.
