Singapore ONE Pass 2026: S$30K Salary Route Explained

Singapore's Overseas Networks & Expertise Pass is the city-state's bet that the world's top talent would rather be judged on a pay slip than a points grid: S$30,000 a month, a five-year personalized pass, no employer sponsor, no COMPASS assessment. But the salary test is stricter than it looks — twelve consecutive months, one employer only, fixed pay — and the renewal at year five is where applications actually die. Here's how the pass really works, who the achievements route is for, and what the new AI & Tech track opening in January 2027 changes.

The ONE Pass requires a fixed monthly salary of at least S$30,000 for the 12 consecutive months before application (or a prospective Singapore role at that level with an established company), or outstanding achievements in sports, arts and culture, or academia and research. It runs five years, renews for five, and skips the Employment Pass COMPASS points system entirely.

Why Singapore set the bar at S$30,000: the COMPASS it skips

To understand the ONE Pass, you have to understand what it replaces. A standard Employment Pass in 2026 runs a two-stage gauntlet published by the Ministry of Manpower: first, a qualifying salary benchmarked to the top third of local professional, managerial, executive and technician salaries by age — S$5,600 a month for most sectors (S$6,200 in financial services), rising with age to S$10,700 at 45 and above — and second, the COMPASS points framework, where the application must score at least 40 points across salary competitiveness, qualifications, workforce diversity and local employment support. Miss stage one and the points don't matter; the employer also has to advertise the role under the Fair Consideration Framework before applying. It is a system built to make employers prove a foreign hire is justified.

The ONE Pass is the opposite philosophy: instead of justifying the hire, you prove you are the kind of person Singapore wants regardless of any specific job. The price of that trust is S$30,000 a month in fixed salary — roughly five times the Employment Pass floor — and in exchange the pass is personalized rather than employer-tied. You can move between employers, hold concurrent roles, and start and operate businesses without reapplying for a new pass each time. No sponsor, no COMPASS grid, no job advertisement. The S$30,000 figure is not arbitrary; it is the number at which Singapore decided the points system becomes redundant.

Note: "Fixed monthly salary" is a defined term at MOM — it means the guaranteed base, not total compensation with bonuses and allowances. If your S$30,000 includes a large variable component, the fixed portion is what counts.

The 12-month salary test and the "one employer only" rule

MOM's eligibility page states the salary test with unusual precision, and every clause matters. You must have earned a fixed monthly salary of at least S$30,000, or its equivalent in foreign currency, for the 12 consecutive months leading up to the date of application. "Consecutive" does the heavy lifting: a single dip below the line in month seven restarts the clock. There is no averaging across a good year, no credit for a signing bonus that pushed one quarter up.

Then there is the rule that surprises even well-advised applicants: if you drew salaries from more than one employer during those 12 months, the qualifying S$30,000 must come from one employer only. MOM illustrates it with a small table that leaves no room for interpretation — S$20,000 from Company A plus S$10,000 from Company B totals S$30,000 and fails; S$30,000 from one employer plus anything from another passes. For consultants, fractional executives and portfolio-career people, this is the clause that kills applications: diversified income, however impressive in total, does not qualify.

The employer side of the test has its own gate. If you are applying on overseas employment, the company must be "established" — MOM's definition is a market capitalisation of at least US$500 million or annual revenue of at least US$200 million, with combined global-office figures considered case by case. If you are applying on prospective employment in Singapore, the Singapore-based company must meet the same established-company test, and the employment must start within 6 months of the application — MOM tells you to apply only when that timing holds. Existing work pass holders need at least a year working in Singapore before switching onto the pass through the local route.

The achievements route: for people money can't describe

Not every person Singapore wants is earning S$30,000 a month, and MOM knows it. The eligibility page carries a second door: you may apply without meeting the salary criteria if you have outstanding achievements in sports, arts and culture, or academia and research. This is the route for the Olympic medallist, the prize-winning novelist, the professor whose papers define a field — people whose value is obvious and whose pay slips are irrelevant.

The assessment here is deliberately human. MOM states that candidates are assessed case by case, and that it works with other agencies — the Ministry of Culture, Community and Youth for sports and arts, the Ministry of Education, the National Research Foundation and A*STAR for academia and research — to review applications holistically on the basis of the work and key achievements each applicant has accomplished. There is no published points table for this route, no salary to benchmark, no formula to game. That is both its promise and its risk: the dossier is everything, and it must read as a body of work, not a CV. If you are considering this route, the question is not "do I qualify" but "can a stranger at a ministry understand in twenty minutes why my achievements are outstanding" — and the answer should be assembled with evidence, not adjectives.

The 5-year renewal trap: what MOM checks at year five

The ONE Pass is granted for five years, and the length is part of the pitch — no annual re-justification, no employer to re-sponsor you. But the pass is renewable for five years at a time only if you meet one of two tests, and the first one is where most holders should be looking from day one: you must have earned a fixed monthly salary of at least S$30,000 on average over the past five years in Singapore. Note the shift from the application test — twelve consecutive months at S$30,000 becomes a five-year average. A year of lower earnings during a career break, a startup gamble or a research stint drags the average, and there is no partial credit.

The second renewal route is the entrepreneur's door: you started and are operating a Singapore-based company that employs at least five locals, each earning a gross monthly salary at or above the prevailing Employment Pass minimum qualifying salary. This is Singapore telling you what it wants the pass to produce — either sustained top-tier earnings in its economy, or a company that hires Singaporeans at professional wages. You may apply to renew up to six months before expiry, which gives you time to fix a shortfall — but only if you've been tracking the numbers. The renewal trap isn't that the test is hidden; it's that five years is long enough to forget you're being measured.

ONE Pass vs Employment Pass: the real comparison

For a senior tech professional choosing between the two, the comparison isn't really about eligibility — if you clear S$30,000 a month for a year, the Employment Pass was never in doubt. It's about what kind of life in Singapore you want. The Employment Pass is employer-sponsored: change jobs and a new application goes in; the pass is typically granted for up to two years initially and the employer owns the process. The ONE Pass is yours: move between employers, take concurrent appointments, start companies, all without touching your immigration status. For founders, advisors and executives with portfolio careers, that independence is the entire value proposition.

The trade is duration against flexibility. The Employment Pass, for all its COMPASS friction, can be held at far lower salaries and renews on the employer's sponsorship — a S$8,000-a-month specialist faces no immigration drama at all. The ONE Pass demands you stay in the top income tier or build a qualifying company, forever, to keep it. And the Employment Pass has one advantage the ONE Pass can't match: it is the ordinary, well-trodden path, processed in about ten business days for local entities, with a vast body of precedent. The ONE Pass is the premium product with premium maintenance.

The 2027 AI & Tech track: a new door opens

The most interesting development on MOM's ONE Pass page isn't the current criteria — it's the dated announcement sitting underneath them. From 28 January 2027, candidates may apply on a dedicated AI and Tech track, and its design fixes the exact complaint tech people have had about the salary route: that base salary understates total compensation in an industry built on equity.

Under the track, you still need at least S$30,000 a month for the 12 consecutive months before application — but only S$22,500 of it must be fixed monthly salary. The remainder may include vested non-cash components such as employee stock options or employee share ownership plans, subject to assessment. You also need at least five cumulative years, within the past ten, in founder or C-suite roles at a tech firm or in technical roles such as senior software engineer — and your current or most recent employment must be at a tech company, a tech division, or a tech venture capital firm meeting scale thresholds: US$500 million in valuation or market capitalisation, US$200 million in annual revenue, US$500 million in assets under management for VCs, or US$30 million in funds raised for tech startups.

For AI engineers and technical founders whose wealth is in vested equity rather than base salary, this is a materially easier door than the standard route — and a signal of where Singapore's talent strategy is heading. If your compensation is equity-heavy and your timeline is flexible, it may be worth timing an application for after the track opens rather than forcing the standard salary test now.

Who should not apply

The ONE Pass is a superb product for a narrow audience, and honesty about the edges saves everyone time. If your S$30,000 is assembled from multiple employers, don't apply — the one-employer rule is explicit and unforgiving. If your salary history has a dip anywhere in the last twelve months, wait until you have twelve clean consecutive months. If you can't document the fixed-salary component separately from bonuses and allowances, get your employer's payroll records in order first, because MOM will look at the fixed figure.

And if you earn well but not at this level — the S$8,000 to S$20,000 band where most senior tech professionals actually sit — the Employment Pass is not a consolation prize. It is the right product: lower bar, employer-sponsored, renewable, and for most careers in Singapore, entirely sufficient. The ONE Pass is for people whose income or achievements put them in the top fraction of a percent. Applying below that bar doesn't get you considered more carefully; it gets you refused, and the refusal is on your record.

Frequently Asked Questions

What salary do I need for the Singapore ONE Pass?

A fixed monthly salary of at least S$30,000, or its equivalent in foreign currency, for the 12 consecutive months before you apply — or a prospective Singapore role at that salary with an established company, with employment starting within 6 months.

How long is the ONE Pass valid?

Five years, renewable for five years at a time. Renewal requires an average fixed monthly salary of at least S$30,000 over the past five years in Singapore, or a Singapore-based company you started and operate that employs at least five locals at or above the Employment Pass minimum qualifying salary.

Can I get the ONE Pass without meeting the salary requirement?

Yes, through the achievements route: outstanding achievements in sports, arts and culture, or academia and research. MOM assesses these holistically and case by case, working with agencies including MCCY, MOE, NRF and A*STAR.

Does the ONE Pass require COMPASS points?

No. The ONE Pass sits outside the Employment Pass framework entirely, so the COMPASS 40-point assessment does not apply. The S$30,000 salary bar is, in effect, the substitute for the points system.

What counts as an established company for the ONE Pass?

A company with a market capitalisation of at least US$500 million or annual revenue of at least US$200 million. Combined figures from the entire global office can be considered on a case-by-case basis.

What is the new ONE Pass AI and Tech track?

From 28 January 2027, tech candidates can apply on S$30,000 a month where at least S$22,500 is fixed salary and the rest may be vested non-cash such as ESOPs, with five cumulative years in founder, C-suite or technical roles and employment at a qualifying tech company or venture capital firm.

Bottom Line

Singapore's ONE Pass trades the Employment Pass's COMPASS points grid for a single brutal number — S$30,000 a month in fixed salary from one employer for twelve straight months — and in return gives you five years of sponsor-free, employer-independent residency; if your income is equity-heavy or your achievements are the story, wait for the achievements route or the January 2027 AI & Tech track rather than forcing the salary test.
