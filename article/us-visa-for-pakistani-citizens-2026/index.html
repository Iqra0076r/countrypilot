US Visa for Pakistani Citizens 2026: B-1/B-2 Requirements, Fee, and Interview

Pakistani passport holders need a US visa for every visit — no ESTA, no visa waiver. This is the complete 2026 route: fee, form, interview, and realistic timing.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20267 min read

Pakistan is not in the US Visa Waiver Program, so Pakistani citizens need a visa for every US visit — tourism, business, family, study, all of it. For most travellers that means the B-1/B-2 visitor visa: a $185 fee, Form DS-160, and an interview at a US embassy or consulate. This guide sets out the 2026 requirements, the realistic timeline, and the student route.

Quick Answer

Pakistani citizens need a B-1/B-2 visitor visa for tourism or business: the $185 MRV fee (2026), Form DS-160, and an interview at a US embassy or consulate. The visa is often issued for up to 10 years with multiple entries, but each admission is typically up to 6 months (I-94). Interview waits run ~2 weeks to 13+ months by post — check the Global Visa Wait Times tool for your post.

The B-1/B-2 route, step by step

The process runs in a fixed order. 1) Complete Form DS-160 online — every answer must match your documents, because contradictions surface at the interview. 2) Pay the $185 MRV fee (as of 2026). 3) Schedule the interview at the embassy or consulate. 4) Check the Global Visa Wait Times tool for your post's current queue. 5) Attend with a consistent file: passport, DS-160 confirmation, fee receipt, and evidence of purpose and ties — employment, property, family, travel history. 6) After approval, check whether your nationality owes a visa issuance (reciprocity) fee before arranging passport return.

The interview: what it actually tests

The interview is short and focused on two questions: is the purpose genuine, and will you leave on time? Officers look for a coherent story — a purpose that matches the documents, employment or business anchoring you in Pakistan, and a travel history of leaving countries when you said you would. The $185 fee covers the application, not the outcome; a refused application is not refunded, and fees change by regulation, so verify the live schedule before paying.

Timing: the queue is the timeline

For Pakistani applicants the interview wait is the decisive variable — the post-by-post range runs from weeks to 13+ months, and it moves weekly. Enter the queue early (DS-160, fee, scheduling done), read your post's figure in the Global Visa Wait Times tool, and do not book non-refundable travel until the visa is in hand. See our full US processing-time guide and interview wait-times guide for the detail.

The student route: F-1

Study requires F-1 status: a Form I-20 from the school, SEVIS registration with the $350 I-901 fee (2026), and the $185 MRV fee for the visa application — $535 in government fees. The school's published cost of attendance is the figure used for the financial-evidence requirement, so the funds file must match it. Start the SEVIS/I-20 track and the visa-interview track together; they run in parallel, not in sequence.

Transit through the US

Even an airside-only connection through a US airport needs a C-1 transit visa for Pakistani citizens — at $185 (2026). There is no sterile-transit exception to plan around; if the itinerary touches the US, the transit visa is part of the budget.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Pakistanis need a visa for the USA?

Yes — for every visit. Pakistan is not in the Visa Waiver Program, so there is no ESTA route.

How much is the US visa fee for Pakistanis in 2026?

$185 MRV fee for B-1/B-2 (2026); students add the $350 SEVIS fee.

How long is a US visitor visa valid for Pakistanis?

Often issued for up to 10 years, multiple entries — but each admission is typically up to 6 months on the I-94.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Pakistani citizens the US visa is a $185 application, a DS-160 that matches the documents, an interview testing purpose and ties, and a queue that rewards early scheduling — with the F-1 student route adding SEVIS and the school's I-20 to the stack.
