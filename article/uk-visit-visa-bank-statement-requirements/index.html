UK Visit Visa Bank Statement Requirements: The 6-Month Rule Explained

UKVI publishes no minimum balance and no law says "six months". What GOV.UK actually requires of bank statements — origin of funds, patterns, and why the 6-month convention exists.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 12, 20266 min read

Every UK visit-visa checklist demands six months of bank statements — yet no Immigration Rule mentions six months, and GOV.UK has never published a minimum balance. This guide separates the written rule from the working convention: what the Home Office actually requires, why six months became standard, and what a caseworker really reads in your statements.

Quick Answer

There is no official minimum balance and no rule fixing a statement period for a UK Standard Visitor visa. GOV.UK's supporting-documents guide asks for financial documents showing access to sufficient funds — "such as bank statements which detail the origin of the funds held". Origin and pattern matter more than the total. Six months is the standard window because it shows your normal income cycle, not a balance assembled for the application. The visa costs £135, you can apply up to 3 months ahead, and decisions currently run at 3 weeks.

What the written rules actually say

The legal test sits in the Immigration Rules (Appendix V: Visitor), and it is about you, not a number. You must be a genuine visitor: leave at the end of your visit, support yourself and any dependants during the trip, pay for your return or onward journey and the visit's other costs, and not live in the UK through frequent or successive visits. GOV.UK's supporting-documents guide then explains how applicants evidence this — and its financial-evidence passage is the most misquoted paragraph in UK visa advice. It asks for documents showing sufficient funds are available, "such as: bank statements which detail the origin of the funds held; building society books which detail the origin of the funds held; proof of earnings, such as a letter from your employer."

Notice what is absent: no balance figure, no savings threshold, no statement period. UKVI assesses funds in context — against your income, circumstances, and the cost of the trip you described. That is why a modest balance with a clean history routinely outperforms a large balance that appeared from nowhere.

So why does everyone say "six months"?

Because six months is the shortest window that reliably shows what a caseworker needs. First, a pattern: income arriving regularly, ordinary costs leaving — proof the account is a real financial life, not a stage set. Second, a build-up: savings accumulating gradually reads as genuine planning; a flat account suddenly holding trip money reads as borrowing. Third, consistency: the form asks your income and trip cost, your employer letter states your salary — six months lets the caseworker check those stories against each other. Treat it as the evidence convention that makes the written rule provable — not a rule itself.

What a caseworker reads in your statements

  • Origin of funds. Can every significant credit be traced to a legitimate, documented source — salary, business income, an explained transfer? This is the guide's own wording, and the first filter.
  • Regularity. Income arriving on a cycle matching your stated employment or business. Irregular unexplained cash deposits invite the conclusion that the balance was parked for the application — money that leaves the day the visa is decided.
  • Proportionality. The stated trip cost set against your income and the balance's history. A trip costing many months of earnings, funded by an account that normally holds very little, needs an explanation the file rarely provides.
  • Consistency. Figures matching your form, payslips and employer letter. One contradiction licenses doubt about all of it. Statements should also run up close to the application date — a perfect history ending three months ago leaves the obvious question unanswered.

Assemble the file in that order and the statements stop being a formality: run them through our visa document checklist beside your employer letter and trip budget, until the whole file tells one story. One practical note: a large deposit is fine if its source is documented — a property sale, a matured bond, an inheritance. Unexplained is what damages.

If someone else is paying

A sponsor's money is legitimate — the guide provides for it expressly — but it changes the evidence, not the standard. GOV.UK asks sponsors' files to show what support is provided and whether it extends to dependants, how, that the sponsor has enough for themselves and their own dependants too, your relationship, and that the sponsor is legally in the UK where applicable. Their statements face the same origin-of-funds reading as yours: a generous sponsor with an unexplained balance helps less than a modest one with clean paperwork.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Parking money for the application. A large recent deposit your income cannot explain is the most common financial cause of refusal — visible precisely because the statement covers months, not days.
  • Printing only the summary page. A balance certificate without transactions shows a total, not an origin — the one thing the guide asks for.
  • Statements not in English or Welsh without a verified translation. GOV.UK requires a full translation with the translator's confirmation, name, signature and contact details.
  • Mismatched trip costs. A form that prices the trip at one figure and statements that could never fund it — or fund it only by emptying the account — undermines the genuine-visitor case.
  • Applying on a stale file. Statements, letters and bookings should all be current at submission — our United Kingdom country guide sequences the full application.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Is six months of bank statements legally mandatory for a UK visit visa?

No. No Immigration Rule and no GOV.UK page fixes a statement period. Six months is the standard window because it demonstrates income pattern, savings build-up and consistency — what the written "origin of funds" test requires. Shorter extracts are not automatically refused, but they leave the pattern unproven.

What is the minimum bank balance for a UK Standard Visitor visa?

There is none — UKVI publishes no minimum balance or savings threshold for visitors. Funds are assessed against your specific trip's cost and your circumstances: enough to support yourself and any dependants, pay for your return or onward journey, and remain credible against your income.

How much does the visa cost, and how long does it take?

£135 for a stay of up to 6 months; apply from 3 months before travel. UKVI's published processing time for Standard Visitor applications outside the UK is currently 3 weeks.

Can I use a parent's or sponsor's bank statements instead of my own?

Yes, with the sponsor evidence GOV.UK lists: what support is provided, how, your relationship, proof the sponsor can afford it alongside their own commitments, and — if they are in the UK — proof they are there legally. Your own ties are still assessed; sponsorship funds the trip, not the genuine-visitor test.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

The "6-month rule" is a convention with a purpose: GOV.UK's real requirement is statements that detail the origin of the funds held, inside a genuine-visitor case built on leaving at the end of the visit and affording the trip described. Give the caseworker six months of clean, consistent, explainable banking, and the missing official threshold stops mattering — you have answered the question the rules ask.
