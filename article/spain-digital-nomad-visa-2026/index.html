Spain Digital Nomad Visa in 2026

Spain's digital nomad visa threshold is €2,442/month in 2026 — 200% of the €1,221 SMI. Who it covers, how it's set, and what to verify before applying.

What the digital-nomad threshold is

Spain's defining 2026 fact for remote workers is a number: €2,442 per month for the main applicant. It is an income threshold — proof of earnings — not an application fee, and the two should never be confused when budgeting. The verified material this guide is built from publishes the main-applicant figure only, so treat any family add-on number quoted on a third-party blog as unconfirmed until the competent consulate states it. For the broader entry picture, read this alongside the Spain Visa & Entry Requirements Guide.

Where the €2,442 figure comes from

The threshold is derived from the 2026 minimum wage (SMI): €1,221/month paid in 14 payments, set by Real Decreto 126/2026. The digital-nomad threshold sits at 200% of that monthly figure (€1,221 × 2 = €2,442). Because the SMI is re-set by royal decree each year, the threshold moves with it — and 2025 figures, based on the old SMI, are stale. Recheck the current SMI on the official pages before finalizing any application budget.

The legal framework around the route

Spain's immigration authority is split: the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (exteriores.gob.es) runs visas through consulates, while the Ministry of Inclusion, Social Security and Migration (inclusion.gob.es) governs residence and work authorizations decided by the Oficina de Extranjería. The defining recent event is the new Reglamento de Extranjería (Royal Decree 1155/2024), in force since 20 May 2025 — the largest immigration reform in years. Under it, initial residence and work permits run 1 year, renewable in 4-year blocks. Know which institution owns your question before you ask it — the consulate is the front door for the visa, the extranjería office is the back office for residence.

Fees and timelines: what is not published

Two things this guide deliberately does not print. First, no separately published application fee for the digital nomad route appears in the verified 2026 fee schedules — confirm the consular fee with the competent consulate rather than budgeting from a blog. Second, no officially published processing time exists for Spanish work and residence authorizations — the Oficina de Extranjería decides, with timelines varying by province and season. Any site quoting a precise number for either is guessing. Plan around the consulate's current estimates, not a third party's.

Insurance compliance for residence files

Residence files live or die on insurance. Spanish consulates are notably strict: the policy must offer 100% coverage with no copayments, no deductibles and no waiting periods (2026) — policies worded as 'travel insurance' are routinely rejected for long-stay applications. Broader residency-compliant policies range €600–4,500/year depending on age (2026). For long stays, also check the policy's maximum duration per trip and any return-home requirements before committing. The Spain Travel Insurance & Health Guide walks through choosing a policy consulates accept.

After arrival: the TIE

A national-visa holder is admitted for the visa's duration and must apply for the TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero) at the Oficina de Extranjería/Policía Nacional after arrival — the visa alone does not complete residence. Under the 2025 Reglamento, the 1-year initial / 4-year renewal rhythm should be diarized from day one, so renewal deadlines never creep up unannounced.

A Schengen visa never authorizes work

Do not test remote work on a tourist entry: a Schengen visa never authorizes work in Spain, and Schengen stays are capped at 90 days in any 180-day period, counted across the whole area. Passports must have been issued within the last 10 years and remain valid at least 3 months beyond intended departure from the Schengen area. Entry as a remote worker needs the proper route — the short-stay route is for actual tourism.

Applying through official channels

Spain offers no eVisa: applications are made in person at the consulate or BLS centre, with first-time biometrics — fingerprints and a photograph, stored in VIS for 59 months. Track consular files at sutramiteconsular.maec.es. Exteriores.gob.es and the consulate are the only official channels; official fees are receipted Modelo 790 forms — never pay a 'visa fee' to a personal account. The Spain Complete Country Travel Guide covers practical arrival planning once the visa is in hand.

A final checklist

  • €2,442/month income evidence for the main applicant (2026 figure)
  • Threshold tied to the 2026 SMI (€1,221/month, 14 payments, Real Decreto 126/2026) — re-verify when the SMI updates
  • Compliant 100% insurance policy (no copay/deductible/waiting) — not 'travel insurance'
  • TIE application planned for after arrival
  • 1-year initial / 4-year renewal rhythm diarized
  • No reliance on unpublished processing times — use the consulate's current estimate
  • Passport within the 10-year/3-month Schengen rule
  • Consulate contact saved for emergencies

What is Spain's digital nomad visa income requirement in 2026?

Where does the €2,442 figure come from?

Can I work remotely on a Spain tourist visa?

No. A Schengen visa never authorizes work in Spain, and stays are capped at 90 days in any 180-day period across the whole Schengen area. Entry as a remote worker needs the proper route.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Spain's digital nomad route hinges on one verified number — €2,442/month in 2026, 200% of the €1,221 SMI in 14 payments — and that number moves when the minimum wage moves. Build the file around it: genuinely compliant insurance, the post-arrival TIE, and the consulate's current answers on fees and timelines, because this guide publishes neither an invented fee nor an invented timeline. Use exteriores.gob.es and the extranjería system as your authorities, and leave the Schengen short-stay route for actual tourism.
