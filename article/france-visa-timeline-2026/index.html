France Visa Timeline 2026

The France visa timeline in 2026: 15 working days for Schengen short-stay decisions, around 30 days for work visas and 15 days to 3 weeks for students — plus the booking windows that actually control the trip.

Quick Answer

The France visa timeline in 2026 runs on two clocks. France decides most Schengen short-stay visas in about 15 working days, with complex cases extendable to 45 days. Long-stay work visas average around 30 days, and student visas take 15 days to 3 weeks — longer during the June–August rush. Short-stay applications can be filed from 6 months before travel (no later than 15 days ahead); long-stay applications cannot be filed more than 3 months before arrival. All appointments are booked online through France-Visas, which is now mandatory.

Short-stay Schengen: about 15 working days

France’s 2026 guidance for Schengen short-stay visas (tourism, business or family visits up to 90 days) is a decision in around 15 working days. Complex cases can be extended to 45 days — the same ceiling the EU-wide rules set for Schengen decisions, which normally arrive within 15 days.

First-time applicants attend in person with biometrics (fingerprints and a photograph, stored in the Visa Information System for 59 months) and pay the €90 fee (2026). The fee itself does not change the timeline — see Schengen visa cost 2026 for the full cost breakdown.

Long-stay work visas: around 30 days

French long-stay work visas — including the passeport talent for highly skilled profiles — average around 30 days for a decision. The long-stay visa fee is €99 in 2026 (€50 in countries covered by the Études en France procedure), with residence-permit tax and stamp duty of about €200 + €25 payable after arrival.

Student visas: 15 days to 3 weeks

French student visas take 15 days to 3 weeks in 2026, stretching during the June–August rush when applications peak. Student files now stand or fall on the financial-resources evidence: the threshold rose to €877.50 per month (€10,530 for 12 months) effective 1 August 2026, and consulates check the arithmetic — the France income requirement overview breaks down the current figures.

When you are allowed to apply

The filing windows are strict. Short-stay: apply from 6 months before travel, and no later than 15 days before. Long-stay: applications cannot be filed more than 3 months before arrival. Filing a long-stay application too early, or a short-stay one too late, puts the timeline — and the trip — at risk.

Build the document work into the plan as well: the France application documents guide describes what a complete file looks like, and a complete file is what keeps a decision inside the standard window.

Appointments run through France-Visas

Appointments are booked online through France-Visas — this is now mandatory, and peak-season slots go early. The France-Visas Wizard tailors the document list to your nationality and purpose; in the research hierarchy it overrides everything else, so start there and treat third-party timelines as background only.

What stretches a decision toward 45 days

The extension to 45 days exists for complex cases — files that need closer examination. The delays you can control are simpler: the wrong visa type (Schengen covers stays of 90 days or fewer; anything longer needs the long-stay route), non-compliant insurance (under €30,000 of cover, or cover that is not valid Schengen-wide, means refusal rather than delay), and filing inside the June–August student rush without accounting for it.

Keep the basics tight — passport issued within the last 10 years, valid 3+ months beyond departure, with blank pages — and re-check the France visa and entry requirements guide for the full entry framework before you file.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

In 2026, plan on 15 working days for a France Schengen visa, around 30 days for a long-stay work visa, and 15 days to 3 weeks for a student visa — with the student rush stretching June to August. File inside the correct window (6 months and 15 days out for short-stay; 3 months out for long-stay), book the France-Visas appointment early, and arrive with a complete file: the appointment date usually matters as much as the decision window.
