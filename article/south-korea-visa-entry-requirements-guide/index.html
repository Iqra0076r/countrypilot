How to use this South Korea visa and entry guide

South Korea runs two parallel entry systems and mixing them up is the classic error: visa-waiver nationals need a K-ETA (electronic travel authorization) before boarding, while everyone else needs a conventional visa from a Korean mission. There is no visa on arrival. The controlling portals are the Korea Visa Portal (visa.go.kr) for e-visas and applications, k-eta.go.kr for the travel authorization, and hikorea.go.kr for immigration matters after arrival.

Two 2026 developments matter most. First, the Ministry of Justice extended the temporary K-ETA exemption through 31 December 2026 for previously exempt nationalities — check whether you actually need a K-ETA before paying the KRW 10,000 fee. Second, D-2 student rules were eased for 16 designated technical majors on a trial basis (TOPIK Level 3+ exempts the bank-balance proof; in-semester work rises to 35 hours per week). This guide was verified against official Korean sources in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport must be valid for the entire period of stay — South Korea does not impose a blanket six-months-beyond-stay rule, but airlines and officers expect a passport that comfortably covers the trip, and a passport expiring mid-stay creates problems at extension or departure. Carry at least one blank page.

The practical risk is the K-ETA linkage: the authorization is tied to the passport number, so renewing the passport voids the K-ETA and you must reapply. If your passport is close to expiry, renew before applying for the K-ETA or visa.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Work the routes in order. First, K-ETA: visa-waiver nationals must hold an approved K-ETA before boarding — KRW 10,000, valid 3 years with multiple entries, decisions commonly within 24–48 hours (apply at least 72 hours ahead). Note the exemption extension through 31 December 2026 for previously exempt nationalities: confirm your current status on k-eta.go.kr rather than assuming. Second, the conventional short-term visa: visa-required nationals apply at a Korean mission — single entry up to 90 days costs USD 40 (fees vary by nationality; e.g., Indian nationals pay INR 5,400 for long-stay single entry effective 1 August 2026). Third, long-stay routes (D-2 study, E-series work) need a Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance before the visa application.

Transit and onward travel

South Korea allows airside transit without a visa for most nationalities only if you stay in the transit area and hold onward boarding passes — but visa-waiver nationals transiting still need the K-ETA question resolved, because the exemption extension and waiver status determine whether you may enter the transit flow without prior authorization.

If your connection requires passing immigration (terminal change, overnight layover, separate tickets), you need the appropriate entry permission for your nationality — K-ETA or visa — exactly as if South Korea were the destination. Check the transit rules for your specific itinerary on the Korea Visa Portal before booking split tickets.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Border officers in South Korea decide admission on the spot, and a visa in the passport is permission to request entry, not a guarantee of it. Keep the return or onward ticket, the address of your first night's stay, and evidence of funds within reach — officers most often ask how the trip is funded and when you plan to leave.

Answer plainly and keep every answer consistent with what you put on the application form. If someone else is funding the trip, carry their details too, but understand the underlying test: officers are assessing whether you will leave when you say you will.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Check health-entry rules for South Korea before you book anything non-refundable: requirements turn on where you have recently been, not just the passport you hold. Yellow-fever certificates are the most common trigger across Africa and South America, and some countries demand proof at the border even for transit.

Beyond certificates, look at the destination's health reality — malaria prophylaxis, dengue precautions, and whether your travel insurance covers medical evacuation. None of this is visa paperwork in the strict sense, but arriving without a required certificate produces the same outcome as arriving without a visa: refusal.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Whether or not South Korea makes insurance a visa condition, treat it as one for yourself. Medical bills for visitors are charged at full private rates in most countries, and a single hospital admission can exceed the entire trip budget several times over.

Match the policy to the trip: medical expenses with a high limit, emergency evacuation and repatriation, and coverage for every activity you will actually do. If South Korea sets a minimum coverage figure for the visa, buy above it — minimums are entry thresholds, not adequate protection.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

The stamp or record you receive on arrival in South Korea — not the visa in your passport — sets how long you may legally stay. Check the admitted-until date before leaving the immigration hall; the visa's validity period and the permitted stay are different things, and confusing them is one of the most common causes of accidental overstay.

If the permitted stay is shorter than your plans, address it through official channels before the date expires — overstaying, even by days, can void the visa and complicate every future application to South Korea and elsewhere.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A visitor entry to South Korea does not authorize work — not for a local employer, not remotely for a foreign one while you are physically present, not 'unpaid help' at a family business. Work requires a work-authorized category, applied for and approved before you start.

Study is equally separate: enrollment needs a student or residence status with the institution's sponsorship or admission letter in the file. Entering as a tourist intending to convert to work or study later is misrepresentation in most systems and can end with removal rather than a status change.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Children need their own travel documents for South Korea — their own passport, and where visas apply, their own application. A child listed in a parent's passport does not inherit the parent's visa.

Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the absent parent(s) with contact details, plus the birth certificate where surnames differ. Dual nationals should check which passport South Korea expects them to use: some countries require entry on their own passport if you hold their citizenship, and using the 'wrong' one can cause refusal.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

A reliable sequence for South Korea: 1) Confirm the correct route for your nationality on Korea Visa Portal (Ministry of Justice) — visa-free, e-visa, or consular visa. 2) Assemble documents before paying any fee: passport, photographs to specification, itinerary, funds evidence. 3) Apply through the official channel only, never a copycat site. 4) Track the application and do not book non-refundable travel until the visa is in hand.

Build slack into the timeline: add two weeks to whatever processing estimate you find, and keep copies of everything submitted. If an interview or biometrics appointment is required, that appointment — not the published processing time — is usually the critical path.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Applying for the wrong category — the requirements you meet must match the visa you request, not the one with the lower fee.
  • Copycat websites — unofficial portals that charge several times the government fee for submitting the same form. Use Korea Visa Portal (Ministry of Justice) only.
  • Non-refundable bookings before approval — never commit money the consulate has not yet earned you the right to spend.
  • Inconsistent paperwork — dates, names and employment details must match across every document.
  • Assuming visa validity equals permitted stay — the admission stamp governs, not the visa foil.
  • Ignoring the funds test — 'I have money' without statements to prove it fails everywhere.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Strong applications to South Korea share one trait: every claim is evidenced. Employment is shown with a dated letter stating role, salary and approved leave — not just a business card. Funds are shown with 3–6 months of statements in your name — not a single screenshot. Ties to home are shown with leases, enrollment letters or family documents — not asserted in a cover letter alone.

Write the cover letter a stranger could follow: who you are, why you are going, what you will do each week, when you return, and how it is funded — each point pointing at an attached document. Officers process volume; clarity is persuasive.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

For South Korea, the only safe online channel is Korea Visa Portal (Ministry of Justice). Copycat domains mimic official portals closely — check the URL character by character, and never arrive at the 'official' site via a search ad.

After submitting, use the portal's own status tracking rather than emailing the mission for updates; consulates do not expedite because you ask. Keep the application reference, payment receipts and the final approval document together — you will need the approval at boarding and at the border.

Translations, certifications and document format

Beyond the mandatory items, the documents that actually decide South Korea applications are funds evidence and ties to home. Bank statements should run 3–6 months, show the account holder's name, and reflect a balance consistent with the trip's cost — a sudden large deposit just before applying raises more questions than it answers.

Translations: any document not in the mission's working language needs a competent translation; requirements vary, so check Korea Visa Portal (Ministry of Justice) rather than assuming. Keep originals and one full copy of everything submitted.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Biometric enrollment for South Korean visas follows the mission's procedure: applicants should expect fingerprints and a digital photograph to be captured at the visa application center or embassy, consistent with standard consular practice. Confirm the exact requirement with the mission where you apply, as procedures vary by post.

On arrival, immigration may verify identity against the visa record. Keep the passport used for the application — switching to a renewed passport mid-process breaks the linkage (as with the K-ETA) and requires reapplication.

Families and group travel

Each traveller to South Korea needs individual documentation — including children. Where electronic authorizations apply, every person needs their own approval; there are no family bundles, and a child's approval is not implied by the parent's.

When one family member faces a refusal, do not simply reapply with identical papers — address the refusal reason first. For groups, align the applications: same itinerary, consistent purpose statements, and appointments booked as close together as the system allows.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Customs is the second border after immigration in South Korea, and it has its own rules. Declare currency above the reporting threshold — declaration is not taxation, but failure to declare can mean seizure. Food, plants and animal products face the strictest controls in most countries; when in doubt, declare.

Medicines need original packaging and, for controlled substances, a prescription or doctor's letter. Check South Korea's prohibited list before packing: drones, satellite communicators and certain medications surprise travellers regularly.

Departure and future travel

Leave South Korea the way you entered: on time and documented. Keep boarding passes and entry/exit stamps — a clean travel history is an asset on every future visa application you will ever make.

If plans change mid-trip, act before the permitted stay expires: seek an extension through official channels where available, or depart and reapply. An overstay record must be declared on future applications to most countries and can void multi-entry visas.

How to research a route without getting misled

Research South Korea entry rules in a strict hierarchy. First, Korea Visa Portal (Ministry of Justice) — the official portal overrides everything else. Second, the specific embassy or consulate handling your nationality, for local procedures. Third, official advisory pages for the security context.

Treat forums, agent blogs and social media as leads, never answers: one traveller's smooth entry is not a rule, and procedures change without announcement. Anything promising 'guaranteed approval' is selling fiction.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid for the required period beyond your stay, with blank pages
  • Visa or authorization approved before non-refundable bookings — approval document printed and backed up
  • Return/onward ticket and first accommodation address accessible offline
  • Funds evidence and ties-to-home documents packed, not only on a phone
  • Travel insurance covering medical, evacuation and the full trip duration
  • Required health certificates (e.g., yellow fever) in the WHO booklet format
  • Medications in original packaging with prescriptions where needed
  • Cash declaration prepared if carrying above the threshold
  • Embassy/consulate emergency contact saved

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a K-ETA for South Korea in 2026?

It depends on your nationality and the exemption extension. Visa-waiver nationals normally need an approved K-ETA (KRW 10,000, valid 3 years) before boarding — but the Ministry of Justice extended the temporary K-ETA exemption through 31 December 2026 for previously exempt nationalities. Check your current status on k-eta.go.kr before paying; if you need one, apply at least 72 hours before departure.

How much is a South Korean student visa?

The D-2/D-4 long-stay single-entry visa fee is USD 60 on the Embassy of Korea fee schedule (2026), but fees vary by nationality — Indian nationals, for example, pay INR 5,400 effective 1 August 2026. Budget separately for the mandatory NHIS health insurance (KRW 79,320/month in 2026) and the proof-of-funds requirement (KRW 16–20 million for D-2).

How long does a Korean student visa take?

Typically 2–4 weeks at the embassy once the application is lodged — but the Certificate of Confirmation of Visa Issuance must be secured before the visa application, adding lead time. Start the whole process at least two months before the semester.

Can I work while studying in South Korea?

On a trial basis from 2026, D-2 students in 16 designated technical majors with TOPIK Level 3+ may work up to 35 hours per week during the semester (up from 30). Other students remain under the standard work-hour limits — confirm the current rule for your visa and major on hikorea.go.kr.

Does South Korea offer visa on arrival?

No. There is no visa-on-arrival system. Visa-waiver nationals use the K-ETA; everyone else obtains a visa from a Korean mission before travel. Do not board expecting to sort out entry on arrival.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For South Korea, the first check is K-ETA status: visa-waiver nationals confirm whether the 2026 exemption extension covers them at k-eta.go.kr, everyone else applies at a Korean mission (USD 40 short-stay / USD 60 long-stay, 2026 schedules). Students budget KRW 16–20 million in seasoned funds plus mandatory NHIS at KRW 79,320/month, and start at least two months early to accommodate the issuance certificate. Verify every fee on the live portal — Korean consular fees vary by nationality.
