How to use this Singapore visa and entry guide

Singapore splits authority cleanly: the ICA (Immigration & Checkpoints Authority) at ica.gov.sg handles tourist visas, the Student's Pass and all entry matters, while the MOM (Ministry of Manpower) at mom.gov.sg handles work passes. Go to the wrong agency's site and you'll find nothing — this split is the first thing to internalize.

This guide was verified against ICA and MOM publications in October 2026. Two 2026 specifics shape planning: the S Pass salary floor rose to S$3,600/month on 1 September 2026, and the Employment Pass qualifying salary climbs to S$6,000/month from 1 January 2027 (announced in 2026) — both move the goalposts for work-route budgeting.

Passport validity and document condition

Singapore requires the passport to be valid for at least 6 months on arrival — one of the stricter validity rules in Asia, and airlines enforce it at boarding. Renew before travel if yours is close.

Every traveller, including children, needs a separate passport. Keep the passport in good condition with blank pages for immigration procedures; damaged documents are a common refusal-at-check-in cause on Singapore-bound flights.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

First, visa-free entry: most Western nationalities — including the US and UK — enter visa-free for short visits (typically 30 days). Second, the tourist visa: required for nationals of countries including India, China, Pakistan and Bangladesh — fee S$30 per application (non-refundable, 2026), plus any agent service charge. Third, work passes: the Employment Pass (employer applies via the myMOM Portal; S$105 application + S$225 issuance = S$330 total, plus S$30 for a multiple-journey visa if needed, 2026) and the S Pass for mid-skilled roles. Fourth, the Student's Pass: S$45 processing on submission + S$60 issuance after approval (plus S$30 multiple-journey visa if applicable), all non-refundable (2026).

Transit and onward travel

Singapore offers the 96-hour Visa-Free Transit Facility (VFTF) — free — for eligible transit passengers: nationals of countries including India and CIS states who are transiting to or from a third country, hold an onward ticket departing within 96 hours, and hold a valid visa or long-term pass for Australia, Canada, Germany, Japan, New Zealand, Switzerland, the UK or the USA (ICA official criteria).

There is no general visa-on-arrival outside the VFTF. If you don't meet the VFTF conditions and your nationality requires a visa, arrange it before travel — Changi will not issue one at the counter.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

ICA officers may ask for the onward or return ticket, accommodation details, and evidence of funds. Keep the visa approval letter (for visa nationals) accessible — approval is electronic and linked to the passport, but the letter speeds things up.

Work-pass and Student's Pass holders should carry the IPA (In-Principle Approval) letter on first entry. The IPA is not the pass itself — it authorizes the single entry that starts the pass-issuance process.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Singapore requires no vaccination certificate for ordinary visitors and operates no pandemic-era border health checks. The SG Arrival Card — the free electronic arrival and health declaration submitted via ICA within 3 days before arrival (including the arrival day) — is the entry formality: it is not a visa, but it is mandatory.

Yellow-fever certificates apply only for arrivals from endemic countries, per standard international rules.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is not a Singapore entry condition for tourists. For students it is effectively mandatory: medical insurance is compulsory for the Student's Pass, and most institutions arrange mandatory group plans bundled into fees, typically S$200–600 per year (2026); private top-ups run higher.

Given Singapore's private healthcare costs, standalone travel cover is strongly advisable even where not required — budget it as part of the trip, not as an optional extra.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Short-visit admissions are typically 30 days, set by the ICA officer at entry — the period granted can be shorter than the maximum, and the visit pass cannot be extended casually. Overstaying is an offence with fines and potential imprisonment; it also destroys future applications.

Work-pass and Student's Pass holders are admitted for the pass duration once issued. The IPA letter covers only the initial entry — complete the pass formalities (including the medical check where required) promptly after arrival.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A tourist visa or visa-free entry never authorizes employment — Singapore enforces this strictly. Work routes: the Employment Pass (employer applies via myMOM; S$330 total in 2026; no levy or quota) with a qualifying salary rising to S$6,000/month (S$6,600 for financial services) for new applications from 1 January 2027 (renewals from 1 January 2028), up from S$5,600/S$6,200 — announced in 2026 with an age-progressive ladder reaching S$11,500–S$12,700 at 45+. The S Pass floor rose to S$3,600/month (S$4,000 financial services) effective 1 September 2026, with 8 new occupations added to the NTS Occupation List.

Students: the institution registers the Student's Pass application via SOLAR+ / ICA e-Service (S$45 + S$60, 2026). There is no fixed government minimum for proof of funds — ICA assesses whether funds cover actual tuition plus living costs for the full program (bank statements, education loans and sponsor income accepted); a practical planning figure is around S$10,000–12,000 per year in living costs. Do not enter as a tourist intending to 'convert' to a work pass — the passes are employer-petitioned from the start.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Each child needs a separate passport and, where applicable, a separate visa application — including infants. The S$30 tourist-visa fee applies per application (2026).

Dual nationals should enter on the passport matching their visa or visa-free status and keep both documents available. Singapore does not permit dual citizenship for adults — a separate nationality-law matter, but one that occasionally confuses long-term planners.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Tourist visa (visa-required nationalities): 1) Confirm your nationality requires a visa on ica.gov.sg. 2) Apply through an authorized visa agent (individuals generally cannot apply direct). 3) Pay the S$30 processing fee (2026, non-refundable) plus the agent's service charge. 4) Allow 3–5 working days (2026). 5) Submit the free SG Arrival Card via ICA within 3 days before arrival.

Employment Pass: 1) The Singapore-registered employer applies via the myMOM Portal (self-assessment tool first). 2) Pay S$105 + S$225 (2026). 3) Allow ~10 business days for Singapore-registered companies, up to 8 weeks for overseas companies — 3+ weeks is typical (2026). 4) Enter on the IPA letter and complete issuance.

Student's Pass: 1) Accept the institution's offer; the institution registers you on SOLAR+. 2) Complete the e-Form and pay S$45 (2026). 3) After approval, pay S$60 issuance (+S$30 MJV if needed). 4) Allow 2–4 weeks (2026; 1–2 weeks for IHLs, longer at peak intake).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping the SG Arrival Card. It's free and mandatory — not a visa, but required for entry.
  • Assuming visa-free means no paperwork. Visa-free nationalities still need the Arrival Card.
  • Working on a visit pass. Strictly enforced — the EP route is employer-petitioned from day one.
  • Budgeting the old EP salary. S$6,000/month (S$6,600 finance) applies to new applications from 1 January 2027.
  • Overstaying 'just a few days'. An offence with fines and possible imprisonment — and it kills future applications.
  • Applying for the tourist visa direct. Use an authorized agent; direct individual applications aren't the normal channel.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Singapore tourist-visa decisions turn on intent to leave: a return ticket, hotel bookings, employment at home and prior compliant travel. Because applications go through authorized agents, choose one that actually reviews the file rather than just forwarding it — a refused S$30 application still costs the agent's fee.

For work passes, quality is the employer's filing: correct salary against the applicable threshold, complete documentation in myMOM, and realistic timelines (overseas-company applications can take 8 weeks). For students, the institution's SOLAR+ registration must be accurate — errors there, not at ICA, cause most delays.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Singapore's tourist visa is electronic but agent-mediated — there is no self-service 'Singapore eVisa' portal for individuals. The SG Arrival Card is self-service and free via ICA: anyone charging for it is running a markup scam. Work passes run through the employer's myMOM Portal; Student's Pass through SOLAR+/ICA e-Service.

Scam avoidance: the S$30 tourist-visa fee is fixed — agents adding large 'express' surcharges for the standard 3–5-day service are padding. No one can guarantee pass approval; MOM and ICA decide on the file.

Translations, certifications and document format

Tourist applications: passport bio page, compliant photograph, completed agent form, and supporting evidence of purpose and funds. Work passes: degree certificates (verification may be required), employment history, and the employer's business profile — all submitted by the employer in myMOM.

Documents not in English need certified English translations. Photographs must meet ICA specifications; rejections for photo non-compliance waste the non-refundable fee, so get it right the first time.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Singapore tourist visas involve no interview as standard. Biometrics (photograph, fingerprints) are captured during work-pass and Student's Pass issuance formalities after arrival on the IPA letter.

At Changi, ICA officers may ask about purpose, accommodation and onward travel — answer directly with the documents to hand. The SG Arrival Card must already be submitted; officers check it electronically.

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs a separate visa application and SG Arrival Card — including infants — at S$30 per visa application (2026). Dependant's Passes for work-pass holders are applied for by the employer alongside or after the main pass.

Group applications through one agent are routine; a refusal for one member doesn't affect the others, but address the reason before re-applying.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Singapore requires declaration of cash above S$20,000 (or equivalent) on entry. The city-state is famously strict on controlled goods: chewing gum (except therapeutic), e-vaporisers, and certain medications are prohibited or restricted — check ICA's prohibited-items list before packing.

Duty-free allowances apply per adult traveller for alcohol and tobacco; the allowances are modest by regional standards.

Departure and future travel

Depart before the visit pass expires — overstaying is an offence, not an administrative footnote, and it is the fastest way to ensure future refusals. Keep the departure record clean; Singapore's systems remember.

Pass holders (EP, S Pass, Student's Pass) should keep passes valid and carry them when travelling; the multiple-journey visa (S$30) is what enables re-entry for pass holders who need it.

How to research a route without getting misled

Use this hierarchy: 1) ica.gov.sg for visas, entry and the Student's Pass. 2) mom.gov.sg for work passes — including the EP self-assessment tool. 3) The authorized agent's guidance for tourist-visa practicalities. 4) The institution's international office for Student's Pass specifics.

Ignore blogs quoting the old EP salary (S$5,600) without the 2027 date — the S$6,000 figure applies to new applications from 1 January 2027. Anything selling 'guaranteed' passes or charging far above S$30 for the tourist visa is monetizing anxiety.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid 6+ months on arrival, in good condition
  • Tourist visa approval (visa-required nationalities) or visa-free eligibility confirmed
  • SG Arrival Card submitted within 3 days before arrival (free, via ICA)
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation proof accessible offline
  • Funds evidence for the stay
  • IPA letter (work/student first entries)
  • Insurance — compulsory for Student's Pass (S$200–600/yr)
  • Cash >S$20,000 declaration readiness; prohibited-items list checked
  • Agent/MOM/ICA contacts saved for emergencies

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Singapore?

Yes. Indian passport holders need a tourist visa applied for through an authorized agent — S$30 processing fee (2026, non-refundable), typically decided in 3–5 working days. There is no general visa on arrival, though the free 96-hour VFTF covers eligible transit passengers with onward tickets and a qualifying onward visa.

What is the SG Arrival Card?

A free electronic arrival and health declaration submitted via ICA within 3 days before arrival (including the arrival day). It is mandatory for all travellers — including visa-free nationalities — but it is not a visa. Anyone charging you for it is running a scam.

What is the minimum salary for a Singapore Employment Pass in 2026–2027?

For new applications from 1 January 2027: S$6,000/month (S$6,600 for financial services), up from S$5,600/S$6,200 — with an age-progressive ladder reaching S$11,500–S$12,700 at 45+. The S Pass floor is S$3,600/month (S$4,000 financial services) from 1 September 2026.

Can I work in Singapore on a tourist visa?

No — working on a visit pass is strictly enforced against. Work requires an employer-petitioned Employment Pass or S Pass applied for via the myMOM Portal before starting work (S$330 total EP fees, 2026).

How much money do I need to show for a Singapore Student's Pass?

There is no fixed government minimum — ICA assesses whether your funds cover the actual tuition plus living costs for the full program (bank statements, education loans and sponsor income all accepted). A practical planning figure is around S$10,000–12,000 per year in living costs, plus compulsory medical insurance (S$200–600/year).

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Singapore, use the right agency: ICA (ica.gov.sg) for visas and entry, MOM (mom.gov.sg) for work passes. Visa-required nationalities pay S$30 for the tourist visa (3–5 working days, 2026); everyone files the free SG Arrival Card before flying. Work routes run on the new salary math — S$3,600/month S Pass floor from September 2026, S$6,000/month EP qualifying salary for new applications from January 2027. Keep 6 months' passport validity, never work on a visit pass, and never overstay.
