Current official context — checked 6 October 2026

The United States does not have one generic sponsored-work visa. H, L, O and other employment classifications have different petition, qualification and employer requirements. Many employment routes start with a petition filed with USCIS before a visa application abroad, so a job offer alone is not the immigration approval.

Official page: https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/employment.html

Separate the job search from immigration eligibility

Search for jobs and immigration eligibility in parallel. The U.S. separates visa issuance, status, petitions and admission; employer sponsorship mechanics vary sharply by category. Create a one-page route summary before applying: route name, sponsor requirement, occupation rule, salary/skill requirement, language rule, professional registration and whether dependants can accompany.

This prevents a common failure: investing months in a job that cannot support the intended immigration route. How to verify an employer or sponsor

Use official sponsor/accreditation registers where they exist, the company's own careers site, business registry details and a professional email domain. Compare the legal employer name on the offer with the entity in the sponsor register. A recruitment agency may be legitimate, but it should be clear who the actual employer is. Red flags include payment for a job offer, requests to reimburse prohibited sponsor costs, interviews conducted only through anonymous messaging apps, contracts with a different company name, and salary promises that are inconsistent with the written offer. From job offer to lawful work

The employer and applicant may have separate steps. In some systems the employer first obtains accreditation, sponsorship approval, labour-market approval or a job check; only then can the worker apply. In others, the processes are linked. Use https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/employment.html to map the actual sequence.

Do not start work until the status authorises it. Entry as a visitor or student does not automatically permit full-time employment. Use a sponsorship-first job-search workflow

A job vacancy and an immigration pathway are two separate questions. Before investing time in an application, identify whether the employer actually hires international candidates for the role, whether the role fits a lawful work route, and which party is responsible for the immigration filing. A recruiter saying “visa available” is not enough.

Build a sponsorship-ready evidence pack before interviews

Keep a concise folder containing your passport identity page, current immigration documents if applicable, degree certificates, transcripts, professional licences, employment letters, role-specific portfolio evidence and a one-page chronology of education and work history. Do not send sensitive documents in the first unsolicited message to a recruiter; provide them only through a verified employer or authorised representative when they are actually required.

For technology roles, use the US IT Sponsorship Jobs guide. If a case becomes legally complex, review the US Immigration Lawyer guide before paying for representation.

Check an employer's H-1B petition history when relevant

For roles where H-1B sponsorship is relevant, USCIS provides an H-1B Employer Data Hub that lets the public search historical petition data by employer name, fiscal year, location and other fields. Use it as an additional verification layer—not as proof that a current vacancy is genuine or that the employer will sponsor your specific role.
