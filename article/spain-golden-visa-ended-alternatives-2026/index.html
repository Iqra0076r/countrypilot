Spain Golden Visa Ended: Best Alternatives in 2026

Madrid switched off Europe's most famous residency-by-investment scheme in April 2025 — and nothing replaced it like-for-like. If you had your heart set on buying a home in Spain and calling it residency, that door is closed. But the landscape didn't go empty: Portugal's fund route, Greece's tiered property route, and Hungary's new investor permit picked up the demand, while Spain itself still runs strong residence routes that ask for income instead of capital. This guide lays out every working option, with the numbers verified for October 2026.

Quick Answer

Spain's golden visa closed to new applicants on April 3, 2025, when Organic Law 1/2025 repealed the investor-residence articles of Law 14/2013. Buying property in Spain — at €500,000 or €5 million — no longer produces any residence right. People who held permits, or who filed before the cut-off, keep their rights under transitional provisions. What replaced it? Nothing direct. The investment alternatives that absorbed the demand are Portugal's €500,000 fund route (funds only — real estate exited in 2023), Greece's zone-tiered property route (€400,000 outside prime zones, €800,000 in Attica, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini and larger islands, €250,000 for commercial conversions and heritage restorations), Hungary's €250,000 real-estate fund route, and Italy's investor visa from €250,000. And if your plan was Spain itself rather than a passport portfolio, Spain's digital nomad visa (about €2,849/month income) and non-lucrative visa (about €2,400/month, no work allowed) are the natural landing spots.

The day Spain's golden visa died

For eleven years it was the simplest deal in European immigration: buy a Spanish property worth €500,000 and collect a residence permit that let you live in Spain, work there, and travel across Schengen. Law 14/2013 made it official in 2013, and thousands of investors — many from China, Russia, the US, and the UK — used it.

The obituary is precise. Organic Law 1/2025, published in the Official State Gazette (BOE) on January 3, 2025, emptied Articles 63 to 67 of Law 14/2013 — the five articles that created the investor visa and the investor residence permit — and the repeal took effect on April 3, 2025. From that date, no new investor-residence applications can be filed. Period.

Two groups still hold rights under the old framework, and this is where most confusion lives. If you already held an investor permit, it stays valid until it expires and renewals continue under the rules in force when your permit was granted — in practice, five-year renewal cycles as long as the qualifying investment is maintained. If you filed before April 3, 2025 and are still waiting, your file is decided under the law as it stood on the day you filed. Both protections come from the transitional provisions added by the same repealing law.

Everyone else faces a hard fact: there is no grandfathering for intent. Planning to apply, looking at flats in Marbella, even owning Spanish property already — none of that converts into a residence right. Owning a home in Spain and holding a Spanish residence permit were always two separate files; now the second file simply no longer exists for new capital.

Why Madrid shut it down

Spain's government framed the closure around the housing crisis. The argument, made repeatedly by ministers in 2024, was that foreign investor demand in places like Barcelona, Madrid, and the Balearics was distorting local property markets and squeezing out residents. Whether you believe the €500,000 bracket really drove street-level prices — economists argued about this for months — is beside the point now. The politics had won.

There was also pressure from Brussels. The European Parliament had spent years calling golden-visa schemes a security and money-laundering risk, and the Commission was nudging member states toward winding them down. Spain wasn't the first domino — the UK shut its Tier 1 investor route in February 2022, Ireland in 2023, the Netherlands in 2024 — and it wasn't the last either. But it was the biggest: Spain's was the most-used residency-by-investment programme in the EU, which is why its closure redrew the whole map.

The uncomfortable truth: nothing replaced it

Ask agents what "replaced" the Spain golden visa and you'll get five different answers, most of them paid marketing. Here's the honest version: nothing. No Spanish route now converts capital into a residence permit. The programme that existed from 2013 to 2025 had no successor, no transition scheme, no consolation prize.

What actually happened is that the old demand split into two streams. Investors who wanted a return on capital plus residence moved to other countries' programmes — mostly Portugal, Greece, and Hungary. People whose real goal was living in Spain shifted to Spain's income-based routes: the digital nomad visa, the non-lucrative visa, and the entrepreneur route. The rest of this guide covers both streams in that order, so find your stream before you pick your route.

The three routes that absorbed the demand

If you liked the old Spain model — capital in, residence out — three programmes are doing the heavy lifting in 2026.

Portugal: the fund route, €500,000. Since October 2023 Portugal has been fund-only; real estate no longer qualifies (Law 56/2023). The main route is a €500,000 subscription to a qualifying investment or venture-capital fund regulated by Portugal's securities commission (CMVM), with a minimum five-year maturity and at least 60% of the fund's capital deployed in Portuguese companies. Hospitality, technology, and renewable-energy funds are the common flavours. Hold the investment at least five years; the stay requirement is famously light — about seven days a year on average. Portugal also keeps cheaper doors ajar: a €250,000 contribution to cultural heritage (€200,000 in low-density areas) or a €500,000 research contribution. Family can be included — spouse, dependent children up to 26, dependent parents. One honest caveat: the migration agency AIMA carries a large backlog, so published processing timelines run 12 to 24 months in 2026, and several sources report a 2026 nationality-law reform lengthened the path to citizenship — confirm the current figure with Portuguese counsel before you plan around a passport. Our deep-dive covers every number: Portugal Golden Visa 2026: The Fund Route, Explained.

Greece: the property route, tiered by zone. Greece is now the de facto last major EU programme where buying a home still buys residence — but Law 5100/2024 repriced it hard. In the prime zones (the Attica region including Athens, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini, and islands over 3,100 residents) the floor is €800,000; elsewhere it's €400,000; and a narrow €250,000 track survives for converting commercial buildings to residential use or restoring listed heritage buildings, anywhere in the country. Three rules bite people: the property must be a single unit of at least 120 square metres in the €400k/€800k tracks, and since September 2024 golden-visa properties cannot be let short-term — no Airbnb, no Booking.com, with violations carrying revocation and a reported €50,000 fine. The permit runs five years, renewable, with no minimum stay. Full zone map and rules: Greece Golden Visa 2026: Zones, Thresholds & Property Rules.

Hungary: the €250,000 real-estate fund. Hungary relaunched its investor programme in 2024–25 as the Guest Investor Residence Permit, and as of early October 2026 both routes were still open: €250,000 in qualifying real-estate fund units, or a €1,000,000 donation to an eligible university. Note the trap: a €500,000 direct property-purchase route was written into the original rules but removed by amendment in December 2024 before it ever took effect — buying a flat in Budapest gives you an asset, not a permit. The prize is a permit valid for ten years. One political note worth watching: Hungary elected a new government in April 2026, and while it hasn't touched the programme, commentators expect a review. Check the official gazette before committing capital.

The quieter bench: Italy, Cyprus, Malta, Bulgaria

Four more programmes round out the European field. They're less talked about, which cuts both ways — less hype, less hand-holding.

A few footnotes before you compare prices. Italy's cheapest route is the startup one — €250,000 into an innovative Italian startup — which means the money rides on a startup's survival. Cyprus still offers direct permanent residence from €300,000 in property, a genuinely simple structure. Malta's MPRP is the structural oddity: it's permanent residence, not a time-limited permit, but the money splits three ways (contribution, property, donation) and there is no passport at the end — only ordinary naturalisation years later. Bulgaria gets overlooked; it adopted the euro on January 1, 2026, which cleaned up one long-standing investor objection. One route I won't price for you: Romania's proposed golden visa, which was withdrawn in 2025 — if an agent quotes it, they're working from an old brochure.

The EU is done with passports-for-sale

We need to separate two products that marketing loves to blur: residency by investment (a permit — everything in the table above) and citizenship by investment (a passport, up front). The second product is now effectively dead inside the EU, and the corpse belongs to Malta.

On April 29, 2025, the Court of Justice of the European Union ruled in Case C-181/23 that Malta's investor-citizenship scheme — citizenship in exchange for a contribution, property, and donation — amounted to the commercialisation of Union citizenship and breached EU law. Malta honoured the judgment and wound the scheme down through a citizenship-amendment act in July 2025. The ruling didn't claw back passports already issued; it simply ended the programme going forward.

The practical consequence is clean: no EU member state currently sells citizenship. Malta was the last. Any page still quoting a €650,000 contribution for a Maltese passport is describing a programme that no longer exists. Golden-visa residence can start the clock toward citizenship in many countries — Portugal, Greece, Malta all have naturalisation routes — but the passport comes years later through ordinary residence, not at the checkout.

Still want Spain? The non-investment routes

Maybe the portfolio was never the point. Maybe you just wanted to live in Spain. Spain's immigration law still offers that — it just asks for income or enterprise instead of capital. Four routes absorb almost all the old golden-visa audience:

Digital Nomad Visa (~€2,849/month income in 2026). Built for people who work remotely for companies outside Spain, or freelancers earning at least 80% of their revenue from non-Spanish clients. The income test is the 2026 headline figure; family can join with additional amounts. Paperwork that surprises applicants: a criminal background check covering every place you've lived in the last five years, apostilled and translated, and private health insurance with no copays and cover matching Spain's public system. Employees on this visa can potentially access Spain's Beckham Law — a 24% flat rate on employment income up to €600,000 for the year of arrival and the five following years — which is a genuine draw for high earners. Guide: Spain Digital Nomad Visa 2026.

Non-Lucrative Visa (~€2,400/month, no work). The retiree and sabbatical favourite. Prove roughly €2,400 a month or about €28,800 in savings, show private health insurance, and you can live in Spain — with one hard rule: you cannot legally work in any capacity while it's active. Retirees and people selling a business before relocating are the natural audience. One tax warning that catches people out: choosing the non-lucrative route generally forecloses Beckham Law eligibility for that period of residence, because the special regime needs a work-related trigger. If the flat 24% rate matters to your maths, take tax advice before you file.

Startup / Entrepreneur Visa. For genuinely innovative, scalable businesses — not buying a franchise and calling it innovation. Spain's ENISA body certifies the project's innovativeness, and the law sets no fixed investment figure; "sufficient means" is the test, with practitioners working from roughly €2,442/month for the main applicant (plus amounts for family). The harder test is the business plan itself. This is the right route if you actually want to build something in Spain; it's a bad route if you just want residence and planned to park money.

Self-employed (autónomo) visa. The workhorse route for freelancers with Spanish clients and small local businesses. You'll need a viable business plan, proof of funds, and registration with Spain's tax agency and social security. It's more paperwork than the nomad visa but it fits a local business properly.

How to choose: a decision framework

Start from the goal, not the country. Three questions settle most cases.

1. Do you want Spain, or do you want residence? If the answer is Spain — the lifestyle, the coast, the kids in Spanish schools — stop reading about Greece and price the nomad or non-lucrative route. If the answer is "an EU foothold with minimal time there," Portugal's fund route has the lightest stay requirement in the field (about a week a year on average).

2. Capital or cashflow? The investment routes want capital parked for years — five minimum in Portugal's funds, indefinite in Greece's property. The Spanish routes want documented income or savings: roughly €34,000 a year of remote income for the nomad visa, or about €28,800 in savings for the non-lucrative. People with assets but little formal income do better on the investment side; people with high salaries but modest savings do better on the Spanish side.

3. What's your time horizon? None of these is fast. Portugal's AIMA backlog means 12 to 24 months from filing to card in 2026; Greece runs roughly 6 to 12 months; Spain's income routes move at consular speed — weeks to a few months depending on the consulate. If someone promises you a residence card in weeks, they're promising something the state hasn't agreed to.

Scams that multiplied after the closure

Programme closures are a fraud festival. Since April 2025, Spain-focused forums have filled with agents still "filing" golden-visa applications — the files go nowhere, because the legal basis no longer exists. The patterns to watch: anyone who asks you to wire the investment to a personal account or an offshore company before a permit exists; "grandfathering" services claiming they can backdate your filing to before April 2025; Malta passport packages quoting the old €650,000 figure after the April 2025 court ruling; and guaranteed-approval promises on any country's route. Real fees go through official channels, real timelines are measured in months, and no consultant can sell you a permit the law doesn't provide. Before you sign anything, verify the programme's current status on the country's official immigration or government-gazette site — these rules change quarterly.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Spain's golden visa really over?

For new applicants, yes. The repeal by Organic Law 1/2025 took effect on April 3, 2025, and no new investor-residence applications can be filed. Existing holders and people who filed before the cut-off keep their rights under transitional provisions.

Can I still buy property in Spain and get residency from it?

You can buy — foreign buyers are welcome — but the purchase produces no immigration right at any price since April 3, 2025. Property also doesn't help the financial test on Spain's other routes, which want liquid, documented income or savings.

What replaced the Spain golden visa in 2026?

Nothing like-for-like. The investment demand went to Portugal's €500,000 fund route, Greece's €400,000–€800,000 zone-tiered property route, Hungary's €250,000 fund route, and Italy's investor visa from €250,000. People who wanted Spain itself moved to the digital nomad and non-lucrative visas.

What is the cheapest European golden visa in 2026?

Among real EU programmes, Hungary's €250,000 real-estate fund and Italy's €250,000 startup route are the lowest single-applicant entry points, with Portugal's €250,000 cultural contribution below the flagship €500,000 fund route. But entry price isn't total price — donations don't come back, and every route adds government, legal, and due-diligence fees.

Which EU country gives citizenship for investment now?

None. The EU Court of Justice struck down Malta's investor-citizenship scheme on April 29, 2025, and Malta wound it down that year. Citizenship comes only after years of lawful residence and ordinary naturalisation. Any site still selling an "EU golden passport" is describing a dead programme.

What is the difference between Spain's digital nomad visa and the non-lucrative visa?

The nomad visa is for people who work remotely (about €2,849/month income, at least 80% from outside Spain for freelancers); the non-lucrative visa is for people with savings or passive income (about €2,400/month) who do not work at all while in Spain. The nomad visa can open the door to Beckham Law's 24% flat tax; the non-lucrative route generally doesn't.

How long does Portugal's golden visa take in 2026?

Plan for 12 to 24 months from application to residence card. Portugal's migration agency AIMA is working through a large backlog; the 2026 state budget allocated funding to digitise files and clear it, but timelines remain the programme's weak point.

Can I rent out my Greece golden visa property on Airbnb?

No. Since September 2024, golden-visa properties may not be let short-term (Airbnb, Booking.com, or sublet). Violations can lead to permit revocation and a reported €50,000 fine. Long-term leases to tenants are allowed.

I filed for Spain's golden visa before April 2025. What happens to my application?

It's decided under the law in force on the day you filed — the first transitional provision protects you. Check your file's status through your lawyer or the UGE (the large-companies unit that handled investor files), and keep the qualifying investment in place.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

The Spain golden visa is gone — April 3, 2025, Organic Law 1/2025 — and no amount of capital reopens it. If you want Spain, price the digital nomad visa or the non-lucrative route and our Spain visa & entry guide. If you want residency by investment, the serious 2026 field is Portugal's €500k fund route, Greece's tiered property route, Hungary's €250k fund, and Italy's investor visa — with Portugal and Greece each getting a full deep-dive on this site. Get tax and legal advice in the relevant country before moving capital, and re-check every threshold on the official source: this corner of immigration law moves quarterly.
