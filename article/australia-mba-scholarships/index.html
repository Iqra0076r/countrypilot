MBA Scholarships in Australia

This guide compares current Australian MBA scholarship pathways and explains how international applicants can distinguish genuinely eligible awards from scholarships restricted by citizenship, residency or institution-specific conditions.

By CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated 6 October 2026

Do not assume every Australian MBA scholarship is open to international students

Scholarship pages often mix domestic-only, staff-only, Indigenous, diversity and international awards. The fastest way to avoid wasted applications is to verify citizenship/visa eligibility before spending time on essays and references.

AGSM Global Reach Scholarship

UNSW’s AGSM states that the Global Reach Scholarship gives exceptional students from diverse international backgrounds access to the Full-Time MBA. Candidates are automatically considered after they submit an application to the Full-Time MBA. AGSM currently describes multiple tuition-waiver levels, including a full tuition waiver in exceptional circumstances and additional 75%, 50% and 25% awards.

Melbourne Business School scholarships

Melbourne Business School says its scholarship portfolio is designed to support applicants from a variety of backgrounds, including international students. The MBS merit scholarship lists Full-time MBA among its eligible programs, with value varying and consideration tied to the admission application.

One particularly notable international award is the Ian Harper AO Scholarship for Excellence, which MBS describes as a full-tuition scholarship for an international Full-Time MBA applicant. Its 2026 application window is closed, but it remains a useful example of the type of major scholarship to monitor for the next intake.

University of Sydney MBA scholarships

The University of Sydney Business School publishes several MBA and Executive MBA scholarships. Eligibility differs sharply. For example, the Future Leaders MBA Scholarship is a full-tuition part-time MBA award but its current eligibility is domestic/Australian permanent resident/New Zealand based rather than international-student-visa applicants. By contrast, the full-time MBA Director’s Scholarship page states that local and international self-funded candidates can be considered.

How to build an MBA scholarship shortlist

  1. Choose the MBA format first. Full-time, part-time, executive and online MBA scholarships are not interchangeable.
  2. Verify citizenship/visa eligibility. Do not assume an award named “MBA scholarship” accepts international candidates.
  3. Check whether admission is required first. Some awards automatically consider admitted applicants; others require a separate scholarship application.
  4. Record the value correctly. “Full tuition”, “50% tuition” and a fixed dollar amount have very different effects on total study cost.
  5. Check deadlines against the intake. A scholarship can remain online after its current application round closes.
  6. Prepare leadership evidence. MBA scholarships commonly weigh leadership, career progression, academic performance and impact—not just grades.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are MBA scholarships in Australia available to international students?

Yes, some are. AGSM and Melbourne Business School publish awards that can include international applicants, but many other MBA scholarships are domestic-only or have specific citizenship criteria.

Can an MBA scholarship cover full tuition?

Some awards can. For example, AGSM describes a full tuition waiver in exceptional cases under its scholarship framework, and Melbourne Business School has published international MBA awards with full-tuition value.

Do I apply for the MBA or scholarship first?

It depends on the award. Some scholarships automatically consider applicants after the MBA application, while others require a separate scholarship form. Follow the institution's current instructions.

Are scholarship deadlines permanent?

No. Scholarship windows change by intake and year. Always verify the current closing date on the university's official page before applying.
