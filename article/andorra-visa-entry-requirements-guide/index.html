How to use this Andorra visa and entry guide

Visa rules are not a single yes-or-no fact attached to a destination — they depend on your passport, purpose, length of stay, transit airports and sometimes recent travel history. Start with those facts before reading a visa list. The entry-status table below is the fast answer for the most common passports; the detail follows it. Always confirm the decisive rule on the official channel — French / Spanish consulate (Schengen) — before paying or booking.

Passport validity and document condition

Schengen rules apply: passport issued within the last 10 years and valid for at least three months beyond your planned departure from the Schengen area.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Andorra issues no visas at all — entry is governed entirely by France and Spain. To visit, you need whatever entitles you to enter the Schengen area through either neighbour: a Schengen visa for nationalities that require one, or visa-free entry for those that don't. The uniform Schengen Type C fee is €90 for adults (2026).

Practically, Andorra has no airport — you arrive by road from France or Spain, and leaving Andorra means re-entering Schengen, so a multiple-entry Schengen visa matters if your Andorra trip sits inside a wider European itinerary.

There is no Andorran eVisa because Andorra issues no visas. Applications, where needed, go to the French or Spanish consulate as a Schengen Type C visa (€90 adults, 2026).

Andorra has no visa on arrival — entry depends on your Schengen status via France or Spain.

Transit and onward travel

There are no airports in Andorra — you transit France or Spain by road. Your Schengen visa must cover re-entry: a single-entry visa used to enter France, then Andorra, then back to Spain can cause problems. Get a multiple-entry visa for multi-country itineraries.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Schengen consulates assess means of subsistence — a commonly cited reference is around €113 per day (2026 reporting). Carry bank statements; the consulate decides sufficiency.

Common documents beyond the visa itself: onward or return ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can support yourself. Print everything — phone batteries die at the worst moments.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

No vaccination certificate required for entry. Standard European travel health guidance applies.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Schengen travel medical insurance of €30,000 is mandatory for the visa application and is checked at the French/Spanish border.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Your permitted stay is set by the visa, stamp or entry record — visa validity and authorised duration are not necessarily the same thing. Read the entry stamp and calculate days using the method specified for your route. Overstays in Andorra can mean fines, detention or a future entry ban — know your date.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Andorran work and residence permits are a separate domestic system run by Andorra's immigration service — a Schengen tourist visa does not authorize work in Andorra. Fees and quotas are not published online; engage the Andorran authorities or an employer sponsor directly.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Minors generally need their own passport and sometimes a parental consent letter — check the requirements for your departure country too. Dual nationals should check the rules for the passport they plan to enter on, since visa treatment follows the document presented at the border. Refugees, stateless persons and emergency travel-document holders should confirm with Andorra’s embassy directly, as standard routes may not apply.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

2025–26 policy changes

  • ETIAS (reported, expected late 2026, EU-wide): visa-exempt travellers (including US/UK) are expected to need ETIAS authorization before Schengen travel — as reported in 2026; confirm before booking.
  1. Determine whether your nationality needs a Schengen visa (US/UK: no; IN/PK/NG/PH/BD: yes).
  2. If yes, apply for a Schengen Type C visa at the French or Spanish consulate — €90 adults (2026); request multiple entry.
  3. Meet the Schengen requirements: €30,000 insurance, funds evidence, 3-months-beyond-departure passport.
  4. Travel to France or Spain, then enter Andorra by road — there are no Andorran border visa formalities.
  5. Keep the visa valid for the return leg into Schengen.

Follow the French or Spanish consulate's Schengen checklist: form, photographs, passport, insurance, funds, accommodation and itinerary. Andorra-specific documents are not required — but carry proof of your Andorra accommodation for the border.

Common mistakes to avoid

    Getting a single-entry Schengen visa. Andorra sits between two Schengen states — you need multiple entry for the return. Looking for an Andorran visa. None exists; the French/Spanish Schengen visa is the document. Forgetting the 90/180 clock. Days in Andorra don't pause your Schengen allowance — it's all one zone. Skipping insurance. €30,000 Schengen medical cover is mandatory for the visa. Assuming ETIAS is live. As of October 2026 it is expected, not operational — check before travel.

Only use the official channel — French / Spanish consulate (Schengen). Copycat sites charge markups or harvest passport data. An eVisa approval is an email or a portal record: carry the confirmation and check it shows your name, passport number and permitted entries.

Translations, certifications and document format

Documents not in the local or accepted language may need certified translations — the official checklist states the rule. Certifications, bank statements and invitation letters should be recent (typically within 3 months). Photograph standards matter more than people expect: follow the portal’s specification exactly.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Schengen visa applicants give fingerprints (VIS) at the French/Spanish consulate — valid 59 months for repeat applications.

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs their own application or authorisation — visas are not transferable within a family or group. Apply together where the portal allows, and carry the same document set for each person. With minors, bring birth certificates or consent documentation in addition to passports.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

A visa is not a customs clearance. Declare cash above the local reporting threshold, check restricted items before packing (medications, drones, satellite equipment and certain food products are common flashpoints), and know that border officials can inspect devices. Keep receipts for high-value items you intend to bring back out.

Departure and future travel

Before leaving, confirm your exit is recorded — keep boarding passes and entry/exit stamps. If you overstayed or had any immigration incident, note it: future applications to Andorra and other countries ask about it. Some nationalities need to deregister or obtain exit clearance after long stays; check before a long-term visit.

How to research a route without getting misled

Rank sources by authority: Andorra’s immigration authority and foreign ministry first, then Timatic/IATA, then consular posts, then recent reputable reporting. Treat aggregator visa sites as leads, not answers — they go stale. If two credible sources conflict, say so in your notes and confirm with the authority; this guide flags conflicts the same way.

A final document audit before travel

Forty-eight hours before departure, check the passport number on the visa or authorisation, validity dates, name spelling, permitted entries and any conditions. Confirm transit airports and terminals have not changed. Put the approval, insurance and accommodation in an offline folder and print critical documents when the destination or airline may expect paper evidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for Andorra?

Andorra issues no visas. You need whatever gets you into Schengen via France or Spain — a Schengen visa (€90, 2026) for nationalities that require one, or visa-free entry for US/UK citizens.

Can I enter Andorra with a single-entry Schengen visa?

Risky. Andorra lies between France and Spain, so leaving and re-entering Schengen needs a multiple-entry visa. Get multiple entry for any Andorra trip inside a wider itinerary.

How do I get to Andorra?

By road only — there is no airport. Bus or car from Barcelona, Toulouse or other nearby French/Spanish cities.

Does time in Andorra count toward my Schengen 90 days?

Effectively yes for planning purposes — Andorra applies Schengen entry rules, so keep the 90/180 clock in mind across the whole trip.

Can I work in Andorra on a Schengen visa?

No. Andorran work and residence permits are a separate domestic system — the Schengen tourist visa does not authorize employment there.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Andorra, there is no Andorran visa to get: your document is the French or Spanish Schengen visa (€90, 2026) — multiple-entry — or visa-free entry for US/UK nationals. Arrive by road, watch the 90/180 clock, and sort Andorran work permits through Andorra's own immigration service, not the Schengen visa.
