Visa and entry planning

Entry to Russia is passport- and purpose-specific. The practical categories to check are visa-exempt entry, electronic authorisation or eVisa where offered, visa on arrival where offered, and a visa obtained before travel. Transit, work, study, family residence and long stays can have different rules from an ordinary visit.

Before booking non-refundable travel, identify the rule for the exact passport and purpose. Check passport validity, visa or electronic authorisation, transit permissions, permitted length of stay, proof of funds or accommodation, onward-ticket rules and health documents. Re-check close to departure because airlines use current travel-document data at check-in. For this Russia Complete Country Travel Guide research path, the practical checkpoint is “Visa and entry planning”; use WHO Travel Advice to confirm the live requirement where it can change.

Transport and route planning

Russia has multiple land neighbours, including Azerbaijan, Belarus, China, Estonia, and Finland. Complex overland routes create extra transit, visa, vehicle-document and border-hours questions; verify every country on the route rather than checking only the final destination.

Plan the arrival transfer before boarding the flight. Know the official taxi or public-transport options, approximate travel time and what happens after the last scheduled service. For domestic travel, leave realistic connection time between separate tickets and build weather or traffic disruption into important onward journeys. Health and medication preparation

Check routine vaccinations, destination-specific advice and whether a yellow-fever certificate or other health document is triggered by the itinerary. Carry enough prescription medicine for the trip plus a reasonable delay, subject to local import rules. In hot, remote or high-altitude travel, prevention and evacuation planning matter as much as access to a clinic. A low-stress first day in Russia

  1. Keep passport, entry evidence and accommodation details accessible through border control.
  2. Use an official or pre-researched transfer option.
  3. Get local connectivity working before moving far from the arrival point.
  4. Confirm the next day’s transport or appointment rather than leaving it to the morning.
  5. Store valuables securely and carry only what is needed for the first outing.
  6. Check local weather and any new advisory alerts.

Match preparation to the style of trip

A short city break, family visit, road trip, remote trek, business journey and long stay create different risks. City travellers may prioritise public transport and theft prevention; drivers need licence, vehicle insurance and road knowledge; remote travellers need weather, communications and evacuation planning. The visa may be the same while the practical preparation is completely different. Food, water and dietary planning

Travellers with allergies, coeliac disease, diabetes or religious dietary requirements should prepare key phrases in the local language and identify suitable food options near the first accommodation. Where water quality is uncertain, follow current health guidance. Keep essential snacks in hand luggage for delays and long transfers. Shopping, tax and customs

Keep receipts for high-value purchases and understand the customs allowance on return home. Tax-refund schemes, where available, have their own eligibility and export-validation rules. Do not assume that an item sold legally in Russia can be imported legally into the next country.

Remote work can also create immigration, tax or employer-compliance issues even when the traveller entered as a tourist; permission to visit is not automatically permission to work locally.
