How to use this ETIAS guide

ETIAS — the European Travel Information and Authorisation System — is the EU’s planned travel authorization for visa-exempt travellers to the Schengen area. As of October 2026 it is not in operation, and this guide is built on that fact: what ETIAS is, what its current status is, who it will affect when it launches, and what to do for travel in the meantime. Anything claiming ETIAS is live, quoting a fee, or selling applications is ahead of the official position.

What ETIAS is

ETIAS is an EU-wide travel authorization, not a visa. When operational, visa-exempt travellers — people who today enter the Schengen area without a visa — are expected to need ETIAS authorization before travel, as reported in 2026. It sits alongside the existing system, not on top of it: travellers who need a Schengen visa continue on the visa route regardless of ETIAS, and nothing about the current 90-days-in-180 rule changes until the EU says otherwise.

ETIAS status as of October 2026

ETIAS is not in operation as of October 2026. The earlier Q4 2026 target was removed in July 2026, and no new date has been published. Our country guides track this consistently — Austria lists ETIAS as reported and expected late 2026 while noting it is not operational as of October 2026, and Portugal records the same status. Treat any firm launch date as unconfirmed until it appears on the EU’s official pages, and confirm before booking.

Who ETIAS will cover when it launches

When ETIAS launches, it will apply to visa-exempt travellers — nationalities that enter the Schengen area without a visa. The clearest examples from our France guide: US and UK citizens, who may currently visit for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa. Their passports are now stamped on entry and exit, which is how the 90-day count is enforced. If you travel visa-free to the Schengen area today, you are in the group ETIAS is designed for — but you need nothing extra until it is operational.

Who ETIAS will not cover

ETIAS is not for travellers who need a Schengen visa. Passport holders of India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh need a Schengen short-stay visa before travel: €90 for adults, €45 for children 6–11, free for under-6s (2026), with a 15-day decision rule and the application made at the embassy or consulate — biometrics on the first visit. ETIAS does not replace, shorten or bypass this route. If you need a visa, the online-booked appointment is still the whole process.

ETIAS vs a Schengen visa: the difference

The distinction matters because the two systems ask for different things. A Schengen visa is a full application: the form, a photograph, a passport issued within the last 10 years and valid 3+ months beyond departure, €30,000 of Schengen-wide medical insurance, evidence of purpose, funds, accommodation and return, biometrics stored in the Visa Information System for 59 months, a €90 fee, and around 15 working days for a decision. ETIAS, as reported, is an authorization for people who need no visa at all. Until the EU publishes operational details, the practical rule is simple: if you need a visa today, ETIAS news changes nothing for you.

What to do for 2026 travel

For 2026 travel, follow the rules actually in force. Visa-exempt travellers: keep your passport inside the 10-year issue and 3-month-beyond-departure rule, and count the 90 days in any 180-day period across all Schengen states — a week in Spain counts against the same allowance as a week in France. Visa-required travellers: run the official Visa Wizard, book the appointment online through the official channel, and bring the full document file. Either way, re-check ETIAS status on the EU’s official pages before booking — as our Austria guide puts it, confirm before booking.

Common ETIAS mistakes

  • Assuming ETIAS is live. It is not in operation as of October 2026.
  • Treating ETIAS as a visa substitute. Visa-required nationalities still need the full Schengen visa route.
  • Paying for an ETIAS application. With no operation running, no legitimate ETIAS channel exists — treat sellers like the copycat visa sites.
  • Ignoring the 90/180 count because “ETIAS is coming”. The 90-day rule is in force now, enforced from entry and exit stamps.
  • Planning around a rumored launch date. The Q4 2026 target was removed with no replacement — plan on current rules.

What to watch before you book

Watch the EU’s official sources: the European Commission’s Schengen visa policy pages and the EU Immigration Portal. A real launch will arrive as an official announcement with a date and an application channel — not as a rumor on a visa blog. Until then, the Commission’s and consulates’ pages are the only positions that matter for your trip.

Final check before you travel

  • Route confirmed: visa-free 90/180, or Schengen visa via the official portal
  • Passport: issued within 10 years, valid 3+ months beyond departure
  • 90/180 count checked across all Schengen states
  • Insurance of €30,000+, Schengen-wide (visa route)
  • Appointment booked on the official channel (visa route)
  • ETIAS status re-checked on official EU pages before booking

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ETIAS in operation?

No. ETIAS is not in operation as of October 2026. The earlier Q4 2026 target was removed in July 2026, and no new date has been published.

Who will need ETIAS when it launches?

Visa-exempt travellers to the Schengen area — for example, US and UK citizens, who currently enter visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. As reported in 2026; confirm before booking.

I need a Schengen visa. Does ETIAS affect me?

No. Visa-required nationalities — including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh — use the Schengen C-visa route (€90 for adults, 2026) regardless of ETIAS.

Does ETIAS replace the visa appointment?

No. ETIAS is a separate authorization for visa-exempt travellers. If you need a Schengen visa, you still book the appointment online through the official channel and attend in person.

What should I do for a trip in late 2026?

Follow the current rules for your nationality — visa-free 90/180 or the C-visa route — keep your passport inside the 10-year/3-month rule, and re-check ETIAS status on the EU’s official pages before booking.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

ETIAS is the EU’s planned travel authorization for visa-exempt Schengen travellers — reported and expected, but not operational as of October 2026, after the Q4 target was withdrawn with no replacement date. If you enter visa-free today, nothing changes yet; if you need a Schengen visa, the €90 route with its online-booked appointment is still the whole process. Check the official EU pages before you book — and pay no one for ETIAS until a real channel exists.
