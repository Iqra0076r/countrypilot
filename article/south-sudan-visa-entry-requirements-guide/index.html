How to use this South Sudan visa and entry guide

South Sudan's entry system is young and thinly documented: the official e-Visa portal (evisa.gov.ss), run by the Ministry of Interior's Department of E-Visa and Immigration Services, is the fastest route for most travellers, with the fee schedule published by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation (mofaic.gov.ss). There is no reliable visa on arrival for most nationalities — arrange the visa before travel.

Two 2026 realities dominate planning. First, US Presidential Proclamation 10998 (effective 1 January 2026) fully suspended US visa issuance to South Sudanese nationals — relevant if your travel party includes South Sudanese passport holders heading onward. Second, the US Embassy in Juba paused all visa services from 18 May 2026 as a public-health precaution during the regional Ebola outbreak. Check your own government's advisory before planning any travel: consular support inside South Sudan is limited. Figures below were verified in October 2026; where no official figure exists, this guide says so plainly instead of inventing one.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport should be valid well beyond your planned stay with at least one blank page — South Sudan's published guidance does not state a precise validity formula, so apply the conservative standard: six months beyond the trip. Given limited consular infrastructure, a passport problem on arrival is far harder to resolve here than elsewhere.

Carry photocopies of the passport data page and visa stored separately from the originals. If the passport is lost, replacement options inside South Sudan are extremely limited — keep digital backups accessible offline.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Most foreign nationals need a visa before arrival, and the e-visa is the fastest channel. Visa-free entry (up to 90 days) applies to Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda and Egypt; visa on arrival in Juba is available to Rwanda and Burundi nationals. Yellow-fever vaccination proof is required for entry — carry the WHO yellow card. Entry is limited to Juba International Airport and designated land borders.

Transit and onward travel

Do not plan airside transit through Juba without the correct documentation. South Sudan is not a transit hub with sterile international connections for most itineraries — if your routing touches Juba, treat it as an entry: hold the e-visa (or qualify for one of the narrow exemptions) before boarding.

Entry points are limited to Juba International Airport and designated land borders, so 'transit' here effectively means entering South Sudan. Verify the current position with the e-visa portal before booking any itinerary that routes through the country.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

At the South Sudan border, expect questions before you get a stamp. The standard set covers purpose of visit, length of stay, accommodation, and means of support — have the onward ticket, hotel booking or host address, and bank evidence ready rather than buried in luggage.

A visa lets you travel to the border; the officer's decision is what lets you in. Short, truthful, consistent answers beat long explanations. If your plans involve anything beyond tourism — work, study, volunteering — say so only if your visa category actually covers it.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Health documents are a separate gate from the visa for South Sudan. The key question is certificate-based: does South Sudan require proof of yellow-fever vaccination (common if you arrive from an endemic country), and are there any current outbreak-related declarations?

Carry the WHO yellow card (International Certificate of Vaccination) if there is any chance it applies — digital photos are not accepted at most borders. Also confirm your insurance covers the actual medical environment: in countries with limited hospital capacity, evacuation cover matters more than the policy's headline medical limit.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance sits in a grey zone for South Sudan: sometimes a formal visa requirement, always a practical one. Where the embassy or portal names a minimum medical coverage, treat that number as the floor and buy a policy that meaningfully exceeds it.

Read the exclusions before you pay: adventure activities, pre-existing conditions and alcohol-related incidents are the three most common claim rejections worldwide. For long stays, check whether the policy covers the full visa duration — a 90-day policy on a 180-day trip leaves a dangerous gap.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

In South Sudan, the admission decision at the border may involve more layers than usual — immigration, security screening, and document verification can each add hours. The admitted stay is still set by the officer, not the visa.

Keep the entry stamp or record safe: in disrupted environments, proving legal entry later (for extension, exit, or any dispute) depends on documents you kept yourself, because official systems may not have reliable records.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Keep the three purposes strictly apart for South Sudan: visiting, working, studying. Each has its own permission, its own evidence, and its own application channel — a tourist visa or visa-free entry covers none of the other two.

The expensive mistake is assuming intent does not matter: taking a job, enrolling, or freelancing while admitted as a visitor can lead to detention, removal, and multi-year bans. If the plan might involve work or study, secure that status before travelling, not after arrival.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

For families heading to South Sudan: every traveller, including infants, needs individual documentation. Where an authorization or e-visa system applies, each person — children included — needs their own approval; there are no family bundles.

A consent letter from any non-travelling parent is cheap insurance against difficult questions at the border. Dual citizens must verify South Sudan's rule on which nationality to travel under — entering on a foreign passport while holding South Sudan citizenship is an offence in some countries.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

For South Sudan, the normal workflow is disrupted: embassies may be closed, portals offline, and published procedures unreliable. 1) Identify which third-country mission currently handles South Sudan visas — this changes, so verify it now, not from a 2024 guide. 2) Contact that mission directly before preparing anything; ask what they actually accept today. 3) Build the file around what can be verified: passport, photographs, itinerary, funds evidence.

Do not pay any fee until a functioning official channel confirms the amount and the payment method. In disrupted environments, 'agents' selling guaranteed visas are the most active fraud vector — every fee goes through an official mission or it does not get paid.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming any published procedure still works — in South Sudan's disrupted environment, verify every step with the currently operating mission.
  • Paying 'fees' to intermediaries — with official channels disrupted, fraud flourishes; no legitimate fee is paid to a personal account.
  • Planning travel before a visa exists — processing is unpredictable; nothing is booked until the visa is in hand.
  • Ignoring the security advisory — check your own government's current advisory for South Sudan; 'do not travel' designations have insurance and legal consequences.
  • Expecting consular help in-country — many embassies in South Sudan are closed or operating remotely; your government's ability to assist inside the country may be near zero.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Think of the South Sudan application as an argument with exhibits. The argument: 'I am a genuine visitor who will leave on time and can afford the trip.' Every exhibit should support exactly that — and nothing should contradict it.

Common contradictions that sink files: an itinerary showing a luxury trip against bank statements showing a thin balance; employment dates that do not match the leave letter; a stated purpose ('conference') with no invitation or registration. Re-read the whole file as a skeptic before submitting.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Online application for South Sudan is unreliable by definition right now: portals launch, go offline, or never worked. Before trusting any site claiming to be the South Sudan e-visa portal, verify it is linked from an official government source — a standalone domain with no government linkage is not trustworthy, however professional it looks.

Where no functioning online channel exists, the route is a third-country embassy in person. Confirm which mission, what it accepts, and its current fee by direct contact — and keep every receipt, because disrupted systems lose records.

Translations, certifications and document format

For South Sudan, assemble two piles: what the checklist demands, and what proves your story. The second pile — employment letter with approved leave, property or tenancy documents, enrollment confirmation, prior travel history — is where approvals are won.

Photographs deserve more care than they get: follow the portal's dimensions, background and recency rules exactly, because a rejected photo restarts the clock. Where documents need legalization or apostille, start early — that process has its own timeline independent of the visa.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Biometrics are captured at entry in South Sudan — the e-visa process requires an approval letter before boarding, with biometric enrollment on arrival. No separate pre-departure biometric appointment procedure could be verified; confirm the current process on evisa.gov.ss when applying.

Keep the e-visa approval letter accessible (printed and on your phone): airlines check it before boarding, since there is no visa on arrival for most nationalities.

Families and group travel

Families should budget per person for South Sudan: per-person fees, per-person forms, per-person approvals. Children's applications need the same care as adults' — birth certificates where surnames differ, and consent letters where only one parent travels.

Group applications rise or fall together in practice: if the group's stated purpose is a wedding, every file should reflect the wedding. One member with a contradictory story invites scrutiny of all.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

After the immigration stamp in South Sudan comes customs. The two questions that matter: are you carrying more than the declarable cash threshold, and are you carrying anything restricted — food, medicine, electronics, cultural goods?

Cash thresholds are about reporting, not limits: declare honestly and keep walking. Restricted goods are different — undeclared prohibited items can mean fines or confiscation. Read the customs authority's list for South Sudan before you pack, not after you land.

Departure and future travel

Plan your exit from South Sudan before you enter: routes, border crossings and flight availability can change with little notice. Keep your passport, visa and travel documents on your person, not in checked luggage, and register with your embassy's traveller program if one exists.

Monitor the security situation throughout the stay and leave early if it deteriorates — commercial options disappear fast in a crisis, and evacuation is never guaranteed. A clean, documented, on-time departure matters even more from a conflict zone.

How to research a route without getting misled

For South Sudan, rank your sources: South Sudan e-Visa portal (Ministry of Interior) first, the responsible embassy second, official advisories third. Everything else — forums, influencers, visa-agent marketing — is background noise until confirmed against the top two.

Be especially careful with dated content: fee schedules, processing times and even visa categories change year to year. A 2023 guide quoting fees is not a 2026 answer. Check the publication or 'last updated' date on anything you read, then verify against South Sudan e-Visa portal (Ministry of Interior) anyway.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for South Sudan?

Almost certainly yes, arranged before travel via the official e-visa portal (evisa.gov.ss). The only exceptions are visa-free entry (up to 90 days) for Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda and Egypt, and visa on arrival in Juba for Rwanda and Burundi nationals. There is no general visa on arrival.

How much is a South Sudan visa in 2026?

The official 2026 fee schedule (mofaic.gov.ss): USD 100 single-entry for most nationalities (USD 160 for US citizens), USD 200 for 3-month multiple-entry, USD 350 for 6-month multiple-entry. Ignore third-party 'bundle' prices — those are agency markups.

Is South Sudan safe to visit in 2026?

Check your own government's current advisory before planning anything — consular support inside the country is limited, and the US Embassy in Juba paused visa services from May 2026 over the regional Ebola outbreak. Travel here requires more contingency planning than a normal trip.

Do I need yellow fever vaccination for South Sudan?

Yes — proof of yellow-fever vaccination is required for entry. Carry the WHO yellow card; it is checked.

Can I get a visa on arrival in Juba?

Only nationals of Rwanda and Burundi. Everyone else must hold the e-visa approval before boarding — airlines enforce this, since on-arrival issuance ended for general travellers.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For South Sudan, the e-visa at evisa.gov.ss is the route — USD 100 single-entry (USD 160 for US citizens) on the 2026 official schedule, approval letter in hand before boarding, yellow-fever certificate packed. Where the research found no official figure (student/work fees, funds minimums), this guide refuses to invent one: verify on the official portal before paying anything, and check your government's advisory before planning travel at all.
