New Zealand NZeTA 2026

The NZeTA is New Zealand's mandatory travel authority for visa-waiver visitors — $17 by app or $23 online, plus the $100 IVL. Who needs it, how to apply and what it does not do.

Quick Answer

The New Zealand Electronic Travel Authority (NZeTA) is mandatory before departure for travellers from around 60 visa-waiver nationalities, including the US and UK. What the NZeTA is

The New Zealand Electronic Travel Authority (NZeTA) is an electronic authorisation linked to your passport — it is not a visa, and it is not a sticker. It exists because New Zealand has no visa on arrival: visa-waiver travellers must hold it before boarding, and airlines are required to check it.

It covers short visits — tourism, business trips and visiting family. Work, study and residence run through entirely separate visa routes, such as the Accredited Employer Work Visa for workers.

Who needs an NZeTA

Around 60 visa-waiver nationalities travel without a visa but must obtain an NZeTA — including the United States and the United Kingdom. If your passport is from a waiver country, you need the NZeTA even for short tourism trips.

You do not need one if you are: an Australian citizen or permanent resident (the Trans-Tasman arrangement applies), a New Zealand visa holder, or from a nationality that requires a full Visitor Visa instead — including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh, who apply for the Visitor Visa at immigration.govt.nz (NZD $441 total in 2026; see our New Zealand visa fee 2026 guide). Each traveller needs their own NZeTA — including infants.

Cost and validity

In 2026 the NZeTA costs NZD $17 through the official app or $23 through the website, plus the $100 IVL (International Visitor Conservation and Tourism Levy). The real total is therefore $117 (app) or $123 (web) — charged per traveller, adults and children alike.

The NZeTA is valid for 2 years once issued. Note the distinction: the 2-year validity is the authorisation's life, while each stay is separately determined at the border — typically up to 3 months for visa-waiver travellers, and up to 6 months for British citizens.

How to apply

1) Use the official NZeTA app ($17) or the official website ($23) — the app is cheaper. 2) Have your passport ready; the NZeTA is linked to the passport number exactly, so enter it carefully. 3) Pay the IVL alongside the application ($100). 4) Apply well before departure: INZ advises allowing up to 72 hours, even though approvals are usually much faster.

Because the NZeTA is tied to a specific passport, a renewed passport after issue means the authorisation must be transferred or re-applied for — check INZ's guidance if your passport changes. New Zealand also requires the passport to be valid for at least 3 months beyond your intended departure; the NZeTA does not waive that rule.

Transit and the NZeTA

Visa-waiver nationals transiting through New Zealand still need an NZeTA — transit is not exempt. Visa-required nationals transiting through Auckland generally need a transit visa even without leaving the airport, unless covered by a specific transit arrangement.

Pacific routings through Auckland catch travellers out regularly: check INZ's transit rules for your nationality before booking, not after.

What the NZeTA does not do

  • It does not authorise work. An NZeTA never permits employment — the work route is the employer-led Accredited Employer Work Visa.
  • It does not authorise study. Study requires the Fee Paying Student Visa ($750 + $100 IVL in 2026, plus $20,000/year in funds evidence and mandatory insurance).
  • It does not guarantee entry. It gets you on the plane; the INZ officer at the border decides admission and may ask for onward tickets, accommodation details and funds evidence (guideline: around NZD $1,000/month, or $400 with prepaid accommodation).
  • It does not replace passport validity. The 3-months-beyond-departure rule still applies.

Avoiding copycat sites

The official NZeTA costs $17/$23 plus the $100 IVL. Copycat sites charge multiples of that for submitting the same form — search results and ads are full of them. The cheapest legitimate route is the official app.

No agent can speed up or influence NZeTA processing, and no one can sell you a guaranteed approval. Apply through the official channels only, keep the confirmation accessible, and ignore any 'visa service' quoting fees far above INZ's published numbers.

NZeTA checklist before flying

  • Confirmed your nationality is a visa-waiver one (not visa-required, not Trans-Tasman Australian)
  • NZeTA obtained before departure — $17 app / $23 web + $100 IVL per traveller, including infants
  • NZeTA passport number matches the passport you are travelling on exactly
  • Applied at least 72 hours before departure
  • Passport valid 3+ months beyond intended departure
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation details accessible offline
  • Funds evidence (~NZD $1,000/month guideline)
  • Declare all food, plants and animal products — New Zealand biosecurity fines are routine and substantial

Frequently Asked Questions

Who needs an NZeTA for New Zealand?

Travellers from around 60 visa-waiver nationalities — including US and UK citizens — must hold an NZeTA before departure. Australians travel under the Trans-Tasman arrangement, and nationalities like India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh need a Visitor Visa instead (NZD $441 total in 2026).

How long does the NZeTA take?

Approvals are usually fast, but INZ advises allowing up to 72 hours. Apply well before departure — carriers check the authorisation and will deny boarding without it.

Can I work in New Zealand with an NZeTA?

No. The NZeTA never authorises employment, including remote work while in New Zealand. Work requires the Accredited Employer Work Visa, whose accreditation and job-check sequence is employer-led.

Do children need an NZeTA?

Yes — each traveller needs a separate NZeTA, including infants, and the $17/$23 + $100 IVL charges apply per person.

What is the difference between the NZeTA and a Visitor Visa?

The NZeTA is a pre-travel authorisation for visa-waiver nationalities (US, UK and ~60 others): $17/$23 + $100 IVL, valid 2 years. The Visitor Visa is a full visa application for non-waiver nationalities (India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines, Bangladesh and others): NZD $441 total in 2026, applied for online before travel. Neither authorises work or study.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

If your passport is from a visa-waiver country, get the NZeTA before you fly: $17 through the official app or $23 online, plus the $100 IVL, valid 2 years — apply at least 72 hours ahead and use the official channel, not a copycat site. It gets you boarding; it does not authorise work or study, and the border officer still decides your stay. From a non-waiver country, skip the NZeTA entirely and apply for the $441 Visitor Visa.
