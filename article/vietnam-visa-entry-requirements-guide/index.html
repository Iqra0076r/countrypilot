How to use this Vietnam visa and entry guide

Vietnam's e-visa is one of Southeast Asia's best-documented systems in 2026: multi-entry e-visas up to 90 days issued on the official National Web Portal on Immigration (evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn), with fees of USD 25 single-entry / USD 50 multi-entry paid online in 2026. Visa-exempt nationalities get 45 days; visa on arrival exists only with a pre-approved visa letter (landed at 14 airports, 16 land borders, 13 seaports) — there is no walk-up VoA without the letter.

The 2026 policy story: Decree 219/2025 (effective 1 January 2026) overhauled the work-permit framework — longer permits and extended exemptions — while the Decree 221/2025 proposal for 10-year Golden and 5-year Investor visas (with visa exemption for Phu Quoc, Con Dao and Van Don) was not in force as of mid-October 2026 and is excluded from this guide's facts. Study fees: USD 50 visa application + USD 145 temporary residence card issuance (per Circular 25/2021/TT-BTC, as converted by the Ministry of Public Security). This guide was verified against official Vietnamese sources in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months beyond your date of entry into Vietnam, with blank pages. This is a published requirement — the e-visa application enforces it against the passport's expiry.

The e-visa is tied to the passport number. VoA-with-letter travellers: the passport presented at the stamping counter must match the letter exactly.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Visa-exempt: 45 days, no application. e-visa: up to 90 days multi-entry, USD 25 single / USD 50 multi, paid online on the official portal. VoA with pre-approved letter: obtain the letter first, then the visa is stamped at the designated ports. Work: work permit under Decree 219/2025 (longer durations, extended exemptions) — arranged via the employer. Study: DH student visa + temporary residence card (USD 50 + USD 145).

Transit and onward travel

Vietnam's designated ports (14 airports, 16 land borders, 13 seaports) handle VoA-with-letter arrivals; airside transit on through-tickets without clearing immigration generally needs nothing. But anyone exiting the transit area needs the appropriate entry permission — and remember there is no walk-up visa on arrival without the pre-approved letter.

Overland transits at the 16 designated land borders follow the same rule: the letter (or e-visa) must exist before the first leg. Do not assume the border will sort it.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

At the Vietnam border, expect questions before you get a stamp. The standard set covers purpose of visit, length of stay, accommodation, and means of support — have the onward ticket, hotel booking or host address, and bank evidence ready rather than buried in luggage.

A visa lets you travel to the border; the officer's decision is what lets you in. Short, truthful, consistent answers beat long explanations. If your plans involve anything beyond tourism — work, study, volunteering — say so only if your visa category actually covers it.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Health documents are a separate gate from the visa for Vietnam. The key question is certificate-based: does Vietnam require proof of yellow-fever vaccination (common if you arrive from an endemic country), and are there any current outbreak-related declarations?

Carry the WHO yellow card (International Certificate of Vaccination) if there is any chance it applies — digital photos are not accepted at most borders. Also confirm your insurance covers the actual medical environment: in countries with limited hospital capacity, evacuation cover matters more than the policy's headline medical limit.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance sits in a grey zone for Vietnam: sometimes a formal visa requirement, always a practical one. Where the embassy or portal names a minimum medical coverage, treat that number as the floor and buy a policy that meaningfully exceeds it.

Read the exclusions before you pay: adventure activities, pre-existing conditions and alcohol-related incidents are the three most common claim rejections worldwide. For long stays, check whether the policy covers the full visa duration — a 90-day policy on a 180-day trip leaves a dangerous gap.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

On arrival in Vietnam, the officer's admission decision is the operative document. Note the exact date you must leave and keep evidence of it (a photo of the stamp or the electronic record) — 'I thought the visa was valid longer' is not a defence against an overstay finding.

Extensions, where they exist, must be sought before the current permission expires and are never guaranteed. Plan the itinerary inside the granted stay rather than assuming an extension will be available.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Keep the three purposes strictly apart for Vietnam: visiting, working, studying. Each has its own permission, its own evidence, and its own application channel — a tourist visa or visa-free entry covers none of the other two.

The expensive mistake is assuming intent does not matter: taking a job, enrolling, or freelancing while admitted as a visitor can lead to detention, removal, and multi-year bans. If the plan might involve work or study, secure that status before travelling, not after arrival.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

For families heading to Vietnam: every traveller, including infants, needs individual documentation. Where an authorization or e-visa system applies, each person — children included — needs their own approval; there are no family bundles.

A consent letter from any non-travelling parent is cheap insurance against difficult questions at the border. Dual citizens must verify Vietnam's rule on which nationality to travel under — entering on a foreign passport while holding Vietnam citizenship is an offence in some countries.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Work the Vietnam application backwards from the travel date. First, identify the exact visa category and its document list on Vietnam e-Visa portal (National Web Portal on Immigration). Second, gather funds evidence and bookings — most refusals trace to weak or inconsistent documentation, not to eligibility. Third, submit via the official portal or mission and keep the reference number.

Fourth, wait for the decision before committing money to flights and hotels. Published processing times are targets, not promises; consulates do not compensate for missed trips caused by slow decisions.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Trusting forum anecdotes over Vietnam e-Visa portal (National Web Portal on Immigration) — procedures change; the official portal is the only source that matters.
  • Paying fees to personal accounts — official fees go through official channels; anything else is a scam.
  • Letting the passport drop below the validity threshold — airlines deny boarding, and there is no appeal at the gate.
  • Working or studying on a visitor entry — the fastest route to removal and a multi-year ban.
  • Overstaying 'just a few days' — it voids multi-entry visas and must be declared on future applications worldwide.
  • Forgetting onward travel proof — one of the most common refusal reasons at the border itself.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Think of the Vietnam application as an argument with exhibits. The argument: 'I am a genuine visitor who will leave on time and can afford the trip.' Every exhibit should support exactly that — and nothing should contradict it.

Common contradictions that sink files: an itinerary showing a luxury trip against bank statements showing a thin balance; employment dates that do not match the leave letter; a stated purpose ('conference') with no invitation or registration. Re-read the whole file as a skeptic before submitting.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Online applications for Vietnam live or die on the portal you choose. Vietnam e-Visa portal (National Web Portal on Immigration) is the official channel; everything else is a private business. The tell-tale signs of a copycat: fees several times the official schedule, 'guaranteed approval' language, and payment to personal accounts.

Screenshot every confirmation page and save every email receipt. If the portal issues an approval letter or authorization to board, print it — phone screens fail at exactly the wrong moment, and some carriers require paper.

Translations, certifications and document format

For Vietnam, assemble two piles: what the checklist demands, and what proves your story. The second pile — employment letter with approved leave, property or tenancy documents, enrollment confirmation, prior travel history — is where approvals are won.

Photographs deserve more care than they get: follow the portal's dimensions, background and recency rules exactly, because a rejected photo restarts the clock. Where documents need legalization or apostille, start early — that process has its own timeline independent of the visa.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

No biometric enrollment procedure could be verified for Vietnam's e-visa or VoA routes. Expect standard passport and visa checks at the immigration counter, with the e-visa approval (or VoA letter) matched to the passport.

Keep the e-visa approval accessible (printed and digital): it is checked at boarding and again at the Vietnamese counter.

Families and group travel

Families should budget per person for Vietnam: per-person fees, per-person forms, per-person approvals. Children's applications need the same care as adults' — birth certificates where surnames differ, and consent letters where only one parent travels.

Group applications rise or fall together in practice: if the group's stated purpose is a wedding, every file should reflect the wedding. One member with a contradictory story invites scrutiny of all.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

After the immigration stamp in Vietnam comes customs. The two questions that matter: are you carrying more than the declarable cash threshold, and are you carrying anything restricted — food, medicine, electronics, cultural goods?

Cash thresholds are about reporting, not limits: declare honestly and keep walking. Restricted goods are different — undeclared prohibited items can mean fines or confiscation. Read the customs authority's list for Vietnam before you pack, not after you land.

Departure and future travel

Departure from Vietnam deserves the same attention as arrival. Confirm the exit requirements — some countries check for departure cards, exit stamps or proof of onward travel even when leaving.

A documented, on-time departure builds the travel history that makes the next visa easier. Conversely, even a short overstay creates a record that follows you across borders and across years. When in doubt, leave early.

How to research a route without getting misled

For Vietnam, rank your sources: Vietnam e-Visa portal (National Web Portal on Immigration) first, the responsible embassy second, official advisories third. Everything else — forums, influencers, visa-agent marketing — is background noise until confirmed against the top two.

Be especially careful with dated content: fee schedules, processing times and even visa categories change year to year. A 2023 guide quoting fees is not a 2026 answer. Check the publication or 'last updated' date on anything you read, then verify against Vietnam e-Visa portal (National Web Portal on Immigration) anyway.

A final document audit before travel

  • Correct visa category for the actual purpose — visiting, working and studying are different permissions
  • Passport validity and blank pages verified against Vietnam's rule, not a generic 'six months' assumption
  • Application reference, payment receipts and approval kept together
  • Itinerary, funds and purpose consistent across every document
  • Biometrics/interview appointment completed where required, with confirmation
  • Insurance policy documents carried, emergency assistance number saved offline
  • Customs-relevant items checked against Vietnam's prohibited list
  • Someone at home holds copies of your passport, visa and itinerary

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the Vietnam e-visa?

USD 25 single-entry / USD 50 multi-entry, paid online on the official portal evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn (2026). The e-visa allows up to 90 days multi-entry. Apply only on the official domain — copycat sites overcharge for the same application.

How long does the Vietnam e-visa take?

3–5 working days (official 2026 figure). The VoA pre-approval letter takes 5–7 working days standard (2–3 expedited). Apply with margin either way.

Is there visa on arrival in Vietnam?

Only with a pre-approved visa letter — obtained before travel, then stamped at one of the designated ports (14 airports, 16 land borders, 13 seaports). There is no walk-up visa on arrival without the letter. The e-visa is usually simpler.

What changed for Vietnam work permits in 2026?

Decree 219/2025, effective 1 January 2026, overhauled the framework: longer permit durations and extended exemptions. Arranged through the employer as before — but verify the current documentary requirements under the new decree.

Is the Vietnam Golden Visa available?

No — not as of mid-October 2026. The 10-year Golden Visa and 5-year Investor Visa under Decree 221/2025 (with Phu Quoc, Con Dao and Van Don exemptions) are proposed, not in force. Any site selling them is premature at best.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Vietnam in 2026, the playbook is clean: 45 days visa-free for the exempt list, otherwise the USD 25/50 e-visa (3–5 working days) on the official portal — paid there, approved in hand, multi-entry up to 90 days. VoA exists only with the pre-approved letter. Work permits run under the new Decree 219/2025; the Golden Visa doesn't exist yet. Verify on evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn and ignore everything the copycat domains promise.
