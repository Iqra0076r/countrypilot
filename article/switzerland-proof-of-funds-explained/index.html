Proof of funds for Switzerland is not simply ‘show a bank balance’. The immigration authority may care about the required amount, family size, how long the money has been held, who owns it, whether it is genuinely available and how unusual deposits are explained. This guide shows how to build a traceable financial file and where to verify the live threshold for the exact route.

What proof of funds is trying to prove

Proof of funds is not a ceremonial bank statement. It is evidence that the applicant can meet the financial condition of a specific immigration route without relying on unauthorised work or public support. The required amount, holding period, acceptable account type and exemptions can differ by route and family size.

Always identify the legal purpose of the money first: settlement funds, study maintenance, visitor expenses, business funds or another requirement. Then use the exact official rule for that route.

Build a clean money trail

Decision-makers may care about ownership, accessibility and source of funds. Keep account statements covering the required period, bank letters where requested, salary evidence, sale documents or gift evidence as appropriate. Large unexplained deposits should be documented instead of left for the officer to interpret.

If funds are provided by a parent or sponsor, explain the relationship and whether the money is a gift, loan or continuing support. Do not move the same money through several accounts merely to create a larger-looking balance.

Currency and family-size calculations

Where the authority publishes a required amount, calculate it using the correct family size and exchange rate convention. Some systems count family members even if they are not travelling. Others distinguish tuition already paid from living-cost funds.

Because exchange rates move, leave a reasonable buffer. A balance that only meets the threshold by a few units of currency can fall below it before the application is assessed.

Red flags and verification

Borrowed money, unverifiable cash deposits, temporary transfers, false bank statements and inconsistent account-holder names are high-risk. False financial evidence can lead to refusal and, in serious cases, misrepresentation findings.

The safe approach is simple: use genuine accessible funds, document their source, and re-check the official amount immediately before submission.
