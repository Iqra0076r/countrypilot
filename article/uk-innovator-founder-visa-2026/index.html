UK Innovator Founder Visa 2026: Endorsement to ILR

The UK's founder visa is the rare immigration route where the money matters less than the idea. There is no minimum investment, no job offer, and no points test — instead, an approved endorsing body must believe your business is innovative, viable, and scalable, and then keep believing it for three years. This guide walks through the 2026 rules exactly as the Home Office writes them: the three-word test, the endorsing bodies, the contact-point meetings, the true costs, and the settlement route at the end.

The UK Innovator Founder visa grants 3 years (extendable) to founders whose business an approved endorsing body judges innovative, viable and scalable. There is no minimum investment; you need £1,270 in maintenance funds, B2 English, and an endorsement letter no more than 3 months old. After 3 years you can settle permanently if you meet the residence rules and 2 of 7 business achievements.

The headline nobody believes: there is no minimum investment

Every founder who remembers the old Tier 1 Entrepreneur visa asks the same question, because the old answer was £200,000 (or £50,000 from approved sources). The new answer, confirmed across the Home Office's 2026 guidance, is zero. The Innovator Founder route has no minimum investment requirement at any stage — not for the visa, not for the extension, not for settlement.

This is not an oversight; it is the design. The route replaced both the Start-up and Innovator visas in April 2023 precisely to stop measuring founders by their bank balance and start measuring them by their business. Capital still helps — a funded startup is easier to argue as viable — but the legal threshold is gone. What replaced it is a maintenance requirement: you must hold £1,270 for 28 consecutive days before applying (ending no more than 31 days before the application), with £285 for a partner, £315 for a first child, and £200 for each additional child, per the caseworker guidance updated 25 February 2026.

The practical effect is that the gate moved from your wallet to your idea. A founder with £5,000 and a genuinely innovative, well-evidenced plan can be endorsed; a founder with £500,000 and a generic e-commerce reseller cannot. That sounds liberating until you meet the people who decide — which brings us to the three words that actually run this visa.

Innovative, viable, scalable: the three-word test that decides everything

Appendix Innovator Founder of the Immigration Rules requires your endorsing body to be satisfied on three criteria, and the Home Office's own language is worth reading literally, because each word eliminates a different kind of applicant.

Innovative means the business idea is new — not new to you, new to the market. The guidance is explicit that simply competing with existing businesses is not enough. Your tenth food-delivery app with a slightly different logo fails here; a novel approach to a real problem, with evidence that the market lacks it, passes. Endorsing bodies look for differentiation they can defend in writing, because their own Home Office approval depends on the quality of their endorsements.

Viable means you can actually do it. This is where the business plan, your skills and experience, and the financial projections get interrogated. A brilliant idea with a founder who has no relevant background and no credible route to delivery fails on viability even if it sparkles on innovation. Bodies want to see that you have thought about customers, costs, regulation, and the first eighteen months in concrete terms — not adjectives.

Scalable means the business can grow: create jobs, expand into markets, and generate the kind of growth the UK wants to attract. A consultancy that will forever be you and a laptop struggles here, not because consultancies are bad businesses, but because the route is designed for ventures with structural growth potential.

Two further rules frame the test. First, the business must be new — you cannot join or take over an existing trading business. Second, your endorsement letter must be no more than 3 months old on the date you apply, so the endorsement and the visa application are a tightly sequenced pair, not a letter you bank for a year.

The endorsing bodies, and why picking one is a strategy

Endorsement is not a rubber stamp you buy; it is a relationship with an organization the Home Office holds accountable for its judgment. As of 2026, the approved endorsing bodies for the Innovator Founder route are UK Endorsing Services, Innovator International, and Envestors Limited — plus the Global Entrepreneur Programme, which operates on an invitation-only basis and is not something you can simply apply to.

Choosing between the three open bodies is a strategic decision most founders underweight. Each body has its own sector strengths, assessment style, fee structure, and — critically — its own view of what "innovative" looks like in practice. A deep-tech hardware venture and a fintech SaaS product will not get the same hearing everywhere. Before you pay anyone, study what each body has actually endorsed: their portfolio is the most honest statement of their taste. A body that has never endorsed anything in your sector is telling you something.

The endorsement itself costs £1,000 under the published 2026 fee schedule, and the body will also charge for the two mandatory contact-point meetings (see below). Be wary of intermediaries selling "guaranteed endorsement" packages at multiples of that — the Home Office monitors endorsement quality, bodies that endorse weak businesses risk their status, and no legitimate body guarantees outcomes. If someone promises you a letter for cash with no assessment, you are looking at a scam, not a shortcut.

One more timing detail that catches people out: because the endorsement letter expires 3 months after issue, you want your visa application fully prepared before the letter lands — English test certificate, maintenance funds seasoned for 28 days, tuberculosis certificate if your country requires one, and a clean immigration history. Founders who get endorsed first and then discover their funds have only been held for 12 days have to start the 28-day clock again while the letter ticks toward expiry.

The three-year clock: contact points, extensions, and what can go wrong

The Innovator Founder visa is granted for 3 years, and the Home Office does not simply trust you and walk away. Your endorsing body is required to stay in contact and to check, at two formal contact point meetings — roughly 12 and 24 months after the visa is granted — that the business is progressing against the plan you were endorsed for. Each meeting costs £500 under the published schedule.

These meetings are the route's immune system, and you should treat them as the serious assessments they are. The body is checking for genuine progress: trading activity, product development, customer traction, hiring — evidence that the endorsed plan is being executed, not shelved. If the body concludes you are not making reasonable progress, it can withdraw its endorsement, and withdrawal puts the visa itself at risk. This is the single biggest structural difference from a points-based visa: your permission to stay is tethered to a third party's ongoing judgment of your business.

That sounds alarming, but in practice it rewards exactly what it is meant to reward: founders who actually build. Keep contemporaneous records from day one — contracts, invoices, product milestones, hiring evidence, marketing spend — organized against the milestones in your original plan. When the 12-month meeting arrives, you want a file that tells the story without you having to reconstruct it from memory.

At the end of the three years, you can extend the visa if you are still running the business and can secure fresh endorsement, or you can apply for settlement (below). Extensions follow the same logic as the initial grant: new endorsement letter, maintenance funds, and evidence the business remains genuine and progressing.

Settlement after three years: the two-of-seven rule

Here is where the Innovator Founder route quietly becomes one of the fastest settlement paths in the UK system: indefinite leave to remain after just 3 years, compared with 5 years on most work routes. But the 3-year clock has conditions, and the business-achievement test at the end is specific.

The residence conditions are the standard strict ones: continuous residence in the UK, with absences of no more than 180 days in any 12-month period, and you must pass the Life in the UK test. The absence rule deserves emphasis for founders, because building a "scalable" business often means travel — fundraising in the US, suppliers in Asia, customers in Europe. Those trips count. Track every departure and return from the day the visa is granted; reconstructing three years of travel at the settlement stage is where applications quietly fail.

The distinctive requirement is the fresh endorsement at settlement, in which your endorsing body must confirm you meet at least 2 of 7 specified business achievements — metrics like investment raised, jobs created, revenue growth, and innovation milestones defined in the Immigration Rules. This is the moment the whole route has been building toward: the 12- and 24-month contact points were rehearsals, and settlement is the performance. Founders who treated the contact points as admin tend to discover at year three that they never built the evidence the 2-of-7 test demands.

English at settlement follows the standard settlement requirement — and note that the initial visa already demanded B2 in all four skills (reading, writing, speaking, listening), a higher bar than many work routes. If your English is borderline, invest in it early: it is tested at the start, and weakness there compounds into every assessment that follows.

The true cost of the route, line by line

Founder-visa articles love to quote a single fee. The real number is a stack, and in 2026 it looks like this for a single applicant applying from outside the UK. Treat every figure below as the published 2026 schedule — confirm the current amounts on gov.uk before you pay anything, because Home Office fees move most Aprils.

  • Visa application fee: £1,357 applying from outside the UK; £1,693 extending or switching inside the UK (fees effective 8 April 2026).
  • Immigration Health Surcharge: £1,035 per year of the visa for adults (£776 per year for children) — so roughly £3,105 for the full 3-year grant, payable upfront.
  • Endorsement fee: £1,000 to the endorsing body for the initial assessment.
  • Contact-point meetings: £500 each at roughly months 12 and 24 — £1,000 across the visa.
  • Maintenance funds: £1,270 held for 28 days — not a fee, but money that must sit untouched in your account.

Add it up and a single founder is looking at roughly £6,500–£7,000 in unavoidable official costs for the three years, before flights, legal advice, or the English test. A family multiplies the surcharge and maintenance lines fast: each child adds £776/year of surcharge plus the maintenance tranches. This is still dramatically cheaper than the old entrepreneur route's capital requirements — but it is not a cheap visa, and the surcharge is the line that surprises most people because it is due in one lump sum with the application.

Who should not apply (and what to do instead)

This route is badly matched to several common founder profiles, and the kindest thing a guide can do is say so plainly.

If your business already trades, you are ineligible — the rules require a new business idea. Look at the UK's expansion or skilled-worker routes if you are moving an existing company.

If your idea is a better version of something that exists, the innovation test will likely fail you. Endorsing bodies have seen every "Uber for X" variant; differentiation must be substantive and evidenced, not asserted.

If you cannot evidence viability, pause. A founder with deep domain expertise but no written plan, no market research, and no financial model is not ready for assessment — and a refusal burns time and the endorsement fee. Build the dossier first.

If you need the visa quickly, be realistic about sequencing: finding the right body, surviving assessment, sitting the B2 English test, and seasoning 28 days of funds is a multi-month project before the Home Office even sees the file.

If you mainly want to live in the UK and the business is secondary, this is the wrong door. The contact points and the 2-of-7 settlement test will find you out by year two. The route rewards founders who actually want to build in Britain — which, when it works, is exactly why the settlement prize arrives in three years instead of five.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a minimum investment for the UK Innovator Founder visa?

No. The Innovator Founder route has no minimum investment requirement — a deliberate break from the old Tier 1 Entrepreneur visa. You must show £1,270 in maintenance funds held for 28 days, but there is no capital you must put into the business itself.

Who can endorse my business for the Innovator Founder visa?

Endorsement must come from a Home Office-approved endorsing body: UK Endorsing Services, Innovator International, or Envestors Limited. The Global Entrepreneur Programme route is invitation-only. Your endorsement letter must be no more than 3 months old when you apply for the visa.

How long does the Innovator Founder visa last?

The visa is granted for 3 years and can be extended with fresh endorsement. After 3 years of continuous residence you may qualify for indefinite leave to remain, provided you meet the absence limits, pass the Life in the UK test, and satisfy the business-achievement requirement.

What are the Innovator Founder contact point meetings?

Your endorsing body must meet you at around 12 and 24 months after the visa is granted to verify the business is progressing against your endorsed plan. If it is not, the body can withdraw endorsement — which puts your visa at risk. Each meeting costs £500 under the published fee schedule.

What English level do I need for the Innovator Founder visa?

You need English at CEFR level B2 in all four skills — reading, writing, speaking and listening — as confirmed in the Home Office caseworker guidance updated 25 February 2026.

Can my family join me on the Innovator Founder visa?

Yes. Partners and children can apply as dependants. You must show additional maintenance funds: £285 for a partner, £315 for a first child and £200 for each further child, held for 28 days — plus the Immigration Health Surcharge for each family member.

How do I get ILR as an Innovator Founder?

After 3 years you need continuous residence (absences of no more than 180 days in any 12-month period), a pass in the Life in the UK test, and a fresh endorsement confirming you meet at least 2 of the 7 specified business achievements set out in the Immigration Rules.

Bottom Line

The UK Innovator Founder visa is the best founder deal in the English-speaking world for people with a genuinely new idea and the evidence to prove it: no minimum investment, three years to settlement, and an assessment that cares about the business rather than the bank balance. Its price is scrutiny — endorsement, two contact-point reviews, and a 2-of-7 achievement test at settlement. Go in with a real plan, a body whose taste matches your sector, and meticulous records from day one, and the route does exactly what it promises.
