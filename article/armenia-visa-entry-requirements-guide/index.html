How to use this Armenia visa and entry guide

Visa rules are not a single yes-or-no fact attached to a destination — they depend on your passport, purpose, length of stay, transit airports and sometimes recent travel history. Start with those facts before reading a visa list. The entry-status table below is the fast answer for the most common passports; the detail follows it. Always confirm the decisive rule on the official channel — Armenian MFA eVisa portal — before paying or booking.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for the duration of your stay with blank pages — six months beyond travel is the safer standard.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Armenia's entry system is refreshingly simple: apply for the eVisa at evisa.mfa.am — USD 8 for up to 21 days or USD 39 for up to 120 days (2026, official MFA portal) — decided within up to 3 working days. It is among the cheapest and fastest eVisa systems in the region.

Many nationalities also qualify for visa-free entry or visa on arrival; the eVisa is the predictable route for everyone else, including Indian, Pakistani, Nigerian, Filipino and Bangladeshi travellers. Confirm your category on the MFA portal before applying.

Armenia's eVisa is in operation at evisa.mfa.am: USD 8 (up to 21 days) or USD 39 (up to 120 days), decided within up to 3 working days (2026, official portal).

Visa on arrival exists for many nationalities at Yerevan airport — but the eVisa is the predictable, pre-approved route; use it unless you have confirmed VoA eligibility for your passport.

Transit and onward travel

Zvartnots (Yerevan) airside transit on a through ticket normally needs no visa. Leaving the airport requires the eVisa or an eligible exemption — when in doubt, get the $8 eVisa; it's cheap insurance.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Proof of funds for entry is reported as required with no stated amount (2026). Carry bank statements as a matter of course.

Common documents beyond the visa itself: onward or return ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can support yourself. Print everything — phone batteries die at the worst moments.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

No vaccination certificate required for entry from most countries. Standard travel immunizations apply.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Health insurance is reported as mandatory (2026). Buy a policy covering the full stay before applying.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Your permitted stay is set by the visa, stamp or entry record — visa validity and authorised duration are not necessarily the same thing. Read the entry stamp and calculate days using the method specified for your route. Overstays in Armenia can mean fines, detention or a future entry ban — know your date.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work and study need their own permits with Armenian sponsorship — the eVisa covers tourism and business visits only. Category fees are not published on the portal; confirm with the embassy.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Minors generally need their own passport and sometimes a parental consent letter — check the requirements for your departure country too. Dual nationals should check the rules for the passport they plan to enter on, since visa treatment follows the document presented at the border. Refugees, stateless persons and emergency travel-document holders should confirm with Armenia’s embassy directly, as standard routes may not apply.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

2025–26 policy changes

  • eVisa fee schedule (reported, effective 1 Jan 2026): $8/$39 tiers — as reported by VisaVerge in 2026; the portal shows current fees at application.
  1. Check visa-free/VoA eligibility for your nationality on evisa.mfa.am.
  2. If the eVisa fits, apply online — USD 8 (21 days) or USD 39 (120 days), 2026.
  3. Upload passport scan and photograph as the portal requests.
  4. Allow up to 3 working days (official); a week is comfortable.
  5. Print the eVisa approval and carry it with your passport.

The eVisa needs a passport scan and a compliant photograph, plus travel details entered on the portal. For longer stays, have accommodation and financial evidence ready for the border.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Only use the official channel — Armenian MFA eVisa portal. Copycat sites charge markups or harvest passport data. An eVisa approval is an email or a portal record: carry the confirmation and check it shows your name, passport number and permitted entries.

Translations, certifications and document format

Documents not in the local or accepted language may need certified translations — the official checklist states the rule. Certifications, bank statements and invitation letters should be recent (typically within 3 months). Photograph standards matter more than people expect: follow the portal’s specification exactly.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Biometric capture is not documented for the eVisa. Expect standard passport control on arrival.

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs their own application or authorisation — visas are not transferable within a family or group. Apply together where the portal allows, and carry the same document set for each person. With minors, bring birth certificates or consent documentation in addition to passports.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

A visa is not a customs clearance. Declare cash above the local reporting threshold, check restricted items before packing (medications, drones, satellite equipment and certain food products are common flashpoints), and know that border officials can inspect devices. Keep receipts for high-value items you intend to bring back out.

Departure and future travel

Before leaving, confirm your exit is recorded — keep boarding passes and entry/exit stamps. If you overstayed or had any immigration incident, note it: future applications to Armenia and other countries ask about it. Some nationalities need to deregister or obtain exit clearance after long stays; check before a long-term visit.

How to research a route without getting misled

Rank sources by authority: Armenia’s immigration authority and foreign ministry first, then Timatic/IATA, then consular posts, then recent reputable reporting. Treat aggregator visa sites as leads, not answers — they go stale. If two credible sources conflict, say so in your notes and confirm with the authority; this guide flags conflicts the same way.

A final document audit before travel

Forty-eight hours before departure, check the passport number on the visa or authorisation, validity dates, name spelling, permitted entries and any conditions. Confirm transit airports and terminals have not changed. Put the approval, insurance and accommodation in an offline folder and print critical documents when the destination or airline may expect paper evidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for Armenia?

Many nationalities are visa-free or VoA-eligible; everyone else uses the eVisa at evisa.mfa.am — USD 8 (21 days) or USD 39 (120 days), decided within 3 working days (2026).

How much is the Armenia eVisa?

USD 8 for up to 21 days or USD 39 for up to 120 days (2026, official MFA portal).

How long does the Armenia eVisa take?

Up to 3 working days (official). Apply a week ahead to be comfortable.

Is there visa on arrival in Armenia?

Yes, for many nationalities at Yerevan airport — but the eVisa is the predictable pre-approved route.

Can I work in Armenia on the eVisa?

No — the eVisa covers visits only. Work and study need separate sponsored permits.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Armenia, the eVisa is the sweet spot: USD 8–39 (2026), decided in up to 3 working days at evisa.mfa.am. Check visa-free eligibility first, pick the validity tier that matches your trip, and carry insurance — the system is cheap, fast and predictable.
