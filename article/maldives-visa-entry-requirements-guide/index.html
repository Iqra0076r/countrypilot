How to use this Maldives visa and entry guide

The Maldives runs one of the world's simplest entry systems — and one absolute rule. The rule: a free 30-day visa on arrival is granted to all nationalities except holders of Israeli passports, who are banned from entry under the 3rd Amendment to Immigration Act 1/2007 (effective 15 April 2025). The simplicity: no advance eVisa, no fee, no embassy visit for tourists — but one mandatory step applies to everyone: the free IMUGA Traveller Declaration, submitted electronically within 96 hours before arrival.

The controlling source is Maldives Immigration (Ministry of Homeland Security and Technology) at immigration.gov.mv, with consular guidance published by Maldives missions abroad. This guide was verified against official Maldivian sources in October 2026. Note: the Maldives publishes no dedicated student visa category — foreign students enter on tourist or institution-sponsored special visas, a verified fact, not a gap in this guide.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport must be machine-readable and valid for the duration of your stay — Maldives Immigration checks this at the counter when issuing the 30-day visa on arrival. In practice, arriving with at least one month of validity beyond your planned departure avoids airline and counter friction; confirm the current enforcement on immigration.gov.mv before travel.

Israeli passport holders — including dual nationals travelling on an Israeli passport — are refused entry under the April 2025 amendment. This applies regardless of visas held for other countries.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Tourists (all nationalities except Israeli passport holders): free 30-day visa on arrival, issued at the counter — no advance application, no fee. It can be extended to 60–90 days through Maldives Immigration before expiry. Work/business: no arrival facility — employment requires pre-approval through the employer's Xpat Online System quota and work-permit process before travel. Students: there is no published student visa stream; institutions sponsor foreign students on tourist or special visas — confirm the exact arrangement with your institution, not with a generic "student visa" checklist.

Transit and onward travel

The Maldives is a destination, not a transit hub — but if you connect through Velana International Airport (Malé), the same entry rules apply the moment you clear immigration. Passengers staying airside on a single through-ticket follow airline guidance; anyone entering Malé, even briefly, needs the IMUGA declaration and receives the standard 30-day arrival visa.

Because onward seaplane and domestic transfers are booked against your arrival details, keep the IMUGA confirmation and onward bookings together — resort representatives at the airport work from them.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Maldives Immigration grants the arrival visa against three checks: a valid machine-readable passport, the IMUGA Traveller Declaration (submitted within 96 hours before arrival), and proof of sufficient funds — USD 100 plus USD 50 per day of stay, or a confirmed hotel/resort booking (official Maldives government guidance, 2026). A return or onward ticket rounds out the standard package.

In practice, resort-booked travellers clear fastest: the confirmed reservation simultaneously satisfies the funds and accommodation questions. Independent travellers should carry a bank statement or card alongside the booking.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

The Maldives sets no general vaccination requirement for tourist arrivals, and no health document is routinely checked at the border. The IMUGA declaration includes health-related questions — answer them accurately, as it is the health-screening instrument.

Requirements can change with public-health conditions — check Maldives Immigration guidance shortly before travel rather than relying on older guides.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is not an entry condition for the Maldives — but the geography makes it unusually important: you are on scattered atolls, and medical evacuation by seaplane to Malé (or onward to Colombo or India) is expensive and paid upfront by the patient. Buy comprehensive cover with high medical and evacuation limits before departure.

The Maldives publishes no dedicated international-student insurance product or cost range — it does not market a formal study-abroad visa stream. Expatriate workers must hold employer-arranged medical insurance under the employment regulations.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

The 30-day visa on arrival is issued at the immigration counter on presentation of your passport, IMUGA confirmation and funds/accommodation evidence. It is free. Extensions to 60–90 days are handled by Maldives Immigration before the initial 30 days expire — apply in Malé, not at the resort desk, and allow processing time.

Overstaying is treated seriously and complicates any future Maldivian visa matter (including work-permit applications through the Xpat system). Diarise your exit date from the entry stamp.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

The tourist arrival visa never authorizes work — not for a resort, not remotely. Employment in the Maldives runs through the employer's Xpat Online System: quota approval first, then the work permit, all before the worker travels. No official Maldivian work-permit fee schedule is published on the immigration site — figures circulating online (quota and monthly permit fees) come from law-firm summaries, not the government, so confirm every figure with your employer and the Xpat system, never with a blog.

Study has no dedicated visa stream: the Maldives does not publish a student visa category, fee schedule or insurance requirement. Foreign students enter on a tourist visa or an institution/employer-sponsored special visa — the exact arrangement must come from the admitting institution in writing before travel.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every traveller needs their own IMUGA Traveller Declaration — children cannot be included on a parent's submission. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter and birth certificate.

Dual nationals: the Israeli-passport entry ban applies to holders of Israeli passports — a dual national holding an Israeli passport alongside another nationality is refused entry when travelling on, or identified by, the Israeli document. Travel on the non-Israeli passport and carry it consistently throughout the trip.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Tourist: 1) Confirm your passport is machine-readable and valid for your stay (and that you do not hold an Israeli passport). 2) Book accommodation — a confirmed resort/hotel reservation satisfies the funds question. 3) Submit the free IMUGA Traveller Declaration at imuga.immigration.gov.mv within 96 hours before arrival. 4) Carry USD 100 + USD 50/day in accessible funds (or rely on the booking), plus a return/onward ticket. 5) Receive the free 30-day visa at the counter. Work: the employer completes Xpat quota and permit approval before you travel — never fly in on a tourist visa to "sort out" employment.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping the IMUGA declaration — it is mandatory and checked; the 96-hour window is strict.
  • Assuming a student visa exists — it does not as a published category; get the institution's sponsorship arrangement in writing.
  • Quoting work-permit fees from blogs — no official fee schedule is published; confirm via the employer and Xpat system.
  • Working on the tourist arrival visa — it never authorizes employment, including resort or remote work.
  • Israeli passport holders attempting entry — refused under the April 2025 amendment, no exceptions published.

Application quality: getting it right first time

The Maldives system is document-light for tourists, which makes the few requirements load-bearing: the IMUGA declaration must match the passport exactly — transliteration mismatches between the declaration and the passport biodata page cause counter delays. Submit it yourself rather than delegating it to a third party.

Keep the IMUGA confirmation (screenshot plus email) accessible offline — Velana airport Wi-Fi is not something to depend on at the immigration counter.

Fees, appointments and processing time

Official 2026 figures: the 30-day tourist visa on arrival is free (USD 0); the IMUGA Traveller Declaration is free. Extensions to 60–90 days are processed by Maldives Immigration (fee per current schedule — confirm on immigration.gov.mv).

No official Maldivian fee schedule is published for work permits, and no official processing-time standard is published for work visas — figures circulating online are unofficial. Confirm all employment costs and timelines with the sponsoring employer through the Xpat Online System, never with a blog or forum.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

The Maldives has no tourist eVisa — the arrival visa is issued at the counter, and the only pre-travel online step is the free IMUGA declaration at imuga.immigration.gov.mv. Any website selling a "Maldives eVisa" or charging for the arrival visa is not the government.

Work-permit processing runs through the employer's Xpat Online System — individual applicants do not apply for work authorization themselves. Never pay a "visa agent" for Maldivian tourist entry; there is nothing to buy.

Supporting documents checklist

For tourist entry: machine-readable passport, IMUGA confirmation (within 96h), proof of funds (USD 100 + USD 50/day) or confirmed hotel/resort booking, return/onward ticket. For extension: passport, current entry stamp, reason and supporting evidence, fee per current schedule. For work: the employer handles Xpat quota approval and the work permit — the worker's documents (passport, qualifications, medicals as requested) go to the employer, not to an embassy.

Keep digital and paper copies of the IMUGA confirmation and booking — the counter occasionally re-checks what the system already holds.

Interviews and biometrics

The Maldives operates no visa interview system for tourists — the arrival visa is decided at the counter on documents. Biometric capture is not a published pre-travel step for tourist entry.

Work-permit applicants may undergo procedures directed through the Xpat system and the employer — follow the employer's instructions rather than assuming a consular process.

Families and groups travelling together

Each family member — including infants — needs their own IMUGA declaration; there is no family bundling. Resort bookings typically cover the whole party for the funds question, but keep one shared folder with every traveller's IMUGA confirmation.

Groups arriving on the same flight should stay together through immigration — if one member is questioned, the group's shared bookings resolve it fastest.

Customs and border formalities

The Maldives enforces strict prohibitions: alcohol, pork products and religious materials contrary to Islam are prohibited and will be confiscated at the border — this catches first-time visitors every week. Declare medications with prescriptions.

Duty-free allowances are modest; the practical rule is to arrive clean — resort islands operate under the same prohibitions, so "buy it at the resort" is not a workaround.

Return and onward travel proof

A return or onward ticket is part of the standard arrival package alongside the IMUGA declaration and funds evidence. Officers check it as the core proof of a temporary stay.

Seaplane transfers to resorts are booked against your arrival flight — keep the resort transfer confirmation with your travel documents, as immigration occasionally cross-checks it.

Route-specific research pointers

Tourist: IMUGA within 96 hours, funds or booking, free 30-day arrival visa — the whole system. Work: employer-led Xpat process; research the Regulation on Employment of Expatriate Workers through official channels and confirm every fee with the employer. Study: no published student stream — get the institution's sponsorship arrangement in writing. Extension: apply to Maldives Immigration in Malé before day 30.

Final document audit before you fly

Run this checklist 96 hours before departure: 1) Passport machine-readable, valid for your stay (not an Israeli passport). 2) IMUGA submitted at imuga.immigration.gov.mv. 3) Confirmed hotel/resort booking (satisfies funds question) or USD 100 + USD 50/day accessible. 4) Return/onward ticket. 5) Travel insurance with evacuation cover. 6) No alcohol/pork in luggage. 7) For workers: Xpat approval completed by employer before travel. 8) For students: institution sponsorship arrangement in writing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa in advance to visit the Maldives?

No. Tourists receive a free 30-day visa on arrival at the immigration counter — no advance application, no fee, for all nationalities except holders of Israeli passports (banned from entry since 15 April 2025). You must still submit the free IMUGA Traveller Declaration within 96 hours before arrival.

What is the IMUGA Traveller Declaration?

The mandatory, free electronic arrival declaration at imuga.immigration.gov.mv, submitted within 96 hours before arrival. Every traveller — including children — needs their own. It covers immigration and health screening and is checked at the counter.

How much money do I need to show on arrival?

Official Maldives guidance: USD 100 plus USD 50 per day of stay, or a confirmed hotel/resort booking — the booking satisfies the requirement on its own. Carry a return/onward ticket as well.

Can I extend my 30-day visa in the Maldives?

Yes — extensions to 60–90 days are granted by Maldives Immigration. Apply in Malé before your initial 30 days expire; confirm the current fee on immigration.gov.mv.

Is there a student visa for the Maldives?

No — the Maldives publishes no dedicated student visa category, fee schedule or insurance requirement. Foreign students enter on a tourist visa or an institution/employer-sponsored special visa. Get the exact arrangement from your institution in writing before travelling; do not follow generic "student visa" checklists.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For the Maldives, the system is simple but strict: free 30-day visa on arrival for all nationalities except Israeli passport holders (banned since April 2025), the mandatory free IMUGA declaration within 96 hours before arrival, and USD 100 + USD 50/day or a confirmed booking as funds evidence. There is no tourist eVisa to buy, no student visa stream, and no official work-permit fee schedule — employment runs through the employer's Xpat system and study through institutional sponsorship. Confirm everything on immigration.gov.mv before you fly.
