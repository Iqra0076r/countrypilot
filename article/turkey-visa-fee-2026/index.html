Turkey Visa Fee 2026

How Turkey visa fees work in 2026

Türkiye prices visas by nationality, not from a global menu. The official Electronic Visa Application System (evisa.gov.tr) — run by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs — calculates your eVisa fee at checkout, based on your passport nationality. There is no single published global fee schedule, so any site quoting "the" Turkey visa price is guessing or quoting one nationality's figure.

This is the most important fact about Turkey visa fees: your checkout total on the official portal is the authoritative number. Budget around a per-nationality total, confirm the exact amount on evisa.gov.tr, and never pay a third party's quoted figure without checking the portal first.

eVisa fees: what to expect

For eVisa-eligible nationalities, the fee is calculated per nationality when you apply. Third-party sites report roughly US$50 as a typical figure, but that is unverified as a general figure — your nationality's actual total may differ, and the checkout screen is what counts.

Pay only on the official portal. Copycat websites charge multiples of the official fee for the same eVisa, and anything sold as a "guaranteed approval" surcharge is a scam — official fees go through official channels. For the full eligibility and application walkthrough, see the Turkey eVisa 2026 guide.

Consulate sticker-visa fees

Nationalities that are neither visa-exempt nor eVisa-eligible apply at a Turkish mission or application centre. Sticker-visa fees vary by nationality and consulate, and no single schedule is published here. Check the responsible mission's fee page before you budget — and include the appointment costs, since sticker routes require fingerprints and a digital photograph in person.

Allow weeks, not days: third parties report sticker-visa processing in weeks (student routes 2–8 weeks, work routes 15–30 days to 4–8 weeks), but no official processing-time schedule is published, so apply early.

Student visa fees and the money you must show

The education (student) visa is a 90-day single-entry visa costing approximately US$100–150, varying by nationality and consulate. After arrival, the residence permit is applied for on e-ikamet (eikamet.goc.gov.tr) — the visa gets you in, the residence permit lets you stay.

The visa fee is only part of the student budget. You must demonstrate roughly US$500–600 per month of funds capacity (about US$6,000–7,200 for one year) or present a scholarship award letter. Health insurance covering the full planned stay is mandatory — typical premiums run US$200–600, and some consulates require minimum EUR 30,000 coverage. Read the Turkey travel insurance and health guide before choosing a policy.

Work visa and permit fees

Workers need a work visa first, then a work permit. Permit fees are set annually in Turkish lira under the Harçlar tariff — no verified 2026 figure is given here, and no foreign-currency conversion is performed, because the lira figure moves with the annual tariff. Confirm the current tariff with the responsible mission or employer sponsor before budgeting.

The expensive mistake is trying to work on a visitor entry: visitor entries do not authorize work in any form, and taking a job on a tourist entry can lead to detention, removal and multi-year bans.

Health insurance: mandatory cover and typical cost

For stays in Türkiye, health insurance is mandatory, with typical premiums of US$200–600 depending on duration and cover level; some consulates set a floor of EUR 30,000 minimum coverage. Treat any stated minimum as the floor and buy a policy that meaningfully exceeds it, covering the full visa duration — a 90-day policy on a 180-day trip leaves a dangerous gap.

Read the exclusions before paying: adventure activities, pre-existing conditions and alcohol-related incidents are the most common claim rejections worldwide.

Paying safely: official channels and scam red flags

Per-nationality pricing is exactly what copycat sites exploit: a fake portal can quote a plausible-looking fee and pocket the difference. The rules: pay only on evisa.gov.tr or at the mission itself; never pay fees to personal accounts; and treat "guaranteed approval" language, pressure deadlines, and fees several times the official figure as scam signals. Screenshot every confirmation page and save every email receipt.

For the full fee-by-route table and entry rules, see the Turkey visa and entry requirements guide.

A fee audit before you pay

  • Identify your route first — visa-exempt, eVisa-eligible, or sticker visa — fees differ completely between them
  • Confirm the exact fee on the official portal or mission page for your nationality
  • Students: budget ~US$100–150 for the visa, US$500–600/month demonstrable funds, and mandatory insurance (US$200–600)
  • Workers: confirm the current Harçlar tariff figure — do not use last year's number
  • Pay through official channels only; keep payment receipts with the application reference
  • Build in buffer time: third parties report eVisa approval in 24–72 hours and sticker visas in weeks — all unverified, so apply early

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the Turkey eVisa fee in 2026?

It is calculated per nationality at checkout on the official portal (evisa.gov.tr) — there is no single published global fee. Third parties report around US$50, but that is unverified as a general figure. Your checkout total on the official site is the authoritative number.

Why is there no official Turkey visa fee list?

Türkiye prices visas per nationality: the portal computes your fee when you apply, so no single global schedule exists to publish. Any site quoting one fixed 'Turkey visa price' is quoting one nationality's figure at best.

How much does a Turkey student visa cost?

The 90-day single-entry education visa runs approximately US$100–150 by nationality and consulate. On top of that, budget roughly US$500–600/month in demonstrable funds (or a scholarship letter) and mandatory health insurance (US$200–600); the residence permit is applied for on e-ikamet after entry.

Do I need to show money for a Turkey visa?

Students must demonstrate about US$500–600/month of funds capacity or a scholarship award letter. General visitors are not asked for a fixed sum, but border officers may ask to see means of support — have bank evidence and an onward ticket ready.

Is health insurance mandatory for Turkey?

Yes for stays: health insurance covering the full planned stay is mandatory, with typical premiums of US$200–600. Some consulates require minimum EUR 30,000 coverage — treat any stated minimum as the floor.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

In Türkiye, the portal is the price list: the eVisa fee is calculated per nationality at checkout (no global schedule exists), the student visa runs ~US$100–150 plus US$500–600/month in demonstrable funds and mandatory insurance (US$200–600), and work permits price off the annual Harçlar tariff. Pay only through official channels, keep every receipt, and verify your nationality's exact total on evisa.gov.tr before committing money to flights.
