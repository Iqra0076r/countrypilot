Start with the current official advisory

Safety information is one of the fastest-changing parts of travel planning. Check a current government advisory for United Kingdom before booking, again before departure and during the trip if conditions are changing. Crime, scams and theft prevention

Routine precautions work best when they are specific. Use secure transport after late arrivals, avoid displaying large amounts of cash, protect phones in crowded areas, and keep passport copies separate from the original. At ATMs, prefer machines inside banks or controlled locations and shield the PIN. When paying, do not allow a card to disappear from view unnecessarily. For readers using United Kingdom Travel Safety & Security Guide, this is a “Crime, scams and theft prevention” issue rather than a stand-alone rule; verify it against IATA Travel Centre before committing money or documents.

Common travel scams worldwide include fake police or officials, manipulated taxi meters, unsolicited “guides,” friendship or romance setups, card skimming, accommodation-payment fraud and pressure to move a transaction to an unprotected channel. The local pattern differs, so read the crime section of the current advisory. Applied to United Kingdom Travel Safety & Security Guide, this belongs under “Crime, scams and theft prevention”. Transport and road safety

Road injury is a major travel risk globally. Check whether local driving is on the left or right, whether an International Driving Permit is needed, seat-belt and helmet enforcement, road quality, fuel availability and night-driving risk. A technically legal rental is not automatically a sensible choice for an unfamiliar road environment. For United Kingdom Travel Safety & Security Guide, connect this point to the “Transport and road safety” decision and re-check IATA Travel Centre before relying on it.

For taxis and ride-hailing, use established services and verify the vehicle and driver before entering. For trains, buses and ferries, protect valuables during boarding and overnight journeys and understand what happens if the last connection is cancelled. For this United Kingdom Travel Safety & Security Guide research path, the practical checkpoint is “Transport and road safety”; use IATA Travel Centre to confirm the live requirement where it can change.

Demonstrations, elections and major events

Political gatherings, demonstrations and large sporting or religious events can become crowded or disruptive even when they are peaceful. Avoid confrontations, obey local police instructions, and know alternative routes back to the accommodation. In some countries, photographing security forces or government facilities can itself create legal problems. Applied to United Kingdom Travel Safety & Security Guide, this belongs under “Demonstrations, elections and major events”. Local laws and cultural rules

Local law applies to visitors. Research alcohol, drugs, photography, dress, public behaviour, online speech, relationships, prescription medicine, drones and religious-site rules where relevant. Penalties can be much more severe than the traveller expects from home-country practice. Phone, data and digital security

Before departure, enable a device passcode, account recovery method and two-factor authentication that will still work if the main SIM is lost. Avoid accessing sensitive financial accounts over unknown public Wi-Fi without appropriate protection. Keep offline copies of bookings, insurance and emergency contacts in case mobile data is unavailable. Solo travellers, families and vulnerable travellers

Risk can differ by gender, age, disability, ethnicity, religion, sexual orientation and travel style. Review destination-specific laws and social norms before travel, especially where identity or relationships may have legal consequences. Families should agree on a meeting point and contact method if separated; solo travellers should share an itinerary with a trusted person when heading to remote areas. Create an emergency plan before you need it

  1. Save local police, ambulance and fire numbers where available and the contact details of your embassy or consulate.
  2. Keep one payment method and a small emergency reserve separate from the main wallet.
  3. Store passport, visa, insurance and booking copies securely offline.
  4. Know the route to a suitable hospital and the insurer’s assistance number.
  5. Enable official alert services where available.
  6. Have a plan for leaving a region if flights, roads or borders close unexpectedly.

Safety advice and insurance

Government warnings can interact with travel insurance. Some policies exclude travel against official advice or restrict cover for conflict, civil unrest, natural disasters or known events. Read the policy and advisory together before paying for non-refundable transport or accommodation. Accommodation safety

Check recent reviews for location, access control and late-night arrival experience, not only room quality. On arrival, understand emergency exits and how the building is secured. Avoid advertising the room number in public areas and use the in-room safe appropriately for documents and valuables if it is reliable. For readers using United Kingdom Travel Safety & Security Guide, this is a “Accommodation safety” issue rather than a stand-alone rule; verify it against IATA Travel Centre before committing money or documents.

For apartments and private rentals, verify who controls the property before sending a large deposit. A copied listing or pressure to pay by irreversible transfer is a warning sign. Keep the address offline so a lost internet connection does not prevent the traveller from returning. In United Kingdom Travel Safety & Security Guide, treat this as part of the “Accommodation safety” check; the live IATA Travel Centre reference below should be consulted before action.

Nightlife, alcohol and drink safety

Use the same risk controls abroad that would apply at home, with extra caution where language and legal systems are unfamiliar. Do not leave drinks unattended, avoid accepting transport from strangers, and have a plan for getting back without driving after alcohol. In some destinations, alcohol possession or public intoxication is restricted or illegal, so local law should be checked first. For readers using United Kingdom Travel Safety & Security Guide, this is a “Nightlife, alcohol and drink safety” issue rather than a stand-alone rule; verify it against WHO Travel Advice before committing money or documents.

Outdoor, marine and adventure activities

Food, water and heat safety

Food and water risk is highly local. Follow current health advice, use safe drinking water where tap-water quality is uncertain, and pay attention to hand hygiene. Heat illness can affect travellers quickly when sightseeing schedules are packed; hydration, shade and adjusted activity times are simple risk controls. Know the local alert channel and what the accommodation expects guests to do during severe weather, earthquake, wildfire, flood or other hazard relevant to the destination. Keep shoes, phone, essential medicine and identification accessible overnight. If authorities order an evacuation, follow local instructions rather than trying to preserve the original itinerary. If a passport is lost or stolen

Report the loss if local procedure requires it, contact the traveller’s embassy or consulate and obtain emergency documentation. Then check the destination immigration authority’s exit procedure; an emergency passport alone may not reproduce an entry visa or electronic record. Airlines and transit countries must also accept the replacement document. For United Kingdom Travel Safety & Security Guide, connect this point to the “If a passport is lost or stolen” decision and re-check IATA Travel Centre before relying on it.

If money or a phone is stolen

Freeze cards immediately, change important passwords if the phone was unlocked, and contact the mobile carrier to block the SIM where appropriate. Use a pre-planned backup payment method. Avoid relying on one banking app whose two-factor authentication is tied to the stolen SIM. What consular assistance can and cannot do

An embassy or consulate can usually help with emergency travel documents, contacts and information after serious incidents, but it cannot normally cancel local law, pay hotel or medical bills, guarantee release from detention or provide ordinary legal representation. Know the limits before an emergency so expectations are realistic. A simple daily safety check-in

For solo or remote travel, agree on a lightweight check-in routine with a trusted contact. Share the next major movement, not a minute-by-minute itinerary. If plans change, send the new destination before entering an area with weak signal. This is particularly useful for hiking, long drives and border routes. Cash, ATM and payment safety

Use ATMs in controlled locations where possible, inspect the machine for obvious tampering and shield the PIN. Split cash and cards between secure places. If a card is retained by an ATM, contact the bank through a verified number rather than accepting help from a stranger nearby. Decline unexpected requests to move money for someone else. Strikes, closures and sudden transport disruption

Keep enough time and money to recover from a cancelled train, ferry or domestic flight. Save a second route to the airport or border. During large demonstrations, severe weather or security incidents, transport may stop before an official warning reaches every traveller, so local news and operator alerts can be useful alongside government advice. Review the current regional advisory, save emergency and consular contacts, check weather, confirm the first transfer, understand local laws relevant to the itinerary and verify that insurance still covers every region being visited. Share the initial itinerary with a trusted contact and decide how often to check in.
