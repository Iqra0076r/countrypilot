How to use this Italy visa fee guide

Italy's visa fees are set by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MAECI) and published in official consular fee schedules — this guide draws every figure from those schedules and MAECI consular pages, verified in October 2026. Fees are paid at the competent Italian consulate as part of the application, and official payments are receipted.

Keep three buckets separate as you read: (1) the consular visa fee itself, (2) the residence-permit cost after arrival, and (3) the insurance and proof-of-funds amounts that consulates check in the same file. Confusing the buckets is the most common budgeting mistake. For the full route picture — who needs which visa and how applications work — see our Italy visa & entry requirements guide.

The Schengen visa fee in 2026

The short-stay Schengen visa (Type C, up to 90 days) costs €90 for adults, €45 for children aged 6–12, and is free for children under 6. This fee level has been in force since 11 June 2024 and is unchanged for 2026 — if an agent or website quotes a different adult fee for 2026, the figure is wrong or padded.

The fee applies per applicant, and each traveller — including infants — needs a separate application and appointment. At €45 for ages 6–12 and free under 6, a family of two adults and one 8-year-old pays €225 in consular fees. Always confirm the live amount on vistoperitalia.esteri.it before paying, since fee schedules are reissued periodically.

National visa fees: study, work, family

Stays over 90 days use national Type D visas, with their own 2026 fee schedule:

Students pay the lower €50 — but the study visa also requires the Universitaly pre-enrolment validation and the €10,179.85 per year funds evidence before the consulate will process it. Residence permit cost after arrival

The national visa gets you into Italy; the permesso di soggiorno (residence permit) completes your legal stay. Apply within 8 working days of arrival via the post-office kit. The permit itself costs €40–100 depending on its length, plus roughly €76 in extras:

  • €16 — marca da bollo (revenue stamp)
  • €30.46 — electronic card issuance
  • €30 — postal processing

Budget about €116–176 total for the permit step. Renewals later key off a different money figure entirely — the €7,101.12 per year assegno sociale (social allowance) baseline — which is a permit-income test, not a visa fee, and should not be confused with the €10,179.85 study-funds requirement. Track the permit on portaleimmigrazione.it.

Insurance and funds: the budget beside the fee

These are not visa fees, but consulates check them in the same file, so budget them together:

  • Schengen travel insurance: minimum €30,000 medical cover, valid Schengen-wide — a visa condition, not an option.
  • Student insurance benchmark: the consulate-accepted INA Assitalia foreign-student policy — €98 for 12 months (€49 for 6 months), purpose-built and widely accepted.
  • Alternatives (2026): voluntary SSN registration from €700 per year minimum for students, or private expat plans from about €27 per month.
  • Study proof of funds: €10,179.85 per year for 2026/27 — strictly enforced, and strictly separate from the assegno sociale figure above.

Read the full comparison in our Italy travel insurance & health guide before buying a policy — the wrong product wastes money and can stall the file.

Paying without getting scammed

Italy offers no eVisa — applications are in person at the competent consulate with biometrics on first application. So anyone 'selling' an Italy eVisa online is running a scam, and official fees are receipted at the consulate: never pay a 'visa fee' to a personal bank account or crypto wallet.

Two more rules from the official pages: no agent can secure a Flussi quota place after the click days close, and none can speed the nulla osta. And as a sanity check — anything charging far above the €90 / €50 / €116 official fees is selling anxiety, not service. If your plans may stretch beyond 90 days, read the Italy visa extension guide; for the whole trip picture, the Italy complete country travel guide covers planning end to end.

Fee questions to settle before applying

Is the fee per person or per application? Per person — each traveller needs a separate application and appointment, including infants (free under 6 for Schengen).

Why do different websites show different 2026 fees? The official figures are €90 / €50 / €116. Higher numbers usually bundle agency service charges — the agency keeps the difference. Ask what is official fee and what is service fee before paying.

Are the fees refundable if refused? Official guidance treats the fee as payment for processing the application — confirm the refund position on your consulate's own page before applying, and keep the receipt either way.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an Italy visa cost in 2026?

The Schengen visa costs €90 for adults, €45 for children aged 6–12, and is free for under-6s (in force since June 2024, unchanged for 2026). The national study visa (Type D) costs €50; national work, family, elective residence and EU Blue Card visas cost €116. Figures are from official 2026 consular fee schedules.

Are there fees after arrival in Italy?

Yes. National-visa holders must apply for the permesso di soggiorno within 8 working days of arrival: €40–100 for the permit by length, plus about €76 in extras (marca da bollo €16, card €30.46, postal €30) — roughly €116–176 total.

Does the visa fee include travel insurance?

No. Insurance is separate and mandatory for the visa file: minimum €30,000 Schengen-wide cover for Schengen visas. For study visas, the consulate-accepted benchmark is the INA Assitalia policy at €98 for 12 months.

Why is an agency quoting more than €90 for a Schengen visa?

The official adult fee is €90. Agencies add their own service charges on top — ask for a written split of official fee vs service fee. Official fees are receipted at the consulate; never pay a 'visa fee' to a personal account.

Is the €116 fee the same for the EU Blue Card?

Yes — the €116 national visa fee (2026) applies to the Blue Card route too. The Blue Card advantage is procedural, not financial: it is quota-exempt, so there is no Decreto Flussi click day to wait for.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Budget Italy's 2026 visa costs in three buckets: the consular fee (Schengen €90 adults / €45 children 6–12 / free under 6; study Type D €50; work, family, elective and Blue Card €116), the residence permit after arrival (€40–100 + ~€76), and the file costs beside them (INA Assitalia €98 for students, €10,179.85/year study funds). Pay only at the consulate with an official receipt, and treat anything charging far above €90/€50/€116 as a markup to walk away from.
