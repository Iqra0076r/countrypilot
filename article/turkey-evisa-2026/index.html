Turkey eVisa 2026: Fees, Eligibility & How to Apply

What the Turkey eVisa is

The Turkey eVisa is an online travel authorization issued through the Republic of Türkiye Electronic Visa Application System (evisa.gov.tr), run by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. It is a purely online authorization — there is no sticker in the passport and no biometric enrollment before departure (sticker-visa routes, by contrast, require fingerprints and a digital photograph at the mission). The eVisa is linked to the passport number entered in the application, so the passport you travel on must be the passport the eVisa was issued against.

There is no general visa-on-arrival in Türkiye: eligible travellers get the eVisa, the visa-exempt enter freely, and everyone else needs a sticker visa from a mission.

Who is eligible for the eVisa

Eligibility is set per nationality. Three broad groups exist: (1) visa-exempt nationalities — many enter freely for up to 90 days in 180; (2) eVisa-eligible nationalities — apply online before travel; (3) everyone else — sticker visa at a Turkish mission. The only reliable way to place yourself is the official portal: check your per-nationality position on evisa.gov.tr rather than trusting a friend's experience with a different passport.

Note that the portal is also where you apply — eligibility check and application live in the same place. If the portal does not offer you an eVisa, that is your answer: use the mission route.

How to apply on the official portal

  • Use evisa.gov.tr only. It is the official channel; every other domain is a private business.
  • Check eligibility for your nationality on the portal.
  • Complete the application with the passport you will travel on — the eVisa is linked to its number.
  • Pay the fee at checkout — calculated per nationality; the checkout total is the authoritative figure (see the Turkey visa fee guide).
  • Save everything: screenshot confirmations, keep email receipts, and print the approval — phone screens fail at the wrong moment and some carriers require paper.

What the eVisa costs

The eVisa fee is calculated per nationality at checkout; there is no single published global fee schedule. Third-party sites report around US$50, but that is unverified as a general figure — your nationality's actual total is whatever the official checkout shows. Never pay a third party's quoted figure without checking the portal.

How long eVisa approval takes

No official processing-time schedule is published. Third parties report eVisa approval in 24–72 hours, but that is unverified — treat published times as targets, not promises, and apply well before booking non-refundable flights. Sticker-visa routes take weeks, so if you are near a deadline, check eligibility early enough to switch routes.

Passport rules for the eVisa

Your passport must be valid for at least 60 days beyond the visa's expiry — Türkiye's distinctive formulation — with blank pages. Because the eVisa is linked to the passport number, a new or renewed passport means a new eVisa application: the old authorization does not transfer. Airlines deny boarding when the validity arithmetic fails, and there is no appeal at the gate.

Copycat sites and scam avoidance

Online applications live or die on the portal you choose. The Republic of Türkiye Electronic Visa Application System is the official channel; everything else is a private business. Tell-tale signs of a copycat: fees several times the official figure, "guaranteed approval" language, and payment to personal accounts. Per-nationality pricing makes fakes plausible — the only safe number is the one on evisa.gov.tr.

For the full fee-by-route table and entry rules, see the Turkey visa and entry requirements guide.

Pre-travel eVisa checklist

  • Eligibility confirmed on evisa.gov.tr for your nationality
  • Application completed on the passport you will travel with; approval printed
  • Fee paid through the official portal; receipts saved
  • Passport valid 60+ days beyond visa expiry, with blank pages
  • Onward ticket, accommodation and funds evidence ready for the border
  • Plans beyond tourism (work, study) matched to the correct permission — visitor entry covers neither
  • Departure planned inside the granted stay; extensions are never guaranteed
