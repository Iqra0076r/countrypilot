How to use this United Arab Emirates visa and entry guide

The UAE's visa system is sponsor-based: almost every visa is issued through a sponsor — an airline, hotel, employer, university, or family member — via the ICP (Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs & Port Security) at icp.gov.ae or GDRFA Dubai at gdrfad.gov.ae. The official information portal is u.ae. There is no UAE embassy tourist-visa application in the conventional sense; the sponsor applies inside the UAE.

This guide was verified against GDRFA Dubai's fee schedule and 2026 policy reporting in October 2026. The headline 2026 change: visa-on-arrival eligibility was expanded in June 2026 to six more nationalities holding qualifying residence permits. Two figures this guide deliberately omits — a rumoured $4,000 bank-balance rule and any single 'official' student-visa fee — because neither could be verified against a government source.

Passport validity and document condition

The UAE generally requires the passport to be valid for at least 6 months from the date of arrival for visitors. Airlines enforce this strictly on UAE-bound flights — a passport under 6 months means denied boarding at origin.

Carry at least one blank page. For residence-visa applicants, the passport must cover the visa's validity period, and the medical-fitness and Emirates ID steps after arrival all key off the same document — renew before starting if expiry is near.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

First, visa on arrival: around 50+ nationalities — including the US, UK, EU states, Japan and Singapore — receive 30 to 90 days on arrival depending on nationality. Since June 2026, nationals of Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, the Philippines, Kenya and South Africa who hold valid residence permits of the US, EU, UK, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand or Canada also qualify: 14 days for AED 100 or 60 days for AED 250. Second, the pre-arranged tourist visa: applied for inside the UAE by a sponsor (airline, hotel, travel agency). GDRFA Dubai's 2026 schedule: AED 250 (30-day single-entry), AED 690 (30-day multiple), AED 600 (90-day single), AED 1,740 (90-day multiple), plus 5% VAT — the exact fee depends on the channel and emirate. Third, residence visas: employment, Green Visa (self-sponsored 5-year residence), student, family — each with its own sponsor and fee structure.

Transit and onward travel

The UAE issues dedicated 48-hour and 96-hour transit visas for stopover passengers — applied for before travel through the airline, single-entry, and not extendable. If your layover is under the transit window and you hold an onward ticket, this is the purpose-built route.

Do not confuse transit visas with visa-on-arrival eligibility: if your nationality qualifies for VoA, you may simply enter. If it does not, arrange the transit visa in advance — Dubai and Abu Dhabi airports will not issue one at the counter to non-eligible nationalities.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

UAE immigration may ask for the return or onward ticket, hotel booking or host details, and evidence of funds for the stay — particularly for first-time visitors on pre-arranged tourist visas. Keep the visa copy (electronic) accessible on your phone; the e-visa is linked to the passport number, but having the PDF avoids counter delays.

Residence-visa holders entering for the first time should carry the entry permit copy. Note the persistent agency rumour of a $4,000 bank-balance requirement for tourist visas: no UAE government source publishes such a rule, and this guide does not repeat it — carry normal funds evidence and ignore the claim.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

The UAE requires no vaccination certificate for ordinary visitors and operates no pandemic-era border health checks. No health document is presented at the UAE border.

The health step that matters is the medical fitness test for residence-visa applicants (blood test and chest X-ray at a government health centre after arrival) — a residence formality, not a visitor requirement.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is not a UAE entry condition for tourists, though it is strongly advisable given private healthcare costs. For student residence, health insurance is mandatory: basic plans run about AED 600–1,200 per year, comprehensive cover up to about AED 3,000 per year (2026).

Dubai's mandatory health-insurance regime means every resident must hold cover — universities typically bundle or require proof of a compliant policy before issuing the residence visa. Budget the insurance as part of the first-year cost, not as an afterthought.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Pre-arranged tourist visas are issued for the exact validity purchased (30 or 90 days, single or multiple entry) — the stay cannot exceed the visa's validity, and overstaying accrues daily fines. Visa-on-arrival entries are typically 30 days, extendable per current GDRFA rules.

Residence-visa holders must complete the post-arrival steps (medical, Emirates ID, visa stamping/issuance) within the entry permit's validity — usually 60 days. An entry permit is not the residence visa; it only authorizes the arrival that starts the process.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A tourist visa never authorizes employment — working on a visit visa is an offence for both worker and employer. Employment requires an employer-sponsored work permit and residence visa (5–15 working days processing in 2026).

The notable self-sponsored route is the Green Visa (5-year residence): government fee components are AED 200 residence permit + AED 10 Knowledge Dirham + AED 10 Innovation Dirham (plus AED 500 if applied for inside the UAE), with a base of about AED 740 including delivery — but total first-year all-in costs (medical, Emirates ID, insurance) typically run AED 3,500–6,000 (2026). For students: the university sponsors the entry permit and residence visa, with all-in costs of roughly AED 3,560–5,560 per year covering the medical (~AED 300–500), Emirates ID (~AED 270/year) and permit fees — there is no single officially published 'student visa fee', and any source quoting one as official is guessing. Renewals typically run AED 2,000–3,500 per year (2026). No UAE-government-published minimum proof-of-funds figure exists for students; universities require bank statements or a sponsor letter demonstrating living-expense capability.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Each child needs a separate visa linked to their own passport — UAE visas are per-person, including infants. Minors travelling without both parents should carry parental consent documentation.

Dual nationals should enter on the passport that carries the visa or VoA eligibility, and use the same passport consistently through the trip — the e-visa is tied to a specific passport number.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Tourist visa (pre-arranged): 1) Choose a sponsor — airline (Emirates, Etihad, flydubai), hotel, or licensed travel agency. 2) Submit the passport copy, photo and application through the sponsor's portal. 3) Pay the applicable fee (e.g. AED 250 for 30-day single-entry, plus 5% VAT, 2026 GDRFA schedule). 4) Receive the e-visa, typically within ~48 working hours (2026). 5) Verify the details match the passport exactly before flying.

Residence (work/student): 1) The sponsor (employer or university) applies for the entry permit via ICP/GDRFA. 2) Enter the UAE on the entry permit. 3) Complete the medical fitness test. 4) Apply for the Emirates ID (biometrics). 5) Health insurance is issued/verified. 6) The residence visa is issued — total process typically 2–4 weeks for students, 5–15 working days for employment (2026).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Believing the $4,000 balance rumour. No government source publishes it — don't reshape your finances around agency folklore.
  • Working on a tourist visa. An offence for both sides; the work permit route takes only 5–15 working days.
  • Assuming VoA eligibility. Check the current list — it expanded in June 2026, but most South Asian and African nationalities still need pre-arranged visas.
  • Letting the entry permit lapse. The 60-day entry permit is not the residence visa — complete medical + Emirates ID inside it.
  • Booking before the e-visa details are verified. A passport-number typo voids the visa at the counter.
  • Ignoring the 5% VAT. Quoted GDRFA fees exclude it — budget accordingly.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

UAE tourist-visa decisions hinge on the sponsor's filing: a clear passport copy, a compliant photograph, and details matching the passport exactly. Most 'rejections' are actually data mismatches — re-check the passport number character by character before the sponsor submits.

For residence routes, quality means completing the chain without gaps: entry permit → medical → Emirates ID → insurance → visa issuance. Applicants who stall between steps risk the entry permit expiring, which restarts the process and the fees.

Tourist e-visas are typically issued within ~48 working hours (2026). Employment residence visas take 5–15 working days; student residence via university sponsorship 2–4 weeks after document submission (2026). There are no embassy interviews for tourist visas — the process is entirely sponsor-driven and online.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

The UAE's tourist 'e-visa' is real but sponsor-issued: it arrives as a PDF linked to your passport number, applied for through the airline, hotel or agency — not through a government application you file yourself. Track it via the sponsor or the ICP/GDRFA reference.

Scam avoidance: never pay a 'visa fee' to an individual's bank account or crypto wallet — legitimate fees go through the sponsor's official channel with a receipt. Be skeptical of agents quoting rules with no government source (like the $4,000 balance myth). The ICP and GDRFA websites, plus u.ae, are the only authorities.

Translations, certifications and document format

Tourist applications need little: a clear passport bio-page copy (6+ months validity), a compliant photograph, and the application details matching the passport exactly. Residence applications add attested degree certificates (for work), admission letters (for study), and the medical fitness result.

Degree attestation for employment visas runs through UAE embassy + home-country foreign ministry channels — start it early, as it is the longest lead-time item in the work-visa chain and entirely separate from the visa itself.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

There are no interviews for UAE tourist visas. Biometrics enter the picture for residence: the Emirates ID application captures fingerprints, photograph and signature at an ICP service centre after arrival, and the medical fitness test runs in parallel.

At the border, immigration may verify the visa electronically against the passport — which is why the passport number on the e-visa must match the physical document exactly.

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs a separate visa linked to their own passport — including infants. Family residence visas are sponsored by the employed family member, subject to salary thresholds set by the emirate.

Group tourist applications through one sponsor are routine; ensure every passport copy is legible, as one bad scan can hold up the whole group's batch.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Travellers must declare cash and monetary instruments above AED 100,000 (or equivalent) — the UAE's declaration threshold. Standard restrictions apply to medications (controlled substances need prior approval and prescriptions), and the UAE has strict prohibitions on certain goods — check the ICP customs list before packing.

Departure and future travel

Tourist visas cannot be converted into residence visas by simply staying — exiting and re-entering on the correct permit is the compliant path, and overstay fines accrue daily. Keep the e-visa PDF until departure; it is occasionally re-checked.

Residence-visa holders should keep the Emirates ID valid and be mindful of the outside-UAE duration rules that can invalidate residence — check current ICP guidance before long absences.

How to research a route without getting misled

Use this hierarchy: 1) u.ae for the official overview of every visa type. 2) GDRFA Dubai (gdrfad.gov.ae) for Dubai-specific fees and VoA lists. 3) ICP (icp.gov.ae) for federal residence matters. 4) Your sponsor (airline/university/employer) for the practical filing steps.

Discount any rule quoted without a government source — the UAE visa space is full of agency folklore (the $4,000 balance claim being the prime example). If it isn't on u.ae, GDRFA or ICP, treat it as unverified.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid 6+ months from arrival, with a blank page
  • E-visa PDF received and passport number triple-checked against it
  • VoA eligibility confirmed on the current list if relying on arrival issuance
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation proof accessible offline
  • Funds evidence for the stay (ignore the $4,000 myth; carry normal proof)
  • Travel insurance (mandatory for student residence: AED 600–3,000/yr)
  • Sponsor contact saved
  • For residence: degree attestation started early; medical + Emirates ID scheduled
  • Customs list checked for medications and restricted goods

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Dubai?

Yes, in most cases. Indian passport holders need a pre-arranged tourist visa (from AED 250 for 30 days single-entry, plus 5% VAT, 2026) applied for through an airline, hotel or agency — typically issued within 48 working hours. There is no visa on arrival for Indian nationals without a qualifying US/EU/UK (etc.) residence permit.

What changed in UAE visa-on-arrival rules in 2026?

In June 2026, the UAE expanded visa-on-arrival to nationals of Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, the Philippines, Kenya and South Africa who hold valid residence permits of the US, EU, UK, Singapore, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand or Canada — 14 days for AED 100 or 60 days for AED 250. Around 50+ other nationalities (US, UK, EU, Japan, Singapore) already received 30–90 days on arrival.

Is there really a $4,000 bank balance requirement for a UAE tourist visa?

No UAE government source publishes such a rule. The claim circulates through visa agencies only and could not be verified against u.ae, ICP or GDRFA publications. Carry normal evidence of funds for your stay and disregard the figure.

Can I work in the UAE on a tourist visa?

No — working on a tourist visa is an offence for both worker and employer. Employment requires an employer-sponsored work permit and residence visa (processed in 5–15 working days, 2026). The self-sponsored Green Visa (5 years) is the main alternative, with government fee components from AED 220.

How much does a UAE student visa cost?

There is no single officially published student-visa fee — the university sponsors the entry permit and residence visa, with all-in costs of roughly AED 3,560–5,560 per year (2026) covering the medical (~AED 300–500), Emirates ID (~AED 270/year) and permit fees, plus mandatory health insurance (AED 600–3,000/year). Any source quoting one 'official' fee is guessing.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For the UAE, the sponsor is the system: tourist visas come pre-arranged through an airline, hotel or agency (from AED 250 + 5% VAT, ~48-hour issuance, 2026), while residence runs through ICP/GDRFA with post-arrival medical and Emirates ID steps. Check the June-2026-expanded visa-on-arrival list before assuming you need a pre-arranged visa, keep 6 months' passport validity, and ignore unsourced agency folklore like the $4,000 balance myth. Confirm fees on u.ae or GDRFA — they vary by emirate and channel.
