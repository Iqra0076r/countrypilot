Canada eTA vs Visitor Visa 2026: Which One You Need and What Each Costs

Canada has two doors for short visits and they are not interchangeable. Here is how to tell which one your passport uses in 2026 — and what each costs.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20266 min read

Travellers to Canada constantly price the wrong product: the eTA when they need the visitor visa (TRV), or the full TRV process when the eTA would do. The two are different documents for different passports, with different prices and timelines. This guide puts each traveller in the right lane for 2026.

Quick Answer

Nationals of visa-exempt countries (the UK among them) need only an eTA — applied for online before flying. Nationals of India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines, and Bangladesh need the full visitor visa (TRV): CAD $100 plus CAD $85 biometrics per person (2026), applied for through an IRCC account, allowing stays of up to 6 months per entry. The test is nationality, nothing else.

TRV CAD $100 + $85 · Stay up to 6 months/entry · Updated 2026-10-14 · Reading time 6 minutes

The eTA lane: visa-exempt passports

The Electronic Travel Authorization is advance permission to fly to Canada for nationals of visa-exempt countries — the UK among them. It is applied for online before travel, it is not a visa, and it involves no interview and no biometrics. If your passport is visa-exempt, the eTA is the whole process: do not apply for a TRV you do not need, and do not pay TRV fees for an eTA trip.

The TRV lane: everyone else

If your country is not visa-exempt — India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines, Bangladesh among them — the route is the Temporary Resident Visa: online application through an IRCC account, CAD $100 visa fee plus CAD $85 biometrics per person, biometrics at a Visa Application Centre, then the decision wait. Families of 5+ applying together cap visa fees at CAD $500. The TRV allows stays of up to 6 months per entry, with the officer at the port of entry setting the actual length.

Which lane are you in?

Check your nationality against IRCC's lists — not your residence, not your travel history. The common errors run both ways: visa-required travellers assuming a light eTA will cover them, and visa-exempt travellers overcomplicating a simple eTA trip with a TRV application. One check on canada.ca settles it before any money is paid.

Timelines compared

The lanes differ in time as well as money. The eTA is typically quick — an online authorisation, not a casework queue. The TRV runs on IRCC's processing pipeline: a 14-day service standard for visitor applications outside Canada, against recent actuals around 27 days (September 2026) — and actuals move weekly, so check IRCC's processing-times tool. Full detail in our Canada processing-time guide.

Paying correctly

Both products are paid through official IRCC channels — the eTA on its application page, the TRV inside the IRCC account flow with the published fee list as the schedule. Third-party sites selling either at a markup add nothing the government does not provide directly. Keep every receipt: the eTA confirmation for boarding, the TRV fee receipts for the biometrics step.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an eTA or a visitor visa for Canada?

Visa-exempt nationalities (e.g. the UK): eTA. India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines, Bangladesh: the full visitor visa (TRV).

How much is the Canada visitor visa in 2026?

CAD $100 plus CAD $85 biometrics per person; families of 5+ applying together cap visa fees at CAD $500.

Can I enter Canada with just an eTA if my country needs a visa?

No. The eTA does not replace the TRV — visa-required nationals must hold the visitor visa before travel.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

eTA for visa-exempt passports, TRV at $100-plus-$85 for everyone else: your nationality picks the lane — check it on canada.ca before paying, and never buy the wrong product for the right trip.
