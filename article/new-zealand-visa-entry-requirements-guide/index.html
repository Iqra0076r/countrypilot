How to use this New Zealand visa and entry guide

New Zealand runs one of the more transparent immigration systems in the world: Immigration New Zealand (INZ) at immigration.govt.nz publishes an official interactive tool covering fees, decision times and where to apply for every visa type. In 2026, the single most useful habit is checking that tool for your specific visa before trusting any static figure — including the representative numbers in this guide.

New Zealand also has its own vocabulary worth learning early: the NZeTA (electronic travel authority for visa-waiver travellers), the IVL (International Visitor Conservation and Tourism Levy, NZD $100), the AEWV (Accredited Employer Work Visa), and the median wage (NZD $35.00/hour from 9 March 2026) that anchors work-visa thresholds. This guide was verified against INZ publications in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

INZ requires the passport to be valid for at least 3 months beyond your intended departure from New Zealand — for both visa-waiver travellers and visa holders. This is stricter than it sounds: a passport expiring two months after your planned return fails the rule.

Keep at least one blank page. Because New Zealand issues visas electronically (no passport sticker), the passport number on the approval must match the travel document exactly — a renewed passport after visa issuance needs the visa transferred.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

First, visa-waiver + NZeTA: around 60 visa-waiver nationalities — including the US and UK — travel without a visa but must obtain an NZeTA before departure: NZD $17 via the app / $23 via the web, plus the $100 IVL, valid 2 years (2026). Second, the Visitor Visa: for nationalities including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh — NZD $441 total for most applicants (Band C outside NZ: $300 application fee + $41 immigration levy + $100 IVL), plus the VAC service fee (increased 1 January 2026). A temporary 12-month reduction to NZD $161 applies for eligible Pacific nationals from 1 June 2026. Third, work: the Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) — NZD $1,540 (raised from $750 in October 2024, unchanged since), with employer accreditation ($775) and job check ($735) paid separately by the employer. Fourth, study: the Fee Paying Student Visa — NZD $750 (from October 2024) plus the $100 IVL.

Transit and onward travel

New Zealand has no visa-on-arrival, and transit is not exempt: visa-required nationals transiting through Auckland generally need a transit visa even without leaving the airport, unless covered by a specific transit arrangement. Check INZ's transit rules for your nationality before booking — Pacific routings through Auckland catch travellers out regularly.

Visa-waiver nationals transiting still need the NZeTA. Australian citizens and permanent residents travel under the Trans-Tasman arrangement and are treated separately.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

INZ officers may ask for the onward or return ticket, accommodation details, and evidence of funds — the visitor funds guideline is around NZD $1,000 per person per month (or $400 with prepaid accommodation). Keep the visa approval or NZeTA confirmation accessible; both are electronic and linked to the passport.

Work and student visa holders should carry the job offer or enrollment confirmation backing the visa. First-time AEWV holders should have the employer's accreditation and job-check details available if questioned.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

New Zealand requires no vaccination certificate for ordinary visitors and operates no pandemic-era border health checks. No health document is presented at the border.

Longer-stay applicants (generally over 12 months) undergo INZ medical and chest X-ray requirements as part of the visa application — a pre-departure step, not a border document.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is not a border requirement for tourists, but comprehensive medical and travel insurance is a mandatory condition of the student visa: the common plans (Studentsafe, UniCare, Southern Cross, nib) run NZD $500–900 per year, with Studentsafe Inbound University at $899 single / $1,798 couple for 2026.

New Zealand's accident-compensation scheme (ACC) covers injuries but not illness — and it is no substitute for the mandatory student insurance. Buy compliant cover before the visa application, since INZ checks it in the file.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Visitor-visa holders are admitted for the period on the visa; visa-waiver travellers are typically granted up to 3 months (British citizens up to 6 months). The NZeTA itself is valid 2 years but each stay is separately determined.

Work and student visa holders are admitted for the visa duration. Overstaying in New Zealand carries serious consequences including deportation liability — and INZ systems link records across all future applications.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A visitor visa or NZeTA never authorizes work. The work route is the Accredited Employer Work Visa: the employer must first hold accreditation ($775) and pass a job check ($735) — both employer-paid and both prerequisites to the worker's $1,540 application (2026). From 9 March 2026, the immigration median wage rose to NZD $35.00/hour (from $33.56): although the general AEWV median-wage requirement was removed in March 2025, the median wage still anchors Green List pay rates, 2×-median advertising exemptions and family support thresholds. Also from 9 March 2026, 47 new skill level 1–3 occupations joined the National Occupation List as INZ transitions from ANZSCO to NOL for AEWV settings.

Students: the Fee Paying Student Visa costs NZD $750 + $100 IVL (from October 2024, current through 2026), with a living-costs requirement of NZD $20,000 per year for tertiary courses of a year or more ($1,667 per month for shorter courses) — tuition and outward travel are separate additional requirements. Do not arrive as a visitor intending to job-hunt into an AEWV; the accreditation and job-check sequence starts with the employer, from outside.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Each child needs a separate passport and, where applicable, a separate visa or NZeTA — including infants. The IVL and NZeTA charges apply per traveller.

Dual nationals should travel on the passport matching their visa or visa-waiver status. Australian citizens and permanent residents use the Trans-Tasman arrangement rather than the standard visitor system.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Visitor Visa (visa-required nationalities): 1) Check the INZ 'fees, decision times and where to apply' tool for your visa type. 2) Apply online at immigration.govt.nz with the passport bio page, photo, funds evidence and itinerary. 3) Pay NZD $441 total (2026; plus VAC service fee). 4) Allow an average of ~1 week, most completed within 2 (INZ official tool, 2026).

NZeTA (visa-waiver nationalities): 1) Apply via the app ($17) or web ($23) plus the $100 IVL (2026). 2) Allow up to 72 hours — apply well before departure despite usually-fast approvals.

AEWV: 1) Employer secures accreditation and job check. 2) Worker applies online ($1,540, 2026). 3) Allow an average of 1.5–3 weeks, most within 3–8.5 (INZ tool, 2026).

Student visa: 1) Accept the offer and pay tuition as required. 2) Arrange the $20,000/year funds evidence and mandatory insurance. 3) Apply online ($750 + $100 IVL). 4) Allow ~20 working days online baseline, 4–8 weeks typical in practice (2026).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping the NZeTA. Visa-waiver does not mean document-free — no NZeTA, no boarding.
  • Forgetting the IVL. The $100 levy sits on top of the NZeTA and most visa fees — budget it.
  • Using the old AEWV fee. It's $1,540 since October 2024, not the $750 older guides quote.
  • Working on a visitor visa/NZeTA. Never authorized — the AEWV sequence is employer-led from the start.
  • Ignoring the median-wage change. NZD $35.00/hour from 9 March 2026 still anchors Green List and family thresholds.
  • Under-insuring as a student. Comprehensive cover is a visa condition, not a recommendation — $500–900/year is the real line item.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

INZ visitor decisions turn on genuine intent and funds: a return ticket, accommodation bookings, employment at home and bank statements showing the NZD $1,000/month guideline (or $400 with prepaid accommodation). Prior compliant travel to comparable countries helps; previous overstays anywhere hurt.

For AEWV, quality is the employer's sequence — accreditation, then job check, then the worker's application, each complete. For students, the $20,000 funds evidence plus mandatory insurance plus the tuition arrangement form a three-legged file; missing any leg stalls it.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

New Zealand's system is genuinely digital: visitor, work and student visas are applied for online at immigration.govt.nz and issued electronically — no sticker, no passport submission. The NZeTA is applied for via the official app or website.

Scam avoidance: the NZeTA costs $17/$23 + $100 IVL — copycat sites charge multiples for the same submission. No agent can influence INZ processing times; the weekly-published tool is the honest picture. Never pay a 'visa fee' outside INZ's official payment flow.

Translations, certifications and document format

Upload clear scans: passport bio page, compliant photograph, bank statements, employment letter, and the itinerary. INZ is specific about photo and scan quality — poor uploads are a common avoidable delay.

Documents not in English need certified English translations. For AEWV, the employer's accreditation and job-check documents are filed by the employer; for students, the offer of place and fee receipts anchor the file.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

New Zealand visa applications are document-driven with no routine interview. Identity is established through the passport, photograph and — for longer stays — the INZ medical and police requirements rather than in-person biometrics at a visa centre.

At the border, officers may verify purpose, funds and onward travel — carry the evidence behind the application. INZ's systems link all prior applications, so consistency across filings matters.

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs a separate visa or NZeTA — including infants — with per-person fees and the per-person IVL. Partners and dependent children of work/student visa holders apply for their own visas linked to the principal applicant.

Group applications are routine; a refusal for one member doesn't affect the others, but address the reason before re-applying since INZ records are linked.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

New Zealand's biosecurity is among the world's strictest: declare all food, plants, animal products and outdoor equipment — on-the-spot fines for undeclared items are routine and substantial. Cash above NZD $10,000 must be declared.

Medications should be in original packaging with prescriptions. Check INZ and MPI guidance before packing anything organic.

Departure and future travel

Depart within the permitted stay — overstaying creates deportation liability and poisons future applications across all categories. Keep electronic copies of every visa approval; with no stickers, the approval email is your record.

Work and student visa holders should keep visas valid and be aware of conditions (e.g. student work-hour limits) — breaching conditions can lead to deportation liability even with a valid visa.

How to research a route without getting misled

Use this hierarchy: 1) immigration.govt.nz — including the official fees/decision-times tool, which overrides every static figure. 2) The specific visa page for your category. 3) Licensed immigration advisers for complex cases (only use licensed advisers — it's a legal requirement in NZ). 4) The institution or employer for student/work practicalities.

Ignore guides quoting the old $750 AEWV fee or omitting the IVL — both have been wrong since October 2024. Anything charging far above INZ's published fees is selling anxiety.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid 3+ months beyond intended departure
  • Visitor visa approval, or NZeTA obtained before departure (visa-waiver nationalities)
  • IVL paid where applicable ($100)
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation proof accessible offline
  • Funds evidence (~NZD $1,000/month guideline)
  • Student file: $20,000/yr funds + mandatory insurance ($500–900/yr)
  • Work file: employer accreditation + job check complete before worker applies
  • Biosecurity declaration prepared — declare ALL food/plant/animal items
  • Cash >NZD $10,000 declaration readiness
  • INZ contact saved for emergencies

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for New Zealand?

Yes. Indian passport holders need a Visitor Visa applied for online at immigration.govt.nz — NZD $441 total (2026: $300 fee + $41 levy + $100 IVL, plus VAC service fee), with average processing of about 1 week. There is no visa on arrival; the NZeTA is only for visa-waiver nationalities.

What is the NZeTA and how much does it cost?

The New Zealand Electronic Travel Authority — mandatory for visa-waiver travellers (including US and UK citizens) before departure. It costs NZD $17 via the app or $23 via the web, plus the $100 IVL (2026), and is valid 2 years. Apply at least 72 hours before travel; copycat sites charge multiples of the official price.

How much does a New Zealand work visa cost in 2026?

The Accredited Employer Work Visa is NZD $1,540 (raised from $750 in October 2024, unchanged since). The employer separately pays $775 accreditation + $735 job check before the worker can apply. From 9 March 2026, the immigration median wage is NZD $35.00/hour, which still anchors Green List pay and family thresholds.

Can I work in New Zealand on a visitor visa?

No. Visitor visas and NZeTAs never authorize work. Employment requires the Accredited Employer Work Visa, whose accreditation and job-check sequence is employer-led and must complete before the worker's application.

How much money do I need for a New Zealand student visa?

NZD $20,000 per year in living-costs evidence for tertiary courses of a year or more ($1,667 per month for shorter courses) — plus tuition fees and outward travel separately. Comprehensive medical and travel insurance is a mandatory visa condition (NZD $500–900/year, 2026).

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For New Zealand, start at immigration.govt.nz and check the official fees-and-wait-times tool for your exact visa — it overrides every static figure. Budget the real 2026 numbers: $441 visitor visa ($161 for eligible Pacific nationals from June 2026), $1,540 AEWV, $750 student visa, each plus the $100 IVL; NZeTA $17/$23 + $100 IVL for visa-waiver travellers. Keep 3 months' passport validity beyond departure, carry the $20,000/year student funds evidence plus mandatory insurance, and declare everything at biosecurity — New Zealand fines first and asks later.
