How to use this Kenya visa and entry guide

Kenya's 2026 system is in motion. The baseline: the electronic Travel Authorisation (eTA) at etakenya.go.ke costs US$30, is mandatory for all visitors, and the old visa-on-arrival is abolished — there is no airport fallback. Processing runs about 72 hours (not an official SLA).

The October 2026 change: Kenya scrapped the eTA requirement for African and Caribbean nationals (excluding Somalia and Libya) — they now enter visa-free for up to 2 months (EAC nationals 6 months). Everyone else still needs the eTA. Student passes run KES 5,000/year. This guide was verified in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for at least 6 months beyond arrival with blank pages. Apply for the eTA at least 72 hours before travel — airlines check it at boarding, and there is no VOA fallback.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Check the October 2026 exemption first: African/Caribbean nationals (except Somalia, Libya) no longer need the eTA. Everyone else — including all seven nationalities below — applies online before flying.

Transit and onward travel

Kenya's eTA covers transit-style entries — but the eTA itself takes ~72 hours, so a tight connection booked without one is a denied-boarding risk. The October 2026 African/Caribbean exemption covers transit too. Confirm the current exemption scope before relying on it.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever Kenya's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the eTA portal approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

No vaccination certificate is a standard Kenyan entry requirement for ordinary tourists arriving from non-endemic areas. Check current advisories — requirements can be added during health events.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance is not a Kenyan eTA condition, but it is strongly recommended. No official student-insurance figure is published.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Have the eTA approval accessible — airlines check it before boarding and immigration on arrival. Keep the return ticket and accommodation details ready. The eTA is the entry document: without it (and outside the exemption), you don't board.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Student pass: KES 5,000/year — institution-sponsored. Work permits (Class D etc.) are employer-sponsored through the Department of Immigration; no official work-fee schedule was verified for 2026. An eTA never authorises work or study.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own Kenya permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether Kenya restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

eTA route: 1) Apply at the official etakenya.go.ke ≥72 hours before travel. 2) Pay US$30. 3) Receive approval (~72h). 4) Board with the approval. Exempt route (African/Caribbean, excl. Somalia/Libya): travel visa-free (up to 2 months; EAC 6 months) — verify the current scope. Student route: institution-sponsored pass (KES 5,000/year).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Flying in expecting VOA. Abolished — the eTA is mandatory with no airport fallback.
  • Applying for the eTA at the last minute. ~72h processing — airlines check at boarding.
  • Missing the October 2026 exemption. African/Caribbean nationals (excl. Somalia, Libya) no longer need the eTA.
  • Using lookalike eTA sites. Only etakenya.go.ke is official — agencies charge multiples.
  • Working on an eTA. Employer-sponsored permit required.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

etakenya.go.ke is the only official online channel — eTA applications live there. Agency lookalikes charge multiples of the US$30 government fee. There is no consular-visa alternative for short tourist visits; the eTA (or the exemption) is the route.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in Kenya's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the eTA portal for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where Kenya requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Declare currency and restricted goods as required. Keep the eTA approval, return ticket and accommodation details with your passport — the eTA is checked at boarding and on arrival.

Departure and future travel

eTA stays follow the granted admission (typically 90 days) — extensions go through the immigration authorities before expiry. Overstaying draws fines and future-entry consequences.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) etakenya.go.ke — the portal's own fee and requirement pages. 2) Official Kenyan government announcements for the October 2026 exemption scope. Distrust any site still selling 'Kenya visa on arrival' — it's abolished.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per Kenya's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Kenya is an eTA-or-exemption destination: US$30 at etakenya.go.ke, 72h ahead, no VOA fallback — unless you're an African/Caribbean national covered by the October 2026 exemption. Check the exemption scope, apply early, and verify student/work routes with the institution or employer.
