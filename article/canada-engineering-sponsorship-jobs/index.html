Current official context — checked 5 October 2026

IRCC distinguishes employer-specific and open work permits. An employer-specific permit is tied to the named employer/conditions and usually begins with a job offer plus an LMIA or an LMIA-exempt employer process where applicable. An open work permit is available only in specified situations and is not a general alternative for anyone without an employer. IRCC currently lists a CAD 155 work-permit processing fee in the standard fee schedule; open-work-permit applicants can also face the open-work-permit holder fee.

Engineering: where sponsorship is most plausible

For engineering roles in Canada, sponsorship is most plausible where employers have persistent skill needs, the occupation fits an immigration route and the candidate can satisfy professional or technical requirements. Do not infer sponsorship from sector demand alone. Verify the occupation classification, licensing/registration and employer's legal ability to hire from overseas.

Tailor the CV to the country's terminology and make regulated credentials obvious. For healthcare, teaching and some engineering roles, professional registration can be the real bottleneck even when jobs are plentiful. verify it against Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) before committing money or documents.

Separate the job search from immigration eligibility

Search for jobs and immigration eligibility in parallel. A work permit is not the same thing as a permanent-residence job offer; LMIA rules, exemptions and program definitions matter. Create a one-page route summary before applying: route name, sponsor requirement, occupation rule, salary/skill requirement, language rule, professional registration and whether dependants can accompany.

This prevents a common failure: investing months in a job that cannot support the intended immigration route. compare it with the current Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) guidance linked below.

How to verify an employer or sponsor

Use official sponsor/accreditation registers where they exist, the company's own careers site, business registry details and a professional email domain. Compare the legal employer name on the offer with the entity in the sponsor register. A recruitment agency may be legitimate, but it should be clear who the actual employer is. the live Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) reference below should be consulted before action.

Red flags include payment for a job offer, requests to reimburse prohibited sponsor costs, interviews conducted only through anonymous messaging apps, contracts with a different company name, and salary promises that are inconsistent with the written offer. re-check Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) before relying on it.

Build an overseas-ready application

A useful international CV is evidence-led: role, employer, dates, quantified responsibilities, tools/technologies, regulated credentials and language ability. In the cover letter, answer the employer's risk questions directly: Are you eligible for the occupation? Do you need sponsorship? Are your qualifications recognised? When can you relocate? If the employer uses a formal sponsorship programme, say which route you believe applies without pretending to give legal advice. use Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) to confirm the live requirement where it can change.

Offer-stage checks

Before resigning or paying relocation costs, verify salary, hours, probation, location, remote-work expectations, relocation support, visa costs, health insurance and family benefits. Check whether the contract is conditional on immigration approval. the live Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) reference below should be consulted before action.

A genuine offer can still be unsuitable if the salary fails the immigration threshold, the job title does not match the actual duties, or the employer is not eligible to sponsor. compare it with the current Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) guidance linked below.

From job offer to lawful work

The employer and applicant may have separate steps. In some systems the employer first obtains accreditation, sponsorship approval, labour-market approval or a job check; only then can the worker apply. In others, the processes are linked. Use https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/work-canada/permit.html to map the actual sequence.

Do not start work until the status authorises it. Entry as a visitor or student does not automatically permit full-time employment. the live Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) reference below should be consulted before action.
