Schengen Visa Fee 2026: Current Cost, Child Fees, and What It Covers

The Schengen fee is set once, at EU level, for every member state. Here is exactly what you pay in 2026 — and what the money does and does not buy.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20265 min read

Of all the numbers in a Schengen application, the fee is the simplest: it is fixed at EU level, identical in every member state, and paid per application. No consulate discounts it, and no refusal refunds it. This guide states the 2026 fee plainly and explains exactly what it covers, so you can budget the real cost of applying.

Quick Answer

The Schengen visa fee in 2026 is €90 for adults and €45 for children aged 6 to 12. The fee is collected at or around the appointment, charged per application, and not refunded if the visa is refused. Budget one full fee for every application you lodge.

Fee €90 · Children 6–12 €45 · Updated 2026-10-14 · Reading time 5 minutes

The 2026 fee table

The fee structure is short enough to state in full:

  • Adults: €90 per application
  • Children 6–12: €45 per application
  • Children under 6: no fee is charged

Because the fee is set by the EU Visa Code, it does not change between France, Spain, Italy, Germany, or any other member state. A cheaper application in one country and a dearer one in another is not a real price difference — the fee is €90 everywhere.

What the fee actually pays for

The €90 covers processing the application: the consulate's examination of your documents, the biometric capture, the checks run against shared systems such as the Visa Information System, and the decision itself. It does not buy a visa, a decision timeline, or a particular outcome. Paying the fee entitles you to have your case examined — nothing more, and nothing less.

Refused? The fee is gone

This is the point that stings: the fee is not refunded on refusal. The refusal form tells you the grounds and your appeal rights, but the €90 stays with the processing you received. Every fresh application after a refusal costs a fresh €90, which is why the cheapest strategy is to get the first file right — our Schengen refusal guide maps the 12 grounds to their fixes before you pay again. Before reapplying, total your real outlay with our Schengen visa cost calculator.

The full cost is more than €90

The visa fee is only one line in the true budget. A realistic total also includes travel medical insurance meeting the €30,000 minimum, document costs (photos, translations, bank statements), and travel to the appointment city if the consulate is not local. None of these are the visa fee, but all of them are part of what applying costs. Planning the whole figure up front — not discovering it mid-process — is the point of budgeting before you book the Schengen appointment.

When to pay

The fee is collected at or around the appointment, when the file is lodged and biometrics taken. Keep the payment receipt with your documents: if the file is ever questioned, the receipt is your proof the application was properly lodged and paid.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a Schengen visa in 2026?

€90 for adults, €45 for children aged 6–12, per application.

Is the Schengen visa fee refunded if I am refused?

No. The fee pays for processing, which you received; refusals and withdrawals are not refunded.

Does the fee differ between Schengen countries?

No. It is set at EU level and is identical in every member state.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

€90 per adult application, €45 for children 6–12, paid at lodging and gone for good — the only way to spend it once is to file a complete, honest application the first time.
