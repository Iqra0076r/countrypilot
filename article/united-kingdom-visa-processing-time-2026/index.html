UK Visa Processing Time 2026: Route-by-Route Timetables, Priority Options, and Peak Seasons

The UK's 2026 timelines run from minutes (ETA) to months (settlement): 3 weeks for the main routes outside the UK, 8 weeks inside it. Here is the full timetable by visa type — plus the priority upgrades that buy speed.

Quick Answer

For Visitor, Student and Skilled Worker applications made outside the UK, the 2026 service standard is a decision in 3 weeks, measured from the biometrics appointment. Applications made inside the UK run to an 8-week standard. The ETA is a different animal entirely: most decisions arrive in minutes. Paid priority services can cut the 3-week standard to 5 working days (priority) or the next working day (super-priority) on eligible routes.

The UK is unusual in publishing service standards for visa decisions — published targets UKVI aims to meet for each route, rather than the vague 'several weeks' most countries offer. In 2026 the headline standards are a decision in 3 weeks for the main routes applied for outside the UK, and 8 weeks for applications made inside the UK.

A standard is a target, not a guarantee — but it is a target most straightforward applications meet. This guide lays out the 2026 timetable route by route, the priority and super-priority services that shorten it, and the seasonal peaks when even the standards slip. All figures reflect the April 2026 fee round (£127→£135 for a Standard Visitor visa, £16→£20 for the ETA).

The clock starts at the biometrics appointment at the visa application centre — not at the online submission or fee payment. The days (sometimes weeks at busy VACs) between paying online and attending biometrics sit outside the standard entirely, so book the earliest biometrics slot you can.

Priority and super-priority: buying speed

UKVI sells two legitimate upgrades on eligible routes and locations. Priority service targets a decision within 5 working days of biometrics; super-priority targets a decision by the end of the next working day. Both are purchased during the online application, and availability varies by country and route.

The upgrades only accelerate the decision queue — they do not excuse a weak file. If your application needs further checks (unverifiable employment, funds under scrutiny), it drops out of the priority stream and into standard casework, and the fee is not refunded. Priority is worth it when the file is airtight and the travel date is fixed; it is wasted money on a file with known complications.

Peak seasons when standards slip

Three periods strain the system every year: the autumn student intake (July–September, when hundreds of thousands of Student applications land at once), the summer travel season (June–August visitor surge), and the December holiday wave. In these windows, even straightforward files can drift past the 3-week standard.

The practical response is calendar arithmetic: in peak season, add 2–3 weeks of margin beyond the standard, or buy priority if the route offers it. For September-start students, the golden window is applying in June — early enough to beat the rush, late enough that the CAS and 28-day funds evidence are settled.

What slows a UK file down

  • The 28-day funds rule (students). Maintenance funds must be held for 28 consecutive days ending no more than 31 days before application — misjudging this window is the classic Student delay.
  • Verification checks. Employment letters, bank statements or qualifications the caseworker cannot verify go into a manual-check queue.
  • Credibility interviews. Where the caseworker doubts the purpose of travel, an interview is scheduled — adding weeks.
  • Biometrics slot scarcity. At busy VACs the appointment itself can be weeks out; the standard never covers this wait.
  • Previous refusals or overstays. History flags route the file into enhanced scrutiny.

When to apply: the planning formula

Work backwards: take your travel date, subtract the 3-week standard (or 8 weeks in-country), subtract 2–3 more weeks in peak season, and that is your biometrics deadline — then remember the online submission, fee payment and VAC booking all come before it.

For visitors: 6–8 weeks before travel in normal periods, 8–10 weeks in summer. For students: as soon as the CAS arrives and the 28-day funds window closes — June for September starts. Never book non-refundable travel before the decision; the standard is a target, and a delayed file plus fixed flights is the expensive combination.

Checking the live position

GOV.UK publishes the service standards and notes when particular routes are running behind — check the visa decision waiting-time pages the week you apply. After biometrics, the VAC or TLScontact/VFS tracking shows the passport's movement; UKVI does not offer a meaningful case-progression tracker, so silence after submission is normal, not a signal.

Re-check fees before paying: the 8 April 2026 round moved the Visitor fee £127→£135 and the ETA £16→£20, a reminder that last year's numbers expire. Fees and standards change by regulation.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a UK visitor visa take in 2026?

The service standard is 3 weeks from the biometrics appointment for applications outside the UK — a target, not a guarantee. In peak summer season, allow 6–8 weeks from application to passport return.

When does the 3-week clock start?

At the biometrics appointment at the visa application centre, not at online submission or payment. The wait for the appointment itself is outside the standard.

Is UK priority processing worth it?

Priority (5 working days) and super-priority (next working day) are worth it for airtight files with fixed travel dates, on routes and at locations where they are offered. They do not help files that need further checks — those drop back into standard casework.

How fast is the UK ETA?

Most ETA decisions arrive within minutes to a few hours of applying (£20 from April 2026). Apply at least 3 working days before travel as the official guidance advises.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Three weeks from biometrics outside the UK, eight weeks inside, minutes for the ETA — those are the 2026 standards to plan around. Add margin in peak season, buy priority only for clean files, and re-check GOV.UK before paying: the standards are targets, and targets slip.
