How Canada's visa fee list works

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) publishes an official fee list on canada.ca, and those figures are the only ones that matter — third-party quotes, agents' fee tables and old forum posts go stale. This guide was verified against IRCC publications in October 2026. Fees change by regulation (restoration fees were adjusted in December 2025), so re-check the live fee list before paying. The controlling source is IRCC on canada.ca: its fee list, the 'check processing times' tool, and the eTA pages.

The total cost is almost never one number: the application fee and the biometrics fee are charged separately, and most fees are per person unless a family cap applies.

Visitor visa (TRV) fee

A visitor visa (Temporary Resident Visa, TRV) costs CAD $100 per person in 2026, with a family cap of CAD $500 for five or more family members applying together. It is applied for online through an IRCC account and covers nationals of visa-required countries — including nationals of India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh — regardless of whether they arrive by air, land or sea.

The TRV permits stays of up to 6 months per entry (the officer may set a shorter stay). Extend it via a visitor record before expiry — that extension has its own CAD $100 fee. Timelines vary: see our Canada visa processing time 2026 guide for the actual numbers.

eTA fee

The eTA (Electronic Travel Authorization) costs CAD $7, applied for on canada.ca with the passport you will travel on. Most approvals arrive within minutes, though some take days — do not fly until approved, because airlines verify eTA status before boarding.

The eTA is for visa-exempt foreign nationals flying to Canada (including the UK), is linked to the passport (a renewal means a new eTA), and cannot substitute for a visitor visa — TRV holders need no eTA. US citizens are visa-exempt and eTA-exempt entirely. For transit: eTA for visa-exempt travellers flying through, and a free transit visa for visa-required nationals.

Biometrics fee

Biometrics cost CAD $85 per individual, with a family maximum of CAD $170, charged on top of the visa application fee. Most applicants aged 14–79 give biometrics at a Visa Application Centre (VAC) — and VAC appointments add their own lead time in peak season, so book early. See the Canada visa entry requirements guide for the step-by-step workflow, including when to submit the passport (only when IRCC requests it).

Study permit fee

A study permit costs CAD $150 in 2026. Study of more than 6 months needs the permit, plus a separate financial test that is not a fee: proof of funds of CAD $23,448 per year for a single applicant outside Quebec (from 1 September 2026 — up from CAD $22,895), plus first-year tuition and travel. Quebec sets its own figures. Do not confuse the two: the CAD $150 goes to IRCC; the CAD $23,448 is money you hold and demonstrate, not pay — see our Canada proof-of-funds guidance.

Work permit fee

A work permit costs CAD $155 per person in 2026, plus a CAD $100 open-work-permit holder fee where that applies — so an open work permit holder pays CAD $255 in fees. Restoration with a new work permit is CAD $401.25. If you are entering Canada on an employer-specific route, review the Canada work visa guide alongside these figures.

Restoring or extending status

Extend your stay as a visitor: CAD $100 (2026) — apply before your admitted stay expires. Restore visitor status: CAD $246.25 (2026, following the December 2025 adjustments); restoring worker status with a new permit is CAD $401.25.

Family caps and what fees do not cover

IRCC caps two charges for families applying together: the visitor visa fee is capped at CAD $500 for a family of five or more (instead of CAD $100 × 5), and biometrics are capped at CAD $170 per family (instead of CAD $85 per person) — the easiest savings in the fee list, and there is no general family cap on study or work permit fees. Note what the application fee buys: processing of the application only. It does not cover VAC travel, translations, or tuition, and the proof-of-funds amounts for study permits are holdings you demonstrate, not payments to IRCC.

Common fee mistakes to avoid

  • Paying for an eTA on top of a TRV. A visitor visa holder does not need an eTA — one authorization per trip.
  • Forgetting biometrics. The CAD $100 visa fee and the CAD $85 biometrics fee are separate charges; budget both.
  • A family of five paying per person. The CAD $500 fee cap (and CAD $170 biometrics cap) apply — do not overpay.
  • Letting the passport expire mid-process. A visa cannot outlast the passport, and a passport renewal means a new eTA (CAD $7).
  • Using the old study proof-of-funds figure. The requirement rose from CAD $22,895 to CAD $23,448 for applications on or after 1 September 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a Canada visitor visa fee in 2026?

CAD $100 per person, plus CAD $85 biometrics (most applicants aged 14–79). A family of five or more applying together is capped at CAD $500 in application fees and CAD $170 in biometrics.

How much does a family of five pay for Canada visitor visas?

A maximum of CAD $670: CAD $500 in application fees (the family cap) plus CAD $170 in biometrics (the family maximum) — instead of CAD $925 without the caps.

How much is a Canada study permit in 2026?

CAD $150 for the permit itself. Separately, a single applicant outside Quebec must show proof of funds of CAD $23,448 per year (for applications on or after 1 September 2026), plus first-year tuition and travel.

Is there a fee for a Canada transit visa?

No — the transit visa is free for visa-required nationals. Visa-exempt travellers flying through Canada use an eTA instead (CAD $7).

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Budget the full stack, not just the visa fee: CAD $100 for the TRV plus CAD $85 biometrics for a single adult, family caps of CAD $500 + CAD $170 for five or more, and CAD $7 for an eTA if you are visa-exempt. Study and work routes add their own fees and financial tests. Verify every figure on IRCC's live fee list on canada.ca before paying — fees move by regulation.
