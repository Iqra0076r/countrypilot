This guide separates qualification/registration, employer requirements, salary research and the legal work-authorisation process.

Current official context — checked 5 October 2026

The Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) currently requires an offer of full-time work from an accredited employer and, depending on the job, qualification/experience and English requirements. INZ lists a stay of up to five years based on the job, a cost from NZD 1,540, and 80% of recent AEWV applications completed within about 5.5 weeks. The job must offer at least 30 hours of work a week.

Official page: https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/accredited-employer-work-visa/

Separate the job search from immigration eligibility

Search for jobs and immigration eligibility in parallel. For the AEWV, employer accreditation, job-check requirements and the employment agreement are central; workers should verify the employer in INZ systems. Create a one-page route summary before applying: route name, sponsor requirement, occupation rule, salary/skill requirement, language rule, professional registration and whether dependants can accompany.

This prevents a common failure: investing months in a job that cannot support the intended immigration route. verify it against Immigration New Zealand (INZ) before committing money or documents.

How to verify an employer or sponsor

Use official sponsor/accreditation registers where they exist, the company's own careers site, business registry details and a professional email domain. Compare the legal employer name on the offer with the entity in the sponsor register. A recruitment agency may be legitimate, but it should be clear who the actual employer is. compare it with the current Immigration New Zealand (INZ) guidance linked below.

Red flags include payment for a job offer, requests to reimburse prohibited sponsor costs, interviews conducted only through anonymous messaging apps, contracts with a different company name, and salary promises that are inconsistent with the written offer. use Immigration New Zealand (INZ) to confirm the live requirement where it can change.

Build an overseas-ready application

A useful international CV is evidence-led: role, employer, dates, quantified responsibilities, tools/technologies, regulated credentials and language ability. In the cover letter, answer the employer's risk questions directly: Are you eligible for the occupation? Do you need sponsorship? Are your qualifications recognised? When can you relocate? Offer-stage checks

Before resigning or paying relocation costs, verify salary, hours, probation, location, remote-work expectations, relocation support, visa costs, health insurance and family benefits. Check whether the contract is conditional on immigration approval. Confirm the current position using Immigration New Zealand (INZ) in the source list below.

A genuine offer can still be unsuitable if the salary fails the immigration threshold, the job title does not match the actual duties, or the employer is not eligible to sponsor. Confirm the current position using Immigration New Zealand (INZ) in the source list below.

From job offer to lawful work

The employer and applicant may have separate steps. In some systems the employer first obtains accreditation, sponsorship approval, labour-market approval or a job check; only then can the worker apply. In others, the processes are linked. Use https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/accredited-employer-work-visa/ to map the actual sequence.

Do not start work until the status authorises it. Entry as a visitor or student does not automatically permit full-time employment. re-check Immigration New Zealand (INZ) before relying on it.

How to research salary properly

Use several sources: official occupation data where available, major job boards, professional associations and real vacancies in the same city/region. Compare base salary separately from bonus, overtime, housing and pension.

For immigration, use the salary definition in the visa rules rather than a market-salary website. Some routes exclude allowances or use an hourly/annualised calculation.
