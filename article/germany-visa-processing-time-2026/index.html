Germany Visa Processing Time 2026: Schengen Speed vs National Visa Realities

Germany's Schengen visas clear in about 15 working days in 2026 — but national work and student visas take 4 to 12 weeks, with a second authority approving behind the scenes. Here is how both clocks work.

Germany operates two entirely separate visa timelines. Schengen Type C visas for short visits are decided in about 15 working days in 2026. National Type D visas for work and study take 4 to 12 weeks — because the German mission is not the only decider: the local Ausländerbehörde (foreigners' authority) and, for work routes, the Federal Employment Agency must approve behind the scenes.

This guide sets out the 2026 timelines for both tracks, why the national track varies so much by mission, and how to schedule around the hidden approval chain. All figures are October 2026; always confirm on the mission page for your country before planning.

The Schengen track: 15 working days

For tourism, business and family visits up to 90 days, the Schengen Type C visa (€90 adult, €45 ages 6–11, 2026 — set at EU level) is decided typically within 15 working days of the biometrics appointment, stretching to 45 days for complex cases. First-time applicants appear in person for biometrics at the mission or VFS centre; the visa is a sticker in the passport — Germany offers no eVisa.

Applications can be lodged up to 6 months before travel: use the full window in peak season rather than testing the 15-day legal minimum. Track the file through the mission or VFS reference system.

The national track: 4–12 weeks and why

Work visas: with Federal Employment Agency pre-approval — the standard path for the EU Blue Card — the national visa takes about 4 weeks. Without pre-approval, the mission must consult the Agency itself and the timeline stretches to 12 weeks. The lesson: pre-approval is the single biggest lever on the work-visa clock.

Student visas: 4–12 weeks depending on the mission, because the local Ausländerbehörde in the university's city must approve the residence. Big student cities process in volume; smaller missions can be slower. The national Type D fee is €75 (2026, mission fee schedules).

What slows a Germany file down

  • Missing FEA pre-approval (work). The difference between 4 weeks and 12 weeks is usually this single document.
  • Ausländerbehörde backlogs (study). University cities process thousands of student files each intake; late applications queue behind them.
  • Blocked-account timing (students). The blocked account (Sperrkonto) must be funded and confirmed — open it early, not after the admission letter.
  • Mission variation. Fees and processing times vary by mission — 2025-era advice is already stale on several points; always confirm on the mission page for your country.
  • Agent scams. The only legitimate channels are the German mission, VFS Global where designated, and auswaertiges-amt.de. No agent can guarantee approval or sell an earlier appointment.

When to apply: the planning formula

Schengen: lodge 6–8 weeks before travel normally, earlier in summer — the full 6-month window exists for a reason.

Work: start the FEA pre-approval the moment the job offer is signed; with pre-approval in hand, apply 8 weeks before the start date, 16 weeks without it.

Study: apply the week the admission letter and blocked-account confirmation are both in hand — for October intakes, that means June at the latest, because the 4–12 week range is mission-dependent and you cannot know which end you will get.

Tracking and the live picture

Schengen files are tracked through the mission or VFS reference system; national-visa files through the mission that took the application. The mission page for your country is the live source for current processing reality — national timelines are not published as a single interactive tool, so the mission's stated estimate is the number to plan from.

Official fees are paid at the mission or VFS with a receipt — never to a personal account.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a Germany Schengen visa take in 2026?

Typically within 15 working days of the biometrics appointment, up to 45 days for complex cases. Lodge up to 6 months before travel; first-time applicants attend in person for biometrics.

How long does a German student visa take?

4–12 weeks depending on the mission, because the local Ausländerbehörde must approve. Apply as soon as admission and blocked-account confirmation are in hand.

How long does a German work visa take?

About 4 weeks with Federal Employment Agency pre-approval (the Blue Card route), up to 12 weeks without it. Get the pre-approval first — it is the biggest lever on the timeline.

Does Germany have an eVisa?

No. The Schengen application starts with an online or paper form depending on the mission, but first-time applicants appear in person for biometrics and the visa is a sticker.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Fifteen working days for Schengen; 4 weeks to 12 weeks for national work and student visas, with FEA pre-approval and the Ausländerbehörde as the hidden gears. Start the pre-approvals early, use the full 6-month Schengen window, and confirm the mission's current estimate before planning.
