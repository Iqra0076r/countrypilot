If you are planning to work in United Kingdom, the first task is to identify the exact work-authorisation route—not to search for a generic ‘work visa’. Employer sponsorship, open permits, skilled-worker routes and job-search permissions can have completely different rules. This guide explains how to match a job and applicant profile to the legal route, what evidence normally matters, and which official page should control the final decision.

Current official context — checked 6 October 2026

For a standard Skilled Worker case, GOV.UK says the job must be eligible, the employer must be approved by the Home Office, and pay must normally meet the higher of £41,700 a year or the applicable going rate. Different salary rules and transitional provisions can apply, including for some healthcare or education jobs and some existing Skilled Worker/Tier 2 holders. Check the exact occupation code and live rule before relying on a headline threshold.

Official checks: GOV.UK — Skilled Worker job and salary requirements → · Register of licensed sponsors →

Legacy search terms: “UK work permit” and “Tier 2 visa” are still widely used, but the principal sponsored route discussed here is the Skilled Worker visa. Tier 2 (General) was replaced by the Skilled Worker route; older Tier 2 history can still matter for some transitional cases.

Choose the legal work route before the job search

United Kingdom does not have one universal 'work visa'. The relevant route may be Skilled Worker visa. Start by checking whether the occupation, employer and applicant profile fit the route; then target employers and jobs that can actually use it. Employer sponsorship is formal: applicants should verify the sponsor and the exact occupation/salary rules on GOV.UK.

This saves time because many vacancies are perfectly genuine but are not suitable for overseas sponsorship. A candidate should know the occupation classification, qualification requirements, professional registration and any salary or skill threshold before accepting a role.

Employer and job requirements

Employer sponsorship, where required, is a legal role rather than a marketing phrase. Verify the employer in any official sponsor/accreditation register and confirm the job title, duties, hours, location and pay. The written offer should match the facts later entered into the immigration application. If a recruiter asks the worker to reimburse prohibited sponsorship or recruitment costs, buy a fake qualification, or pay for a job offer, stop. Legitimate immigration systems increasingly publish anti-exploitation guidance and sponsor compliance rules.

Qualification, language and registration

A visa may accept a job only if the applicant has the skills the role requires. Regulated occupations—healthcare, teaching, engineering, law and others—can require professional registration in addition to immigration approval. Foreign qualifications may also need formal recognition or an educational assessment. For this United Kingdom Skilled Worker Pathway research path, the practical checkpoint is “Qualification, language and registration”; use UK Visas and Immigration / Home Office to confirm the live requirement where it can change.

Language evidence can come from an approved test, nationality, degree taught in an accepted language or another route-specific exemption. Use only the proof method the official rules recognise.

Application evidence and timing

The strongest work-visa file links the employment evidence to the legal criteria: employer eligibility, contract or offer, occupation code, salary, qualifications, language, identity and any health/character documents. Keep a one-page checklist showing which document proves each criterion. For readers using United Kingdom Skilled Worker Pathway, this is a “Application evidence and timing” issue rather than a stand-alone rule; verify it against UK Visas and Immigration / Home Office before committing money or documents.

Timing matters. Some sponsorship documents expire, some permits must be approved before entry, and some routes allow in-country switching only from specified statuses. Check the rule on the date the application is submitted, not when the job search began.

A practical verification worksheet

Build a one-page eligibility sheet before applying: purpose of stay, route name, sponsor/institution if required, status from which you are applying, eligibility tests, evidence, fees, biometrics/identity step, expected decision point, visa conditions and next-stage options. Then label every item as 'officially confirmed', 'still to verify' or 'not applicable'. This prevents a common failure: collecting generic documents without proving the specific legal requirement. Verify sponsor, role and visa route in that order

The strongest job-search workflow starts with the employer's sponsor status, then checks whether the role and occupation code can fit the route, and only then moves to salary and application evidence. The Home Office register of licensed sponsors is updated frequently and should be checked close to the time of application.

Run an offer audit before paying immigration fees

Confirm the legal employer name, work location, job title, duties, occupation code, salary basis, weekly hours, sponsorship contact and proposed start date. Compare those details across the contract, Certificate of Sponsorship information and employer communications. Resolve inconsistencies before filing.
