How to use this Grenada visa and entry guide

Grenada has no e-Visa and no visa-on-arrival programme for standard travellers — visa-required nationals apply at embassies or consulates, while UK, US, CARICOM and EU nationals are visa-exempt. The 2026 change that affects everyone: the online Embarkation/Disembarkation (ED) Card at edcard.gov.gd became mandatory on 2 March 2026, replacing paper arrival cards.

Fees are steep and old: US$200 single-entry / US$375 multiple-entry (2018 embassy schedule, still the published figure), with work permits running EC$1,000–5,000 by nationality plus a EC$100 application form. This guide was verified against Grenadian embassy and investment-agency documents in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for the duration of your stay with blank pages. Complete the online ED Card at edcard.gov.gd before travel — it has been mandatory since 2 March 2026 and replaces the paper arrival card.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Most Western travellers never need a Grenadian visa — the UK, US, EU and CARICOM are visa-exempt. The consular visa exists for the nationalities outside the exemption list, including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh.

Transit and onward travel

Grenada's small airport means most connections route through larger Caribbean hubs — transit the hub's rules, not Grenada's. For itineraries that do touch Grenada, visa-required nationalities need the consular visa in hand; there is no transit-visa workaround and no visa on arrival to fall back on.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever Grenada's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the Embassy of Grenada / Immigration Division approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Grenada requires no vaccination certificate for ordinary visitors. No health document is checked at Grenadian border control for standard arrivals.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel/health insurance is not a Grenadian entry condition, though it may be requested and is strongly recommended — medical evacuation from the island is expensive. No official student-insurance figure is published.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Complete the online ED Card (edcard.gov.gd) before departure — mandatory since 2 March 2026. Visa-exempt visitors are typically admitted for up to 90 days. Keep the return ticket and accommodation details accessible; immigration may ask for proof of funds at the officer's discretion.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work permits are nationality-tiered and expensive: CARICOM nationals EC$0 (entertainer) / EC$1,000; Commonwealth including US, EU, UK EC$2,500 (entertainer) / EC$3,000; other nationalities EC$2,500 (entertainer) / EC$5,000 — plus a EC$100 application form, permits valid max 1 year renewable, fees paid at the Treasury within 48 hours of approval (Invest Grenada manual). Student routes run under the standard visa schedule — no official student-visa fee or proof-of-funds minimum is published; verify with the nearest embassy. A visitor entry never authorises work.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own Grenada permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether Grenada restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Visa-exempt route: 1) Complete the ED Card online before travel. 2) Travel with return ticket and funds evidence. Consular visa route: 1) Apply at the nearest Grenadian embassy/consulate (US$200 single / US$375 multiple per the published schedule). 2) Allow about 10 business days from receipt. 3) Complete the ED Card. Work route: employer-led permit application; budget EC$1,000–5,000 by nationality tier plus the EC$100 form.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Skipping the ED Card. Mandatory online since 2 March 2026 — the paper card is gone.
  • Expecting visa on arrival. No VOA programme exists; visa-required nationals must apply at missions.
  • Under-budgeting work permits. Non-CARICOM/non-Commonwealth permits reach EC$5,000 — confirm the tier first.
  • Assuming student fees are published. No official student-visa fee or funds minimum exists — ask the embassy.
  • Trusting the 2018 fee schedule blindly. It is the latest published figure, but confirm with the mission before paying.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Grenada's only official online immigration step is the ED Card at edcard.gov.gd (mandatory since March 2026). There is no e-Visa system — visa applications go through embassies and consulates. Any 'Grenada e-Visa' seller is not official.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in Grenada's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the Embassy of Grenada / Immigration Division for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where Grenada requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Standard Caribbean customs allowances apply — declare large cash sums and restricted goods. Keep the ED Card confirmation, return ticket and visa (if any) with your passport.

Departure and future travel

Visitor admissions run up to 90 days; extensions go through the Immigration Division before expiry. Work permits max out at 1 year and are renewable — start renewal before the current permit lapses.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) The Embassy of Grenada's published visa requirements (the 2018 schedule is the latest official fee document). 2) The Invest Grenada Investment Facilitation Manual for work-permit tiers. 3) edcard.gov.gd for the arrival form. Treat the 2018 fees as 'latest published, confirm before paying'.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per Grenada's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Grenada is simple: exempt nationalities just file the ED Card; everyone else applies at a mission (US$200/US$375 on the published schedule). Work permits are pricey and nationality-tiered — budget EC$1,000–5,000. The 2026 must-do is the online ED Card. Verify fees with the mission, since the published schedule dates to 2018.
