Transit and onward travel

When your itinerary routes through Eritrea to a third country, the transit permission — if one is required for your passport — must be secured before the trip. Airlines enforce it at departure; Eritrea airports will not improvise a solution at the counter.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Admission to Eritrea is decided at the port of entry, not when the visa is issued. Pack an entry folder: passport with visa, e-Visa or approval printout, return ticket, hotel booking or host address, and bank statements or equivalent funds proof.

Officers may ask how the trip is funded and when you leave. Short, truthful, consistent answers beat long explanations every time.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

For Eritrea, verify any health-entry requirements on the Eritrean embassy or consulate — no online portal shortly before departure. As a rule of thumb, yellow-fever rules depend on where you have recently been, not just your nationality — carry the certificate if you have transited an endemic country.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Check the Eritrean embassy or consulate — no online portal for any insurance mandate tied to your visa type. Either way, travel with real medical cover: visitors have no access to subsidised care in Eritrea, and one emergency can cost more than a decade of premiums.

If your trip later extends to study, confirm whether the student route has its own insurance requirement — several countries in this group do, and the minimum cover is specified by the authorities, not by you.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

On arrival in Eritrea, your permitted stay is the period stated on your visa or entry stamp. Verify the date on the stamp or system record the same day — errors are far easier to fix at the counter than after you have left the airport.

Do not work on a visitor permission, and do not assume a long-validity visa equals a long stay. Each entry is a fresh admission decision.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Plan the Eritrea trip around the correct category from the start. A visitor permission covers tourism, family visits and (where the rules allow) short business meetings — it does not cover taking a job, enrolling for a degree, or freelancing for local clients.

If the purpose changes, change the permission first. Entering on the wrong status and hoping to fix it later is how people end up refused, removed, or barred.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Children need individual passports and, where applicable, individual visas or e-Visas for Eritrea — there is no family visa. If a child holds two nationalities, use one passport consistently for the whole journey and make sure any required authorisation matches that passport.

Consent letters for minors travelling without both parents: not universally mandatory, frequently asked for. Bring one.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Do it in this order and Eritrea entry admin stays boring (which is the goal): 1) Confirm your nationality's route in the table above. 2) Apply on the official portal (the Eritrean embassy or consulate — no online portal) and pay only there. 3) Save the approval offline as well as on your phone. 4) Verify the approval is linked to the passport you will actually travel on.

Build in buffer: consular queues and e-Visa approvals both slip, especially in peak season.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming visa on arrival means no preparation. For Eritrea, have the fee in the right currency and the documents ready — the counter is not the place to discover a missing printout.
  • Showing up without a visa. Eritrea has no visa on arrival — travellers who bank on sorting it at the airport are denied boarding instead.
  • Trusting a random blog's fee figure. No verified 2026 Eritrea fee schedule was findable — get the number from the Eritrean embassy or consulate — no online portal itself, not a search result.
  • Booking non-refundable travel before the visa is decided. Processing slips everywhere; the approval comes first, the flights second.
  • Underestimating the timeline. Eritrea's consular process can take up to four weeks with an interview — start two months out, not two weeks.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

The Eritrea application is won on specifics: named employer, dated itinerary, booked (refundable) accommodation, and funds that plausibly cover the trip. Generic statements — ‘tourism’, ‘visiting friends’ — unsupported by documents are the classic weak file.

Answer only what is asked, truthfully and briefly. Volunteering complications you were not asked about rarely helps.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Bookmark the Eritrean embassy or consulate — no online portal before you need it. For Eritrea, third-party ‘visa services’ add no authority to your application — at best they retype your answers into the same official form for a fee, at worst they steal your documents.

Pay fees only through the portal's own payment flow, keep every receipt, and never send passport scans over unsecured chat apps to strangers.

Translations, certifications and document format

The Eritrea supporting file has four piles: who you are, why you are going, how you pay for it, and why you will come back. Cover all four with originals plus copies, translated where needed with a statement of accuracy.

Match every name and date across documents exactly — inconsistencies, even innocent ones, invite the scrutiny you are trying to avoid.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

If your Eritrea route includes an interview, treat it as a verification conversation: the officer is checking that the person matches the paperwork. Bring the file, answer directly, and do not volunteer unrelated complications.

Fingerprint and photo capture is normal. At ports of entry the same applies, plus possible device inspection — keep the trip's documents accessible, not buried in luggage.

Families and group travel

For Eritrea, plan one application per person and budget the fees and appointment slots accordingly. Groups should align their interview or submission dates, and everyone should carry their own approval — shared printouts cause confusion at counters.

Where the system allows linked or group submissions, use it; where it does not, do not improvise shortcuts.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Do a customs pre-check for Eritrea before packing: cash-reporting threshold, food/agriculture restrictions, medication rules, and duty-free allowances. These differ by country and change — the ten minutes of checking beats the argument at the red channel.

Original packaging and prescriptions for all medication; nothing in unlabelled containers.

Departure and future travel

Think of each Eritrea trip as building a record: compliant entries and exits strengthen every future application to Eritrea and elsewhere. One overstay can undo years of clean history.

Need more time? File for it properly and early. Letting permission lapse and hoping no one notices is the most expensive mistake on this list.
