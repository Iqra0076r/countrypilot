Schengen Visa Processing Time 2026: How Long It Takes and Why

The 15-day figure everyone quotes is a legal ceiling, not a promise. Here is how Schengen decision times actually work in 2026 — and how to plan around them.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20266 min read

"How long will it take?" is the question every Schengen applicant asks first — and the answer the Visa Code gives is a two-part one: a standard window, and the conditions under which it stretches. This guide explains the 2026 processing timeline as the rules actually define it, what slows files down, and how early to apply.

Quick Answer

A Schengen decision is normally taken within 15 days of the application being lodged. In cases needing further scrutiny — typically consultation with other member states — the window extends to 45 days. You may apply from 6 months before travel and no later than 15 days before, so the honest planning rule is: lodge as early as the window allows.

Decision 15–45 days · Apply from 6 months ahead · Latest 15 days before travel · Updated 2026-10-14 · Reading time 6 minutes

The standard 15-day window

The Visa Code sets the standard decision deadline at 15 calendar days from lodging — lodging being the appointment at which your file, biometrics and the €90 fee are accepted. Most straightforward files — complete documents, clean travel history, verifiable employment — are decided inside this window. But 15 days is a maximum for the standard case, not a guarantee: the clock starts at lodging, not at booking the appointment, and a thin or inconsistent file does not get a fast answer.

When it stretches to 45 days

The window extends to 45 days in individual cases requiring further scrutiny. The most common trigger is consultation: some nationalities and some travel histories require the deciding consulate to consult other member states before issuing. Files with unusual itineraries, first-time applicants with limited travel history, and documents needing verification are the usual candidates. Nothing about your file is "wrong" for landing here — it is a defined, legal extension — but it is why applying late is a gamble.

What actually slows a file down

Three things add days reliably: incomplete files (insurance that misses the €30,000 minimum or the full trip dates, accommodation missing for some nights), documents that need verification (an employer the consulate cannot reach, statements that need confirming), and peak-season volume (spring and summer backlogs at high-demand consulates). Each is avoidable. Work through our visa document checklist before the appointment, keep one consistent story across every document, and lodge early in the season.

How early to apply: the honest math

The lodging window opens 6 months before travel — use it. A file lodged 6 months out has the full 45-day extended window available twice over; a file lodged 20 days out has no margin at all. The consulate need not accept applications lodged later than 15 days before travel, so anything inside that edge is not "cutting it fine" — it is outside the rules. Plan the appointment booking first, then count backwards from your travel date.

After the decision

Refusal is not the end of the timeline: the refusal form states your grounds and appeal rights, and you may reapply at any time — there is no waiting period, though an unchanged reapplication gets the same answer and costs another €90. Our Schengen refusal guide maps each of the 12 grounds to its fix before you spend again.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a Schengen visa take in 2026?

Normally 15 days from lodging, extendable to 45 days for cases needing further scrutiny.

When does the processing clock start?

At lodging — the appointment where your file, biometrics and fee are accepted — not when you book the appointment.

Can I get a faster decision by paying extra?

No. The Visa Code sets the fee at €90 with no priority-processing tier; the only lever you control is lodging early with a complete file.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Fifteen days is the standard ceiling, 45 days the extended one, and the calendar only starts at lodging — so the only real speed strategy is an early appointment with a file that needs no follow-up questions.
