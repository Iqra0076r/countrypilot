How to use this Iraq visa and entry guide

Iraq runs two different immigration systems, and mixing them up is the single biggest planning error. Federal Iraq uses the e-Visa portal evisa.iq (from March 2025): Group A nationalities (including EU countries, the UK, US, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea and the GCC states) get US$160 e-Visas issued in 6–72 hours, valid for 60 days, extendable once for 30 days. The federal visa on arrival was suspended on 1 March 2025 — it is gone, not 'paused pending review'.

Kurdistan (Erbil, Sulaymaniyah) operates a separate e-Visa system — a federal Iraqi visa does not necessarily cover Kurdistan, and vice versa. Group B nationalities (including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh) cannot use the e-Visa and must secure a pre-arranged sponsor visa (~US$1,000, 2–6 weeks) — which itself requires a Kurdistan-entry exemption when transiting Erbil. This guide was verified in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for at least 6 months beyond arrival with blank pages. Federal e-Visa applications are processed in 6–72 hours, but Group B travellers should allow 2–6 weeks for sponsor-visa processing — plan around that, not the Group A timeline.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Nationality decides everything: Group A (EU, UK, US, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea, GCC) uses the fast e-Visa; Group B (including all five South Asian/African nationalities below) needs the slow sponsor route. Then decide whether your itinerary touches Kurdistan.

Transit and onward travel

Iraq's transit logic hinges on the federal/Kurdistan split. Transiting Baghdad on a Group A e-Visa is straightforward; transiting Erbil on a federal sponsor visa requires the Kurdistan entry exemption — this is where Group B travellers get refused. Check the security situation and transit rules with the Iraqi mission before booking multi-segment itineraries.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever Iraq's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the Iraqi e-Visa portal (Ministry of Interior) approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

No vaccination certificate is a verified Iraqi entry requirement for ordinary visitors. Check your government's travel advisory — the security picture matters more than paperwork in Iraq.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance is not a verified Iraqi entry condition, but comprehensive cover including medical evacuation is essential — facilities are severely limited. No official student-insurance figure is published.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Group A e-Visas are valid 60 days, extendable once for 30 days. Have the e-Visa approval, return ticket and accommodation details accessible. Do not fly in expecting visa on arrival — federal VOA has been suspended since 1 March 2025.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work and study visas are employer/sponsor-led with Ministry of Interior approval — not self-service products. No official work or student fee schedule was verified for 2026; the ~US$1,000 Group B figure is the sponsor-visa market rate, not a government tariff. Verify every figure with the Iraqi mission or your sponsor before budgeting. A Group A e-Visa never authorises work.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own Iraq permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether Iraq restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Group A route: 1) Apply at the official evisa.iq. 2) Pay US$160. 3) Receive approval in 6–72 hours. 4) Enter federal Iraq (60 days, extendable once). Group B route: 1) Secure an Iraqi sponsor 2–6 weeks before travel. 2) Budget ~US$1,000. 3) Get the Kurdistan exemption if transiting Erbil. Kurdistan-only route: use the KRG's separate e-Visa system — a federal visa may not cover it.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Flying in expecting VOA. Federal visa on arrival was suspended 1 March 2025.
  • Using a federal visa for Kurdistan. Separate system — check KRG requirements.
  • Group B travellers using Group A timelines. 2–6 weeks for sponsor visas, not 72 hours.
  • Transiting Erbil without the exemption. Federal sponsor visas don't automatically cover Kurdistan.
  • Ignoring the security advisory. Entry rules are secondary to the security reality.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

evisa.iq is the federal Ministry of Interior's official portal — Group A applications live there. Kurdistan runs its own separate e-Visa system; confirm which system covers your itinerary. 'Iraq e-Visa' agencies are not official channels.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in Iraq's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the Iraqi e-Visa portal (Ministry of Interior) for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where Iraq requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Declare currency and restricted goods as required. Keep the e-Visa approval, sponsor documents and return ticket with your passport — the federal/Kurdistan distinction matters at internal checkpoints too.

Departure and future travel

Federal e-Visas extend once (60 + 30 days) — process before expiry. Overstaying carries fines and detention risk. Kurdistan stays follow the KRG's own rules.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) evisa.iq for the federal system. 2) The KRG's portal for Kurdistan. 3) Your government's travel advisory for the security picture. Never plan on the pre-March-2025 VOA — it is suspended, and stale guides still claim it exists.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per Iraq's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Iraq is a two-system destination: evisa.iq (US$160, 6–72h) for Group A, a slow sponsor route (~US$1,000, weeks) for Group B, and a separate Kurdistan system on top. The VOA is gone since March 2025. Check which system covers your itinerary — and your government's advisory — before anything else.
