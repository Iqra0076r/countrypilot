Thailand Visa Fees 2026

The official 2026 Thailand visa fee schedule — every consular fee under the revised structure from 27 April 2026, what is free, and how to pay the government amount only.

Quick Answer

Under Thailand's revised consular fee structure from 27 April 2026: single-entry tourist visa THB 1,000, multiple-entry tourist visa THB 5,000, Non-ED (student) / Non-B (work) single-entry THB 2,000 (multiple-entry THB 5,000), visa on arrival THB 2,000, and the Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) THB 10,000 base government fee. The 60-day visa exemption and the TDAC arrival card are free.

The 2026 fee schedule in one paragraph

Thailand revised its consular fee structure on 27 April 2026, and these are the numbers that now apply. A single-entry tourist visa (60-day stay) costs THB 1,000; the multiple-entry tourist visa costs THB 5,000. Non-ED (student) and Non-B (work) visas cost THB 2,000 for a single entry (THB 5,000 multiple-entry). Visa on arrival at designated checkpoints is THB 2,000 for a 15-day stay. The Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) carries a THB 10,000 base government fee. This page was verified against official Thai sources in October 2026 — but consular fees can change, so always confirm the live figure on the Thai e-Visa portal before paying.

Visa exemption and visa on arrival costs

The 60-day visa exemption is free — it covers most Western nationalities plus India and China, and no application is involved. The catch is the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC), mandatory online within 72 hours before travel for everyone including the visa-exempt, and it is also free. Any site charging you a "TDAC fee" is not the official channel.

The visa on arrival costs THB 2,000, paid at designated checkpoints, for a 15-day stay for eligible nationalities. If your nationality is on the exemption list, the VoA is the wrong (and more expensive) route — check the exemption list first. For the full entry framework behind these routes, see our Thailand Visa & Entry Requirements Guide.

Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) fee

The DTV's base government fee is THB 10,000 — substantially more than the tourist visa, reflecting the 5-year multiple-entry validity and 180 days permitted per entry. On top of the fee, applicants must prove roughly THB 500,000 in funds — a financial threshold, not a payment to anyone. The final amount paid depends on the embassy processing the application, which sets its own local-currency equivalent.

Read our full Thailand DTV Visa 2026 breakdown for eligibility, who qualifies, and how the funds evidence is assessed.

Where and how to pay

Pay only through the official channel: the Thai e-Visa system at thaievisa.go.th, run by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, or directly at the checkpoint for visa on arrival. Visa on arrival fees are paid at the counter — carry the exact amount in Thai baht.

Legitimate portals never ask for payment via crypto, gift cards, or personal bank transfers. If a website presents itself as the "official" e-Visa portal but the fee is a multiple of the numbers on this page, you are looking at a copycat site selling a service bundle as if it were the government fee.

Local-currency equivalents at embassies

The figures above are denominated in Thai baht, but Thai embassies and consulates abroad charge the local-currency equivalent of the standard schedule. The equivalent is set by the mission — so the same visa can cost a slightly different local amount in London, Islamabad, or Delhi. The THB figure is the anchor; if a mission's published amount is wildly out of line, verify it against the portal before paying.

This matters most for the DTV: because it is a newer visa category, its local-currency equivalent is embassy-dependent, and missions may bundle additional service charges. Confirm the all-in amount with the specific mission handling your application.

Who pays nothing

Two parts of the Thailand entry system are free: the 60-day visa exemption (no application, no fee for eligible nationalities) and the TDAC (mandatory but free, completed within 72 hours before travel). Everything else on the fee schedule is paid before travel through the e-Visa portal, or at the border for visa on arrival. Nobody on a visa-exempt entry should be paying anyone for a "visa fee" — that is either a scam or a wrong-route mistake.

Fee scams to avoid

  • Copycat "official" portals — always verify the domain is thaievisa.go.th before entering card details.
  • Agent markups presented as government fees — the government fee is published on the portal; everything above it is someone's margin. If you must use help, verify the agency against the embassy's listed partners.
  • Paid TDAC services — the arrival card is free and separate from any visa; charging for it is a markup you can skip.
  • Phishing payment requests — crypto, gift cards, or personal transfers are never how Thailand collects visa fees.

Extensions and the overstay record

The fee schedule on this page covers the application — not the consequences of overstaying. Treat the admitted-until date granted at the border as a hard deadline: an overstay record can void a multi-entry visa, trigger entry bans, and must be declared on future applications to most countries. No fee paid retroactively fixes an overstay. Plan departure inside the permitted stay, with buffer days.

Admission to Thailand is granted by the officer at the port of entry, whatever your visa says — carry printed onward travel, your first accommodation address, and proof you can support yourself for the stated duration.

How to verify a fee before you pay

The method that works: start at the Thai e-Visa system (thaievisa.go.th, Ministry of Foreign Affairs), confirm with the embassy handling your application, and sanity-check against a current official advisory. Three agreeing official sources beat thirty forum posts.

Two honest gaps worth knowing: no fixed nationwide Non-ED proof-of-funds figure is published (requirements vary by embassy — do not trust a single number), and work permits are issued separately by the Department of Employment after entry on a Non-B visa, outside this fee schedule. Keep a complete duplicate of your submitted file — if the mission queries anything, you answer from the same papers they hold. Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a Thailand tourist visa in 2026?

THB 1,000 for a single-entry tourist visa (60-day stay) under the revised consular fee structure effective 27 April 2026; the multiple-entry tourist visa is THB 5,000. Embassies abroad charge the local-currency equivalent.

What is the Thailand visa on arrival fee?

THB 2,000 at designated checkpoints for a 15-day stay for eligible nationalities. If your nationality is on the 60-day visa-exemption list (most Western nationalities plus India and China), exemption — not VoA — is the right free route.

How much does the Thailand DTV cost?

The Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) carries a THB 10,000 base government fee, with the final local-currency amount set by the processing embassy. Applicants must also prove roughly THB 500,000 in funds — a threshold, not a payment.

When did Thailand's visa fees change?

A revised consular fee structure took effect 27 April 2026. The fees on this page reflect that schedule as verified in October 2026 — confirm the live figures on thaievisa.go.th before paying, since fees can change.

Is the Thailand Digital Arrival Card free?

Yes. The TDAC is mandatory online within 72 hours before travel for everyone — including visa-exempt travellers — but it carries no fee. Sites charging for it are selling a service you can do yourself.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Thailand's 2026 fee schedule (from 27 April 2026): tourist single-entry THB 1,000, multiple-entry THB 5,000, Non-ED/Non-B single-entry THB 2,000, visa on arrival THB 2,000, DTV THB 10,000 — exemption and TDAC free. Pay only on thaievisa.go.th or at the checkpoint, confirm local-currency equivalents with your mission, and ignore anything demanding crypto, gift cards, or a TDAC fee.
