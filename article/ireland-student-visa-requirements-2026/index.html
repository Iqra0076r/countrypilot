Ireland Student Visa Requirements 2026

Ireland student visa requirements 2026: €10,000 funds proof, €6,000 tuition deposit, mandatory insurance, VFS biometrics and the AVATS application steps.

Quick Answer

To get an Irish student visa in 2026 you need: an admission offer from an eligible institution, €10,000 per academic year in living-costs evidence plus tuition, a minimum €6,000 tuition deposit already paid, mandatory private health insurance, and mandatory biometrics at a VFS centre. The visa fee is €60/€100. Applications run through AVATS and take 4–8 weeks (8–12 in peak season).

How to use this student visa guide

Ireland issues student visas under its own system through Immigration Service Delivery (ISD) at irishimmigration.ie — Ireland is not in Schengen, so Irish rules apply on their own.

Applications are lodged online via AVATS, but the passport must still be submitted in person or at a visa centre, and the visa is issued as a sticker — there is no Irish eVisa.

This guide was verified against ISD guidance in October 2026; rules can change, so confirm the live pages before applying.

The student framework in force since June 2025

Students operate under a revised framework in effect since 30 June 2025 (still current in 2026) with four mandatory pillars: biometrics at VFS centres for all applicants, a standardized €10,000 per academic year proof of funds (plus tuition fees), mandatory upfront tuition payment (minimum €6,000 deposit), and mandatory private health insurance.

Pre-June-2025 guides quoting looser funds rules are out of date — ignore them.

Admission and course eligibility

You need an admission offer from an eligible Irish institution before touching the visa application.

Start with recognition and programme fit — degree length, academic calendar, and whether the programme leads to professional registration — then shortlist realistically on admission requirements.

As our Ireland International Student Checklist notes, study permission, immigration registration and any graduate or post-study permission are distinct steps — check the institution's current status rather than relying only on its admissions material.

Proof of funds: the €10,000 rule

The funds requirement is formulaic: €10,000 per academic year in living-costs evidence plus tuition fees.

Courses of 8 months or less use €833/month (maximum €6,665) instead.

The money must be genuinely available and traceable — decision-makers look at ownership, holding period, and source, and large unexplained deposits should be documented rather than left for the officer to interpret.

Build the money trail properly; our Ireland Proof of Funds Explained guide shows what officers check and what flags verification problems.

The €6,000 minimum tuition deposit

Before applying, you must have paid a minimum €6,000 upfront tuition deposit — the deposit receipt is a core visa document. This is separate from and additional to the €10,000 living-costs evidence. Keep the receipt with the application file: it proves both the financial commitment and that the admission offer is real.

Mandatory private health insurance

Non-EU students must hold private health insurance as a condition of both the study visa and subsequent immigration registration.

Specialist student plans cost about €150–200 per year, university group schemes €150–300, and comprehensive private cover (Vhi, Laya, Irish Life) €500–1,200 (2026).

Buy the policy before the visa application — the consulate/visa office checks it as part of the file, and arriving without compliant cover stalls registration.

More detail in Ireland Mandatory Health Cover Explained.

Mandatory biometrics at VFS centres

Under the framework in effect since 30 June 2025, biometrics at VFS centres are mandatory for all student applicants. Irish student applications are otherwise document-driven rather than interview-driven — the decision rests substantially on the quality of the submitted file, so every document should be complete and consistent.

The AVATS application workflow

Study: 1) Secure admission and pay the minimum €6,000 tuition deposit. 2) Arrange the €10,000 funds evidence plus private insurance. 3) Complete the AVATS online application, submit the passport in person or at a visa centre, and complete biometrics at VFS. 4) Pay the visa fee — €60/€100 (2026). 5) Allow processing time before booking non-refundable travel. Passport validity must cover the entire stay, and documents not in English need certified English translations. The route overview sits in our Ireland Visa & Entry Requirements Guide.

After arrival: immigration registration

The entry visa is not the end of the process — after arrival, non-EEA nationals must register with immigration (GNIB/IRP) and obtain the IRP (Irish Residence Permit) card, paying the €300 first-issue residence stamp fee (2026).

At registration, fingerprints and a photograph are captured and the compliant insurance policy is checked again.

Overstaying the permitted stay jeopardizes every future Irish application.

If plans later change, see the Ireland Visa Extension Guide.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using pre-June-2025 funds guidance. The €10,000/year + €6,000 deposit + biometrics framework is mandatory now.
  • Budgeting only the €10,000. You need the funds evidence plus tuition plus the paid €6,000 deposit — all three.
  • Arriving without compliant private insurance. It is a visa and registration condition, not an optional extra.
  • Skipping the biometrics step. Mandatory at VFS for every applicant under the current framework.
  • Treating admission as immigration permission. The college offer, the visa, registration and any post-study permission are four separate steps.
  • Working beyond the conditions of student permission — work and study are separate questions under Irish rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much money do I need for an Ireland student visa?

€10,000 per academic year in living-costs evidence plus tuition fees, along with a minimum €6,000 upfront tuition deposit already paid and mandatory private health insurance (€150–1,200/year). Courses of 8 months or less use €833/month (max €6,665).

Is biometrics mandatory for the Ireland student visa?

Yes. Under the framework in effect since 30 June 2025, biometrics at VFS centres are mandatory for all student applicants.

How long does the Ireland student visa take in 2026?

Standard processing is 4–8 weeks, stretching to 8–12 weeks in the April–August peak. Apply as early as your admission timeline allows.

Is health insurance mandatory for international students in Ireland?

Yes. Non-EU students must hold private health insurance as a condition of the study visa and of immigration registration — student plans run about €150–200/year, university group schemes €150–300, and comprehensive private cover €500–1,200 (2026).

Is Ireland part of Schengen for student travel?

No — Ireland is not in Schengen. An Irish student visa does not admit you to the Schengen area, and a Schengen visa does not admit you to Ireland. They are entirely separate systems.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

The 2026 Ireland student visa is a formula: admission, €10,000/year funds evidence plus tuition, €6,000 minimum tuition deposit paid, mandatory private insurance, biometrics at VFS, and the €60/€100 fee through AVATS.

Files that hit all six move in 4–8 weeks; files missing one stall.

After arrival, register for the IRP (€300 stamp) — the visa alone does not complete residence.

Confirm everything on irishimmigration.ie.
