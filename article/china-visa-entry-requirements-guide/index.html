How to use this China visa and entry guide

China controls visitor entry through the Chinese consulate or visa application service centre. China requires most visitors to hold a visa obtained before travel; applications go through Chinese consulates and the official visa application service centres, with an L tourist visa the standard route for sightseeing.

Everything below was checked against current sources in October 2026. Treat any figure without an as-of date as suspect, and always confirm on the Chinese consulate or visa application service centre itself before you pay or travel.

Passport validity and document condition

Most countries — China included — expect your passport to be valid well beyond your trip; six months beyond your planned stay is the common airline-enforced standard. Because the exact rule for China was not verifiable from an official source in our October 2026 check, confirm the requirement on the Chinese consulate or visa application service centre or via the IATA Timatic database your airline uses before you book.

Keep at least one blank page for stamps and make sure the document is undamaged — a frayed passport can be refused at the counter regardless of its expiry date.

Transit and onward travel

Transit is its own immigration question in China, separate from entry. If you change planes on China soil, check whether the regime above requires a transit permission even without leaving the airport — being denied boarding at origin is the common failure mode, not refusal at the China airport.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

A visa or visa-free entitlement gets you to the China border; the officer there decides admission. Carry the passport (with the visa or e-Visa approval), a return or onward ticket, the address where you will stay, and evidence you can fund the trip.

Expect questions on purpose, length of stay and funding. Answer directly and consistently with whatever you declared in the application — contradictions between the form and the interview are the fastest route to secondary inspection.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Our October 2026 check found no China-specific vaccination certificate required for ordinary visitors beyond the standard international rules. That can change with outbreaks — check the current health-entry page on the Chinese consulate or visa application service centre and the WHO destination guidance within two weeks of departure.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Our October 2026 research could not verify whether China mandates travel health insurance for visitors. Buy it anyway: a policy with solid medical limits and emergency evacuation costs little against the price of an uninsured hospital stay abroad.

If your trip later extends to study, confirm whether the student route has its own insurance requirement — several countries in this group do, and the minimum cover is specified by the authorities, not by you.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

The permission you receive on arrival to China — the period stated on your visa or entry stamp — is what counts, not the visa's validity window. Check the stamp or electronic record before leaving the counter; the date there is your legal limit.

Overstaying, even briefly, can void the visa and trigger re-entry bans. If you need longer, apply for an extension through the proper channel before the permission expires — do not simply remain.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A visitor entry to China never authorizes employment — not remote work for a foreign employer while physically in China, not unpaid help at a family business. Work and study each need their own permission.

Do not enter as a visitor intending to ‘sort out’ work or study later; misrepresentation at entry is a separate ground for refusal or removal.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every traveller to China needs their own travel document — children included, with no sharing of passports. Where a visa or e-Visa is required, each child needs their own application.

Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s) with contact details, plus the birth certificate when surnames differ. It is not always a legal requirement, but officers may ask and having it avoids delays.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

A reliable sequence for China: 1) Confirm your nationality's route in the table above. 2) Book the consulate appointment early — slots are the usual bottleneck. 3) Assemble the file: passport, photos to specification, funds evidence, itinerary, and purpose documents. 4) Attend, then track the case; do not book non-refundable travel until the visa is in hand.

Build in buffer: consular queues and e-Visa approvals both slip, especially in peak season.

  • Booking non-refundable travel before the visa is decided. Processing slips everywhere; the approval comes first, the flights second.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Most visitor-visa refusals worldwide come down to one doubt: will this person leave? A strong China file answers it directly — stable employment with a leave letter, property or lease documents, family ties, prior compliant travel, and bank statements showing you can fund the trip.

Keep the story identical across the form, the interview and the documents. Vague itineraries read as risk; dated plans with bookings and a return ticket read as a genuine visit.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

For China, start every application at the Chinese consulate or visa application service centre — the only channel whose outcome counts. Copycat sites buy ads on the portal's own name and charge large markups for submitting the same form, or harvest passport scans.

Red flags anywhere: ‘guaranteed approval’, fees far above the official schedule, payment to personal accounts or crypto wallets, and pressure to decide today. Real immigration fees go through official channels only.

Translations, certifications and document format

For a China application, bring originals plus one copy of everything: bank statements (3–6 months), an employment letter with approved leave dates, property or lease documents, prior visas and entry stamps, and the trip itinerary with bookings.

Documents in another language need an English (or locally accepted language) translation with a translator's statement of accuracy. Photographs must meet the specification published for China — wrong-size photos are a needless reason for delay.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where China requires an in-person step, expect identity checks — fingerprints and a digital photograph are standard at interviews and ports of entry. Answer questions on purpose, employment, family, prior travel and funding briefly and truthfully, matching the application exactly.

At the border, devices can be inspected under border-search authority in many countries — travel with a device you are comfortable having examined, and remain calm and direct if sent to secondary inspection.

Families and group travel

Every traveller to China needs their own authorisation — each family member, including infants, needs a separate application or e-Visa where one is required. There is no family visa.

Book appointments as close together as the system allows, and if one member is refused, address the refusal reason before anyone re-applies — do not try to ‘cover’ it with reworded facts.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Customs in China follows the usual pattern: declare cash above the reporting threshold, declare food and agricultural products, and keep medications in original packaging with prescriptions for anything controlled.

Check the current allowances for alcohol and tobacco per adult traveller before you pack — and never carry anything across a border for someone else, no matter how innocent it looks.

Departure and future travel

A clean China travel history — entries and timely departures — is an asset that compounds: future applications get easier, not harder. An overstay does the opposite, and can void the visa plus trigger multi-year bars.

If plans change mid-trip, use the proper extension or status-change channel before the permission expires. And keep copies of every approval after the trip; they are evidence for the next application.

How to research a route without getting misled

Use this hierarchy for China, in order — each level overrides the ones below it:

  1. The official portal and ministry pages for categories, fees and forms.
  2. The specific embassy or consulate handling your nationality, for local procedure.
  3. Timatic / IATA for what the airline will enforce at boarding.

Treat forums, agents' blogs and social-media anecdotes as leads, not answers — and any site charging far above the official fees is monetising your anxiety, not your application.
