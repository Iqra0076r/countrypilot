Japan Visa Fee 2026

How Japan's visa fee works in 2026

Japan's visa issuance fees quintupled on 1 July 2026 — the first revision since 1978. A single-entry visa costs JPY 15,000 and a multiple-entry visa JPY 30,000, and the fee is charged only when the visa is issued. The Consulate-General of Japan in San Francisco officially states the equivalents as USD 101 / USD 201; ignore any other dollar conversions.

The controlling sources are MOFA's visa pages at mofa.go.jp/j_info/visit/visa and the consulate fee notices; this page was verified against them in October 2026.

Paid on issuance, not at application

Japan's model is unusual: you pay only when the visa is granted. There is no application fee to lose on a refusal — the cost is a cost of success, not of trying. Budget JPY 15,000 (or JPY 30,000) as a line item collected at issuance.

What the fees used to be

Before 1 July 2026, issuance cost ¥3,000 single-entry / ¥6,000 multiple-entry — unchanged since 1978. The fivefold jump is the largest revision in the system's history, and pre-July guides are dangerously stale: if a guide still cites ¥3,000, treat everything else it says about Japan visas as suspect too.

Who pays no visa fee at all

Many travellers pay nothing: nationals of the US, UK, EU states and Singapore enter visa-free for short stays (typically 90 days) — no application, no issuance fee. See who is exempt and who needs a consular visa in our Japan Visa Requirements 2026 guide.

eVISA and accredited-agency fees

The JAPAN eVISA (mofa.go.jp/j_info/visit/visa/visaonline.html) has accepted direct online applications since 15 May 2026 for nationals/residents of Australia, Brazil, Cambodia, Canada, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, Taiwan, the UK and the USA, with an accredited-agency route for China, the Philippines, Vietnam (packaged tours), Hong Kong, India, Indonesia, Macau, Mongolia, South Korea, Singapore and the UAE — tourism, single-entry, air/sea entry only. The official issuance fee stays JPY 15,000; 'Japan eVisa' sites charging large service markups are selling convenience, not the visa.

The Certificate of Eligibility is free

The Certificate of Eligibility — the document that unlocks Japanese work and student visas, applied for inside Japan by the employer or school with the Immigration Services Agency over 1–3 months — costs nothing. Budget JPY 15,000 for the visa issuance that follows at the consulate, but never pay anyone for the CoE itself; it is a government filing, not a paid service. See our Japan Work Visa Guide for the full sequence.

Budgeting beyond the visa fee

The issuance fee is the smallest official cost for longer stays. Students should show around JPY 2,000,000 per year (about JPY 2.5 million for Tokyo) on 3–6 months of bank statements — a practical benchmark, not a published threshold. Residents staying 3+ months must enroll in National Health Insurance at about JPY 18,000–30,000 per year (2026), covering 70% of medical costs. Travellers carrying over JPY 1,000,000 must declare it.

Avoiding stale fee quotes

Two filters catch almost every bad fee figure. First: anything quoting ¥3,000/¥6,000 predates the 1 July 2026 revision. Second: any USD conversion other than the consulate's official USD 101/201 is a media estimate. Cross-check the Japan Visa & Entry Requirements Guide and re-confirm on mofa.go.jp before paying.

  • Official USD equivalents: USD 101 / USD 201 — ignore other conversions
  • Visa-exempt nationalities (US, UK, EU, Singapore) pay nothing

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Japan visa cost in 2026?

From 1 July 2026: JPY 15,000 single-entry and JPY 30,000 multiple-entry — the first fee revision since 1978. The San Francisco consulate officially states USD 101 / USD 201.

Do I pay the Japan visa fee before my application is decided?

No. Japan's visa issuance fee is charged only when the visa is issued, so there is nothing to lose on a refusal. Plan the fee as a cost of success, not of trying.

Is the Certificate of Eligibility free?

Yes. The CoE — applied for inside Japan by your employer or school with the Immigration Services Agency, taking 1–3 months — costs nothing. You still pay the JPY 15,000 visa issuance fee at the consulate once the CoE is received.

Do visa-exempt travelers pay any Japan visa fee?

No. Nationals of the US, UK, EU states and Singapore enter visa-free for short stays (typically 90 days), so no application and no issuance fee arise.

Why do I see different Japan visa fees online?

Two sources of bad figures: pre-1 July 2026 guides (still quoting ¥3,000/¥6,000), and unofficial USD conversions. Only the consulate's USD 101/201 are official — verify on mofa.go.jp.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Japan's 2026 fee structure: JPY 15,000 single / JPY 30,000 multiple, charged only on issuance, with a free Certificate of Eligibility for work and study. Check exemption status first, distrust any guide quoting ¥3,000, and confirm the live fee on mofa.go.jp.
