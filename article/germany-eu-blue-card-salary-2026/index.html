Germany EU Blue Card Salary 2026

How to use this Blue Card salary guide

The salary threshold is the headline number of the EU Blue Card: your job contract must meet it for the route to work. Use this guide to check which of the two 2026 thresholds applies to you, what the route costs on top of the salary, and how long the process takes — then verify the live figures on the official portal before signing anything.

The thresholds were raised on 1 January 2026, so 2025 advice is already stale on several points. Treat the figures below as the 2026 reference and the official sources as the final word.

The 2026 salary thresholds

From 1 January 2026, the EU Blue Card salary thresholds for Germany are €50,700 gross per year (standard) or €45,934.20 for shortage occupations and recent graduates. The reduced threshold is the one to check first if you graduated recently or work in an occupation Germany lists as in shortage.

Which list counts as a shortage occupation changes, so check the current shortage-occupation list on the official federal portal, make-it-in-germany.com, rather than relying on third-party lists from previous years.

What the salary figure actually covers

The threshold is gross salary per year, before tax — the figure on the job contract, not take-home pay. For national visas, the file lives or dies on the underlying basis: the job contract with the correct salary threshold. A contract even slightly below the applicable threshold does not qualify, so confirm the gross annual figure in writing before the visa appointment.

Before the appointment, confirm recognition and documentation requirements for your occupation on the official portal — the paperwork behind the salary figure is what the mission checks.

What the Blue Card route costs

The salary is the qualifying condition; the fees are separate. The national work visa fee is €75 (2026), paid at the embassy or consulate, and the Blue Card residence permit itself costs about €100 after arrival in Germany.

Long stays — work, the EU Blue Card, the Chancenkarte — need a national Type D visa issued by the embassy: a different form, a different fee and a different timeline from the Schengen route. See the full Germany visa and entry requirements guide and our Germany visa fee 2026 breakdown for the complete fee picture.

Processing time: about four weeks with pre-approval

National work visas take about 4 weeks for Blue Card and pre-approved employment, and up to 12 weeks otherwise (2026) — the local Ausländerbehörde in Germany is usually involved, which drives the timeline. Federal Employment Agency pre-approval is what keeps the Blue Card at the faster end of that range, so confirm with the employer whether pre-approval is arranged.

Apply at the German embassy or consulate with the job contract showing the qualifying salary. Your passport must have been issued within the last 10 years and be valid at least 3 months beyond your planned stay; after arrival, register your address (Anmeldung) within 14 days.

Where the Blue Card fits in Germany's work routes

The EU Blue Card is the headline work route in Germany's employment framework, which also includes skilled-worker residence permits and the Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) for job search — see the Germany work permit guide, the skilled worker jobs guide and visa sponsorship jobs guide for how the routes compare.

Longer-term, EU Blue Card holders and other skilled workers can have different qualifying rules for the settlement permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis) — the current residence title and employment history matter before calculating a settlement timeline (see the Germany permanent residence overview). Keep every contract and payslip: the salary history that qualified you for the Blue Card is the same paper trail settlement decisions look at.

The controlling sources are the official federal portal make-it-in-germany.com for skilled-worker and Blue Card routes, and the German Federal Foreign Office visa service for the national visa application itself.

Before signing a contract near the threshold, confirm three things on the live sources: the current threshold figure, whether your occupation is on the current shortage list, and the documentation the mission requires. Common mistakes to avoid

  • A contract below the threshold. Gross annual salary must meet €50,700 — or €45,934.20 for shortage occupations and recent graduates — in writing.
  • Using 2025 figures. The thresholds were raised on 1 January 2026; last year's advice is stale.
  • Trying the Schengen route for work. A visitor visa is not a substitute for the EU Blue Card or skilled-worker residence permits — work needs the national Type D visa.
  • Skipping recognition checks. Confirm recognition and documentation requirements for your occupation on the official portal before applying.
  • Treating forums as answers. Thresholds and lists change; verify on make-it-in-germany.com and the mission's pages.
  • Forgetting the permit fee. Budget the ~€100 Blue Card residence permit after arrival, on top of the €75 national visa fee.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the EU Blue Card salary threshold for Germany in 2026?

From 1 January 2026: €50,700 gross per year standard, or €45,934.20 for shortage occupations and recent graduates. The threshold is gross annual salary before tax, as stated on the job contract.

Who qualifies for the reduced €45,934.20 threshold?

The reduced threshold applies to shortage occupations and recent graduates. Check the current shortage-occupation list on the official federal portal make-it-in-germany.com, since the list changes.

How much does the EU Blue Card cost in Germany?

The national work visa fee is €75 (2026) at the embassy, and the Blue Card residence permit costs about €100 after arrival in Germany.

How long does the Blue Card process take?

About 4 weeks for Blue Card and pre-approved employment, and up to 12 weeks otherwise (2026), because the local Ausländerbehörde is usually involved. Federal Employment Agency pre-approval keeps it at the faster end.

Can I apply for a Blue Card while on a Schengen visitor visa?

No. Long stays including the EU Blue Card need a national Type D visa issued by the embassy — a different form, fee and timeline. A Schengen visa does not convert into a work permit.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Germany's EU Blue Card in 2026, the numbers that matter are €50,700 gross per year standard or €45,934.20 for shortage occupations and recent graduates — in force from 1 January 2026. Get the qualifying gross figure in writing on the contract, budget €75 for the national visa plus ~€100 for the permit, expect about four weeks with pre-approval, and verify the live threshold and shortage list on make-it-in-germany.com before you sign.
