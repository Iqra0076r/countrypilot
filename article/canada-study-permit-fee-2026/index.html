Canada Study Permit Fee 2026

Canada's study permit costs CAD $150 in 2026, plus CAD $85 biometrics and proof of funds of CAD $23,448 a year. Full IRCC fee breakdown, payment rules and processing times.

Quick Answer

The Canada study permit application fee is CAD $150 per person in 2026. Add CAD $85 biometrics (CAD $170 family maximum) and — the figure that actually decides budgets — proof of funds of CAD $23,448 per year for a single applicant outside Quebec (applications from 1 September 2026), plus first-year tuition and travel. Fees are paid to IRCC during the online application; verify every figure on the IRCC fee list before paying.

The fee at a glance

IRCC lists the study-permit application fee at CAD $150 per person in 2026. That figure covers the application itself only — it does not include biometrics, travel documents, medical exams or anything else in the file. A single applicant applying from outside Canada therefore pays at minimum CAD $235: CAD $150 for the permit plus CAD $85 for biometrics at a Visa Application Centre.

Keep the fee in perspective. The application fee is the cheapest line in the whole study-permit budget. Proof of funds for a single applicant outside Quebec stands at CAD $23,448 per year for applications on or after 1 September 2026 (up from CAD $22,895), plus first-year tuition and travel — and Quebec sets its own figure of CAD $24,617 (from 1 January 2026). IRCC can change fees by regulation, so confirm the current fee list on canada.ca before submitting.

Note the contrast with work routes: a work permit costs CAD $155 (plus a CAD $100 open-work-permit holder fee where applicable), and restoring visitor status runs CAD $246.25. The study permit at CAD $150 is one of the cheaper IRCC application fees — the financial bar sits in the proof-of-funds requirement, not the fee.

Biometrics: the second mandatory charge

Biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph at a Visa Application Centre (VAC) — cost CAD $85 for an individual and CAD $170 as a family maximum in 2026. Most applicants aged 14–79 give biometrics. If you apply with a spouse and children, the family cap limits the total biometrics bill to CAD $170 regardless of how many family members apply together.

The study-permit fee itself has no family cap: each applicant pays the CAD $150. Budget the permit fee per person and biometrics once up to the family maximum when planning a family application.

Proof of funds: the real cost barrier

IRCC raised the financial requirement for applications on or after 1 September 2026: a single applicant outside Quebec must now show CAD $23,448 per year, plus first-year tuition and travel — up from the previous CAD $22,895. This figure invalidated a wave of older study-permit guides quoting the old number, so ignore anything still citing CAD $22,895.

Quebec sets its own figure: CAD $24,617 per year from 1 January 2026. The requirement is separate from the CAD $150 fee — evidence of sufficient funds is one of the core study-permit eligibility conditions alongside acceptance by a designated learning institution (DLI) and admissibility. How and where to pay

Like other IRCC temporary-resident applications, the study permit is applied for online through an IRCC account, and the fee is paid to IRCC during the application process. Biometrics are paid as part of the application and given afterward at a Visa Application Centre.

Pay only through IRCC's official channels on canada.ca. Never pay a study-permit fee to a personal bank account, a crypto wallet, or an agent who cannot show you the IRCC receipt. The same scam pattern that hits eTA applicants — copycat sites charging $50–80 to 'process' a CAD $7 eTA — exists around study permits, so treat any third party demanding fees outside canada.ca as a red flag. For the full requirements picture, see Canada student visa requirements 2026.

Processing time and when to apply

IRCC publishes service standards for 2026: visitor visa 14 days, study permit 60 days, work permit 60 days — for applications outside Canada, covering 80% of complete applications. Treat these as targets, not guarantees.

The weekly actuals IRCC publishes run longer at busy posts. For the week of 22 September 2026: study permits from India ~5 weeks, Pakistan ~7 weeks, Nigeria ~10 weeks. Actuals move weekly, so check IRCC's check processing times tool before fixing travel plans. IRCC may also request an interview or additional documents (medical exam, police certificate) depending on the file, which adds time. For university timelines alongside the permit, see our Canada university admissions guide.

Scam avoidance around permit fees

The official study-permit pages live on canada.ca only. Sites with 'canada' plus words like 'visa-apply' or 'permit-service' are private businesses that mark up a CAD $150 application — or worse, harvest your documents. No one can guarantee permit approval or sell an earlier processing slot.

The fee is the same CAD $150 regardless of who submits the application. Before paying anything or booking non-refundable travel, verify the current fee on the IRCC fee list and the current processing time on the IRCC tool.

Common fee mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting biometrics. The CAD $150 fee does not include the CAD $85 biometrics charge — the real minimum per applicant is CAD $235.
  • Quoting the old funds figure. Older guides say CAD $22,895; applications from 1 September 2026 need CAD $23,448 (outside Quebec).
  • Assuming the fee covers entry documents. Applicants from visa-required countries (including India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh) also need a TRV (CAD $100 + biometrics); visa-exempt flyers need a CAD $7 eTA.
  • Paying outside canada.ca. Fees paid to agents, personal accounts or crypto wallets are not IRCC fees — they are losses.
  • Applying at the last minute. Service standard is 60 days for study permits outside Canada, and actuals at busy posts run 5–10 weeks — apply as early as your acceptance letter allows.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the Canada study permit fee in 2026?

CAD $150 per person, as listed on the IRCC fee list for 2026. Biometrics cost an extra CAD $85 (CAD $170 family maximum), so a single applicant pays at least CAD $235.

Does the CAD $150 study permit fee include biometrics?

No. Biometrics are a separate charge: CAD $85 for an individual or CAD $170 as a family maximum, given at a Visa Application Centre. The CAD $150 covers the permit application only.

Is there a family discount on the study permit fee?

Not on the permit fee — each applicant pays CAD $150. But biometrics are capped at CAD $170 for a family applying together, whatever the family size.

How much money do I need to show for a Canada study permit?

For applications from 1 September 2026: CAD $23,448 per year for a single applicant outside Quebec, plus first-year tuition and travel. Quebec sets its own figure: CAD $24,617 per year from 1 January 2026. Ignore older guides quoting CAD $22,895.

How long does a Canada study permit take to process?

IRCC's 2026 service standard is 60 days for study permits applied for outside Canada (80% of complete applications). Recent actuals (week of 22 September 2026): India ~5 weeks, Pakistan ~7 weeks, Nigeria ~10 weeks. Check IRCC's live processing-times tool for your country.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

The 2026 Canada study permit fee is CAD $150 per person plus CAD $85 biometrics — CAD $235 minimum for a single applicant. The fee is the easy part: the real bar is proof of funds (CAD $23,448/year outside Quebec, CAD $24,617 in Quebec, from September 2026/January 2026) plus first-year tuition, and the 5–10 week processing window at busy posts. Apply online through IRCC, pay only on canada.ca, and verify fees and times on the live IRCC pages before committing money.
