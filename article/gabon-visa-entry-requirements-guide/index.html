How to use this Gabon visa and entry guide

Gabon's system has one non-negotiable rule: the e-Visa via the DGDI portal (evisa.dgdi.ga) is the route, and it is valid only for arrival through Libreville's Léon Mba International Airport. Do not attempt a visa on arrival without prior e-Visa approval — airlines frequently deny boarding and difficulties are officially recorded.

The 2026 headline: Gabon reciprocally suspended visa issuance for US citizens effective January 2026, answering a US suspension on Gabonese citizens. Fees run €70 for a 1–3 month single entry (plus a €15 file-processing fee), with the e-Visa approval converted to a physical sticker visa on arrival for a separate cash fee. This guide was verified against DGDI guidance and European MFA advisories in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Your passport should be valid for at least 6 months beyond arrival with blank pages for the sticker visa issued on arrival — the e-Visa approval is converted to a physical visa at Libreville, so the passport itself must be in good condition. Carry a printed copy of the e-Visa approval; airlines check it before boarding.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Any nationality that needs a visa for Gabon uses the DGDI e-Visa — there is no consular shortcut that avoids it, and no walk-up visa on arrival to rely on. The one 2026 exception to note: US citizens face a reciprocal issuance suspension.

Transit and onward travel

Gabon has no meaningful airside-transit regime for visa-required nationalities — the e-Visa is tied to arrival at Libreville, and connecting itineraries through other airports are not covered. If your journey touches Gabon beyond a same-aircraft technical stop, secure the e-Visa first rather than testing transit exceptions at the airport.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever Gabon's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the DGDI e-Visa portal approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Yellow-fever vaccination rules apply in this region — carry the certificate, as it is commonly checked on arrival in Central Africa. No other health document is a standard Gabonese entry requirement, but requirements can change with regional health notices.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance is not a published Gabonese e-Visa condition, but it is strongly recommended: medical facilities outside Libreville are limited and evacuation cover matters. No official annual student-insurance figure is published — budget from private providers.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

The e-Visa approval is converted to a physical sticker visa on arrival at Libreville for a separate cash fee — the approval email alone is not the visa. Expect the on-arrival step to take time; have US dollars or euros in cash for the sticker fee and keep the approval printout with your passport.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Long-stay, work and student routes are handled through Gabonese embassies and the DGDI — no official fee schedule is published online for these categories, so verify the current fees directly with the nearest Gabonese mission before budgeting. Do not rely on third-party fee tables. A visitor e-Visa never authorises work or study.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own Gabon permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether Gabon restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

e-Visa route: 1) Apply on the official DGDI portal (evisa.dgdi.ga) — the only legitimate site. 2) Receive the approval (processing about 72 hours after the ETA receipt). 3) Print the approval and fly only to Libreville (Léon Mba). 4) At arrival, convert the approval to the sticker visa and pay the separate cash fee. 5) US citizens: check whether the reciprocal suspension is still in force before starting — it has blocked issuance since January 2026.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Flying to Port-Gentil or another airport. The e-Visa is valid only via Libreville — other arrival points are not covered.
  • Attempting visa on arrival without e-Visa approval. Airlines deny boarding; the Bulgarian MFA officially records difficulties.
  • US citizens assuming normal processing. The reciprocal suspension since January 2026 blocks issuance — verify first.
  • Treating the approval email as the visa. It must be converted to a sticker on arrival for a separate cash fee.
  • Using lookalike e-Visa sites. Only evisa.dgdi.ga is official.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

The DGDI portal (evisa.dgdi.ga) is the only official online channel — applications, approval and the 72-hour processing clock all run there. There is no consular online alternative and no legitimate agent shortcut; lookalike domains charging premium 'express' fees are not official.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in Gabon's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the DGDI e-Visa portal for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where Gabon requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Carry cash for the on-arrival sticker conversion — cards are unreliable at the visa desk. Declare large cash sums as required; keep the e-Visa approval printout with your passport, not in checked luggage.

Departure and future travel

The 1–3 month single-entry e-Visa cannot be extended casually in-country — plan departure within the granted stay. Overstaying risks fines and future refusals; for longer stays, the long-stay route must be arranged before travel through a mission.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) evisa.dgdi.ga — the portal itself. 2) European MFA advisories (Serbian mfa.rs, Bulgarian mfa.bg), which corroborate the portal's official status and record the boarding-denial risk. Distrust any source describing a walk-up visa on arrival, and US citizens must verify the suspension status first.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per Gabon's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a visa on arrival in Gabon without applying first?No — not reliably. The DGDI e-Visa approval must be obtained before travel; airlines frequently deny boarding to passengers without it, and difficulties are officially recorded. The 'visa on arrival' step is only the sticker conversion of an existing approval.
Which airport can I arrive at with the e-Visa?Only Léon Mba International Airport in Libreville. The e-Visa is not valid for arrival at Port-Gentil or land borders.
How much does the Gabon e-Visa cost in 2026?The published DGDI schedule: €70 for a 1–3 month single entry (plus €15 file-processing fee), or €185 for a 6-month multiple entry (plus €15). A separate cash fee is charged on arrival for the sticker conversion — verify the current amount before travel.
Can US citizens get a Gabon visa right now?Issuance for US citizens has been reciprocally suspended since January 2026 (answering a US suspension on Gabonese citizens). Verify whether the suspension is still in force before making any plans.
How long does the e-Visa take?About 72 hours after the ETA receipt, per the DGDI portal's published information. Apply well before booking non-refundable travel.
The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Gabon is a single-portal, single-airport system: evisa.dgdi.ga → 72 hours → Libreville → sticker on arrival. Respect the Libreville-only rule, budget the €70 + €15 plus the on-arrival cash fee, and US citizens must check the suspension status first. Everything else is detail.
