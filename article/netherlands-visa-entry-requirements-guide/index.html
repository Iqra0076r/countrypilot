How to use this Netherlands visa and entry guide

The Netherlands splits immigration authority in a way that confuses first-time applicants: the Ministry of Foreign Affairs handles short-stay Schengen visas through missions and VFS, while the IND (Immigratie- en Naturalisatiedienst) at ind.nl decides everything over 90 days — residence permits, the MVV entry visa, highly skilled migrant and student routes. Know which authority owns your route before you start.

This guide was verified against IND publications and mission guidance in October 2026. Two 2026 specifics to note upfront: IND fees were indexed +4.4% on 1 January 2026, and the student-residence fee and monthly funds norm could not be verified against an official source — where a number is uncertain, this guide says so and points you to ind.nl rather than printing a guess.

Passport validity and document condition

As a Schengen state, the Netherlands applies the EU-wide rule: the passport must have been issued within the last 10 years and remain valid at least 3 months beyond your intended departure from the Schengen area. For MVV and residence-permit applications, the IND expects the passport to cover the requested permit period — renew early if yours expires mid-process.

Travellers from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh should keep at least two blank pages. Airlines enforce validity rules at boarding; a short-validity passport means denied boarding.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

First, visa-free entry: US and UK citizens may visit for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa. Second, the Schengen Type C visa: for tourism, business or family visits up to 90 days — fee €90 for adults (2026, Ministry of Foreign Affairs). Third, stays over 90 days: these need an MVV entry visa plus a residence permit, applied for through the IND — the two dominant routes being the Highly Skilled Migrant (HSM) scheme (employer must be an IND-recognized sponsor) and the EU Blue Card. The 2026 IND application fee for HSM/Blue Card residence permits is €423.

Transit and onward travel

The Netherlands applies the EU airport-transit-visa list at Schiphol: some nationalities need a transit visa even for an airside-only connection. Verify your nationality's position before booking — Schiphol is one of Europe's biggest transfer hubs, and this check is skipped more often than any other.

Leaving the airport during a layover requires a full Schengen visa. For MVV holders arriving for residence, the MVV itself is the entry document — do not attempt to enter on a Schengen visa intending to 'sort out' the residence permit after arrival.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

A Schengen visa lets you present yourself at the border — the Royal Marechaussee officer decides admission. Carry the passport with the visa, accommodation proof, the return ticket, insurance proof and funds evidence. Purpose and financing are the standard questions.

MVV and residence-permit holders should carry the employment contract or university admission plus the IND approval letter. After arrival, register with the municipality (BRP) promptly — the BSN citizen service number issued at registration gates everything from bank accounts to health insurance.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

The Netherlands requires no vaccination certificate for entry and runs no pandemic-era border health checks. No health document is presented at the Dutch border.

The health requirement that bites is insurance (below) — particularly the rule that anyone working in the Netherlands, including working students, must hold Dutch basic health insurance rather than a student plan.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance is a Schengen visa condition: minimum €30,000 cover for emergency care, hospitalization and repatriation, valid Schengen-wide. No compliant proof, no visa.

For students, the Dutch system has a sharp edge worth knowing before you budget: non-working students typically use private international student plans (for example Aon at about €53.70 per month, roughly €644 per year), but any student who works — even a part-time job — must take out Dutch basic health insurance, which averaged €1,911.60 per year in 2026 (range €1,709–€2,220) plus the €385 mandatory deductible (eigen risico). Factor the right tier into the budget from day one.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Schengen stays are capped at 90 days in any 180-day period across the whole area. Overstaying risks fines and future refusals recorded area-wide.

Residence-permit holders are admitted for the permit's duration. The MVV is single-purpose: it gets you into the Netherlands to collect the residence permit — it is not a travel document for repeated entries, so complete the IND appointment promptly after arrival.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A Schengen visa never authorizes work. The Dutch work system is employer-driven: the employer must be an IND-recognized sponsor, and the 2026 salary thresholds are €5,942 per month (age 30+), €4,357 (under 30), with a reduced €3,122 rate and a reduced Blue Card threshold of €4,754. The 2026 IND application fee is €423 (indexed +4.4% from 1 January 2026). Recognized sponsors face new 2026 obligations, including proving salaries were actually paid into the employee's bank account.

For students: the university acts as sponsor and files the residence application with the IND. Two figures this guide deliberately does not print — the 2026 student residence-permit fee and the monthly study funds norm (normbedrag) — because published sources conflict and neither could be verified against ind.nl. Check the current figures on ind.nl or with the sponsoring university before budgeting; the norm is revised every 1 January.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Each child needs a separate passport and application, with Schengen fees of €45 for ages 6–11 and free under 6 (2026). Minors travelling without both parents should carry parental consent documentation.

Dual nationals should enter on the passport matching their visa or visa-free status. Holders of non-standard travel documents should verify the applicable regime with the IND or mission rather than assuming standard treatment.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Schengen short-stay: 1) Identify the mission or VFS centre for your jurisdiction. 2) Complete the form and assemble documents (passport, photos, itinerary, insurance, funds, ties). 3) Attend the biometric appointment (first-time applicants). 4) Pay €90 (2026). 5) Allow ~15 calendar days, up to 45 for complex cases (2026).

Residence (HSM/Blue Card/student): 1) The recognized sponsor (employer or university) starts the IND application — individuals generally do not apply alone. 2) The IND decides; the vast majority of knowledge-and-talent applications are decided within one month (IND monthly figures, August 2026). 3) Collect the MVV at the mission, then travel. 4) Register with the municipality (BRP) and attend the IND appointment for the residence document. Student applications take around 2 months (60–90 days) — longer in the May–August peak.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using stale student numbers. The 2026 student fee and funds norm are disputed online — verify on ind.nl, don't trust a blog's figure.
  • Working on a student insurance plan. Any paid work triggers the Dutch basic-insurance duty (€1,911.60/yr avg, 2026) — budget for it.
  • Employer not a recognized sponsor. HSM applications through non-recognized sponsors fail at the first hurdle.
  • Entering on Schengen to 'arrange' residence. The MVV route starts from outside the Netherlands.
  • Skipping BRP registration. Without the BSN, banking, insurance and salary payment stall.
  • Counting 90 days per country. The Schengen clock is area-wide.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

For Schengen applications, Dutch missions look for the standard trio: credible purpose, sufficient funds, strong ties home — evidenced with dated bookings, genuine bank statements and employment letters. For residence routes, quality means the sponsor's file: a recognized sponsor, a contract meeting the 2026 salary threshold, and complete IND forms move within the one-month service standard; incomplete files do not.

Students: get the funds-norm figure from the university's immigration desk in writing — it is revised every 1 January and blog figures go stale fast, which is exactly why this guide refuses to print an unverified number.

Because the sponsor files the IND application, the applicant's timeline starts when the sponsor's file is complete — chase the sponsor, not the IND.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

The Netherlands offers no eVisa: Schengen applications go through the mission or VFS in person (first time), and residence applications are filed by the recognized sponsor through the IND's employer/university portals. Track Schengen files via the VFS reference.

Scam avoidance: ind.nl and the mission are the only official channels. No agent can guarantee IND approval or sell priority — the IND decides on the file, not on who submitted it. Official fees are receipted at the mission, VFS or via the sponsor's IND portal.

Translations, certifications and document format

Originals plus one copy: bank statements, employment letter, the sponsor's IND approval, insurance proof, itinerary. Documents not in Dutch, English, French or German generally need certified translation — check the mission's list.

Schengen photos must meet the 35×45 mm specification. For HSM files, the employment contract must state the gross monthly salary explicitly — IND caseworkers check the 2026 thresholds (€5,942 / €4,357) against the contract figure, so ambiguity costs weeks.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

First-time Schengen applicants give fingerprints and a photograph (VIS, 59 months). Short interviews on purpose, funding and ties are standard. MVV applicants may be interviewed at the mission as part of the residence process.

At Schiphol, the Royal Marechaussee may re-verify purpose and means — carry the submitted evidence. Inconsistent answers between the interview and the file are a standard refusal ground.

Families and group travel

Separate applications and appointments per traveller, including infants (€45 Schengen fee for ages 6–11, free under 6, as of 2026). Family members of HSM holders apply for residence as dependants through the same recognized sponsor.

A refusal for one member is recorded in the Visa Information System — fix the reason before re-applying rather than resubmitting the same file.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Declare cash of €10,000 or more when crossing the EU's external border — no tax on honest declaration, but seizure is possible for failure to declare. Standard EU restrictions apply to animal and plant products; medications in original packaging with prescriptions for controlled substances.

Departure and future travel

Keep proof of timely departure; a clean Schengen record is the best asset for the next application, and overstays are recorded area-wide. Residence-permit holders should keep the permit valid and carry it when travelling — the permit, not the expired MVV, is the re-entry document.

How to research a route without getting misled

Use this hierarchy: 1) ind.nl for everything over 90 days — fees, thresholds, sponsor obligations. 2) The mission/VFS page for Schengen procedure. 3) The university's immigration desk for the current study funds norm. 4) The municipality's BRP page for post-arrival registration.

Do not trust blog figures for the 2026 student fee or funds norm — sources conflict and the norm resets every 1 January. Anything charging far above the €90/€423 official fees is selling anxiety.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport issued within last 10 years, valid 3+ months beyond departure, with blank pages
  • Correct route — Schengen for ≤90 days; MVV + IND residence for longer
  • Recognized-sponsor confirmation (HSM) or university sponsorship (students)
  • Insurance proof (≥€30,000 Schengen-wide); correct tier if you will work
  • Student funds figure confirmed in writing from ind.nl or the university
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation proof accessible offline
  • Bank statements and ties-to-home documents packed
  • Cash ≥€10,000 declaration readiness
  • Mission/IND contact saved for emergencies

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for the Netherlands?

Yes. Indian passport holders need a Schengen visa for visits up to 90 days (€90 fee, 2026), applied for at a Dutch mission or VFS centre — no visa on arrival, no eVisa. Processing is typically around 15 calendar days.

What is the Highly Skilled Migrant salary threshold for 2026?

€5,942 per month for age 30 and over, €4,357 for under 30 (2026), with a reduced €3,122 rate and a reduced Blue Card threshold of €4,754. The employer must be an IND-recognized sponsor, and the 2026 IND application fee is €423.

How much money do I need to show for a Dutch student visa?

This guide deliberately does not print a figure: published sources conflict (€1,217.96 vs €1,130.77 per month) and neither could be verified against ind.nl. The IND study norm is revised every 1 January — confirm the current figure on ind.nl or with your sponsoring university before budgeting.

Can I work while studying in the Netherlands?

Yes, but it changes your insurance duty: any paid work means you must hold Dutch basic health insurance (averaging €1,911.60 per year in 2026 plus the €385 deductible) instead of a cheaper international student plan. Budget the correct tier from the start.

Is there a Netherlands eVisa?

No. Schengen applications are made in person at the mission or VFS (first-time biometrics required), and residence applications are filed by your recognized sponsor through IND portals. There is no Dutch eVisa.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For the Netherlands, identify your authority first: the mission/VFS for Schengen visits (€90, 2026), the IND via a recognized sponsor for anything longer. Budget the verified 2026 numbers — €423 HSM/Blue Card fee, €5,942/€4,357 salary thresholds — and verify the student fee and funds norm on ind.nl rather than trusting stale blog figures. Keep the passport inside the 10-year/3-month Schengen rule, carry €30,000-compliant insurance (and the correct tier if you'll work), and register with the municipality promptly after arrival.
