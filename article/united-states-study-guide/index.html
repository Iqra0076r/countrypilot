Current official context — checked 5 October 2026

For academic study, the usual route is F-1; vocational/non-academic study commonly uses M-1. A student normally first obtains admission from an SEVP-certified school and Form I-20, pays the required SEVIS fee, completes the visa application and attends any required consular process. Visa issuance and admission/status in the United States are related but legally distinct steps.

Official page: https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/study/student-visa.html

Understand the education system before choosing a university

Start with the qualification structure in United States: degree length, academic calendar, credit system, taught/research distinction and whether the programme leads to professional registration. SEVP-certified schools, EducationUSA and institutional admissions are useful starting points. Rankings can help with broad reputation, but programme accreditation, curriculum, graduate outcomes and location often matter more.

For regulated fields, check the professional body before applying. A university may be recognised while a specific qualification still does not grant the professional outcome the student expects. Admission is not immigration permission

After receiving an offer, review F-1 or M-1 student visa, depending on study type requirements on https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/study/student-visa.html. Immigration authorities may ask for financial evidence, health insurance, language evidence or proof that the course and institution are eligible.

Keep the university and visa timelines aligned. A late admission decision can compress the visa window; a visa delay can affect enrolment. Know the institution's latest-arrival and deferral policy.
