How to use this Indonesia visa-on-arrival guide

Indonesia runs two separate tourist lanes: the visa on arrival (VOA) — bought at the counter or pre-arranged online as an e-VOA — for travellers from roughly 97 countries, and visa-exempt entry (VEA) for 13 places (ASEAN members, Timor-Leste, Suriname, Colombia, Hong Kong). This page covers the VOA lane: the fee, who qualifies, the e-VOA vs counter choice, the 2026 extras, and the mistakes that cost travellers time and money.

Two facts set the scene for 2026: the free All Indonesia Arrival Card is mandatory for all travellers before landing, and Bali charges a one-off IDR 150,000 tourist levy on top of the visa. A proposed VOA fee hike (Rp750,000 e-VOA / Rp1,000,000 counter) has no effective date as of August 2026 — the current fee is still Rp500,000.

Who can get the VOA — and who cannot

The ~97-country VOA list covers most Western and Asian passports — confirm yours on evisa.imigrasi.go.id before flying, because the list is what the officer follows, not a blog summary. If your nationality is on the VEA list of 13 places, entry is free but limited to 30 days, non-extendable — choosing VEA when you need up to 60 days is one of the classic, expensive mistakes. Nationalities on neither list need a consular visa from an Indonesian mission.

e-VOA vs counter VOA: which to take

e-VOA (online, in advance): apply at least 48 hours to a week before departure on evisa.imigrasi.go.id. You land with approval in hand and skip the counter queue — the safer choice in peak season. Counter VOA (on arrival): pay IDR 500,000 at the airport counter; bring crisp US-dollar cash because cards can fail and damaged notes are refused. Outside office hours a US$1 overtime surcharge applies. Both give the same permission: 30 days, extendable once for 30 more.

Application timeline: when to do what

  • 1 week before departure: confirm your nationality is on the ~97-country VOA list at evisa.imigrasi.go.id.
  • 48 hours – 1 week before: pre-apply the e-VOA online (or plan to pay at the counter with crisp USD cash).
  • Before landing: complete the free All Indonesia Arrival Card — it is mandatory, not optional.
  • On arrival: clear immigration; Bali arrivals pay the IDR 150,000 levy.
  • If staying past 30 days: process the one 30-day extension before expiry — never on the last day.

The two 2026 extras: arrival card and Bali levy

The All Indonesia Arrival Card (allindonesia.imigrasi.go.id) is free and mandatory for every traveller before landing — it does not replace the visa, it sits alongside it. Bali's IDR 150,000 tourist levy is a separate one-off charge paid in addition to the IDR 500,000 VOA. Neither is negotiable at the desk; both are checked.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

A VOA or e-VOA is permission to travel to the border — the officer decides admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip (bank statements or a card in your own name). Keep the e-VOA approval saved offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Answer questions directly and consistently with what you declared.

Extension and overstay

The VOA extends once, for 30 days — process the extension before the first 30 days expire. Visa-exempt (VEA) entries cannot be extended at all. Overstaying draws daily fines and can mean detention; the extension system works only if you use it in time.

2026 gotchas: common mistakes to avoid

  • Budgeting the proposed fee hike. Rp750,000/Rp1,000,000 has no effective date (Aug 2026) — the current fee is Rp500,000.
  • Using lookalike e-VOA sites. The official portal ends in .go.id; agency lookalikes charge multiples of the IDR 500,000 fee.
  • Skipping the All Indonesia Arrival Card. Free, but mandatory before landing.
  • Forgetting Bali's levy. IDR 150,000 one-off, separate from the visa.
  • Choosing VEA for a 45-day trip. Visa-exempt entry is non-extendable — take the VOA instead.
  • Arriving with damaged dollar bills. The counter VOA refuses them; bring crisp notes.

Working or studying? Not on a VOA

A VOA or VEA entry never authorises work or study — working on one risks detention and deportation. Work runs on the KITAS system (employer-sponsored, with the US$100/month DPKK skills-development levy per foreign worker) and study on the VITAS C-316 → ITAS conversion. Current official 2026 fee figures for these routes were not verified, so confirm the fee schedule with your sponsor before budgeting.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for at least 6 months beyond arrival, with blank pages. Every child needs their own passport and their own Indonesia permission — there is no family visa. Each traveller, including minors, needs their own VOA/e-VOA approval.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid 6+ months beyond arrival, with blank pages
  • e-VOA approval saved offline and printed (or crisp USD cash for the counter)
  • Free All Indonesia Arrival Card completed before landing
  • Bali levy payment proof, if landing in Bali
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible offline
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • VOA receipt kept with your passport after arrival

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the Indonesia visa on arrival in 2026?

IDR 500,000 for both the e-VOA and the counter VOA — 30 days, extendable once for 30 more. A proposed rise to Rp750,000/Rp1,000,000 has no effective date as of August 2026.

Should I get the e-VOA or pay at the counter?

The e-VOA (apply 48h–1 week ahead at evisa.imigrasi.go.id) skips the arrival queue and is the safer choice in peak season. The counter VOA costs the same IDR 500,000 — bring crisp US-dollar cash, since cards can fail and damaged notes are refused.

What is the All Indonesia Arrival Card?

A free, mandatory digital arrival form all travellers complete before landing (allindonesia.imigrasi.go.id). It sits alongside — not instead of — the visa.

Do I pay the Bali levy on top of the visa?

Yes — Bali's IDR 150,000 one-off tourist levy is separate from the IDR 500,000 VOA and is checked on arrival.

Can the visa on arrival be extended?

Yes — once, for 30 more days, processed before the first 30 days expire. The free visa-exempt (VEA) entry, by contrast, is non-extendable.

Which website is the official Indonesia e-VOA portal?

evisa.imigrasi.go.id — it must end in .go.id. Lookalike agency sites charge multiples of the government fee.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Indonesia's visa on arrival is a pick-the-right-lane decision: IDR 500,000 (e-VOA or counter) when you might need up to 60 days, free VEA only for short fixed trips (non-extendable), the official .go.id portal only, the free arrival card filed before landing, and Bali's IDR 150,000 levy on top. Ignore the proposed fee hike until it gets an effective date — and verify the country list on evisa.imigrasi.go.id before you fly.
