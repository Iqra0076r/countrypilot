Build the funding stack before borrowing

List scholarships/grants, family contribution, savings, expected earnings and the remaining funding gap. Borrow only for the gap. A loan should solve a defined financing need, not substitute for a realistic total-cost budget. re-check WeMakeScholars before relying on it.

International students should also check whether the visa requires funds to be held in a specific way; a loan approval letter may or may not satisfy the immigration rule. compare it with the current EU Immigration Portal guidance linked below.

Eligibility, co-signers and security

Private international education lenders may restrict schools, degrees, nationalities or countries. Some require a local co-signer; others underwrite without one at selected institutions. Understand whether the co-signer is jointly liable and what happens if either borrower misses payments. Interest, fees and currency risk

Compare annual percentage rate/effective rate where available, origination fees, variable versus fixed interest, grace period, prepayment rules and late charges. Alternatives to debt

Revisit lower-cost universities, assistantships, part-time programmes, paid placements, employer sponsorship and scholarship databases. A partial scholarship plus a smaller loan may be safer than a large private loan. use WeMakeScholars to confirm the live requirement where it can change.

A practical verification worksheet

Model the loan in the currency you will actually repay. Record principal, interest type, rate reset rule, fees, disbursement schedule, grace period, repayment term, co-signer obligations, prepayment rule and default consequences.
