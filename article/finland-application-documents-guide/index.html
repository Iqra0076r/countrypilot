Current official context — checked 5 October 2026

Finland's student residence-permit rules allow degree students to work on average up to 30 hours per week under the current framework, while studies remain the basis of residence. Funds, insurance and tuition evidence depend on the case and must be checked with Migri.

Official page: https://migri.fi/en/studying-in-finland

Understand the education system before choosing a university

Start with the qualification structure in Finland: degree length, academic calendar, credit system, taught/research distinction and whether the programme leads to professional registration. Studyinfo.fi and institutions are useful starting points. Rankings can help with broad reputation, but programme accreditation, curriculum, graduate outcomes and location often matter more.

For regulated fields, check the professional body before applying. A university may be recognised while a specific qualification still does not grant the professional outcome the student expects. verify it against ScholarshipPortal before committing money or documents.

Admission requirements and realistic shortlisting

Build a three-tier shortlist: ambitious, realistic and safer options. For each programme, record academic prerequisites, language score, prerequisite courses, portfolio/research requirements, application fee and deadline. Compare the exact programme page, not only the university's general admissions page. use ScholarshipPortal to confirm the live requirement where it can change.

International applicants should also check whether credential evaluation, certified translations or country-specific documents are required. Give yourself time to obtain these before the deadline. the live Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) reference below should be consulted before action.

Intakes and application timeline

Map the intake backwards. A practical sequence is: 10–14 months out, research programmes and funding; 8–12 months out, tests and documents; 6–10 months out, submit university and scholarship applications; after admission, arrange finances, housing and immigration. The exact dates vary by institution. Confirm the current position using Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) in the source list below.

Do not assume every programme has both autumn and spring entry. Competitive programmes and scholarships may have one annual intake. the live Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) reference below should be consulted before action.

Tuition, living costs and funding

For Finland, build a first-year budget before accepting an offer. Include tuition, compulsory fees, visa/residence charges, insurance, housing deposit, furniture or setup costs, transport and emergency funds. Then map confirmed funding sources against that budget.

Scholarships should be verified on the official provider page. If loans are used, model currency movement and repayment rather than looking only at the monthly payment. the live ScholarshipPortal reference below should be consulted before action.

Admission is not immigration permission

After receiving an offer, review residence permit for studies requirements on https://migri.fi/en/residence-permit-application-for-studies. Immigration authorities may ask for financial evidence, health insurance, language evidence or proof that the course and institution are eligible.

Keep the university and visa timelines aligned. A late admission decision can compress the visa window; a visa delay can affect enrolment. Know the institution's latest-arrival and deferral policy. verify it against Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) before committing money or documents.

After admission: the practical handover

Confirm acceptance, deposits, accommodation, visa documents, travel dates, orientation and registration. Keep digital and paper copies of key documents in separate places. compare it with the current Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) guidance linked below.

Before departure, understand work rights, health-care access and any address-registration requirement. These post-arrival rules can be as important as the visa itself. verify it against Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) before committing money or documents.

Document-control system

Use four folders: identity/civil documents; eligibility evidence; financial evidence; application/decision correspondence. Create a master index with issue date, expiry date, translation status and the criterion each document proves. re-check Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) before relying on it.

Check expiry dates early. Passports, language tests, police certificates and medical results can become unusable at different times. Never alter scans to hide information; if a document contains an inconsistency, explain it. verify it against Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) before committing money or documents.

Practical action checklist

  • Open the current official Finnish Immigration Service (Migri) page and confirm the rule still applies on 2026-10-05 or later.
  • Verify programme recognition, admission requirements, refund rules and the latest visa timeline before paying a large deposit.
  • Write down any fee, threshold, deadline or processing estimate with the date it was checked.
  • Keep the official application link separate from private explanatory articles.
  • Save copies of forms, evidence and confirmations before submitting anything.
  • Do not pay an unverified recruiter, adviser, landlord, scholarship agent or lender because of urgency.
