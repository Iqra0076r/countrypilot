How to use this Guyana visa and entry guide

Guyana's system has a twist most travellers miss: visa on arrival exists, but only with mandatory pre-approval through the Immigration Support Services eServices portal — the airport walk-up application largely ended around 2023. Citizens of nearly 60 countries enter visa-free for up to 90 days.

Fees are modest and official: US$25 for a one-month initial stay (US$25 per 30-day extension), US$140 for 3-year student or work visas. The 2026 footnote: Guyana is now the only South American country whose passport holders get Saudi visas on arrival or via e-visa without a US/UK/Schengen visa (October 2026) — an outbound-mobility win, not an entry rule. This guide was verified against Guyanese Home Affairs and MFA sources in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for at least 6 months beyond arrival with blank pages. For the VOA pre-approval, the passport details entered online must match the travel document exactly — mismatches cause refusal at the counter.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Check the visa-free list first — nearly 60 nationalities skip the system entirely. Everyone else pre-approves the visa on arrival through the eServices portal; the old walk-up-at-the-airport model is effectively over.

Transit and onward travel

Guyana's VOA pre-approval covers entries including transit-style short stays, but the pre-approval itself takes time — a tight connection is not the moment to discover the walk-up counter is closed to you. Visa-exempt nationalities transit freely. Confirm any itinerary that touches Georgetown's airport against the pre-approval requirement before booking.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever Guyana's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the Immigration Support Services eServices portal approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Guyana requires no vaccination certificate for ordinary visitors. No health document is checked at Guyanese border control for standard arrivals.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance is not a Guyanese entry condition, but it is strongly recommended. No official student-insurance figure is published.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

VOA pre-approval holders get one month initial stay, extendable in 30-day blocks at US$25 per extension. Keep the pre-approval confirmation, return ticket and accommodation details accessible — officers may ask for proof of funds.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Student and work visas run US$140 for 3 years initial (US$140 per 3-year renewal) under the Home Affairs visa procedure. These are sponsored, longer-lead processes — not airport transactions. A visitor VOA never authorises work or study.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own Guyana permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether Guyana restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Visa-free route: check the ~60-country list on minfor.gov.gy — if listed, just travel. VOA route: 1) Pre-approve through the official eServices portal (eservices.iss.gov.gy) — do not rely on walk-up issuance. 2) Pay US$25. 3) Travel with the pre-approval. 4) Extend in 30-day blocks at US$25 if needed. Student/work route: Home Affairs procedure, US$140/3 years, sponsored.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Flying in expecting walk-up VOA. Airport walk-up applications largely ended ~2023 — pre-approval is mandatory.
  • Skipping the visa-free list check. Nearly 60 nationalities don't need anything.
  • Confusing the Saudi news. The October 2026 Saudi VOA facility is for Guyanese passport holders travelling out, not for entering Guyana.
  • Assuming published processing times exist. They don't — embassy timelines are reported (15–20 business days), not official.
  • Using unofficial portals. Only eservices.iss.gov.gy is official.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

The eServices portal (eservices.iss.gov.gy) is the only official online channel — VOA pre-approval lives there. The MFA's visa-entry-requirements page (minfor.gov.gy) is the authoritative exemption reference. Unofficial 'Guyana e-Visa' sellers are not legitimate.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in Guyana's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the Immigration Support Services eServices portal for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where Guyana requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Declare large cash sums as required. Keep the VOA pre-approval, return ticket and extension receipts with your passport.

Departure and future travel

VOA stays extend in 30-day blocks at US$25 — apply before the current stay expires. Overstaying risks fines and future refusals; the extension system is straightforward if used in time.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) eservices.iss.gov.gy and the Home Affairs visa procedure. 2) minfor.gov.gy for the visa-free country list. Ignore the pre-2023 walk-up VOA lore — and don't confuse the Saudi outbound news with entry rules.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per Guyana's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Guyana's rule is simple: check the visa-free list; otherwise pre-approve the VOA online (US$25). The walk-up era is over, the eServices portal is the only channel, and the Saudi headline is about Guyanese travelling out — not you travelling in. Verify exemptions on minfor.gov.gy.
