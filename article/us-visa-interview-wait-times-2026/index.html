US Visa Interview Wait Times 2026: What the Queue Looks Like by Post

The interview queue is the longest and least predictable part of a US visa application. Here is how to read it, post by post, in 2026.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20266 min read

Everything in a US visitor-visa application funnels through one bottleneck: the interview appointment at the embassy or consulate. The queue behind it is the single biggest variable in the whole process — weeks at some posts, over a year at others — and it moves constantly. This guide explains how to read your post's wait time in 2026 and plan around it.

Quick Answer

US visitor-visa interview waits run roughly 2 weeks to 13+ months depending on the post and visa category, and they move weekly. The State Department's Global Visa Wait Times tool publishes the current figure per post — check it after you schedule (the July 2026 snapshot showed posts like Hyderabad near 10 months for visitor interviews). The process order is: DS-160 → $185 MRV fee → schedule the interview → check the tool.

Wait range ~2 weeks to 13+ months · Fee $185 · Updated 2026-10-14 · Reading time 6 minutes

Why the wait varies so much

Each embassy and consulate runs its own queue with its own staffing, demand, and appointment capacity. High-demand posts in South Asia routinely carry the longest visitor-visa waits; smaller or lower-demand posts can be measured in weeks. The queue also differs by category — student and work categories often move on separate tracks from B-1/B-2. Because capacity and demand both shift, a wait time quoted last month is already stale: the tool is the only current figure.

How to read the Global Visa Wait Times tool

The tool reports the estimated wait for an interview appointment at each post, by visa type. Read it as what it is: the time from scheduling to sitting in the chair, not the total time to visa-in-hand. After the interview, add the decision and passport-return cycle — and for some cases, administrative processing, which carries no published timetable. When you schedule, note the tool's figure, then re-check it periodically: it is the earliest honest signal your timeline has moved.

Planning around a long queue

If your post's wait is measured in months, the strategy is simple and strict: enter the queue as early as possible — DS-160 done, $185 paid, interview scheduled — and keep every downstream plan flexible until the visa is issued. The expensive failure mode is booking non-refundable travel before the interview when the queue is 8+ months: the flight date arrives, the interview does not. Students face a double queue — the $350 SEVIS registration and the school's I-20 on one side, the visa interview on the other — so start both tracks together.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is the wait for a US visa interview in 2026?

Roughly 2 weeks to 13+ months by post and category — the Global Visa Wait Times tool has your post's current figure.

Does the wait time include the decision after the interview?

No. The tool estimates the wait for the appointment; add the post-interview decision and passport return on top.

Can I check another post with a shorter wait?

You apply at the post serving your place of residence; post-shopping for shorter queues is not the designed route and can complicate the file.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

The interview queue is the US visa timeline: schedule early, read your post's live wait in the official tool, keep travel plans flexible until the visa is issued — and treat any quoted wait older than a few weeks as history.
