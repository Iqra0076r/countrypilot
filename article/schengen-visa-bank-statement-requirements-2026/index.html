Schengen Visa Bank Statement Requirements 2026: Funds Proof That Works

Funds refusals are not about being rich — they are about proof. Here is how consulates read your statements in 2026 and what a convincing file looks like.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20267 min read

Two of the twelve Schengen refusal grounds are about money: not proving sufficient means of subsistence, and not showing the means are lawfully available to you. Both are judged from your bank statements. This guide explains how consulates read those statements in 2026, what patterns trigger refusals, and how to build a file that answers the questions before they are asked.

Quick Answer

Consulates judge your funds against daily reference amounts set by each member state — France works on €65 per day where prepaid accommodation is proven — measured against your real trip cost. The money must be demonstrably yours and lawfully available, shown through statements with regular income over several months. A large, sudden, unexplained deposit reads as borrowed money and damages the file.

Benchmark €65/day (France, prepaid stay) · Pattern months of regular income · Updated 2026-10-14 · Reading time 7 minutes

How consulates judge "sufficient means"

There is no EU-wide bank-balance figure. Each member state sets daily reference amounts, judged against your accommodation situation: prepaid accommodation lowers the daily figure, because the biggest cost is already covered. France's published reference is €65 per day where prepaid accommodation is proven. The method is the same everywhere: the consulate totals your real trip cost — flights, accommodation for every night, daily spending — and asks whether your statements show you can carry it with margin. What a convincing statement pattern looks like

The strongest statements tell a boring story: regular income arriving over several months, ordinary spending, a balance that grows or holds steady. Salary credits matching the employer's letter, business income matching registration and tax records, savings accumulated over time. The consulate is not looking for wealth — it is looking for consistency between the statements and the rest of the file. A modest, steady, explainable balance beats a large, sudden one every time.

The patterns that trigger refusals

Three patterns do the damage. First, the sudden large deposit: money appearing days before the application with no source reads as funds parked for the occasion. Second, statements that contradict the file: income claimed on the form that never appears in the account, or an employer letter from a company paying nothing. Third, a balance that cannot cover the trip once the real cost is totalled. The first two are also "lawful availability" problems — a separate refusal ground from simple insufficiency.

When someone else pays: declare it

Sponsored trips are normal and accepted — if declared. The right file says openly who pays, with the sponsor's statements and a signed sponsorship letter explaining the relationship and the commitment. Declared support, documented, beats an unexplained balance in your own account. What fails is the reverse: sponsor money moved into your account silently, looking like your own savings.

How many months of statements?

The Visa Code does not fix a number, and consulate practice varies — which is exactly why several months is the safe answer. A pattern needs time to be visible: one month shows a balance, three to six months show a life. If your income is seasonal or irregular, add the documents that explain the pattern — contracts, invoices, tax filings — so the consulate does not have to guess.

Statements inside the whole file

Funds evidence is one part of the appointment file — alongside the form, photo, passport, €30,000 insurance, and proof of purpose, accommodation, and intention to return. One consistent story must run through all of it. Confirm the full set with our visa document checklist before your appointment.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How much bank balance is needed for a Schengen visa?

No fixed figure exists. States set daily reference amounts (France: €65/day with prepaid accommodation), judged against your real trip cost and months of statements.

Do sudden deposits cause Schengen refusals?

Yes — a large, unexplained deposit reads as borrowed money and hits the lawful-availability refusal ground.

Can a sponsor's statements be used?

Yes, when declared openly with the sponsor's statements and a signed sponsorship letter.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Consulates do not count your money — they read its story. Months of regular, explainable income, a balance that covers the real trip cost with margin, and declared sponsorship where someone else pays: that is the funds file that gets approved.
