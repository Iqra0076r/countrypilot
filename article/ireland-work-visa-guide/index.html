If you are planning to work in Ireland, the first task is to identify the exact work-authorisation route—not to search for a generic ‘work visa’. Employer sponsorship, open permits, skilled-worker routes and job-search permissions can have completely different rules. This guide explains how to match a job and applicant profile to the legal route, what evidence normally matters, and which official page should control the final decision.

Current official context — checked 5 October 2026

Ireland increased employment-permit remuneration thresholds from 1 March 2026. The published roadmap raised the General Employment Permit threshold to €36,605 and the standard Critical Skills threshold to €40,904, with other occupation-specific thresholds. Critical Skills applications should be submitted at least 12 weeks before the proposed employment start date, and the occupation/remuneration rules must be checked on the live permit page.

Official page: https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/

Choose the legal work route before the job search

Ireland does not have one universal 'work visa'. The relevant route may be Critical Skills Employment Permit, General Employment Permit and other permits. Start by checking whether the occupation, employer and applicant profile fit the route; then target employers and jobs that can actually use it. Occupation eligibility, the Critical Skills list, ineligible occupations and permit salary thresholds need to be checked at application time.

This saves time because many vacancies are perfectly genuine but are not suitable for overseas sponsorship. A candidate should know the occupation classification, qualification requirements, professional registration and any salary or skill threshold before accepting a role.

Employer and job requirements

Employer sponsorship, where required, is a legal role rather than a marketing phrase. Verify the employer in any official sponsor/accreditation register and confirm the job title, duties, hours, location and pay. The written offer should match the facts later entered into the immigration application. In Ireland Work Visa Guide, treat this as part of the “Employer and job requirements” check; the live Irish Immigration Service and Department of Enterprise, Tourism and Employment reference below should be consulted before action.

If a recruiter asks the worker to reimburse prohibited sponsorship or recruitment costs, buy a fake qualification, or pay for a job offer, stop. Legitimate immigration systems increasingly publish anti-exploitation guidance and sponsor compliance rules.

Qualification, language and registration

A visa may accept a job only if the applicant has the skills the role requires. Regulated occupations—healthcare, teaching, engineering, law and others—can require professional registration in addition to immigration approval. Foreign qualifications may also need formal recognition or an educational assessment. For this Ireland Work Visa Guide research path, the practical checkpoint is “Qualification, language and registration”; use Irish Immigration Service and Department of Enterprise, Tourism and Employment to confirm the live requirement where it can change.

Language evidence can come from an approved test, nationality, degree taught in an accepted language or another route-specific exemption. Use only the proof method the official rules recognise.

Application evidence and timing

The strongest work-visa file links the employment evidence to the legal criteria: employer eligibility, contract or offer, occupation code, salary, qualifications, language, identity and any health/character documents. Keep a one-page checklist showing which document proves each criterion. For Ireland Work Visa Guide, connect this point to the “Application evidence and timing” decision and re-check Irish Immigration Service and Department of Enterprise, Tourism and Employment before relying on it.

Timing matters. Some sponsorship documents expire, some permits must be approved before entry, and some routes allow in-country switching only from specified statuses. Check the rule on the date the application is submitted, not when the job search began.
