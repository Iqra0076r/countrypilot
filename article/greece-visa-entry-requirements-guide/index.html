How to use this Greece visa and entry guide

Greece runs the standard Schengen playbook with two Greek specifics worth knowing. Short visits use the harmonised Schengen C visa (€90) via consulates or VFS Global centres — no Greek e-Visa or visa on arrival exists. Longer stays use national (D-type) visas: the student route costs €90 for the visa itself, with the in-country student residence permit at €150/year, and requires €400/month proven income under the MFA's student guide.

The 2026 headline: from 2 May 2026, Greece's digital nomad visa went strictly consular-first — applicants must secure the Type-D national visa at a Greek embassy before departure; switching from tourist status inside Greece is no longer accepted. This guide was verified against the Greek MFA's official student visa guide in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Standard Schengen rule: passport issued within the last 10 years and valid at least 3 months beyond planned departure from the Schengen area, with blank pages. For D-type national visas, longer validity is prudent — the residence permit process rewards documents that outlast the first permit year.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

EU-harmonised rules decide short stays: US and UK nationals enter visa-free for 90 days per 180; nationals of India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh need the €90 Schengen C visa. National D-visas are a separate, consular process.

Transit and onward travel

Athens connections follow Schengen transit logic: airside transit without entering Schengen needs no visa for most, but listed nationalities need an airport transit visa. Any itinerary that exits the transit zone — hotel, terminal change landside, onward domestic connection — is an entry and needs the C visa or visa-free status. Ferries to the islands from Turkey count as Schengen entries.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Whatever Greece's official rules say on paper, the officer at the desk decides your admission. Carry a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can fund the trip — bank statements or a card in your own name, not someone else's screenshots. Keep the Greek Ministry of Foreign Affairs approval or visa printout accessible offline; airport Wi-Fi is not a document strategy. Visiting friends or family? Their full address and a contact number answer the two most common questions before they're asked.

Answer questions directly and consistently with your application. If anything changed since you applied — new passport, different flight, longer stay — say so upfront rather than letting the officer discover the mismatch.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Greece requires no vaccination certificate for ordinary visitors and operates no border health screening. No health document is checked at Greek border control for short stays.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

The Schengen C application requires travel medical insurance of at least €30,000. For the national student visa, health/travel insurance is mandatory for at least the visa's duration — no official annual cost figure is published, so budget from private providers against the coverage requirement.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Schengen admission is 90 days per 180 across all member states combined. Greek border officers may ask for the return ticket, accommodation and insurance, and proof of funds. Island-hopping itineraries should keep the entry stamp and documents accessible — checks happen at ports too.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

The student path: national (D) student visa €90, then the in-country student residence permit €150/year, with €400/month minimum income proven via deposit account, bank transfer, scholarship or salary (MFA guide, Law 4251/2014). The digital nomad visa is consular-first since 2 May 2026 — secure the Type-D at an embassy before departure; in-country switching from tourist status is closed. Employment D-visas exist but no official fee figure was located — verify with the consulate. A Schengen C visa never authorises work.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every child needs their own passport and their own Greece permission — there is no family visa or shared authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not always a statutory requirement, but officers routinely ask, and a missing letter can delay or derail entry.

Dual nationals should enter and leave on the same passport, and check whether Greece restricts dual nationality — some countries treat entry on a foreign passport as a declaration with consequences. Travellers on refugee travel documents or laissez-passers face different rules everywhere: verify with the mission, not with forums.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Schengen C route: 1) Book the consulate/VFS appointment. 2) Bring the €90 fee, €30,000-minimum insurance, itinerary and finances. Student route: 1) Secure admission. 2) Apply for the D student visa (€90) at the consulate with the MFA guide's document list, including the €400/month income proof and insurance. 3) After entry, obtain the €150/year residence permit. Digital nomad route: apply at the embassy before travel — the in-Greece conversion path closed on 2 May 2026.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Arriving as a tourist to apply for the digital nomad visa. Since 2 May 2026 this path is closed — consular application first.
  • Under-budgeting student income proof. The MFA guide requires €400/month — have it documented, not just available.
  • Skipping the €30,000 insurance for the C visa. It is a mandatory application document.
  • Counting 90 days per visit. The 90/180 rule spans the whole Schengen area.
  • Assuming D-visa fees from third parties. The employment D-visa fee has no official published figure — ask the consulate.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

Caseworkers and officers are testing two things: will you leave, and can you afford the trip. The strongest files show stable, genuine funds over several months — a single large unexplained deposit reads as funds parking and hurts more than it helps. Tie the itinerary to the money: the trip you're describing should be one your documented income plausibly supports.

Show a reason to return — employment with approved leave, enrolment, family, property — and keep the story consistent across every document. If you've been refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence; resubmitting the same file invites the same answer.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Greece has no e-Visa portal — applications run through consulates and VFS Global application centres with in-person biometrics. The MFA's official student visa guide (hosted via Greek universities) is the authoritative document list. Ignore 'Greece e-Visa' domains; none is official.

Translations, certifications and document format

Any document not in Greece's accepted language(s) needs a proper translation — check the mission or the Greek Ministry of Foreign Affairs for whether certified translation is required, and by whom. Bank statements should be official downloads or stamped originals, not screenshots; employment letters should be on letterhead with a verifiable contact.

Submit scans well before any appointment so there's time to fix rejections. Keep originals for the appointment itself, and keep a full copy of everything you submitted — if the file is queried months later, you'll need to know exactly what the officer saw.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Where Greece requires in-person biometrics — fingerprints and a digital photograph — book the appointment early; centres fill up in peak seasons and the decision clock usually starts at the appointment, not the application. The photograph is captured there, so check whether a separate photo upload is even needed before paying for passport photos.

Interviews are the exception rather than the rule for short stays, but they happen where credibility needs testing. Answer from your actual plans, not from what you think the officer wants to hear — memorised scripts are easy to spot and hard to recover from.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own permission; there is no family authorization that covers a group. Apply in the same window so decisions land together, but understand each application is decided on its own merits: one refusal doesn't cancel the others, though it should prompt the rest of the group to re-check their own files.

For organised groups, keep a shared folder with every member's approval, the full itinerary, and the group leader's contact details. If one member is pulled aside at the border, the group should know where the documents are — not just that they exist somewhere in someone's email.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Cash of €10,000+ entering or leaving must be declared. Standard EU duty-free allowances apply. Keep the visa, insurance and return ticket with your passport — island ferry ports also conduct checks.

Departure and future travel

Schengen C visas are not extendable inside Greece except in narrow force-majeure cases. D-visa and residence-permit holders renew through the Greek authorities before expiry; the €150/year student permit renewal needs the income proof refreshed.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) The MFA's official Visa & Residence Permit Guide for Students (the document, not blogs about it). 2) The consulate with jurisdiction for current D-visa fees. Distrust pre-May-2026 digital-nomad guides describing in-country applications — that route is closed.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid per Greece's rule, with blank pages, matching the application exactly
  • Visa / e-Visa / authorization approval saved offline and printed — verify it's linked to the current passport
  • Return or onward ticket and accommodation details accessible without internet
  • Proof of funds and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance documents with emergency numbers
  • Any required arrival forms (health, customs, digital entry cards) completed in advance
  • Certified translations for documents not in the accepted language
  • Emergency contacts: your embassy, your insurer, and someone at home who has your itinerary
  • Previous refusals or complications declared honestly wherever asked

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I apply for the Greek digital nomad visa after arriving as a tourist?No — not since 2 May 2026. Greece moved to a strictly consular-first model: secure the Type-D national visa at a Greek embassy before departure. Tourist-to-nomad conversions inside Greece are no longer accepted.
What income proof does the Greek student visa need?The MFA guide requires a minimum €400/month adequate income (Law 4251/2014), proven via deposit account, bank transfer, scholarship or salary — plus the €90 D-visa and €150/year residence permit.
Is there a Greece e-Visa?No. Greece offers no e-Visa and no visa on arrival — Schengen C applications go through consulates or VFS Global centres.
How much is the Schengen visa for Greece?€90 for adults (12+), €45 for children 6–11, free under 6 — harmonised EU-wide since June 2024, unchanged through 2026.
Do I need insurance for the Greek student visa?Yes — travel/health insurance is mandatory for at least the visa's duration. No official annual cost figure is published; buy cover meeting the requirement from a private provider.
The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

Greece is straightforward if you respect the two tracks: €90 Schengen C for visits via consulate/VFS, and the D-visa route (€90 + €150/year permit + €400/month proof) for study — with the digital nomad visa now strictly consular-first since May 2026. The MFA's student guide is the controlling document; everything else is commentary.
