Why one "official" 2026 fee figure doesn't exist

Three things keep a single "China visa fee 2026" figure from existing. First, fees differ by nationality — consulates publish schedules for the nationalities they serve. Second, you can apply at a Chinese consulate directly or through a China Visa Application Service Centre, which publishes its own service charges on top of the consular fee. Third, the schedules are updated over time, and old blog posts keep circulating outdated figures. Treat any fee number without a centre name and an as-of date as suspect.

What a China visa fee is made of

Budget for two parts. The consular fee is what the Chinese mission charges for processing the visa itself. Where applications go through a service centre, the centre adds its own service charge for taking documents and returning the passport — listed separately on the centre's fee schedule. Some centres also publish optional express or urgent processing tiers with an added charge; confirm on your centre's schedule whether those tiers exist and what they cost. Because our October 2026 check could not verify a 2026 fee schedule, this page carries no dollar figures — the centre's published schedule is the only number that counts.

What makes your fee different from someone else's

Nationality. The schedule posted for your nationality at your centre is the one that applies — fees can differ between passports, so check the schedule for your own, not a travel companion's.

Visa type and number of entries. Visitor (L), study and work routes have their own schedules, and single, double and multiple-entry variants are typically priced separately. Match the fee line to your entry variant.

Application channel. Submitting through a service centre means paying the centre's service charge as well as the consular fee; confirm exactly what each line item covers.

Processing speed. Express or urgent tiers, where offered, add a surcharge on top of the standard fee. Confirm the tier exists on the centre's own schedule before choosing it.

How to find your exact fee on the official source

A reliable sequence: 1) Go to the China Visa Application Service and find the centre serving your city. 2) Open that centre's fee schedule page and locate your nationality, visa type and number of entries. 3) Note the consular fee, the service charge and any express surcharge as separate lines. 4) Check which payment methods the centre accepts before your appointment — cash, card and bank-transfer rules vary by centre. 5) Save or print the schedule with the date; if the amount quoted at the counter differs, ask to see the published schedule.

The same official sources our China visa and entry requirements guide uses apply here: the China MFA consular pages and visaforchina.cn.

How payment works at the visa centre

Payment happens at the centre, usually when you submit your file. Bring a payment method your centre accepts — card acceptance is common but not universal, so confirm in advance. Keep the receipt: it is your proof of payment and your tracking reference until the passport is returned. If a centre asks for payment to a personal account or a crypto wallet, walk away — real immigration fees go through official channels only.

Fee scams and copycat sites

Copycat websites buy ads on the portal's own name and charge large markups for submitting the same form. Red flags: "guaranteed approval", fees far above the official schedule, payment to personal accounts or crypto wallets, and pressure to decide today. Start every application at the Chinese consulate or the official visa application service centre, and type the address yourself rather than clicking ads.

Ways to avoid paying more than you should

  • Apply early. Consular queues slip in peak season, and a rushed last-minute appointment is how people end up paying for express processing they could have avoided.
  • Don't book non-refundable travel before the visa is decided. A refused or delayed application makes the flight money the expensive part.
  • Check the entry variant. A single-entry visa costs less than multiple entries — buy the variant your trip actually needs.
  • Verify the fee at the centre, not on a blog. Our October 2026 check found no verifiable 2026 fee schedule online — if a random site's number looks cheap, it is probably out of date. The China country page collects the wider planning guides for the rest of the trip.

Final check before you pay

  • Centre's published fee schedule checked, dated and saved
  • Fee quoted at the counter matches the published schedule
  • Receipt kept until the passport is returned
