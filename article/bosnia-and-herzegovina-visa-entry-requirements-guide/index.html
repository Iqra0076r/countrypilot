How to use this Bosnia and Herzegovina visa and entry guide

Visa rules are not a single yes-or-no fact attached to a destination — they depend on your passport, purpose, length of stay, transit airports and sometimes recent travel history. Start with those facts before reading a visa list. The entry-status table below is the fast answer for the most common passports; the detail follows it. Always confirm the decisive rule on the official channel — Embassy of Bosnia and Herzegovina — before paying or booking.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for at least three months beyond your intended departure with blank pages.

Transit and onward travel

Sarajevo airside transit on a through ticket normally needs no visa. Leaving the airport requires visa-free status or the Visa C.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Proof of enough money is reported as required with no fixed amount (2026). Carry bank statements.

Common documents beyond the visa itself: onward or return ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can support yourself. Print everything — phone batteries die at the worst moments.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

No vaccination certificate required for entry from most countries. Standard travel immunizations apply.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is not documented as mandatory, but recommended — carry cover for the full stay.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Your permitted stay is set by the visa, stamp or entry record — visa validity and authorised duration are not necessarily the same thing. Read the entry stamp and calculate days using the method specified for your route. Overstays in Bosnia and Herzegovina can mean fines, detention or a future entry ban — know your date.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work and study need Bosnian permits with local sponsorship — the Visa C/Visa D tourist routes do not authorize employment by themselves. Permit fees beyond Visa D are not verified; confirm with the embassy.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Minors generally need their own passport and sometimes a parental consent letter — check the requirements for your departure country too. Dual nationals should check the rules for the passport they plan to enter on, since visa treatment follows the document presented at the border. Refugees, stateless persons and emergency travel-document holders should confirm with Bosnia and Herzegovina’s embassy directly, as standard routes may not apply.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

  1. Check visa-free status — US/UK/EU citizens enter visa-free up to 90 days.
  2. If a visa is needed, apply at the BiH embassy — Visa C 101 AUD (2026).
  3. Confirm the fee in your local currency with the mission.
  4. Prepare passport, photographs, financial evidence and travel bookings.
  5. Apply at least two months ahead (processing unverified officially).

Only use the official channel — Embassy of Bosnia and Herzegovina. Copycat sites charge markups or harvest passport data. An eVisa approval is an email or a portal record: carry the confirmation and check it shows your name, passport number and permitted entries.

Translations, certifications and document format

Documents not in the local or accepted language may need certified translations — the official checklist states the rule. Certifications, bank statements and invitation letters should be recent (typically within 3 months). Photograph standards matter more than people expect: follow the portal’s specification exactly.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Biometric capture is not documented. Expect standard checks at the embassy and border.

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs their own application or authorisation — visas are not transferable within a family or group. Apply together where the portal allows, and carry the same document set for each person. With minors, bring birth certificates or consent documentation in addition to passports.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

A visa is not a customs clearance. Declare cash above the local reporting threshold, check restricted items before packing (medications, drones, satellite equipment and certain food products are common flashpoints), and know that border officials can inspect devices. Keep receipts for high-value items you intend to bring back out.

Departure and future travel

Before leaving, confirm your exit is recorded — keep boarding passes and entry/exit stamps. If you overstayed or had any immigration incident, note it: future applications to Bosnia and Herzegovina and other countries ask about it. Some nationalities need to deregister or obtain exit clearance after long stays; check before a long-term visit.

How to research a route without getting misled

Rank sources by authority: Bosnia and Herzegovina’s immigration authority and foreign ministry first, then Timatic/IATA, then consular posts, then recent reputable reporting. Treat aggregator visa sites as leads, not answers — they go stale. If two credible sources conflict, say so in your notes and confirm with the authority; this guide flags conflicts the same way.

A final document audit before travel

Forty-eight hours before departure, check the passport number on the visa or authorisation, validity dates, name spelling, permitted entries and any conditions. Confirm transit airports and terminals have not changed. Put the approval, insurance and accommodation in an offline folder and print critical documents when the destination or airline may expect paper evidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for Bosnia and Herzegovina?

US, UK and EU citizens enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Others apply for Visa C at the embassy — 101 AUD (2026).

How much is the Bosnia visa?

101 AUD for short-term Visa C (single/multiple entry); 160 AUD for long-term Visa D (2026, Embassy of BiH Australia).

Is there an eVisa for Bosnia?

No eVisa is reported — applications go through the embassy.

How long does the Bosnia visa take?

Not verified on official sources — apply at least two months ahead and ask the embassy.

Can I work in Bosnia on a tourist visa?

No — work needs a separate Bosnian permit with local sponsorship.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Bosnia and Herzegovina, US/UK visitors arrive visa-free; others get the Visa C at 101 AUD (2026) from the embassy — no eVisa, no VoA. Processing is unverified, so apply two months early and confirm the local-currency fee with your mission.
