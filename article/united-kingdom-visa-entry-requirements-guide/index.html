How to use this United Kingdom visa and entry guide

The UK sorts visitors into three lanes. Nationals of visa-exempt countries — including the United States — need an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA): £20 as of April 2026, applied for on gov.uk or the UK ETA app, valid for stays of up to 6 months. Travellers from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh need a Standard Visitor visa: £135 as of 8 April 2026 (up from £127), applied for online before travel. A small third group — including Irish citizens — needs neither.

The controlling source is gov.uk (UK Visas and Immigration): its visa-checker wizard, the Standard Visitor pages and the visa decision waiting-times pages. Since 2025 the UK has been replacing physical visa stickers with eVisas — a digital immigration status linked to your passport, proved with a share code. This guide was verified against gov.uk in October 2026; fees and service standards change by regulation, so re-check before paying.

Passport validity and document condition

For the UK, the rule is simple: your passport or travel document should be valid for the whole of your stay (gov.uk). There is no general six-month validity requirement for a Standard Visitor visa — a passport expiring mid-visit is the problem to avoid, not a fixed buffer.

A biometric passport (with the chip symbol) also unlocks the eGates at UK airports, which is the fastest way through border control. Since Brexit, EU national ID cards are no longer accepted for entry — travel on a passport. Airlines verify documents before boarding, so check the gov.uk wizard for your nationality rather than relying on generic advice.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Use the gov.uk visa checker first — it gives a definitive answer for your nationality and purpose. The three practical outcomes: (1) ETA — £20 (raised from £16 in the April 2026 fee changes), usually decided within minutes and officially within 3 working days, multiple entries over 2 years or until passport expiry, up to 6 months per visit. You need one if you pass through UK border control even in transit. (2) Standard Visitor visa — £135 (6-month), with 2-year (£506), 5-year (£903) and 10-year (£1,128) long-term options for frequent travellers (as of April 2026). (3) Neither — British and Irish citizens, and holders of valid UK visas or EU Settlement Scheme status.

Note the ETA is permission to travel, not a guarantee of entry — Border Force still applies the Standard Visitor eligibility rules on arrival. And a visa/ETA never authorizes work; that is a separate route.

Transit and onward travel

Transit through the UK has two regimes. Airside transit (not passing through border control): some nationalities need a Direct Airside Transit Visa; others may transit without a visa if they arrive and depart by air with a confirmed onward flight and hold the right documents for their destination. Landside transit (passing through border control, e.g. changing airports or an overnight stay): you need an ETA if your nationality qualifies, or a Visitor in Transit visa if it does not.

The practical rule: if your connection involves Heathrow-to-Gatwick, an overnight, or any step past the UK border, you are entering the UK, not transiting it. Check the gov.uk transit wizard with your exact itinerary before booking.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Border Force applies the Standard Visitor eligibility rules to everyone — including ETA holders and visa-free nationals. Officers may ask about the purpose of the visit, where you are staying, how you are funding the trip, and your ties to home. Carry a return or onward ticket, accommodation details, bank statements or payslips, and evidence of employment or enrollment.

You must show you will leave at the end of the visit, can support yourself and dependants, and will not live in the UK through frequent or successive visits (gov.uk). If you have a previous refusal or a criminal record, consider applying for a Standard Visitor visa even when your nationality would not otherwise require one.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

The UK sets no general vaccination requirement for short-term visitors and no health document is checked at the border for an ordinary visit. The one established exception: a tuberculosis certificate from an approved clinic can be required for visits of 6 months or more if you have lived in a listed country — check the gov.uk TB-test page if a long stay applies to you.

Beyond that, health screening is not part of UK visitor entry. The controlling source for any change is gov.uk, not airline or agent advice.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is not a UK entry or visitor-visa condition. It is still worth buying: visitors are charged for NHS hospital treatment (primary care and A&E are free to all, but inpatient care is not), and a medical bill without cover can be substantial.

For longer routes the position reverses — the Immigration Health Surcharge is mandatory: £776 per year for students, dependants, Youth Mobility and under-18 applicants (2026 rate), paid upfront for the whole grant of leave. It buys NHS access on broadly the same basis as residents. Note that private insurance does not exempt you from the surcharge (gov.uk).

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Standard visitors are admitted for up to 6 months per visit. The critical UK-specific rule: you must not live in the UK through frequent or successive visits or make the UK your main home (gov.uk) — back-to-back 6-month stays with brief absences are the classic refusal pattern at the border and in later applications.

Permitted activities are defined, not open-ended: tourism, family visits, certain business activities (meetings, conferences, interviews), permitted paid engagements, short courses up to 30 days, and volunteering up to 30 days with a registered charity. Paid or unpaid work for a UK business, claiming public funds, and marriage (without a Marriage Visitor visa) are prohibited.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A visitor permission — visa or ETA — never authorizes work. The Skilled Worker route requires a licensed sponsor, a Certificate of Sponsorship (£525 per certificate, 2026), and an application fee of £819–£1,618 outside the UK depending on duration (April 2026 schedule), plus the employer's Immigration Skills Charge of £480–£1,320 per year. Since 22 July 2025 the skill threshold is degree level (RQF 6).

The Student route needs a Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies, costs £558 plus the £776/year health surcharge (2026), and requires maintenance funds of £1,529/month in London or £1,171/month elsewhere (up to 9 months, held 28 days). Note the Graduate visa shortens to 18 months for applications from 1 January 2027. Treat any '10-year settlement' headlines with care: as of October 2026 that is a proposal under consultation, not law.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

British and Irish citizens need no ETA and should travel on their British/Irish passport. Dual nationals holding British citizenship can enter freely on that passport; travelling on the other nationality's passport may require an ETA and invites questions.

Every child needs their own ETA or visa — there is no family authorization. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter from the non-travelling parent(s); it is not a statutory requirement for UK entry but Border Force officers may ask, and safeguarding checks on children are routine.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

ETA route: 1) Apply on gov.uk or the UK ETA app (£20, as of 2026). 2) Decisions usually arrive within minutes, officially within 3 working days — do not travel until approved; carriers check it. Standard Visitor visa route: 1) Run the gov.uk wizard to confirm the route. 2) Complete the online application with 10-year travel history, parents' details, itinerary and finances. 3) Pay £135 (as of 8 April 2026). 4) Book the visa application centre appointment (TLScontact/VFS Global) for biometrics — photograph and fingerprints taken there, so no separate photo upload is needed. 5) Wait the published service standard: 3 weeks outside the UK (measured from the biometrics appointment, 2026). 6) The visa is issued as an eVisa — check it is correctly linked to your passport and learn how to prove it with a share code before you fly.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Confusing the ETA with a visa. The £20 ETA is for visa-exempt nationalities only; if your passport needs a visa, an ETA application will not help.
  • Applying too late. The 3-week service standard is a target, not a guarantee — apply 6–8 weeks before travel in busy periods.
  • Thin financial evidence. Six months of bank statements or payslips showing genuine, stable funds beats a single large unexplained deposit — which caseworkers read as funds parking.
  • Hiding a previous refusal. Non-disclosure is deception and carries far worse consequences than the original refusal.
  • Treating 6 months × repeated visits as residence. The 'genuine visitor' rule specifically targets this pattern.
  • Forgetting the eVisa. New visit visas are digital — confirm the status is linked to the right passport before departure.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

UK caseworkers decide the genuine visitor test: will this person leave, and can they afford the trip? The strongest files show stable income over six months (not a sudden deposit), a clear reason to return (job with approved leave, studies, family, property), and an itinerary that matches the funds — a two-week £4,000 trip on a £900/month salary invites refusal.

Answer the online form's 10-year travel history and family questions fully; inconsistencies between the form and documents are the most common self-inflicted wound. If refused before, address the refusal reasons directly with new evidence rather than re-submitting the same file.

Published service standards (2026): 3 weeks for Standard Visitor, Student and Skilled Worker applications made outside the UK, measured from the biometrics appointment; 8 weeks for applications made inside the UK. Priority and super-priority services exist at some centres for an extra fee but are not available everywhere — check at booking. Apply 6–8 weeks before travel in peak seasons.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

The UK's online system is entirely on gov.uk: the visa-checker wizard, the application forms, the ETA application, and the eVisa view-and-prove service (share codes for proving status to carriers and employers). Since the BRP phase-out, approved visas are digital — verify the eVisa is linked to your current passport, especially after a renewal.

Scam avoidance: only gov.uk (and the official UK ETA app) can issue an ETA or visa. Third-party 'UK visa' sites that charge several times the £135/£20 official fees are not faster and not official. No agent can influence a UKVI decision; anyone guaranteeing approval is lying.

Translations, certifications and document format

Typical supporting documents: 6 months of bank statements or payslips, employment letter, proof of accommodation and itinerary, evidence of ties to home, and travel history. Any document not in English or Welsh needs a certified translation — the translator must confirm accuracy, give the date, and provide their full name, signature and contact details (gov.uk).

Upload scans through the TLScontact/VFS self-upload service before your biometrics appointment, or pay for assisted scanning at the centre. Keep originals for the appointment itself.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

UK visitor applications normally involve no interview — the decision is made on the papers and biometrics. At the visa application centre, staff capture your fingerprints and a digital photograph (which is why no separate photo upload is required) and collect or scan your documents.

UKVI can invite applicants to an interview or request additional documents where credibility needs testing — respond promptly and consistently. At the border, eGate users (biometric passports, eligible nationalities) may pass with no officer contact; others see a Border Force officer who can ask the standard eligibility questions.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including every child — needs their own ETA or visa; there is no family authorization. Children's applications are linked to a responsible adult's in the online system, but the permission is individual and each child needs their own passport.

Groups travelling together should apply in the same window so decisions land together, but note that each application is decided on its own merits — one refusal does not cancel the others, though travelling companions should be aware of it when planning.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Carrying £10,000 or more (or the equivalent in other currencies) into or out of the UK must be declared — the declaration is free, but non-declaration can lead to seizure. Duty-free allowances for arrivals from outside the UK include 200 cigarettes (or equivalent tobacco), 42 litres of beer, 18 litres of still wine, and 4 litres of spirits, plus £390 of other goods (£270 by some arrival modes) — check gov.uk for the current thresholds.

Prohibited items include illegal drugs, offensive weapons, and most meat and dairy products from outside the EU. Declare anything uncertain in the red channel; the penalty for non-declaration exceeds any duty you might have paid.

Departure and future travel

Extending a Standard Visitor stay inside the UK is possible only in limited circumstances (e.g., medical treatment) — plan to leave within the admitted period. A history of compliant visits builds the record that makes long-term visit visas (2, 5, 10 years) viable; a history of overstays or frequent back-to-back visits destroys it.

On settlement: headlines about a move from 5-year to 10-year qualifying periods refer to a government proposal under consultation as of October 2026, not law. Do not make life decisions on it until legislation passes — check gov.uk for the current rules.

How to research a route without getting misled

The hierarchy: 1) gov.uk's visa-checker and the specific visa pages — they override everything. 2) The visa decision waiting-times pages (outside/inside the UK) for timelines. 3) The FCDO travel advice pages for entry requirements by nationality. Immigration-adviser blogs and forums are useful for process colour but not for rules; the April 2026 fee round alone invalidated a large share of older guides quoting £127 and £16.

Be wary of content that still quotes pre-April-2026 fees, still shows BRP cards as current, or presents the 10-year settlement proposal as enacted law — all three are common, all three are wrong as of October 2026.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid for the whole stay (biometric for eGates)
  • ETA approved or Standard Visitor eVisa correctly linked to the passport — verify before departure
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation details accessible offline
  • Bank statements/payslips and ties-to-home evidence packed
  • Travel insurance with UK medical coverage
  • Cash declaration prepared if carrying £10,000+
  • Certified translations for any non-English/Welsh document
  • Share-code know-how if you need to prove eVisa status
  • Previous refusals declared honestly in any application

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for the UK, or is an ETA enough?

It depends on your nationality. Visa-exempt nationalities (including the US) need an ETA (£20, as of April 2026) for visits up to 6 months. Nationals of India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines, Bangladesh and other visa-required countries must obtain a Standard Visitor visa (£135) before travel. The gov.uk visa checker gives the definitive answer for your passport.

How long does a UK visitor visa take?

The published service standard is 3 weeks for applications made outside the UK, measured from the biometrics appointment (2026). In practice, apply 6–8 weeks before travel in busy periods — the standard is a target, not a guarantee.

How long can I stay in the UK per visit?

Up to 6 months per visit on a Standard Visitor visa or ETA. You must not live in the UK through frequent or successive visits — border officers and caseworkers specifically look for that pattern.

Can I work in the UK on a visitor visa or ETA?

No. Visitors may do certain business activities (meetings, conferences, interviews) and permitted paid engagements, but not work for a UK employer or as self-employed. Work requires a route like Skilled Worker, with a licensed sponsor.

What changed for UK visas in 2026?

The 8 April 2026 fee round raised the Standard Visitor visa from £127 to £135 and the ETA from £16 to £20, among other changes. Visit visas are now issued as digital eVisas rather than stickers. The Graduate visa shortens to 18 months for applications from 1 January 2027. A proposed move to 10-year settlement qualifying periods remains a proposal, not law, as of October 2026.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For the United Kingdom, the first step is the gov.uk visa checker: ETA (£20) if your nationality is visa-exempt, Standard Visitor visa (£135) if it is not. Your passport needs to cover the whole stay, decisions take about 3 weeks from biometrics outside the UK, and each visit is capped at 6 months — with frequent back-to-back visits treated as residence. Verify the current fee schedule and the eVisa link to your passport before you fly.
