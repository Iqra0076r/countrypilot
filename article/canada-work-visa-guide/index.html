If you are planning to work in Canada, the first task is to identify the exact work-authorisation route—not to search for a generic ‘work visa’. Employer sponsorship, open permits, skilled-worker routes and job-search permissions can have completely different rules. This guide explains how to match a job and applicant profile to the legal route, what evidence normally matters, and which official page should control the final decision.

Current official context — checked 6 October 2026

IRCC distinguishes employer-specific and open work permits. An employer-specific permit is tied to the named employer/conditions and usually begins with a job offer plus an LMIA or an LMIA-exempt employer process where applicable. An open work permit is available only in specified situations and is not a general alternative for anyone without an employer. IRCC currently lists a CAD 155 work-permit processing fee in the standard fee schedule; open-work-permit applicants can also face the open-work-permit holder fee.

Official page: https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/work-canada/permit.html

Choose the legal work route before the job search

Canada does not have one universal 'work visa'. The relevant route may be employer-specific or open work permit, depending on program. Start by checking whether the occupation, employer and applicant profile fit the route; then target employers and jobs that can actually use it. A work permit is not the same thing as a permanent-residence job offer; LMIA rules, exemptions and program definitions matter.

This saves time because many vacancies are perfectly genuine but are not suitable for overseas sponsorship. A candidate should know the occupation classification, qualification requirements, professional registration and any salary or skill threshold before accepting a role.

Employer and job requirements

Employer sponsorship, where required, is a legal role rather than a marketing phrase. Verify the employer in any official sponsor/accreditation register and confirm the job title, duties, hours, location and pay. The written offer should match the facts later entered into the immigration application. compare it with the current Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) guidance linked below.

If a recruiter asks the worker to reimburse prohibited sponsorship or recruitment costs, buy a fake qualification, or pay for a job offer, stop. Legitimate immigration systems increasingly publish anti-exploitation guidance and sponsor compliance rules.

Qualification, language and registration

A visa may accept a job only if the applicant has the skills the role requires. Regulated occupations—healthcare, teaching, engineering, law and others—can require professional registration in addition to immigration approval. Foreign qualifications may also need formal recognition or an educational assessment. verify it against Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) before committing money or documents.

Language evidence can come from an approved test, nationality, degree taught in an accepted language or another route-specific exemption. Use only the proof method the official rules recognise.

Application evidence and timing

The strongest work-visa file links the employment evidence to the legal criteria: employer eligibility, contract or offer, occupation code, salary, qualifications, language, identity and any health/character documents. Keep a one-page checklist showing which document proves each criterion. re-check Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) before relying on it.

Timing matters. Some sponsorship documents expire, some permits must be approved before entry, and some routes allow in-country switching only from specified statuses. Check the rule on the date the application is submitted, not when the job search began.

Practical action checklist

  • Open the current official Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) page and confirm the rule still applies on 2026-10-05 or later.
  • Identify the exact visa/residence route and confirm that your current status allows you to use it.
  • Write down any fee, threshold, deadline or processing estimate with the date it was checked.
  • Keep the official application link separate from private explanatory articles.
  • Save copies of forms, evidence and confirmations before submitting anything.
  • Do not pay an unverified recruiter, adviser, landlord, scholarship agent or lender because of urgency.

Identify the work-permit type before planning the application

Canada distinguishes between employer-specific work permits and open work permits. You do not simply choose whichever is more convenient; eligibility determines which route is available. IRCC's current work-permit tool and guidance should be the starting point.

QuestionEmployer-specific permitOpen work permit
Job offer normally required?YesNot for the permit itself, but eligibility is limited to defined situations
Employer named on permit?Typically yesGenerally not limited to one compliant employer
Employer action may be required?Yes; this can include LMIA or LMIA-exempt steps depending on the caseUsually a different eligibility basis applies

For an employer-specific route, IRCC says the employer must take required steps before the worker applies. Review the current employer-specific work permit guidance rather than relying on an offer letter alone.

Run a job-offer document check

Verify the employer's legal identity, job title, duties, location, wage, hours, start date and whether the employer says an LMIA is required or the job is LMIA-exempt. Ask for the reference or documentation you will need for the permit application. If the employer cannot explain the immigration step attached to its own offer, pause before paying any recruiter or relocation cost.

Do not confuse job searching with legal work authorisation

A person can search for jobs without already holding the eventual work permit, but should not claim to have Canadian work authorisation if they do not. Keep your application language precise. For employer-focused planning, use the Canada Employer Sponsorship Basics guide.

Use Canada's official Temporary Foreign Worker job search

Canada's Job Bank has a dedicated Temporary Foreign Workers section for jobs from employers that have already obtained or applied for a Labour Market Impact Assessment (LMIA). It is a useful starting point for employer-specific work-permit research because it is an official Government of Canada service.

Search Temporary Foreign Worker jobs on Job Bank →

An LMIA-related Job Bank listing still does not guarantee that you personally qualify for a work permit. Confirm the employer-specific permit requirements, the job offer and any LMIA or LMIA-exempt documentation through IRCC before relying on the vacancy.

Looking for LMIA roles? Use the LMIA Jobs in Canada guide for the official Job Bank workflow and employer verification.
