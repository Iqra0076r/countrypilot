How to use this Malaysia visa and entry guide

Malaysia runs three parallel doors and the right one depends on your passport, not your preference. Door 1 — visa-free entry: many nationalities enter without a visa for social visits; Chinese and Indian nationals currently enjoy visa-free entry until 31 December 2026 under the government's waiver policy. Door 2 — eVisa: for visa-required nationalities including China, India, Nepal, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Myanmar, applied for online through the Immigration Department's official e-Visa portal. Door 3 — visa on arrival: only in limited scenarios (RM 200, arriving from Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia or Brunei) — it is not a general facility.

One step is mandatory for almost everyone: the free Malaysia Digital Arrival Card (MDAC), submitted online within 3 days before travel. The controlling sources are the Immigration Department of Malaysia (Jabatan Imigresen Malaysia) at imi.gov.my and malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my. This guide was verified against official Malaysian sources in October 2026.

Passport validity and document condition

Malaysia generally requires your passport to be valid for at least 6 months beyond your arrival date — this is the standard applied at Malaysian checkpoints and by airlines boarding Malaysia-bound passengers. Confirm the current requirement on the Immigration Department site before travel, as enforcement is strict.

Your passport must be machine-readable and in good condition. If you renewed your passport after obtaining an eVisa, the eVisa is linked to the old document — carry both passports or re-apply, and confirm the procedure on malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Match your passport to the right door. Visa-free social visit: Chinese and Indian nationals — visa-free until 31 December 2026 (check whether the waiver has been extended before you travel); many other nationalities receive visa-free entry for social visits, with permitted stays varying by nationality — confirm your country's position on imi.gov.my. eVisa: required nationalities (including Nepal, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Myanmar) apply online at the official portal malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my — Single Entry Visa RM 50 official fee plus about RM 105 eVisa processing charge; Multiple Entry Visa RM 100 (all figures as of 2026). Visa on arrival: RM 200, only for arrivals from Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia or Brunei in eligible scenarios — do not plan a trip around it.

Transit and onward travel

Malaysia does not publish a single universal transit-without-visa rule on the sources verified for this guide — transit treatment depends on your nationality, whether you stay airside at KLIA, and your onward ticket. Passengers transiting Kuala Lumpur International Airport without clearing immigration generally follow airline guidance, but visa-required nationals should not assume this.

Confirm your exact transit requirement with your airline and on imi.gov.my using your full itinerary before booking — being refused boarding at origin for a missing transit visa is a routine, avoidable failure.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

Malaysian immigration officers assess whether you are a genuine visitor: a real temporary stay for the stated purpose. Carry your passport, your MDAC confirmation, a return or onward ticket, proof of funds, and accommodation details. Officers may ask about the purpose and length of your stay even when you hold a valid visa or visa-free entitlement.

Keep evidence of ties to home (employment letter, enrollment, family) accessible. Overstaying a social visit pass is treated seriously in Malaysia — know your permitted stay from the entry stamp, not from memory.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Malaysia sets no general vaccination requirement for standard short-term visitors, and no health document is routinely checked at the border for tourist entry. Health screening applies to longer-stay categories: Student Pass applicants undergo a medical screening (RM 250) as part of the EMGS process.

Requirements can change with public-health conditions — check the Immigration Department and Ministry of Health guidance shortly before travel rather than relying on older guides.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is not an entry condition for tourist visits to Malaysia — but hospital care is charged at full rates to visitors, so comprehensive cover with high medical limits is strongly advisable before departure.

For students the position is mandatory: EMGS-mandated medical insurance is required for international students on a Student Pass, minimum 12 months' coverage, costing roughly RM 456–958 per year under the EMGS Schedule of Fees v1.6 (November 2025, current through 2026). Universities collect this as part of the Student Pass package.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

A Malaysian visa — including an eVisa — is permission to travel to the border, not an unconditional right of entry; the immigration officer at the checkpoint decides admission and stamps your permitted stay. The MDAC (submitted within 3 days before travel) speeds up the counter process but does not replace the officer's decision.

Visa-free entries and social visit passes are typically 30 or 90 days depending on nationality — the entry stamp controls, not the general rule. Overstaying can lead to detention, fines and future blacklisting; diarise your exit date.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A social visit pass never authorizes work in Malaysia — not even remote work for a foreign employer as a matter of right. Real work needs an Employment Pass (EP) through the Expatriate Services Division (esd.imi.gov.my): the ESD application charge is RM 2,000 (RM 2,160 including 8% SST, raised from RM 800), plus RM 200 per year of EP duration and RM 125 processing on endorsement. From 1 June 2026 a revised salary framework applies: Category I minimum RM 20,000/month (up from RM 10,000), Category II RM 10,000–19,999, Category III RM 5,000–9,999 — with maximum cumulative durations and succession-plan requirements.

Study needs a Student Pass via EMGS (visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my): EMGS processing RM 1,600 + 8% service tax for a new 6–12 month pass, plus RM 150 eVAL, RM 50 i-Kad, RM 250 medical screening and RM 60 pass sticker — a first-year total of roughly RM 2,700–3,300 before university charges (EMGS Schedule of Fees v1.6, November 2025). Malaysia publishes no national proof-of-funds floor for Student Pass applicants — individual institutions set their own financial-evidence requirements, so confirm with your university, not with a generic figure.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Every traveller needs their own MDAC submission and their own visa or visa-free entitlement — children cannot be included on a parent's application. Minors travelling without both parents should carry a consent letter and birth certificate; officers may question the arrangement.

Dual nationals should enter and exit on the same passport throughout the trip, since the eVisa and MDAC are tied to a specific document number. Carry both passports if the visa sits in one and you travel on the other, and confirm the procedure on the official portal.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Visa-free route: 1) Confirm your nationality's current visa-free status and permitted stay on imi.gov.my. 2) Submit the free MDAC within 3 days before travel. 3) Carry return ticket, funds evidence and accommodation details. eVisa route: 1) Apply at the official portal malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my (beware look-alike sites — start from imi.gov.my). 2) Pay the SEV fee RM 50 plus ~RM 105 eVisa processing (2026). 3) Submit MDAC within 3 days before travel. 4) Carry the printed eVisa approval. Student route: apply through your institution to EMGS (visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my), complete the eVAL, medical screening and fee package — budget roughly RM 2,700–3,300 for the first year plus university charges.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming visa-on-arrival is generally available — it is limited to specific arrival corridors (RM 200); most visa-required nationals need an eVisa in advance.
  • Skipping the MDAC — it is mandatory and free; forgetting it means delays at the counter.
  • Using a look-alike eVisa website — start from imi.gov.my or malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my only.
  • Quoting a "national proof-of-funds minimum" for the Student Pass — none is published nationally; check your institution's requirement.
  • Working on a social visit pass — it never authorizes employment; the EP salary thresholds rose sharply on 1 June 2026.

Application quality: getting it right first time

Malaysian eVisa refusals most often come from poor-quality uploads: a blurry passport biodata scan, a photo that does not meet specifications, or an itinerary that does not match the declared purpose. Upload a sharp colour scan of the passport biodata page and a recent ICAO-style photo.

Names must match the passport exactly, including the order of given and family names — transliteration mismatches between the application and the passport are a common cause of MDAC and eVisa hiccups. Double-check before submitting; corrections after approval are slow.

Fees, appointments and processing time

Official 2026 figures: Single Entry Visa RM 50 (+~RM 105 eVisa processing), Multiple Entry Visa RM 100, limited visa-on-arrival RM 200; Student Pass first-year package roughly RM 2,700–3,300 (EMGS Schedule of Fees v1.6, November 2025); Employment Pass ESD charge RM 2,000 (RM 2,160 incl. 8% SST). The MDAC is free.

Malaysia does not publish a single official processing-time standard for tourist eVisas — third-party blogs quote 2–7 working days, but that is not an official figure, so apply early and track your application on the official portal rather than planning around an unofficial number.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

The only official eVisa channel is malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my, reached via the Immigration Department at imi.gov.my. Look-alike commercial sites charge markups and add no value — some harvest passport data. Student applications go through visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my (EMGS) via your institution; expatriate Employment Pass applications go through esd.imi.gov.my.

Never pay an "eVisa fee" on a site you reached through a search ad without verifying the domain. The official SEV fee is RM 50 — any site charging several times that is not the government.

Supporting documents checklist

For the eVisa: passport biodata scan (6+ months' validity), ICAO-style photo, return/onward flight booking, accommodation details, and funds evidence. For the Student Pass via EMGS: institution offer letter, eVAL approval, passport, academic certificates, medical screening report, and the fee package. For the Employment Pass: employment contract meeting the post–1 June 2026 salary thresholds, company ESD registration, educational certificates, and passport.

Keep digital and paper copies of everything submitted — Malaysian counters occasionally ask to see the document behind the approval.

Interviews and biometrics

Malaysia does not operate a general visa-interview system for eVisa applicants — most tourist eVisas are decided on documents alone. Biometric capture (fingerprints and photo) happens at the Malaysian entry checkpoint on arrival, not at an application centre beforehand.

Student Pass and Employment Pass applicants may be directed to provide biometrics or attend in person at a Malaysian mission during processing — follow the instructions in your specific application channel rather than assuming.

Families and groups travelling together

Each family member — including infants — needs their own MDAC and their own visa or visa-free entitlement; there is no family bundling. Employment Pass holders sponsor dependants through a Dependant Pass (ESD charge RM 500) tied to the principal's EP.

Groups should submit MDACs with consistent travel dates and keep a shared folder of bookings — if one member is questioned at the counter, the whole group's documents help resolve it quickly.

Customs and border formalities

Malaysia's customs allowances follow standard international norms — declare cash above the threshold, and note that certain medications, drones and large quantities of tobacco face restrictions. The MDAC submission covers the immigration side; customs is a separate lane.

Keep the customs declaration honest: undeclared restricted items cause far more trouble than the declaration itself. Check the Royal Malaysian Customs Department guidance for the current restricted list before packing.

Return and onward travel proof

A return or onward ticket is effectively mandatory for visa-free and eVisa entries — officers routinely ask for it as the core evidence of a temporary stay. A one-way ticket into Malaysia with no onward plan is the single most common reason for extended questioning at the counter.

The onward ticket should be within your permitted stay. Refundable or changeable tickets are worth the premium if your plans are flexible.

Route-specific research pointers

Tourist: confirm visa-free status or eVisa requirement for your nationality on imi.gov.my; submit MDAC. Student: start with your institution's international office — EMGS processing runs through them (visa.educationmalaysia.gov.my). Work: the employer drives the ESD application (esd.imi.gov.my) — verify the post–1 June 2026 salary category your offer falls into before signing. Long stay: Malaysia My Second Home (MM2H) and related passes sit outside this guide's scope — research them separately on official channels.

Final document audit before you fly

Run this checklist 72 hours before departure: 1) Passport valid 6+ months. 2) Correct route confirmed on imi.gov.my (visa-free / eVisa / VOA). 3) eVisa approval printed (if applicable). 4) MDAC submitted (within 3 days before travel). 5) Return/onward ticket. 6) Funds evidence + accommodation details. 7) Travel insurance documents. 8) For students/workers: EMGS eVAL or EP approval letter. 9) Fees re-checked against the official 2026 schedule — do not budget from memory or old guides.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need an MDAC for Malaysia even if I am visa-free?

Yes. The Malaysia Digital Arrival Card is mandatory for almost all foreign arrivals, is free, and must be submitted online within 3 days before travel — regardless of whether you enter visa-free, on an eVisa, or on arrival. It does not replace a visa; it is a separate arrival requirement.

How much does a Malaysian tourist eVisa cost in 2026?

The official Single Entry Visa fee is RM 50, plus about RM 105 in eVisa processing charges — budget roughly RM 155 total (2026). A Multiple Entry Visa is RM 100. Apply only at the official portal malaysiavisa.imi.gov.my; third-party sites charge markups.

Can I get a visa on arrival in Malaysia?

Only in limited scenarios: RM 200, for arrivals from Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia or Brunei where eligible. It is not a general facility — most visa-required nationals must obtain an eVisa in advance. Do not plan a trip around visa-on-arrival.

What are the new Employment Pass salary thresholds?

From 1 June 2026: Category I minimum RM 20,000/month (up from RM 10,000), Category II RM 10,000–19,999, Category III RM 5,000–9,999 — with maximum cumulative EP durations and succession-plan requirements. The ESD application charge is RM 2,000 (RM 2,160 including 8% SST). Confirm your offer's category on esd.imi.gov.my.

Is there a proof-of-funds minimum for the Malaysian Student Pass?

Malaysia publishes no national proof-of-funds floor for Student Pass applicants. Individual institutions set their own financial-evidence requirements — confirm the exact figure with your university's international office rather than relying on any generic number found online.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Malaysia, the three questions are: which door (visa-free, eVisa, or limited arrival visa), the mandatory free MDAC within 3 days of travel, and — for workers and students — the post–1 June 2026 Employment Pass salary framework or the EMGS Student Pass package (roughly RM 2,700–3,300 first year, 2026). Budget from the official 2026 fee schedule at imi.gov.my, apply only through official portals, and never plan around an unofficial processing-time number. Confirm charges and requirements on the Immigration Department site before you pay anything.
