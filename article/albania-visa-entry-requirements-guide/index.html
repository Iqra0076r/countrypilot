How to use this Albania visa and entry guide

Visa rules are not a single yes-or-no fact attached to a destination — they depend on your passport, purpose, length of stay, transit airports and sometimes recent travel history. Start with those facts before reading a visa list. The entry-status table below is the fast answer for the most common passports; the detail follows it. Always confirm the decisive rule on the official channel — Albanian eVisa portal — before paying or booking.

Passport validity and document condition

Carry a passport valid for at least three months beyond your intended departure from Albania, in line with standard European practice. Confirm the exact rule on the portal when you apply.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

Albania runs its visitor applications through the official eVisa portal at evisa.al. A single-entry Type C eVisa costs €30 and multiple-entry €50–€60, with a reciprocal €80 fee for US, UK and Canadian nationals (2026). There is no visa on arrival — the eVisa (or a visa exemption) is the entry document.

Albania is not in the Schengen area, so its rules are its own: many nationalities, including US and UK citizens, enter visa-free for up to 90 days. In 2026 the system gained an express eVisa option (reported) and a digital-nomad pilot (reported) — both worth checking on the portal if they fit your plans.

Albania's eVisa system is in operation at evisa.al — apply online before travel. Single-entry Type C is €30; multiple-entry €50–€60; US/UK/CA nationals pay a reciprocal €80 (2026). An express option with 3-day processing at +50% fee was reported as added in March 2026 — confirm it on the portal.

Transit and onward travel

Albania has one international airport (Tirana). Transit without entering is possible airside on a through ticket; leaving the airport requires the eVisa or a visa exemption. E-gates opened at Tirana airport in January 2026 (reported), speeding eligible arrivals.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

No fixed official minimum is published; secondary sources cite around €50 per day as a working figure (2026 reporting). Carry bank statements or equivalent evidence — the border officer decides sufficiency.

Common documents beyond the visa itself: onward or return ticket, proof of accommodation, and evidence you can support yourself. Print everything — phone batteries die at the worst moments.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

No vaccination certificate is required for entry from most countries. Standard travel immunizations (routine plus hepatitis A/B for longer stays) are sensible — consult a clinic, not the border.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance of €30,000 is widely reported as required (2026). Buy a policy meeting that cover level before applying — it is routinely checked.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Your permitted stay is set by the visa, stamp or entry record — visa validity and authorised duration are not necessarily the same thing. Read the entry stamp and calculate days using the method specified for your route. Overstays in Albania can mean fines, detention or a future entry ban — know your date.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

Work and study need a Type D national visa — the tourist eVisa does not authorize employment. Type D fees are reported from €50 but conflict by source; confirm on the portal. A digital-nomad pilot with an income requirement around €3,000/month was reported from January 2026 — verify current terms before planning around it.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Minors generally need their own passport and sometimes a parental consent letter — check the requirements for your departure country too. Dual nationals should check the rules for the passport they plan to enter on, since visa treatment follows the document presented at the border. Refugees, stateless persons and emergency travel-document holders should confirm with Albania’s embassy directly, as standard routes may not apply.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

  • Digital-nomad pilot (reported, January 2026): ~€3,000/month income requirement, 1-year permit — as reported in 2026; verify current terms.
  • E-gates at Tirana airport (reported, January 2026): faster processing for eligible passports.
  1. Check whether your nationality is visa-exempt for Albania (US/UK and many others enter visa-free up to 90 days).
  2. If a visa is needed, apply on the official portal evisa.al — single-entry €30 (2026).
  3. Upload passport scan, photograph, travel and accommodation evidence as the portal requests.
  4. Allow 7–15 working days (reported); consider the reported express option in peak season.
  5. Carry €30,000-cover travel insurance and proof of funds for the border check.

The eVisa application typically asks for a passport scan, a compliant photograph, proof of accommodation, onward travel, and financial evidence. Requirements display on the portal as you apply — follow the portal's checklist, not a generic one.

Skipping insurance. €30,000 medical cover is routinely checked. Applying late in summer. Reported processing stretches to 30 working days in June–August. Using a copycat site. Only use the official channel — Albanian eVisa portal. Copycat sites charge markups or harvest passport data. An eVisa approval is an email or a portal record: carry the confirmation and check it shows your name, passport number and permitted entries.

Translations, certifications and document format

Documents not in the local or accepted language may need certified translations — the official checklist states the rule. Certifications, bank statements and invitation letters should be recent (typically within 3 months). Photograph standards matter more than people expect: follow the portal’s specification exactly.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Biometric capture is not documented for the eVisa application itself; expect photographs and possible fingerprints at the border. Confirm the current procedure on the portal.

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs their own application or authorisation — visas are not transferable within a family or group. Apply together where the portal allows, and carry the same document set for each person. With minors, bring birth certificates or consent documentation in addition to passports.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

A visa is not a customs clearance. Declare cash above the local reporting threshold, check restricted items before packing (medications, drones, satellite equipment and certain food products are common flashpoints), and know that border officials can inspect devices. Keep receipts for high-value items you intend to bring back out.

Departure and future travel

Before leaving, confirm your exit is recorded — keep boarding passes and entry/exit stamps. If you overstayed or had any immigration incident, note it: future applications to Albania and other countries ask about it. Some nationalities need to deregister or obtain exit clearance after long stays; check before a long-term visit.

How to research a route without getting misled

Rank sources by authority: Albania’s immigration authority and foreign ministry first, then Timatic/IATA, then consular posts, then recent reputable reporting. Treat aggregator visa sites as leads, not answers — they go stale. If two credible sources conflict, say so in your notes and confirm with the authority; this guide flags conflicts the same way.

A final document audit before travel

Forty-eight hours before departure, check the passport number on the visa or authorisation, validity dates, name spelling, permitted entries and any conditions. Confirm transit airports and terminals have not changed. Put the approval, insurance and accommodation in an offline folder and print critical documents when the destination or airline may expect paper evidence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a visa for Albania?

Many nationalities — including US and UK citizens — enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Others apply for the eVisa at evisa.al (€30 single-entry, 2026). There is no visa on arrival.

How much is the Albania eVisa?

€30 single-entry or €50–€60 multiple-entry Type C; US/UK/Canadian nationals pay a reciprocal €80 (2026, evisa.al).

How long does the Albania eVisa take?

Reported at 7–15 working days, up to 30 in summer peak. A 3-day express option was reported from March 2026 — confirm on the portal.

Is Albania in the Schengen area?

No. Albania runs its own visa system (evisa.al). A Schengen visa does not automatically cover Albania — check the exemption list.

Does Albania have a digital nomad visa?

A pilot was reported from January 2026 with an income requirement around €3,000/month. Verify current terms on official channels before planning around it.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Albania, the path is the official eVisa portal: €30 single-entry (2026), no visa on arrival, and visa-free entry for many nationalities including the US and UK. Apply 2–3 weeks ahead (longer in summer), carry €30,000-cover insurance, and remember Albania is not Schengen — its rules stand alone.
