How to use this Ireland visa and entry guide

Ireland is not in Schengen, and that single fact shapes everything: Irish visas are issued under Ireland's own system through Immigration Service Delivery (ISD) at irishimmigration.ie, applications are lodged online via AVATS — but the passport must still be submitted in person or at a visa centre, and the visa is issued as a sticker. There is no Irish eVisa and no visa on arrival.

Work permits are handled separately by the Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment (enterprise.gov.ie). This guide was verified against ISD guidance in October 2026. The defining 2026 change: from 1 June 2026, administrative appeals were abolished for most short-stay (Type C) visa refusals — a refused applicant must now file a fresh application and pay again.

Passport validity and document condition

Ireland requires the passport to be valid for your entire stay — it does not impose a general 6-month-validity rule. That said, visa-required applicants are commonly advised to carry around 6 months' validity beyond the planned departure, and airlines apply their own boarding checks, so renew well before travel if expiry is near.

Keep at least one blank page for the visa sticker and entry stamp. Because the visa is a physical sticker, a full or damaged passport forces a transfer process — check the passport's condition before applying, not after.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

First, visa-free entry: US citizens enter visa-free for short stays, and UK citizens travel under the Common Travel Area arrangement. Second, the Short Stay 'C' visa: for tourism, business or family visits — fee €60 single-entry / €100 multiple-entry (2026), applied for online via AVATS with the passport submitted in person. Third, the Long Stay 'D' visa: for work, study or stays over 90 days — the study visa costs the same €60/€100 bands (2026). Fourth, work: the Critical Skills Employment Permit (application fee €1,000, 90% refunded if refused — usually paid by the employer, 2026) plus a €300 first-issue residence stamp fee; the entry visa follows the permit.

Transit and onward travel

Ireland has no visa-on-arrival and no transit-visa waiver for visa-required nationalities: if your nationality needs an Irish visa, you need it even for an airside-only connection at Dublin. Check the ISD visa-required list for your nationality before booking — Dublin is a major transatlantic transfer point and this check is skipped too often.

Visa-exempt transit passengers need nothing beyond the onward ticket. Note that a UK visa does not cover Ireland (and vice versa, with limited exceptions under the British-Irish Visa Scheme for certain nationalities via the UK) — the Common Travel Area does not mean shared visas.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

The visa lets you present yourself at the border — the immigration officer decides admission and sets the permitted stay. Carry the passport with the visa sticker, the return ticket, accommodation proof and funds evidence. Purpose and financing are the standard questions.

Work-permit holders should carry the employment permit approval; students the college admission letter and fee receipts. After arrival, non-EEA nationals must register with immigration (GNIB/IRP) — the entry visa alone does not complete the residence process.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

Ireland requires no vaccination certificate for entry and operates no pandemic-era border health checks. No health document is presented at the Irish border.

The health requirement that matters is private insurance (below) — non-EU students must hold it as a visa and registration condition, handled as part of the application, not at the border.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel insurance is not a formal entry stamp requirement for tourists, but non-EU students must hold private health insurance as a condition of the study visa and subsequent immigration registration. Specialist student plans cost about €150–200 per year, university group schemes €150–300, and comprehensive private cover (Vhi, Laya, Irish Life) €500–1,200 (2026).

Buy the policy before the visa application — the consulate/visa office checks it as part of the file, and arriving without compliant cover stalls registration.

Arrival permission and permitted stay

The immigration officer sets the permitted stay at entry — for visa-free US visitors typically up to 90 days. The visa's validity is not the permitted stay; the officer's stamp is.

Long-stay entrants must register with immigration and obtain the IRP (Irish Residence Permit) card. Overstaying — even briefly — jeopardizes every future Irish and UK-adjacent application, since the Common Travel Area means records travel.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A short-stay visa never authorizes work. The skilled route is the Critical Skills Employment Permit: €1,000 application fee (90% refunded if refused, usually employer-paid), typically decided in 6–12 weeks with a priority 8-week path for salaries above €64,000 (2026 service levels) — plus €300 for the first-issue residence stamp and ~4–6 weeks for the entry visa after permit approval where needed.

Students operate under the revised framework in effect since 30 June 2025 (still current in 2026): mandatory biometrics at VFS centres for all applicants, a standardized €10,000 per academic year proof of funds (plus tuition fees), and mandatory upfront tuition payment (minimum €6,000 deposit). Courses of 8 months or less use €833/month (max €6,665). The study visa fee is €60/€100 (2026). Processing runs 4–8 weeks standard, 8–12 weeks in the April–August peak (2026).

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Each child needs a separate passport and visa application — including infants. Minors travelling without both parents should carry parental consent documentation; Irish visa officers and border officials may ask for it.

Dual nationals should travel on the passport matching their visa or visa-free status. Irish citizens — including duals — should enter on their Irish passport where held.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Short Stay 'C': 1) Complete the AVATS online application at visas.inis.gov.ie. 2) Submit the passport and supporting documents in person or at the visa centre. 3) Pay €60 single / €100 multiple (2026, non-refundable). 4) Allow ~8 weeks after ISD receives the documents (2026 guidance; 12–40 working days reported). Remember: from 1 June 2026 there is no administrative appeal for most Type C refusals — get the file right the first time, because a refusal means a fresh application and a fresh fee.

Work (Critical Skills): 1) The employer applies for the permit via the Department of Enterprise (€1,000, 2026). 2) Allow 6–12 weeks (priority 8-week path above €64,000). 3) Apply for the entry visa if needed (+~4–6 weeks). 4) Pay the €300 first-issue stamp fee and register after arrival.

Study: 1) Secure admission and pay the minimum €6,000 tuition deposit. 2) Arrange the €10,000 funds evidence plus private insurance. 3) Complete AVATS, biometrics at VFS, and pay €60/€100 (2026). 4) Allow 4–8 weeks (8–12 in peak, 2026).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming a refused Type C visa can be appealed. Since 1 June 2026 it cannot — a refusal means starting over and paying again.
  • Under-budgeting the student file. €10,000 funds + €6,000 minimum tuition deposit + insurance — all three, not one of them.
  • Confusing UK and Irish visas. A UK visa does not admit you to Ireland (limited BIVS exceptions aside).
  • Arriving without private insurance (students). It is a visa and registration condition, not an optional extra.
  • Working on a short-stay visa. Never authorized — the permit route is the only route.
  • Missing the registration step. The entry visa or permit approval is not the end; GNIB/IRP registration completes residence.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

With no appeal safety net since June 2026, the first application must be the best application. ISD officers look for credible purpose, sufficient funds and strong ties home — evidenced with dated bookings, genuine bank statements and employment letters. Every refusal reason must be anticipated in the file, because there is no second argument.

For students, the file is now formulaic — and that is an advantage: €10,000 funds evidence, €6,000+ tuition deposit receipt, compliant insurance, biometrics done. Files that hit all four move; files missing one stall.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

Ireland has no eVisa: AVATS is an online application system, but the passport must be submitted physically and the visa is a sticker. Track the application through the AVATS reference.

Scam avoidance: irishimmigration.ie and enterprise.gov.ie are the only official channels. No agent can overturn the June-2026 appeal abolition or buy a faster decision — the queue is first-in, first-out. Official fees are receipted; never pay a 'visa fee' to a personal account.

Translations, certifications and document format

Originals plus one copy: bank statements, employment letter, the €6,000+ tuition deposit receipt (students), insurance policy, and itinerary. Documents not in English need certified English translations.

Photographs must meet the ISD specifications. For work permits, the employer's documentation carries the application — the permit is decided substantially on the employer's file, so ensure the employer submits completely.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

Irish visa applications are document-driven rather than interview-driven as standard — but biometrics at VFS centres are mandatory for all student applicants under the framework in effect since 30 June 2025. Fingerprints and photographs are also captured at GNIB/IRP registration after arrival.

At the border, officers may ask about purpose, accommodation and funds — carry the submitted evidence. Inconsistent answers remain a standard refusal ground.

Families and group travel

Each traveller needs a separate AVATS application and passport submission — including infants — at the per-person fee (€60/€100, 2026). Family members of permit holders apply for join-family visas through the same ISD system.

Since June 2026, a refusal for one member cannot be appealed — fix the reason before any re-application, as the refusal is on record.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Travellers must declare cash of €10,000 or more when entering from outside the EU — no tax on honest declaration, seizure possible for failure to declare. Standard EU restrictions apply to animal and plant products; medications in original packaging with prescriptions for controlled substances.

Departure and future travel

Depart within the permitted stay — overstaying damages future Irish applications and, through the Common Travel Area data-sharing, can affect UK-adjacent travel too. Keep the IRP card valid once issued; it is the re-entry document.

A clean Irish immigration history is valuable precisely because the system is small and records are centralized — one overstay follows you.

How to research a route without getting misled

Use this hierarchy: 1) irishimmigration.ie for visa rules and the AVATS portal. 2) enterprise.gov.ie for employment-permit types and fees. 3) ISD's weekly queue updates for current processing reality. 4) The VFS centre page for submission logistics.

Ignore any guide describing a Type C appeal process — abolished 1 June 2026. Ignore student guides quoting pre-June-2025 funds rules. Anything charging far above the €60/€100/€1,000 official fees is selling anxiety.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport valid for the entire stay, with a blank page for the sticker
  • AVATS application completed; passport submitted as instructed
  • Correct visa type — C for short stays, D for work/study
  • Student file: €10,000 funds + €6,000 tuition deposit + insurance + biometrics done
  • Work file: Critical Skills permit approved (€1,000 fee receipt)
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation proof accessible offline
  • Bank statements and ties-to-home documents packed
  • Cash ≥€10,000 declaration readiness
  • ISD/embassy contact saved for emergencies

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for Ireland?

Yes. Indian passport holders need an Irish visa applied for online via AVATS — €60 single-entry / €100 multiple-entry (2026) — with the passport submitted in person or at a visa centre. There is no eVisa and no visa on arrival. Allow about 8 weeks after ISD receives the documents.

Can I appeal if my Ireland tourist visa is refused?

Not anymore for most short-stay visas. From 1 June 2026, Ireland abolished administrative appeals for most Type C refusals — you must submit a fresh application (and pay the fee again) addressing the refusal reasons. Appeal rights remain for Type D refusals and EU Free Movement cases. This makes first-application quality critical.

How much money do I need for an Ireland student visa?

€10,000 per academic year in living-costs evidence plus tuition fees, under the framework in effect since 30 June 2025 — along with a minimum €6,000 upfront tuition deposit already paid and mandatory private health insurance (€150–1,200/year depending on tier, 2026). Courses of 8 months or less use €833/month (max €6,665).

Is Ireland in Schengen? Can I use a Schengen visa?

No — Ireland is not in Schengen. A Schengen visa does not admit you to Ireland, and an Irish visa does not admit you to the Schengen area. They are entirely separate systems (with only limited exceptions under the British-Irish Visa Scheme for certain nationalities travelling via the UK).

How long does an Ireland work permit take?

The Critical Skills Employment Permit typically takes 6–12 weeks (2026), with a priority 8-week path for salaries above €64,000. The application fee is €1,000 (90% refunded if refused, usually employer-paid), plus a €300 first-issue residence stamp fee and ~4–6 weeks for the entry visa where needed.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For Ireland, remember it is not Schengen: visas come from ISD via AVATS (€60/€100, 2026), as stickers, with no eVisa and no arrival issuance. Since 1 June 2026 there is no appeal for most short-stay refusals — so the first application must be complete. Students budget the post-June-2025 formula (€10,000 funds + €6,000 tuition deposit + insurance + biometrics); workers go through the Critical Skills permit (€1,000, 6–12 weeks). Confirm everything on irishimmigration.ie; the weekly queue updates are the honest processing picture.
