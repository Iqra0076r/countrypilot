US F-1 Student Visa Guide 2026: Requirements, Fees, and Process

The F-1 is a two-fee, two-track process: the school's I-20 and SEVIS registration on one side, the visa interview on the other. Here is the 2026 route end to end.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20267 min read

Studying in the US runs through the F-1 student visa — and the F-1 is really two processes in parallel: the school track (admission, Form I-20, SEVIS registration) and the visa track (DS-160, fee, interview). Both must complete before travel, and the timelines interact. This guide maps the 2026 route.

Quick Answer

The F-1 requires a Form I-20 from a SEVP-certified school, SEVIS registration with the $350 I-901 fee (2026), and the visa application itself: DS-160 plus the $185 MRV fee and an interview at a US embassy or consulate — $535 in government fees. Funds are tested against the school's published cost of attendance. Start both tracks together; interview queues run weeks to months by post.

Fees $185 + $350 SEVIS · Document Form I-20 · Updated 2026-10-14 · Reading time 7 minutes

Track 1: the school — I-20 and SEVIS

Everything starts with admission: only a SEVP-certified school can issue the Form I-20, the document that makes you a real student in the system. Against the I-20, you complete SEVIS registration and pay the $350 I-901 fee (as of 2026). The I-20 states the program dates and the school's cost of attendance — the figure the funds test is measured against. No I-20, no SEVIS, no visa interview worth attending.

Track 2: the visa — DS-160, fee, interview

With SEVIS paid, the visa track runs the standard nonimmigrant sequence: complete Form DS-160, pay the $185 MRV fee (2026), schedule the interview, and check the Global Visa Wait Times tool for your post's queue — student categories often move on a separate track from B-1/B-2, but waits still run weeks to months and move weekly. Attend with the I-20, SEVIS receipt, DS-160 confirmation, fee receipt, and the funds file.

The funds test: cost of attendance

The financial requirement is anchored to the school's published cost of attendance — tuition plus living costs as the school states them. The evidence must show the money available and verifiable: bank statements with history, approved loans, scholarships, or declared sponsor funds with the sponsor's statements. A balance that merely equals the figure with no history invites scepticism; funds built and documented over time do not.

The interview: what it tests

The F-1 interview tests bona fide studentship: why this school, why this program, how it fits your plans, and how it is funded — plus the standard intention to return after the program. Answers must match the I-20 and the funds file exactly. The $185 + $350 fees cover processing, not the outcome; refusals are not refunded.

Timing the two tracks

The tracks run in parallel, not in sequence: begin the visa track the moment the I-20 is issued — waiting for "everything to be ready" wastes the queue. Layer the timelines: I-20 issuance, SEVIS payment, DS-160, interview scheduling, the post's wait from the tool, then the decision and passport return. For autumn intake, this routinely means starting in spring. After approval, check for any reciprocity fee before arranging passport return.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the US student visa in 2026?

$185 MRV fee plus $350 SEVIS I-901 fee — $535 in government fees (2026).

What is the I-20?

The Form I-20 is the school-issued document certifying your admission and program details — required before SEVIS registration and the visa interview.

How much funds do I need to show?

The school's published cost of attendance — evidenced as available, verifiable money, not a last-minute balance.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

I-20 from the school, $350 SEVIS, $185 MRV, funds matching the cost of attendance, one interview testing studentship and ties — run both tracks in parallel from the day the I-20 arrives, and let the visa queue, not the intake deadline, set your start date.
