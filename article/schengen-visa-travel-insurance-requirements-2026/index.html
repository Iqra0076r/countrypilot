Schengen Visa Travel Insurance Requirements 2026: The €30,000 Rule

Insurance is not an extra on a Schengen application — it is a legal requirement with a number attached. Here is exactly what your policy must say in 2026.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20266 min read

Among Schengen requirements, travel medical insurance is unusual: the rule is written into the Visa Code with an exact figure, an exact territorial scope, and an exact time span. Get any of the three wrong and the file fails on a standalone refusal ground. This guide states the 2026 insurance requirement precisely and shows how to prove compliance at the appointment.

Quick Answer

Under Article 15 of the Visa Code, your insurance must cover at least €30,000 of emergency medical care, hospitalisation, and repatriation — in all Schengen states, for the entire stay. The policy certificate must state the cover amount, the territory, and the exact trip dates. Inadequate insurance is its own refusal ground.

Minimum cover €30,000 · Territory all Schengen states · Period entire stay · Updated 2026-10-14 · Reading time 6 minutes

The €30,000 minimum, exactly

Article 15 sets the floor: insurance must be valid for emergency medical care, hospitalisation, and repatriation (including repatriation on medical grounds or in case of death), with minimum cover of €30,000. A policy with a lower limit — or one that covers only part of the trip, or only some Schengen states — does not meet the rule, whatever its marketing says. Read the certificate, not the brochure: the consulate checks the certificate.

Territory and dates: the two most common failures

The two details that sink real policies: territory and dates. The insurance must be valid in every Schengen state you will visit — a policy limited to one country fails a multi-country itinerary. And it must cover the whole period of the stay, from entry to exit: a policy ending a day early, or starting a day late, fails the requirement. When you buy the policy, give the insurer your exact itinerary and check that the certificate mirrors it back.

What the certificate must state

The consulate needs to see, on the certificate itself: the cover amount (at least €30,000), the territorial validity (all Schengen states / "Schengen"), and the trip dates matching your application. A vague letter saying "travel insurance included" with none of these details is not evidence. If your insurer issues certificates without the territory line, ask for one that states it — this is a routine request.

Insurance and the rest of the file

Insurance sits inside a bigger document set — form, photo, passport, and evidence of purpose, means, accommodation, and intention to return — and the appointment checks the whole set at once. Budget the policy alongside the €90 visa fee using our Schengen visa cost calculator, and run the full file through our visa document checklist before the appointment.

Mistakes to avoid

  • A limit below €30,000. The number is a legal floor, not a suggestion.
  • Dates that do not match the itinerary. Cover must run entry to exit, with no gaps.
  • Territory limited to one country on a multi-country trip.
  • A certificate without the key lines. Amount, territory, dates — all three, in writing.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How much insurance do I need for a Schengen visa?

At least €30,000 of emergency medical, hospitalisation, and repatriation cover.

Does the insurance have to cover all Schengen countries?

Yes — all Schengen states, for the entire stay, under Article 15 of the Visa Code.

Can weak insurance alone cause a refusal?

Yes. Inadequate travel medical insurance is a standalone ground on the EU refusal form.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

€30,000, every Schengen state, every day of the trip, stated on the certificate: meet all four and insurance becomes the easiest part of your file instead of the reason it fails.
