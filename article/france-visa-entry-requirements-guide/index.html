How to use this France visa and entry guide

France runs its visa system through a single official portal — France-Visas at france-visas.gouv.fr — and in 2026 the practical rule is simple: start there, not with an agent. The portal's Visa Wizard tells you whether you need a visa, which type, the exact document list, and where to book the appointment. Appointments must now be scheduled online through the official channel.

Like Germany, France operates two systems: the Schengen short-stay visa (up to 90 days) and the long-stay visa (work, study, family — the visa itself, with a residence permit applied for after arrival). This guide was verified against France-Visas guidance in October 2026. The headline 2026 change: the student-visa financial threshold jumped 43% in August — read the funds section before budgeting.

Passport validity and document condition

As a Schengen state, France applies the EU-wide rule: the passport must have been issued within the last 10 years and remain valid at least 3 months beyond your intended departure from the Schengen area. Airlines enforce this at boarding, and France-Visas lists document validity among the first checks.

For long-stay visas, ensure the passport covers the full initial visa period — a passport expiring mid-application forces a restart. Travellers from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh should also keep at least two blank pages for the visa sticker and entry stamps.

Identify the correct visa or entry route

First, visa-free entry: US and UK citizens may visit France and the Schengen area for up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa. Second, the Schengen short-stay visa: for tourism, business or family visits up to 90 days — fee €90 for adults, €45 for children 6–11, free for under-6s (2026). Third, the long-stay visa: for work (including the passeport talent), study, or stays over 90 days — fee €99 (or €50 in countries covered by the Études en France procedure), as of 2026. Note the separate residence-permit tax and stamp duty (about €200 + €25) payable after arrival — the visa fee is not the total cost.

Transit and onward travel

France applies the EU airport-transit-visa list: some nationalities need a transit visa even for an airside-only connection at Paris CDG or Orly. Check the France-Visas Wizard for your nationality before booking — a missed transit requirement strands you at the departure airport, not in Paris.

Leaving the airport during a layover requires a full Schengen short-stay visa, not a transit visa. Note that France is often the first Schengen entry point on multi-country itineraries: passport control happens at CDG even if your final destination is elsewhere in Europe.

Evidence immigration officers may ask to see

A French Schengen visa authorizes you to present yourself at the border — the Police aux Frontières officer decides admission. Carry the passport with the visa, proof of accommodation, the return ticket, travel insurance proof, and evidence of funds. Officers commonly ask about the trip's purpose and financing.

Long-stay visa holders should carry the employment contract, work authorization, or university admission letter that underpinned the application. After arrival on a long-stay visa, most holders must validate the visa online and then apply for the residence permit (titre de séjour) — the visa alone does not complete the process.

Health documents and vaccination certificates

France requires no vaccination certificate for entry and operates no pandemic-era border health checks. No health document is presented at the French border.

The health formality that matters is insurance (below) — and for students, registration in the French student health system after arrival, which is a residence step, not a border document.

Insurance as an entry or visa condition

Travel medical insurance is a Schengen visa condition for France: minimum €30,000 cover for emergency care, hospitalization and repatriation, valid Schengen-wide for the whole stay. Applications without compliant proof are refused.

For students, the longer-term picture is good: registration in the state student health system (via etudiant-etranger.ameli.fr) is free and mandatory, and a complementary mutuelle for the non-reimbursed portion typically costs about €200 per year. Private travel-compliant plans suitable for the visa application run roughly €480–€760 per year (2025 figures — confirm current tariffs before buying).

Arrival permission and permitted stay

Schengen stays are capped at 90 days in any 180-day period, counted across all Schengen states — a week in Spain counts against the same allowance as a week in France. UK and US visitors should note their passports are now stamped on entry and exit, which is how the 90-day count is enforced.

Long-stay visa holders are admitted for the visa's duration and must complete the residence-permit process after arrival. Overstaying a Schengen allowance — even slightly — risks fines and future refusals recorded across the area.

Work, study and residence are separate questions

A Schengen visa never authorizes work in France. Work routes run through the long-stay system: the standard route needs an employer work authorization, while the passeport talent covers highly skilled profiles. The long-stay visa fee is €99 (2026), with residence-permit taxes of about €200 + €25 stamp duty after arrival.

Students face the big 2026 change: the financial-resources threshold rose from €615 to €877.50 per month (€10,530 for 12 months) effective 1 August 2026 — a 43% jump announced via Campus France and France-Visas, and tuition must be shown separately on top. Budget against the new figure, not older guides. Long-stay student applications cannot be filed more than 3 months before arrival.

Minors, dual nationals and special documents

Each child needs a separate passport and visa application, with reduced fees (€45 for ages 6–11, free under 6, as of 2026). Minors travelling without both parents should carry parental consent documentation; French consulates and border officers may request it.

Dual nationals should travel on the passport matching their visa or visa-free status. Holders of refugee travel documents or other non-standard documents should verify the applicable regime on France-Visas rather than assuming standard Schengen treatment.

A reliable pre-travel workflow

Schengen short-stay: 1) Run the France-Visas Visa Wizard to confirm the type and document list. 2) Create the France-Visas account and complete the online application. 3) Book the appointment online — appointments must now be scheduled through the official channel. 4) Attend in person with biometrics (first-time applicants) and pay €90 (2026). 5) Allow ~15 working days, up to 45 for complex cases (2026).

Long-stay (work/student): 1) Secure the basis first — work authorization or university admission (Études en France where applicable). 2) Prepare funds evidence against the current thresholds (€877.50/month for students from August 2026). 3) File no earlier than 3 months before arrival. 4) Pay €99 (2026; €50 in Études en France countries). 5) Allow around 30 days for work visas and 15 days to 3 weeks for student visas — longer in the June–August peak (2026).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Budgeting the old student funds figure. €615/month is dead — the threshold is €877.50/month since 1 August 2026.
  • Using an agent instead of France-Visas. The official Wizard is free and definitive; agents add cost, not accuracy.
  • Booking the appointment late. Slots fill in peak season; the online booking requirement is now enforced.
  • Forgetting post-arrival taxes. The €99 visa fee is not the total — residence-permit tax and stamp duty follow.
  • Non-compliant insurance. Under €30,000 cover or not Schengen-wide means refusal.
  • Counting 90 days per country. The clock runs across the whole Schengen area.

Building a stronger visa or entry file

French consulates weigh three things: credible purpose, sufficient funds, and strong ties home. A dated itinerary with bookings, 3–6 months of bank statements showing genuine income patterns, and an employment letter with approved leave dates form the core. For students, the file now stands or falls on the €877.50/month evidence — consulates check the arithmetic.

Use the France-Visas document list for your nationality literally; it is the checklist the officer works from. Submitting exactly what is asked, in the order asked, avoids the most common cause of delay: incomplete files.

eVisas, online forms and scam avoidance

France has no eVisa: the France-Visas online application is the start of the process, not the end of it — first-time applicants still attend in person for biometrics, and the visa is a sticker. Track the file through your France-Visas account.

Scam avoidance: france-visas.gouv.fr is the only official portal. Ignore lookalike domains offering 'guaranteed' visas or priority slots for a fee — the consulate does not sell either. Official fees are paid at the visa centre with a receipt, never to a personal account.

Translations, certifications and document format

Originals plus one copy of everything on the France-Visas checklist: bank statements, employment or enrollment proof, the insurance certificate, itinerary and bookings. Documents not in French or English should carry a certified translation.

Photographs must meet Schengen specifications (35×45 mm). For long-stay students, keep the admission letter, the Études en France reference where applicable, and the funds evidence as a separate clearly-labelled bundle — officers process these files in volume and clarity speeds decisions.

Interviews, biometrics and identity checks

First-time applicants provide fingerprints and a photograph at the visa centre; the data is stored in the Visa Information System for 59 months. A brief interview on purpose, funding and ties is standard for both short-stay and long-stay applications.

Answer consistently with the written file. At the border, Police aux Frontières may re-check purpose and means — carry the same evidence submitted with the application, since 'I left it at home' is not an answer that admits you.

Families and group travel

Each traveller — including infants — needs a separate application and appointment, with per-child fees (€45 for ages 6–11, free under 6, as of 2026). Files are decided individually; a strong parent file does not carry a weak child file.

Book appointments together where the system allows. A refusal for one member is recorded in the Visa Information System — address its reason before re-applying rather than resubmitting identical facts.

Customs, cash and restricted goods

Travellers must declare cash of €10,000 or more when crossing the EU's external border into France — no tax, no confiscation for honest declaration, but seizure is possible for failure to declare. Standard EU restrictions apply to animal and plant products.

Medications should be in original packaging with prescriptions for controlled substances. Duty-free allowances apply per adult for alcohol and tobacco arriving from outside the EU.

Departure and future travel

Keep proof of timely departure — with entry/exit stamps now systematically applied to UK and US passports, the 90-day count is enforced from the stamps. Overstaying risks fines and future refusals across Schengen.

Long-stay holders: the residence permit (titre de séjour) is what enables re-entry and Schengen travel after the initial visa period — complete the post-arrival steps on schedule rather than travelling on an expiring long-stay visa.

How to research a route without getting misled

Use this hierarchy: 1) the France-Visas Visa Wizard for your nationality — it overrides everything else. 2) france-visas.gouv.fr guidance pages for fees and timelines. 3) Campus France for the student route and the current financial threshold. 4) The specific consulate's page for local submission quirks.

Ignore pre-August-2026 student guides still quoting €615/month — they are stale on the single most expensive line of the application. Any service charging far above the €90/€99 official fees is selling anxiety, not visas.

A final document audit before travel

  • Passport issued within last 10 years, valid 3+ months beyond departure, with blank pages
  • Correct visa type — Schengen for ≤90 days, long-stay for work/study
  • France-Visas appointment confirmation and printed application
  • Insurance proof (≥€30,000, Schengen-wide) for Schengen trips
  • Student funds evidence at €877.50/month (post-August 2026 threshold)
  • Return/onward ticket and accommodation proof accessible offline
  • Bank statements and ties-to-home documents packed
  • Cash ≥€10,000 declaration readiness
  • Consulate contact saved for emergencies

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians need a visa for France?

Yes. Indian passport holders need a Schengen visa for visits up to 90 days (€90 fee, 2026) — applied for via France-Visas before travel. There is no visa on arrival and no eVisa. Decisions typically take about 15 working days.

How much money do I need to show for a France student visa in 2026?

€877.50 per month (€10,530 for 12 months) — effective 1 August 2026, up 43% from the old €615/month. Tuition fees must be demonstrated separately. This is the single most common refusal point since the change; budget against the new figure.

How do I book a France visa appointment?

Through france-visas.gouv.fr — complete the online application first, then book the slot in the same portal. Online booking is now mandatory, and peak-season appointments fill early, so start as soon as your travel window opens.

Can I work in France on a Schengen tourist visa?

No. The Schengen visa never authorizes employment. Work requires a long-stay visa (€99, 2026) with an employer work authorization or a passeport talent, plus residence-permit taxes of about €200 + €25 after arrival.

Is there a France eVisa?

No. France-Visas is an online application portal, not an eVisa — first-time applicants still attend in person for biometrics, and the visa is issued as a passport sticker. Beware copycat sites selling 'France eVisas'.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

For France, everything starts at france-visas.gouv.fr — run the Wizard, book the appointment online, and apply in person. Budget the real 2026 numbers: €90 Schengen, €99 long-stay (plus ~€225 in residence taxes after arrival), and the new €877.50/month student funds threshold in force since 1 August 2026. Keep the passport inside the 10-year/3-month Schengen rule, carry €30,000-compliant insurance, and remember the 90-day clock runs across the whole Schengen area.
