UK Student Visa 28-Day Rule Explained 2026: Maintenance Funds Done Right

The 28-day rule is the most quietly failed requirement in the UK student visa. Here is exactly how it works in 2026 — and the mistakes that restart the clock.

CountryPilot Editorial TeamUpdated October 14, 20267 min read

Buried in the UK Student visa's financial requirement is a timing rule with no flexibility: your maintenance money must have been held for 28 days, and the window is measured against a specific closing date. Applicants with genuinely sufficient funds are refused every cycle for getting the dates wrong. This guide explains the 28-day rule exactly as it operates in 2026.

Quick Answer

You must show £1,529 per month in London or £1,171 per month elsewhere (up to 9 months, 2026) held for a continuous 28-day period ending no more than 31 days before your application. The balance must not dip below the required level on any day of the window. The closing balance date — the last day of the 28 days — is the date the caseworker checks.

London £1,529/month · Elsewhere £1,171/month · Held 28 days · Updated 2026-10-14 · Reading time 7 minutes

What the rule actually requires

The maintenance requirement has two halves: the amount and the holding period. The amount is £1,529 per month if you study in London, £1,171 per month elsewhere, for up to 9 months — so up to £13,761 (London) or £10,539 (elsewhere) for a full academic year, on top of any unpaid tuition the CAS shows. The holding period is 28 continuous days during which the funds are present at or above the required level every single day. The 28-day window must end no more than 31 days before the application date.

The closing balance date: the date that matters

Caseworkers do not check "about a month ago" — they check the closing balance date: the final day of your 28-day window, as shown on the statement. Your statement must therefore show the full 28-day run ending on a date within 31 days of applying, with the balance at or above the required level throughout. A statement printed a week too early, or one whose window ended 40 days before the application, fails the rule even if the money is plainly there.

What breaks the 28 days

Three classic failures. 1) A dip below the level on any day inside the window — even one day, even by £1 — restarts the 28 days from the recovery date. 2) Moving the money between accounts mid-window without statements covering both accounts for the full period. 3) A lump sum arriving late: topping up to the required level on day 20 means the 28 days start on day 20, not day 1. The rule measures the lowest balance across the window, not the final one.

Whose money counts

The funds must be yours, or your parent's or legal guardian's — with the relationship evidenced (birth certificate, and a consent letter where a parent's account is used). Money must be in cash funds in a regulated financial institution; investments, overdrafts, and assets that cannot be immediately accessed do not count. Where a parent's account is used, the 28-day holding rule applies to that account exactly as it would to yours.

Proving it: the statement

The bank statement (or official letter) must show the account holder, the institution, the balance across the full 28 days, and the closing date — and it must be dated within the 31-day freshness window. Electronic statements are acceptable where the rules allow them; what matters is that the caseworker can verify the 28-day run without gaps. Before you apply, lay the statement against a calendar and check every day of the window — that ten-minute exercise is the whole of this guide's advice.

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

How much money must be held for 28 days?

£1,529/month in London or £1,171/month elsewhere, up to 9 months (2026) — plus any unpaid tuition shown on the CAS.

Does one day below the level fail the application?

Yes. The balance must stay at or above the required level for all 28 days; a single dip restarts the clock.

Can I use my parents' bank statements?

Yes — a parent's or legal guardian's account counts, with proof of relationship and a consent letter, and the 28-day rule applies to that account.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

The 28-day rule is a calendar exercise, not a wealth test: the right amount, in the right account, untouched below the line for 28 straight days, evidenced by a statement whose closing date sits inside the 31-day window. Check it day by day before you apply.
