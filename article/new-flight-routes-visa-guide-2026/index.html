New Flight Routes 2026: Visa Guide for Travelers

Airlines are opening a wave of brand-new international routes this year and next — from St. Louis to Iceland to Kuala Lumpur to Busan. Here is what each route is, and the visa and entry paperwork you need before you book.

Quick Answer

Four headline new routes for 2026–27: Icelandair opens St. Louis to Reykjavík, Malaysia Airlines returns to Busan from Kuala Lumpur, Etihad links Abu Dhabi with Calgary, and Cathay Pacific plans Hong Kong to Almaty. Each destination has different paperwork — Schengen visa-free or €90 visa for Iceland, K-ETA for South Korea, eTA or visitor visa for Canada, and visa-free or e-Visa entry for Kazakhstan. Sort out the entry route before booking; visa rules apply to your passport, not your flight.

How to use this route visa guide

A route launch says nothing about your right to enter the country at the other end. Entry rules always turn on your passport, not the airline or the city you fly from. Work through each route below: confirm the route details, find your nationality in the entry table, and read the full country guide linked in each section before paying for anything non-refundable.

Icelandair: St. Louis → Reykjavík, Iceland

Icelandair announced in October 2026 that St. Louis Lambert (STL) becomes its newest US gateway next summer: non-stop flights to Reykjavík from May 14 to October 24, up to four times a week (Mondays, Wednesdays, Fridays, Sundays) on a Boeing 737 MAX 8, opening one-stop connections to more than 35 European destinations. For Europeans, it is also a new direct way into the US Midwest.

Iceland is in Schengen. US and UK nationals are visa-free for up to 90 days per 180-day period; travellers from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh need the €90 Schengen C visa. Days spent anywhere else in Schengen count toward the same 90/180 budget, so a Reykjavík stopover plus onward Europe travel can silently exhaust it. Full breakdown: our Iceland visa and entry requirements guide.

Malaysia Airlines: Kuala Lumpur → Busan, South Korea

Malaysia Airlines resumes non-stop Kuala Lumpur–Busan on December 2, 2026 — its first service to South Korea's second city in 28 years (last flown 1998). It runs four times a week (Tuesdays, Wednesdays, Saturdays, Sundays) on a Boeing 737-8 and will be the only non-stop KL–Busan link, complementing the carrier's Seoul flights.

South Korea entry turns on the K-ETA: KRW 10,000, three-year multiple entry, up to 90 days per entry. A K-ETA exemption for previously exempt nationalities runs through December 31, 2026. Travellers from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh generally need a visitor visa (USD 40 single-entry, up to 90 days) before travel. Details: our South Korea visa and entry requirements guide.

Etihad: Abu Dhabi → Calgary, Canada

Etihad launches Abu Dhabi–Calgary — the first non-stop link between the Middle East and Western Canada — from November 3, 2026, four times a week on a Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner: a one-stop route to Alberta, Banff and the Rockies for Gulf and South Asia travellers, and a direct Gulf link for Canadians.

Visa-exempt nationals flying to Canada need an eTA — CAD $7 (2026), linked to your passport, checked at boarding. Everyone else needs a visitor visa (CAD $100 per person) plus biometrics (CAD $85 individual / CAD $170 family max) at a visa application centre. Late-September 2026 processing actuals ran about 27 days for visitor visas from India — apply months ahead. Details: our Canada visa and entry requirements guide.

Cathay Pacific: Hong Kong → Almaty, Kazakhstan

Cathay Pacific announced in June 2026 its first Central Asia passenger route: direct Hong Kong–Almaty, planned from January 2027, three times a week on an Airbus A330 — around seven hours, removing the current routing through mainland Chinese hubs.

57 countries are visa-free for up to 30 days — including the US and UK. Everyone else uses the e-Visa via vmp.gov.kz (tourist single US$60 / double US$90, invitation required, up to 5 working days' processing) with entry points limited to Astana and Almaty airports only. Because the service is announced rather than launched, keep itineraries flexible around the January 2027 start date. Details: our Kazakhstan visa and entry requirements guide.

Transit and connection traps

  • The US has no sterile international transit. Europeans connecting through St. Louis — or any US airport — clear full US immigration. Visa Waiver travellers need ESTA ($40.27); everyone else needs a C-1 transit visa ($185). See our US visa and entry requirements guide.
  • Schengen day-counting is cumulative. Time in Iceland and any other Schengen country shares one 90-days-per-180 budget.
  • Kazakhstan's e-Visa is airport-locked — Astana and Almaty airports only; land-border crossings are not covered.
  • South Korea allows airside transit without a visa for most travellers, but stepping out of the transit area triggers the normal K-ETA or visa requirement.

Pre-booking paperwork checklist

  • Identify your entry route by passport — visa-free, e-authorization, e-Visa, or consulate visa — before comparing fares
  • Confirm processing time against your travel date — weeks for Canada TRVs and Schengen visas, days for K-ETA and the Kazakhstan e-Visa
  • Read the full country guide for your destination before paying non-refundable fares
  • For announced-not-yet-launched routes (Cathay Almaty), keep itineraries flexible until schedules firm up
  • Verify every fact on the official portal on the day you apply — fees and exemptions change

Useful answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Which new flight routes launched in 2026?

Icelandair's new St. Louis–Reykjavík service; Malaysia Airlines returning to Kuala Lumpur–Busan after 28 years (from December 2, 2026); Etihad's first non-stop between the Middle East and Western Canada (Abu Dhabi–Calgary, from November 2026); and Cathay Pacific's planned Hong Kong–Almaty service (from January 2027).

Do I need a visa to visit Iceland on the new St. Louis flight?

US and UK nationals are visa-free for up to 90 days per 180 days under Schengen rules. Travellers from India, Pakistan, Nigeria, the Philippines and Bangladesh need a €90 Schengen C visa. Details: our Iceland visa guide.

What entry paperwork does South Korea require for the new Busan route?

Visa-waiver nationals need a K-ETA (KRW 10,000, three-year multiple entry); previously exempt nationalities are exempt through December 31, 2026. Others need a visitor visa — USD 40 single-entry, up to 90 days. Details: our South Korea visa guide.

Can I transit through the US on the Icelandair St. Louis flight without a visa?

The US has no sterile international transit, so connecting travellers clear full immigration. Visa Waiver nationals need ESTA ($40.27); everyone else needs a C-1 transit visa ($185). Details: our US visa guide.

Is Kazakhstan visa-free for tourists?

For 57 countries — including the US and UK — yes, up to 30 days visa-free. Everyone else applies for an e-Visa (tourist single US$60 / double US$90, invitation required, entry via Astana or Almaty airports only). Details: our Kazakhstan visa guide.

The practical takeaway

Bottom Line

New routes open tempting doors, but entry is decided by your passport, not the airline. Before booking, run the route check for your nationality — K-ETA, eTA, ESTA, Schengen visa or Kazakhstan e-Visa — and leave enough lead time for consulate processing where it applies.
